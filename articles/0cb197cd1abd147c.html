UN Opens Applications for 2026 Young Professionals Programme, Entry Jobs in Nairobi, Other Cities

The United Nations has opened applications for its 2026 Young Professionals Programme (YPP), offering entry-level professional positions in Development and Sustainability at the P-1 and P-2 grades across several UN duty stations, including Nairobi.

The application deadline is 30 November 2026 at 11:59 p.m. New York time.

The YPP is the UN Secretariat&#039;s primary recruitment pathway for early-career professionals seeking to serve as international civil servants.

The programme combines an entrance examination with structured professional development and is open exclusively to nationals of countries that are unrepresented or under-represented within the Secretariat.

The list of eligible countries shifts each year.

Five African nations are included in the 2026 cycle: Angola, Equatorial Guinea, Liberia, Libya, and São Tomé and Príncipe.

Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Nigeria, Ghana, South Africa , and Ethiopia do not appear on the list, making their nationals ineligible to apply through the standard nationality-based route this year.

Kenyans who hold dual nationality with one of the qualifying countries may still be considered, provided they enter that nationality in the "Country of Nationality" field and attach a government-issued photo ID confirming it.

The UN has clarified that nationality at birth and other secondary nationalities are not taken into account for YPP eligibility.

To qualify, candidates must be nationals of a participating Member State, hold at least a bachelor&#039;s degree in a relevant field, be 32 years old or younger during the examination year, meaning born on or after January 1, 1994, and demonstrate proficiency in English or French at UN Level II or above.

Prior work experience is not mandatory, though it may be used to rank applicants when large volumes of applications are received from a single country.

The 2026 examination covers economics, statistics, social affairs, environmental issues, sustainable development, technology and digital transformation, and programme management support.

Assessment proceeds in three stages: application screening, a written examination, and an oral interview.

The written exam is split into two parts, with Part I tentatively scheduled for February 2027 and the oral stage anticipated for mid-2027.

Candidates placed on the resulting roster are eligible to fill vacancies across the UN Secretariat for three years, though placement does not guarantee appointment.

Those offered positions receive a two-year fixed-term contract, with the possibility of a continuing contract subject to satisfactory performance.

Kenyans who are ineligible this cycle are encouraged to monitor future editions of the programme, as the participating country list is revised annually.

Alternative entry routes into the UN system include the UN Volunteers programme, junior professional officer schemes, internships, and mid-level vacancies open to all Member State nationals.
