Wetang’ula says CCTV showed MP pocketing colleague’s phone

Speaker Moses Wetang’ula says CCTV showed an MP pocketing another legislator’s phone, then denying it when confronted with the footage. He says he urged the member to reflect on the incident rather than describing a formal sanction.

Wetang’ula’s account, reported by Citizen Digital on November 25, 2026 , places the incident during a period of heightened tension in Parliament after a disagreement between members. The Speaker said CCTV footage was brought to his office and that he watched it with Majority Leader Kimani Ichung’wah and other MPs.

According to Wetang’ula, the footage showed one legislator putting another member’s phone in his pocket. The member was summoned and shown the video, the Speaker said, but denied taking the phone even while seeing himself in the footage. Citizen Digital quotes Wetang’ula saying, “We actually saw a member pocket the phone of another member.”

The report does not identify either legislator, say when the phone was taken, or describe what happened to the device afterwards. It also does not establish whether the member returned it. The account is therefore a public description by the Speaker, not a report identifying the person involved or detailing an independently examined recording.

Wetang’ula said he advised the MP to “wrestle with his conscience for the rest of this term,” adding that some conduct was not expected of members. He used the episode to urge MPs to behave honourably and protect the dignity of the House.

That is the response the report records. It does not say that the Speaker referred the matter to a parliamentary committee, opened a disciplinary process, or announced a penalty. Nor does it report a response from the unnamed MP. Without those details, it is not possible to tell whether the Speaker’s private rebuke was the only action taken or whether any further process followed.

The coverage also leaves the circumstances of the disagreement unclear. Wetang’ula linked the phone incident to a period when tensions were high, but Citizen Digital does not say what the members were arguing about or whether the disagreement involved the people connected to the phone. The account does not explain why the footage was brought to the Speaker, who first reported the phone missing, or whether other witnesses were consulted.
