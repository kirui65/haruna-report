Kenyans have until Friday, November 20, 2026, at 5:00 pm to submit their views on the government&#039;s proposed spending and borrowing plans for the 2027/28 financial year, following a notice issued by the Clerk of the National Assembly on Monday, November 16, 2026.

The notice, issued under Article 118(1)(b) of the Constitution, invites members of the public and stakeholders to submit memoranda on two documents: the 2027 Budget Policy Statement.

The statement outlines the government&#039;s proposed expenditure priorities for the coming financial year and the Medium-Term Debt Management Strategy, which covers the 2027/28 to 2029/30 period and details how the government plans to finance its budgetary needs while managing the costs and risks of public debt.

According to the National Treasury&#039;s October 2026 draft, total proposed spending for the 2027/28 financial year stands at KSh 5.323 trillion.

Of that, KSh 929.1 billion is earmarked for domestic borrowing and KSh 423.8 billion from external lenders, bringing total projected borrowing to KSh 1.321 trillion.

Recurrent expenditure accounts for the largest portion of the budget at KSh 4.02 trillion, while development spending is allocated only KSh 915.8 billion.

The projected fiscal deficit sits at KSh 1.353 trillion. The disparity between recurrent and development spending is expected to draw significant attention from civil society and professional bodies during the submissions period.

Memoranda may be delivered in person to the Office of the Clerk, First Floor, Main Parliament Buildings, Nairobi, or submitted by email to cna@parliament.go.ke.

Submissions must include the name and contact details of the individual or organisation making them.

The four-day window is notably shorter than periods typically allowed for public participation on budget documents, raising concerns about how effectively citizens and organisations can engage before the deadline.

The relevant departmental committees will consider all submissions before presenting their reports to the full House for a vote.

The 2027/28 budget carries particular significance, as it will be the final full budget of President William Ruto&#039;s first term, with its borrowing levels, spending balance, and revenue measures likely to shape the economic and political environment heading into the 2027 General Election.
