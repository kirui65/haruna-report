A former governor in Kenya has been sentenced to 25 years in prison for his role in the murder of a 26-year-old university student who was seven months pregnant with his child.

The BBC reported that the High Court on Friday handed down the sentence to Zacharia Okoth Obado, former governor of Migori County, alongside two former aides, over the 2018 killing of Sharon Belyne Otieno.

The court established that Obado and Otieno had been involved in an &#8220;intimate relationship&#8221; which &#8220;subsequently led to a pregnancy with the deceased that the governor was unhappy about&#8221;.

Obado&#8217;s former personal assistant, Michael Oyamo, was also sentenced to 25 years in prison, while former county clerk Caspal Ojwang&#8217; Obiero received an 18-year jail term.

In its findings, the court held that Obado supplied the motive and financial resources for the murder, while Oyamo coordinated its execution and Obiero provided logistical support and assisted in concealing the crime.

Presiding judge Cecilia Githua said Obado&#8217;s absence from Migori County when the killing occurred did not remove his responsibility for the crime.

The judge rejected the prosecution&#8217;s request for the death penalty, as well as Obado&#8217;s plea for a non-custodial sentence.

Otieno was killed between September 3 and 4, 2018, after leaving a hotel in Rongo, south-western Kenya, with journalist Barrack Oduor.

She had contacted Oduor to discuss her relationship with Obado, who was governor at the time. The pair were abducted shortly afterwards.

Oduor escaped by jumping out of a moving vehicle, and his account of the incident provided investigators with a crucial lead.

Otieno&#8217;s body was subsequently discovered near Kodera Forest. A post-mortem examination established that she died from strangulation and excessive bleeding caused by multiple stab wounds.

The court also confirmed that Obado was the biological father of Otieno&#8217;s unborn child.

Obado and his co-defendants were convicted of murder on July 23, 2026, after a lengthy trial during which prosecutors presented 42 witnesses and 81 exhibits.

However, the court declined to award compensation to Otieno&#8217;s family, ruling that there was insufficient evidence to establish the financial losses they had claimed.

Before the sentencing, Otieno&#8217;s mother appealed to the judge not to send Obado to prison, describing his imprisonment as &#8220;another death in the family&#8221;.

She urged the court to consider a non-custodial punishment, arguing that Otieno&#8217;s three children, from different relationships, needed his support.

Judge Githua, however, maintained that the family&#8217;s circumstances could not take precedence over the court&#8217;s responsibility to impose an appropriate punishment.

The family, through their lawyer, Antony Opondo, told Kenya&#8217;s Daily Nation that they would appeal the conviction, seeking Obado&#8217;s acquittal.

Otieno&#8217;s murder triggered protests and renewed public discussions about violence against women in Kenya.

A national survey found that at least 34 per cent of women had experienced physical violence in 2022.

The case has also attracted attention because criminal trials involving politicians of Obado&#8217;s stature are relatively rare in Kenya.

Obado served two terms as governor of Migori County in western Kenya. He initially moved between a smaller political party and the region&#8217;s dominant opposition movement before backing President William Ruto.

He formally joined Ruto&#8217;s ruling party in 2023 and became a regional organiser.

Both Obado&#8217;s legal team and those representing Otieno said they would appeal against the court&#8217;s decision.
