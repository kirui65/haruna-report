Inside proposed route, stations, and design of Naivasha-Kisumu-Malaba SGR

WhatsAppFacebookX

Plans to extend Kenya's Standard Gauge Railway from Naivasha to Kisumu and onward to Malaba detail a major rail corridor across western Kenya. The proposed expansion follows earlier ground-breaking efforts by Kenya Railways and arrives alongside ongoing roadworks on the Rironi-Mau Summit corridor.

According to technical specifications released by Kenya Railways, the main railway line will span 263.77 kilometres. The alignment will connect key counties and towns, establishing a continuous rail link from the Rift Valley to the Ugandan border. Within Narok County alone, the track will cover approximately 94.1 kilometres before advancing through Bomet, Sotik, Sondu, and Ahero into Kisumu County.

Civil engineering works along the route include 15 railway bridges with a combined length of 43 kilometres. Engineers also plan to bore eight tunnels spanning 13.863 kilometres in total to navigate the region's varied terrain. Drainage infrastructure will require 460 culverts covering nine kilometres along the corridor.

Land acquisition and right-of-way parameters have been defined for the entire stretch. The railway corridor will measure 70 metres wide along the main line, expanding to 100 metres within designated station areas. Kenya Railways has mapped out 25 stations across the network, comprising one major station, six intermediate stations, and 18 crossing stations to manage train traffic.

Beyond the main track, the blueprint includes an 8.9-kilometre branch line connecting directly to Kisumu Port. This spur will incorporate one bridge and eight culverts. Crucially, the broader project scope encompasses constructing a new port facility in Kisumu to expand cargo handling capacity on Lake Victoria.

Kenya's existing SGR network currently terminates at Suswa in Narok County, following the completion of Phase 1 from Mombasa to Nairobi and Phase 2A from Nairobi to Naivasha. Extending the track to Malaba completes the Kenyan section of the Northern Corridor rail master plan, linking the port of Mombasa directly to landlocked neighbours in East Africa.

Project documentation outlines several economic and logistical objectives tied to the railway extension. Planners expect the line to strengthen the regional integrated transportation network and improve cross-border connectivity. Lower freight transit times and improved passenger mobility are projected to stimulate regional and national economic growth.

Additional projected outcomes include supporting tourism and creating construction and operational employment for local communities along the route. In Kisumu, the upgraded port infrastructure is intended to support blue economy activities around Lake Victoria. Information regarding the proposed project specifications and design was originally reported by People Daily.
