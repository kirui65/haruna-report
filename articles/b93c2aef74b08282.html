“Who is the traitor?”: Babu and Sifuna clash over who leads Linda Mwananchi

The newspapers Friday, November 20, covered an array of topics, including the growing political feud between Linda Mwananchi leader and Nairobi senator Edwin Sifuna and The Mwananchi Party leader and Embakasi East Babu Owino.

The newspaper reported on a public spat between Nairobi senator Edwin Sifuna and Embakasi East MP Babu Owino having escalated into a full-blown dispute over who betrayed whom within the Linda Mwananchi Movement, with both politicians offering conflicting accounts of what was originally agreed.

Sifuna broke his silence on Thursday during an interview on Radio Citizen&#039;s Jambo Kenya programme, responding directly to accusations Babu made on November 13, in which the MP claimed their initial understanding was that Sifuna would contest the presidency while Babu chaired the party.

Babu told listeners that the arrangement shifted later without his knowledge.

Sifuna denied that characterisation, telling the same programme that the founding agreement was simpler: each leader would leave their individual party and join a single platform that could advance their shared political goals.

After reviewing available parties, the group settled on The Equitable Party (TEP) , which was later renamed Linda Mwananchi Movement.

He named South Suba MP Caroli Omondi, former Meru Governor Kawira Mwangaza, and Mavoko MP Patrick Makau among those who abandoned their former parties to join the new vehicle.

Omondi had previously served under ODM, Makau under Kalonzo Musyoka&#039;s Wiper Patriotic Front, and Mwangaza under Umoja na Maendeleo Party.

Sifuna said the group convened to discuss the party&#039;s leadership structure and collectively chose him to lead as both party chair and presidential candidate, insisting the outcome was not self-imposed.

He challenged Babu&#039;s suggestion by questioning whether any political party in Kenya runs its presidential candidate separately from its party leadership.

Tensions between the two leaders first surfaced openly in September, when Babu announced he would scale back his attendance at Linda Mwananchi gatherings to focus on his gubernatorial bid for Nairobi County.

Babu has consistently downplayed suggestions of rivalry, arguing the two are chasing different offices, but the exchange of accusations points to a more fundamental breakdown in trust.

Political analysts have noted that if Sifuna consolidates opposition support for a presidential run, Nairobi&#039;s governorship could become a bargaining chip in broader coalition negotiations, which may complicate Babu&#039;s own ambitions.

The fallout within the movement extends beyond the two principals. Siaya governor James Orengo has since joined the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) after distancing himself from Sifuna&#039;s camp, following internal resistance to Sifuna&#039;s presidential aspirations. Saboti MP Caleb Amisi has also departed to establish his own outfit, the People&#039;s Renaissance Movement (PM).

The newspaper reported on the opposition leaders dramatically walking out of an election preparedness meeting convened by the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC), demanding sweeping reforms before the August 2027 general election.

The walkout, led by DCP leader Rigathi Gachagua , took place Thursday, November 11, after the opposition accused the IEBC of failing to consult stakeholders adequately on election preparations and of ignoring proposals to establish technical teams to examine electoral integrity concerns.

Former Chief Justice David Maraga, who accompanied the opposition delegation, said Kenyans deserve a clean and verifiable voters&#039; register before the next election. The bloc called for an independent audit of the register to remove deceased persons, duplicate registrations, and ineligible voters.

On results transmission, the opposition proposed that each of the 290 constituencies, as well as the diaspora vote, should have its own dedicated server.

They further demanded that political parties, candidates, and media organisations be granted real-time visibility into results as they are relayed from polling stations.

The opposition also wants presidential results declared on the basis of figures announced at polling stations and constituencies, rather than being recalculated at a single national tallying centre.

Additional demands include strengthened electoral offence laws, with severe penalties for officials found to have altered results or interfered with election systems.

The bloc also called for full transparency in the procurement, testing, and auditing of electoral technology, as well as an open recruitment process for election officials.

Ballot paper printing and custody were also flagged as areas requiring closer scrutiny.

Gachagua stated that the opposition could not continue participating in the meeting because the agenda did not address issues of public concern.

Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka said the delegation had attended in good faith but left unconvinced that the commission was prepared to operate independently and impartially.

IEBC chairperson Erastus Ethekon expressed regret over the opposition&#039;s decision to leave, maintaining that the commission had called the meeting specifically to brief stakeholders on its preparedness.

Ethekon disclosed that technology procurement, which was initiated in July, has been delayed by ongoing court proceedings.

The newspaper reported on the oncoming recruitment exercise for Police Constables scheduled for December 10 , running from 8 am to 5 pm at designated centres across all 47 counties in Kenya as announced by the National Police Service (NPS).

The advertisement signed by Inspector General Douglas Kanja Kirocho, sets out the eligibility criteria, application process, and warning against corrupt practices.

To qualify, candidates must be Kenyan citizens aged between 18 and 28 years, holding a valid National Identity Card and a minimum Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) grade of D+, with at least D+ in either English or Kiswahili.

Applicants must also be physically and medically fit, with no criminal record or pending criminal charges.

Height requirements set the minimum at 5 feet 8 inches for male candidates and 5 feet 3 inches for female candidates. Female applicants must not be pregnant at the time of recruitment or at any point during the entire training period.

The recruitment draws its legal authority from Articles 238, 239, 244 and 245 of the Constitution, alongside Articles 10, 27(8) and 232, as well as the National Police Service Act.

Prospective candidates are required to download the prescribed application form from the NPS websites, www.nationalpolice.go.ke, www.kenyapolice.go.ke, www.administrationpolice.go.ke, or www.dci.go.ke, or collect one from the nearest Huduma Centre.

Completed forms, together with originals and copies of academic certificates, a National Identity Card, and a birth certificate, must be presented at the designated recruitment centre on the day of recruitment.

All applicants must arrive at their respective centres by 8 am.

The 423 recruitment centres are spread across sub-counties in all 47 counties, with venues ranging from stadiums and sports grounds to schools, police stations, and District County Commissioners&#039; offices.

Examples include Kisauni Sub-County Police Headquarters in Mombasa and Moi Sports Centre Kasarani in Nairobi.

Candidates affiliated with the National Youth Service (NYS) are directed to report to NYS Headquarters in Ruaraka, which is listed as centre number 423. NYS candidates must additionally present a discharge certificate with a conduct rating of at least "GOOD."

The NPS stressed that the recruitment process is entirely free and open to all eligible candidates. Bribery, corruption, or the submission of false or misleading information to gain an advantage in the process constitutes a criminal offence.

Members of the public are encouraged to report any malpractice to the nearest police station or through the following channels: the toll-free number 0800 722 203, the emergency lines 996 and 911, or the hashtag #FichuaKwaDCI.

The NPS confirmed that all reports will be handled with strict confidentiality.
