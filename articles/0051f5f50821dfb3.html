Nairobi Water Announces Mass Disconnections, Warns Residents of Fines Up to Ksh100,000

WhatsAppFacebookX

Nairobi residents with unpaid water bills or illegal connections face heavy financial penalties, reconnection fees, and possible jail terms after the Nairobi City Water and Sewerage Company (NCWSC) launched a mass disconnection drive on Thursday, November 26.

The operation began in Utawala estate, covering the Mihango area and the wider upper Embakasi region, before expanding to other estates across the city. Under the utility’s enforcement rules, residents caught with irregular connections risk fines of up to Ksh30,000 for domestic use and up to Ksh100,000 for commercial premises, alongside potential prison sentences.

Those whose supply is cut off will also face steep charges to restore services. Water reconnection costs Ksh5,000 plus a double deposit, while sewer reconnection ranges between Ksh15,000 and Ksh25,000 depending on the type of dwelling.

"We are on the ground conducting a mass water disconnection exercise. Pay your water bill early to avoid service disruptions," NCWSC said in a statement.

To prevent wrongful disconnections during the sweep, the company instructed customers with active installment payment plans to place copies of their agreements right next to their water meters.

"If you have an instalment payment agreement, display it next to your water meter. Let's pay our water bills so we can enjoy an uninterrupted water supply," the agency added.

The utility said the drive is meant to recover unpaid arrears, enforce payment obligations, and ensure only customers with regularized, legal connections receive water. It urged residents to clear pending bills and formalize their connections immediately.

Thursday's operation extends a series of aggressive enforcement drives in the wider Embakasi area. In May and August 2026, NCWSC carried out similar disconnections in Embakasi East following a grace period, leaving several households without water for weeks.
