George Ruto: Photos Show Aftermath of Accident Involving First Son's 98 Sacco Matatu

Fresh photos have emerged showing the state of Baba Yaga, the John Wick-themed matatu owned by President William Ruto&#039;s son George Ruto , following a collision on the night of Friday, November 13.

The images reveal considerable damage to the vehicle&#039;s front end.

The bronze-coloured panels are torn and peeling back with jagged edges running across the bonnet and around the grille, while rust-brown patches show through where the trim has come away.

The left side took the worst of it, with a crumpled section around the headlight and both headlight frames twisted out of shape.

The front bumper is visibly bent and uneven. The windscreen still carries the distinctive "John Wick" banner and the orange "Baba Yaga" decal, with the wipers slightly displaced.

Despite the visible wreckage, several of the matatu&#039;s signature features survived the impact.

The chrome grille, the "98 Speed" badge, the three red-rimmed driving lights on the bumper and the roof lights all appear intact.

The collision occurred along Route 125 on the Ongata Rongai road and involved Detroit, another well-known matatu in the area.

Footage circulating online suggested the two vehicles had been racing shortly before they made contact.

Baba Yaga bore the brunt of the damage at the front, while Detroit sustained rear-end damage from the impact. No fatalities were reported in the initial coverage.

The matatu operates under George Ruto&#039;s 98 Sacco franchise and is managed by a crew running under the Raptors banner.

On Saturday, November 14, the crew addressed the incident on Instagram , writing:

The November collision was not an isolated incident. This was at least the third major accident involving Baba Yaga in 2026 alone.

The first occurred in April 2026, shortly after the vehicle joined George Ruto&#039;s fleet, when it reportedly collided with a taxi .

A second, more dramatic incident followed in late September 2026, when the matatu overturned into a roadside ditch along the same Rongai route.

The repeated accidents sparked debate online, with Kenyans calling out reckless driving behaviour and urging the National Transport and Safety Authority to clamp down on dangerous matatu conduct.

Several social media users pressed for stricter enforcement of road safety regulations on the busy route.
