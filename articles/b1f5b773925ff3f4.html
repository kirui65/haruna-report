ADVANCING CRITICAL CARE EXPERTISE FOR MILITARY HEALTHCARE EXCELLENCE

WhatsAppFacebookX

Specialised medical expertise is vital to saving lives, improving patient outcomes and sustaining operational readiness within the Kenya Defence Forces (KDF). As military healthcare demands continue to evolve, investment in advanced clinical training remains essential to developing a skilled, responsive and professionally competent healthcare workforce.

The Defence College of Health Sciences (DCHS), a College of the National Defence University-Kenya (NDU-K), officially opened the November 2026 cohort of the Higher Diploma in Kenya Registered Critical Care Nursing (HDCCN) programme, marking another milestone in advancing specialised nursing education within the Defence Forces.

The programme is designed to equip nursing personnel with advanced clinical knowledge, specialised competencies and practical skills essential for managing critically ill patients and delivering high-quality care in demanding healthcare environments.

Through the training, participants will enhance their professional proficiency in critical care nursing, contributing to improved patient management, clinical decision-making and the delivery of specialised medical services across military healthcare facilities.

Beyond individual professional development, the programme represents a strategic investment in institutional healthcare capacity, ensuring that the KDF maintains a pool of highly trained medical personnel capable of responding to complex healthcare needs in both operational and non-operational settings.

The initiative reflects DCHS&#8217;s central role in advancing medical education, professional excellence and specialised healthcare training within the Defence Forces.

By investing in critical care expertise, the KDF continues to build a resilient, competent and future-ready military healthcare system capable of safeguarding the health of its personnel and delivering quality medical services to those under its care.
