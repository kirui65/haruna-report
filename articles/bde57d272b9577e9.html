The gun within: Crimes involving police officers rise to 248

Police constable Raphael Ojiambo, 24, was shot dead at the Wikithuki Border Patrol Unit training camp in Tseikuru, Kitui County on October 25, 2024.

Investigations pointed to the young officer being killed by another constable, Hussein Yusuf Ali, who was arrested three kilometres from the camp a day later.

The two officers had recently graduated from the Administration Police Training College in Embakasi and were undergoing further training before deployment to the Border Patrol Unit.

In Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County, police constable Lillian Biwott was arrested on September 5, 2026, after allegedly shooting and killing her husband, Victor Kipchumba, following a long-running family dispute.

Biwott, an Administration Police officer attached to the Kenya Bureau of Standards, reportedly returned home after reporting for duty before the argument escalated into the shooting.

Uasin Gishu County Police Commander Benjamin Mwanthi confirmed that the suspect was being held and would be arraigned in court.

The two are among more than 250 crime incidents involving police officers in the past year.

A report on national security tabled before Parliament by President William Ruto has revealed that crimes involving police officers increased to 248 between September, 2025 and August, 2026.

Since conclusion of the report, there have been more incidents, for instance the murder of Raphael Ojiambo.

The National Police Service has advertised for recruitment of officers, less than a year after a similar exercise that netted 10,000 new recruits. A source at the NPS said that the fresh exercise is targeting 5,000 recruits.

As part of initiatives to lower the number of crimes involving police officers, the National Police Service Commission has established a counselling unit to support officers facing mental-health and substance-abuse challenges. Police authorities have also acknowledged the trauma associated with maintaining law and order in difficult circumstances.

On January 14, 2026, a Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) ranger, John Ndichu, allegedly shot dead Police Constable Stanley Karithi in Kinango, Kwale County, following a disagreement over two goats reportedly collected for Christmas festivities.

The ranger also shot and wounded another police officer and a boda boda rider before turning his weapon on himself.

In some cases, the violence has unfolded inside police stations and officers’ quarters. In others, it has spilled into homes, workplaces and public spaces, leaving families devastated and raising concerns about the safeguards governing the use of government-issued weapons.

According to police accounts, the disagreement arose after officers questioned Ndichu about two goats allegedly donated by a rancher for Christmas celebrations but which had not been handed over as expected.

The ranger reportedly became angry, picked up his AK-47 rifle and opened fire.

The incident illustrated how seemingly minor disputes between personnel serving in uniformed agencies can escalate into deadly confrontations when firearms are readily accessible.

Away from the workplace, a series of incidents involving serving officers has also raised concerns about domestic violence and the use of service weapons in family disputes.

On July 20, 2026, Administration Police Sergeant John Kamau Ikenye allegedly shot dead his partner, Betha Wanja Gachoki, before turning his G3 rifle on himself at their staff quarters at Karoti Girls High School in Mwea East, Kirinyaga County.

The bodies were discovered after colleagues became concerned when Ikenye failed to report for duty.

Preliminary investigations indicated that Wanja had sustained gunshot wounds while lying in bed, while the officer was found seated in the sitting room with the rifle.

A neighbour, who said the couple had lived together for about three years, expressed disbelief at the tragedy.

“I used to buy items from his wife’s shop. They were very friendly. The lady comes from Difathas and they had been together for three years. We wonder what went wrong,” said Kimbimbi resident Stella Wangui.

Another neighbour said residents had heard gunshots but did not immediately appreciate their significance.

“We heard gunshots at around 4am but did not take them seriously. Later, we were shocked to learn that a police officer had killed his wife and then committed suicide. We suspect they may have quarrelled, although we do not know the cause,” the neighbour said.

The incident followed another shooting in Naivasha on January 1, 2026, when Corporal Collins Kochei allegedly shot Lucy Wanjiru Njoroge, a 34-year-old bar owner, before turning his AK-47 rifle on himself.

Kochei, who was attached to Tangi Tano Police Post in Gilgil, died after being rushed to hospital, while Njoroge was admitted in critical condition.

Detectives from the Directorate of Criminal Investigations launched investigations into the shooting.

The incidents have also included suspected suicides in which officers used their service firearms.

On February 17, 2026, Constable Joseph Komolomoi was found dead in his house at Forolle Police Lines in Marsabit County after allegedly shooting himself with his AK-47 rifle.

Colleagues reportedly heard a gunshot before breaking into the locked house and finding his body.

A note was recovered from the house, although police said it would undergo forensic examination to establish its authenticity.

On May 14, 2026, a police constable attached to Daystar Police Post in Athi River reportedly shot himself at his residence following a domestic disagreement with his 23-year-old spouse.

He was rushed to hospital in Mlolongo before being transferred to another facility in Kikuyu, where he later died.

A police report indicated that the firearm used had been officially issued to him. Investigators recovered a Scorpion rifle and a spent 9mm cartridge from the scene.

On June 2, 2026, another officer attached to the Diplomatic Police Unit and assigned to a Member of Parliament was found dead in his rented house in Kasarani, Nairobi.

Police recovered his service firearm and treated the incident as a suspected suicide pending investigations.

In Laikipia, Constable Peter Macharia Mwangi shot and killed 29-year-old Benson Kipkemboi Chirchir on December 14, 2025, before returning to his patrol base in Ndurumo, Rumuruti, and shooting himself.

Another officer attached to Ahero Police Station in Kisumu was found hanging in his house on January 21, 2026. According to an incident report, the officer had previously complained to colleagues about stress linked to unresolved domestic issues.

Beyond the individual tragedies, the incidents raise questions about whether the police service has adequate systems to identify officers experiencing psychological distress, domestic problems or conflicts with colleagues before situations escalate.

They also raise questions about firearm management, particularly when an officer is facing a domestic challenges, disciplinary difficulties or other circumstances that affect mental health.
