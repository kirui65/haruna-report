Kenya and Pakistan Move to Deepen Health Cooperation

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Cabinet Secretary (CS) for Health, Aden Duale held bilateral talks with Pakistan’s Federal Minister for National Health Services, Regulations and Coordination, Syed Mustafa Kamal, in Karachi, as the two countries work to formalize a structured Government-to-Government partnership in the health sector.

The meeting, hosted at Aga Khan University, marks a significant step towards concluding a Health Memorandum of Understanding that has been under negotiation since 2021.

The central focus of the discussions was the existing pharmaceutical trade relationship between the two nations. Pakistan currently exports 247 registered medicines to Kenya, with annual imports valued at approximately Sh1.4 billion.

In addition, 26 Pakistani manufacturing facilities have received Good Manufacturing Practice certification from Kenya’s Pharmacy and Poisons Board (PPB).

Duale emphasised the importance of moving beyond informal trade relations to a formalized framework with clear priorities and accountability mechanisms, enabling both countries to maximize the benefits of cooperation.

At the meeting, Kenya proposed a cooperation framework built on three pillars: regulatory cooperation, pharmaceutical manufacturing, and specialist training.

Under regulatory cooperation, the partnership aims to strengthen collaboration between the PPB and Pakistan’s Drug Regulatory Authority through information sharing and joint inspections, with the goal of improving access to safe, quality-assured medicines in both markets.

On manufacturing, the Cabinet Secretary encouraged Pakistani pharmaceutical companies to invest in Kenya’s industrial parks and Special Economic Zones.

“Such investments would support local production, technology transfer and industrial growth, while positioning Kenya as a regional pharmaceutical manufacturing and export hub serving the East African Community and the wider African Continental Free Trade Area,” he noted.

To tackle shortages in specialist healthcare personnel, particularly in oncology and nephrology, Kenya proposed an annual placement programme for Kenyan medical fellows in Pakistan’s specialised training institutions, with the first cohort targeted for 2027.

Similarly, the talks also explored cooperation in digital health, specifically drawing on Pakistan’s experience to strengthen Kenya’s pharmaceutical track-and-trace systems, in line with each country’s data protection and regulatory frameworks.

The meeting was hosted by Aga Khan University Vice-Chancellor and President Dr Sulaiman Shahabuddin and attended by Kenya’s High Commissioner to Pakistan, Lt Gen (Rtd) Peter Njiru, and Pharmacy and Poisons Board Chief Executive Officer Dr Ahmed Mohammed.

With the proposed framework, both countries appear positioned to convert years of trade engagement into a formal, mutually beneficial health partnership. The conclusion of the long-pending Memorandum of Understanding would provide the institutional foundation needed to drive regulatory alignment, investment and workforce development &#8211; outcomes with tangible implications for medicine access and healthcare capacity in Kenya and the wider region.
