"Demand is huge": PSC explains how gov't will hire 50k interns in January 2027

Kenyan graduates could soon have far greater access to government internships after the Public Service Commission outlined plans to grow available placements from 8,000 to 50,000 starting January 2027, following a directive from President William Ruto during the 2026 Mashujaa Day celebrations in Eldoret.

PSC Chairperson Francis Meja explained that the commission had settled on a standardised formula to bring predictability to how positions are spread across ministries, state departments, agencies and parastatals.

Each institution will be required to ring-fence internship slots equal to 10% of its approved staffing establishment, meaning an organisation with 1,000 approved posts would absorb 100 interns.

Previously, the National Treasury channelled roughly KSh 2 billion annually to fund approximately 8,000 internship positions. That figure, however, has long fallen short of demand. When the PSC last advertised 8,000 positions, it received 41,000 applications.

Meja described the internship scheme as a bridge between academic training and the job market, stressing that the target of 50,000 is a starting point rather than a ceiling.

Graduates who complete the programme will reportedly be given priority consideration when institutions post formal vacancies, although completing an internship does not guarantee permanent employment.

The PSC chairperson also revealed that talks with private-sector employers are progressing and that a further 50,000 positions are intended to be unlocked through industry partnerships by January 2027.

The PSC has opened applications for the 2026/2027 financial year, with the deadline set for December 14, 2026. Graduates are urged to apply only through the Commission&#039;s official recruitment platforms to avoid falling victim to fraudsters.

Elsewhere, TUKO also highlighted facts about the Public Service Commission’s 6,000 internship positions under the Affordable Housing Programme, including the fields on offer, eligibility requirements and application deadline.

The 12-month placements span technical and professional roles across the country, with 500 construction management slots among the largest allocations. Applications close on November 9, 2026, and candidates must have graduated from 2020 onwards.
