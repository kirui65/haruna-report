STEM key to realisation of Kenya’s first-world status, PS Oluga

WhatsAppFacebookX

The government is investing heavily in Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics (STEM) to realise its Singapore dream.

Medical Services Principal Secretary (PS), Dr. Ouma Oluga, speaking during a fundraiser for a STEM Training Center at Okela Comprehensive School in Rarieda Sub-county, said STEM is the backbone of the country’s future global standing.

“Today in the first world there are discussions on permanent underclass, which simply means if you are left behind in technology, then you will forever never catch up with the rest of the world, a situation which we want to avoid with our students, the youth and even our teachers,&#8221; enlightens Dr. Oluga.

The government must, therefore, endeavour to ensure students earmarked to build the country towards the first-world status are well trained on the emerging technologies.

&#8220;That&#8217;s why under the clarion call ‘Usawa ni Sawa&#8217;, the government is creating opportunities everywhere for students who want to realise their full potential.

Right now Artificial Intelligence (AI) is being used in manufacturing, assembling, agriculture and even in governance, which then means we can no longer ignore technology, which is the backbone for our future,&#8221; he stressed.

The PS observed that there is a need to properly equip schools across the Nyanza region and to upgrade infrastructure to train learners to help them realise their full potential in STEM education.

&#8220;Across the counties of Migori, Homa Bay, Kisumu and Siaya, there is a need to have adequate schools with proper STEM training facilities, which can effectively direct learners towards STEM education,” he emphasised.

Dr Oluga disclosed that the anticipated Sh250 million STEM training facility at Okela Comprehensive School will not only benefit learners from the locality but will also open doors to other students from the region.

&#8220;In this particular school, at the moment, we only have three students who are undertaking physics, and in the last 14 years, no single student has scored an ‘A’ minus or even a ‘B’ plain in sciences, yet the world is now being run through innovation and critical thinking.

We hope that this facility will change the negative trend. I have also spoken to over 700 head teachers from Siaya County to utilise the facility for their students&#8217; exposure once it&#8217;s completed,&#8221; said the PS.

Dr Oluga further revealed that the facility will also be used to train teachers from the region on AI skills to enable them to deliver their services effectively.
