Inaugural Sisibo Marathon to pay each winner record Sh5m

From left: New York Marathon Champion Sheila Chepkirui multiple Olympics and world champion Faith Kipyegon, Uasin Gishu County Governor Jonathan Bii, former 5,000m Commonwealth Games champion Mercy Cherono, President William Ruto's aide Farouk Kibet, Athletics Kenya President Jack Tuwei, Tokyo Marathon champion Rosemary Wanjiru, and former 800m world champion Janeth Jepkogei, during the launch of Sisibo Marathonin Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County on November 22, 2026.

Local athletes will fight for a share of Sh25 million prize purse in the inaugural edition of Sisibo Marathon.

Speaking on Sunday during the launch at Lobo Village in Eldoret, organisers said winners of men and women’s categories in the inaugural edition planned for December 19, 2026 in Eldoret, will each take home Sh5 million, the highest locally.

Runners-up will take home Sh3 million, with third-place finishers earning Sh1 million. The top 20 finishers in every category will get cash awards, with the fourth-placed finishers taking home Sh700,000.

Cash prizes will trickle down to the top 10 athletes, with the 10th-placed runners in both categories pocketing Sh150,000. Registration will be free of charge for all participants.

The prize money makes Sisibo Marathon the highest paying locally, followed by Nairobi City Marathon and Eldoret City Marathon. Winners of Nairobi City Marathon are paid Sh3.5 million each, while second and third-placed take home Sh2.25 million and 1.5 million respectively.

Each winner at the Eldoret City Marathon takes home Sh3.5 million, while second and third-placed finishers get Sh1.5 million and Sh1 million in that order.

Standard Chartered Nairobi Marathon awards winners cash prize of Sh2 million, while runners-up take home Sh1 million, and second runners-up Sh500,000.

On Sunday, National Olympic Committee of Kenya (NOC-K) First Vice President Barnaba Korir said the race is expected to attract more than 2,000 elite athletes.

“This is going to be a race of its kind in the country. Athletes will share a prize kitty of more than Sh25 million, with the men’s and women’s champions walking away with Sh5 million each,” said Korir.

Two-time Olympics marathon Champion Eliud Kipchoge makes his remarks during the launch of Sisibo Marathon in Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County on November 22, 2026.

Two-time Olympics marathon champion Eliud Kipchoge thanked the government and organisers for bringing a high-stakes race to Eldoret which is popularly known as “city of champions,” and urged corporates to support it so it can rival major races globally.

“The Sisibo Marathon has the potential to grow into a premier international marathon and put Eldoret even better on the global athletics map,” said Kipchoge.

Conversations around athlete welfare, doping, and financial management of local athletics dominated the speeches.

President William Ruto’s aide, Farouk Kibet, challenged the government to exempt athletes from taxes and appoint former sports personalities to key administrative roles to better safeguard athlete welfare.

“It is sad that our athletes bring fame to our country, yet in retirement, they are living in abject poverty because they squandered their earnings instead of investing it for the future,” Kibet said.

He urged the Ministry of Roads to construct dedicated training lanes for athletes to use on roads to curb road accidents involving runners.

From left: President William Ruto's aide Farouk Kibet, Athletics Kenya President Jack Tuwei and and three-time Boston Marathon champion Ibrahim Hussein, during the launch of Sisibo Marathon in Eldoret, Uasin Gishu County on November 22, 2026.

Athletics Kenya President Lt. Gen. (Rtd) Jackson Tuwei urged local athletes to emulate their Ethiopian counterparts by exercising financial discipline.

Tuwei also issued a stern warning to athletes who engage in doping, noting that a single positive test erases years of hard-won glory.

“It is painful to see athletes win races across the globe, only to water everything down by engaging in doping,” said Tuwei.

Uasin Gishu Governor Jonathan Bii welcomed the race to Eldoret and urged active runners to safeguard their legacies by investing in sustainable development projects.
