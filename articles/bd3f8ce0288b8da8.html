Nairobi, Kisumu, Homa Bay, and Migori Top List of Counties With Highest New HIV Infections

WhatsAppFacebookX

Nairobi, Kisumu, Homa Bay, and Migori counties recorded the highest numbers of new HIV infections in Kenya, accounting for nearly a third of all new cases reported nationwide, according to the latest National Syndemic Diseases Control Council report.

Data from the 2026 Kenya HIV Estimates reveals that out of the 13,936 new HIV infections recorded across the country over the past year, Nairobi County topped the chart with 1,516 new cases.

Kisumu County ranked second with 1,057 new infections, followed closely by Homa Bay County with 985 cases and Migori County with 970 cases.

Together, the top four counties recorded 4,528 new infections, underscoring persistent regional concentration of fresh transmission in the capital city and the Lake Victoria basin despite an overall 30 per cent decline in annual infections nationwide.

A demographic breakdown of the new infections shows that adult women bore a disproportionate share of fresh cases, with 7,363 new infections recorded among women aged 15 years and above compared to 3,177 among men.

Young people aged between 15 and 24 years accounted for 4,322 new infections, while adolescents aged between 10 and 19 years recorded 2,077 new cases and children aged 14 years and below accounted for 3,396 new infections.

In terms of cumulative population living with HIV across all 47 counties, 10 high burden counties account for nearly 60 per cent of the estimated 1,481,853 Kenyans living with the virus, led by Nairobi at 189,640, Homa Bay at 128,906, Kisumu at 114,986, Siaya at 96,304, and Migori at 80,105.

Busia, Kakamega, Mombasa, Kiambu, and Nakuru complete the top 10 counties with the highest cumulative HIV populations, while arid and northern counties including Lamu, Wajir, and Mandera recorded the lowest infection numbers nationwide.

The National Syndemic Diseases Control Council urged county governments to intensify targeted community testing, treatment retention, and youth prevention campaigns to curb new infections ahead of the 2030 elimination target.
