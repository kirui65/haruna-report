Court orders DCI boss to appear in court over missing Tanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya

WhatsAppFacebookX

The High Court has ordered the Director of Criminal Investigations (DCI) to appear before court on December 3, 2026, to explain the progress of investigations into the disappearance of Tanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya.

Justice Alexander Muteti issued the summons, directing the DCI to provide a detailed update on efforts to trace Mwaipaya, who was allegedly abducted in Ruai, Nairobi, on October 25, 2026.

In his ruling, Justice Muteti observed that the DCI was a competent and necessary witness capable of providing critical information on the investigations, particularly because the Inspector General of Police had informed the court that the matter was being handled by the DCIO Ruai, whose officers fall under the DCI’s command.

The judge expressed concern over the time that had elapsed since Mwaipaya’s disappearance, stressing that the State could not allow investigations into alleged enforced disappearances to continue indefinitely without providing meaningful updates to the court.

The court underscored that the matter raised serious constitutional concerns, including Mwaipaya’s right to life under Article 26 and his right to freedom and security of the person under Article 29 of the Constitution.

The applicants had sought to have both the DCI and the Deputy Inspector General of Police summoned to explain police operations and the steps taken to establish the activist’s whereabouts.

They argued that the appearance of the senior security officials would enhance accountability and help the court establish what the police had done to locate Mwaipaya.

However, the respondents opposed the application, maintaining that the Inspector General had already provided the National Police Service’s position and that the applicants had failed to demonstrate what additional information the two officers would bring before the court.

The Attorney General’s counsel further argued that the application was an attempt to review previous court orders.

In determining the application, Justice Muteti declined to summon the Deputy Inspector General, finding that his testimony was unlikely to provide substantially new information beyond what had already been presented by the Inspector General.

However, the judge ruled that the DCI’s direct involvement in overseeing criminal investigations made his appearance necessary to account for the progress of the probe.

Justice Muteti further emphasised that investigating alleged human rights violations was a constitutional obligation of the State and that police investigations must be conducted seriously, independently and expeditiously.

The court warned against treating investigations into alleged disappearances as mere formalities or leaving the responsibility of pursuing answers entirely to victims and their families.

The DCI is now expected to appear before the court on December 3 and provide a comprehensive account of investigative steps undertaken to establish Mwaipaya.
