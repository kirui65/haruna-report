Kenya joins global digital public goods alliance and 50-in-5 campaign

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya has joined the Digital Public Goods Alliance (DPGA) and the 50-in-5 campaign, strengthening its commitment to open-source technology, digital sovereignty and inclusive digital transformation.

The Government, through the Ministry of Information, Communications and the Digital Economy (MICDE), announced the dual membership on Monday, describing the move as a significant step towards building secure, accessible and interoperable digital public infrastructure.

The two United Nations-endorsed global initiatives seek to promote the development and adoption of open-source technologies while supporting countries to establish digital systems that improve service delivery and expand economic opportunities.

In a statement, Cabinet Secretary for Information, Communications and the Digital Economy William Kabogo said Kenya’s digital transformation journey had been driven by local innovation and solutions designed to address the country&#8217;s needs.

“Kenya’s digital story has always been written by Kenyans, for Kenyans, from pioneering mobile money to building eCitizen into a single gateway for more than 20,000 government services,” said Kabogo.

“Today we take the next step in that story. By joining the Digital Public Goods Alliance and the 50-in-5 campaign, Kenya is declaring that the future of our digital government will be open, sovereign, and shared,” he added.

The memberships build on Kenya’s recent investments in digital transformation, including the establishment of the Kenya National Open Source Programme Office (OSPO) in 2025, the first national government OSPO in Africa.

The country has also joined the Open Source Ecosystem Enabler (OSEE) initiative, in partnership with the International Telecommunication Union, the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the European Union and the Government of Germany.

Additionally, Kenya has launched the development of a national Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) Roadmap through an Inter-Ministerial Steering Committee. The roadmap is anchored on five pillars: inclusivity, trust and security, interoperability, innovation and sustainability.

The Digital Public Goods Alliance is a multi-stakeholder initiative endorsed by the United Nations Secretary-General. It facilitates the discovery, development, use and investment in digital public goods, including open-source software, open data, open artificial intelligence systems, open standards and open content.

The alliance promotes digital solutions that adhere to privacy and best practices, do no harm and contribute towards achieving the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). It also stewards the Digital Public Goods Standard and Registry, which guide the identification and adoption of qualifying digital public goods.

Meanwhile, the 50-in-5 campaign is a country-led initiative targeting to support 50 countries by the end of 2028 to design, launch and scale at least one component of their digital public infrastructure.

The initiative focuses on digital identity, digital payments and data exchange systems, with an emphasis on safety, inclusivity and interoperability.

Through collaboration with participating countries and development partners, the campaign facilitates knowledge-sharing, exchange of best practices and adoption of appropriate technologies to strengthen national digital infrastructure.

The campaign is implemented in collaboration with the Digital Public Goods Alliance, Co-Develop, the Centre for Digital Public Infrastructure, the Gates Foundation, the United Nations Office for Digital and Emerging Technologies and UNDP.

Kenya’s participation follows its engagement in the UN Open-Source Week in New York in 2024, 2025 and 2026. The establishment of the National Open Source Programme Office was subsequently announced during the Global DPI Summit in Cape Town in November 2025.

In a statement accompanying the announcement, the Ministry said open-source technology was not merely a technological preference but also an economic and sovereignty consideration.

It noted that adopting open-source systems would enable Kenyans to build, understand, secure and improve technologies serving the country, while ensuring accountability under national laws, protecting citizens&#8217; data and reducing dependence on individual technology vendors.

The Ministry further said the initiative would support the development of local technical expertise, enabling Kenyan engineers to move beyond consuming technology to creating digital public goods that could be adopted globally.

Through the 50-in-5 campaign, Kenya also seeks to contribute to the development of foundational digital systems that support its digital economy while ensuring that solutions are safe, inclusive and accessible.

The government said its participation in the two initiatives would position Kenya to share its experience, contribute African perspectives to global digital policy discussions and strengthen collaboration in developing digital public infrastructure.

It added that Kenya intended to play an active role in shaping the future of digital transformation rather than simply adopting solutions developed elsewhere.
