TikTok, ICC Launch Commerce Labs to Train Kenyan Small Businesses with Growth Skills

TikTok and the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) brought together hundreds of small business owners, digital creators and industry representatives in Nairobi on November 18, 2026, for the launch of the Digital Commerce Labs (DCL).

This is a programme designed to build digital commerce skills among entrepreneurs across Sub-Saharan Africa.

The flagship community event featured keynote sessions, programme showcases and networking, with a central focus on how Kenyan small businesses can leverage digital tools to grow and scale.

It marks the start of a broader initiative through which TikTok and the ICC plan to train thousands of businesses in the region through both online and in-person formats.

Kenya&#039;s digital retail landscape provides compelling context for the launch. A GSMA survey found that 49% of Kenyan businesses sell exclusively through social media, with a further 13% combining social platforms with a website or marketplace.

Across Nigeria, Kenya and South Africa , over 40% of internet users have already made purchases through social commerce platforms.

Kushal Prakash, Head of Global Institution Engagement at TikTok, said the partnership with ICC is intended to ensure that the gains of digital transformation reach every small business in the region.

Julian Kassum, Deputy Secretary General of the ICC World Business Organisation, described digital commerce as "a powerful engine for inclusive growth," adding that the DCL is designed to open new pathways for entrepreneurship, trade and job creation for Kenyan SMEs.

Caroline Kaua, Director of Business Development, Marketing, and Trade at the Micro and Small Enterprises Authority (MSEA), welcomed the initiative, noting that micro and small enterprises are central to Kenya&#039;s economy.

Beginning in mid-November, participants can access self-taught online modules covering topics ranging from building a digital presence and creating content, to using AI-driven e-commerce tools and preparing for domestic and international growth.

The modules are delivered in a mobile-first format and are tailored specifically to the Kenyan market. Live, trainer-led virtual classroom sessions will complement the self-paced learning.

The launch also featured a panel discussion with three Kenyan entrepreneurs: Cherie Kihato, founder of Savannah Space and a TikTok 2026 Global Discover List creator; Alix, founder of Mammacita in Pink; and Emmy Rono, Executive Director of Urembo Craft.

The three shared how they have incorporated digital content into their business strategies to build brand visibility and reach new customers.

Business leaders and entrepreneurs interested in joining the programme can pre-register through the Digital Commerce Labs website .
