Kenyan, Nigerian, Ugandan startups named as Latitude59 Kenya finalists

WhatsAppFacebookX

Ten startups from Kenya, Nigeria and Uganda have been selected as finalists for the Latitude59 Kenya Pitch Competition, and will now pitch on the main stage at the event in Nairobi on December 4.

Disrupt Africa reported last month startup and technology event Latitude59 was returning to Kenya for the fourth year, with startups invited to compete for non-equity funding, partner prizes, and the opportunity to pitch on the main stage.

Ten finalists have now been chosen from a record 509 applications from 25 countries &#8211; seven from Kenya, two from Nigeria and one from Uganda.

The Kenyan ventures include Inner Circle, which runs a marketplace where African creators earn money from their audiences through mobile money, paid sessions, subscriptions and direct messages in six markets; Farmsky, which builds AI-powered infrastructure for farmers and agricultural SMEs; Sustainable Africa, which builds data infrastructure designed to make African businesses and value chains verifiable and financeable; and ElementPay, which gives businesses in emerging markets bank accounts in their own names in more than 27 countries.

The other Kenyan startups are MasomoGPT, which helps users learn by drawing on things they already enjoy; Chargebyte, which makes solar-powered charging and Wi-Fi stations to widen access to energy and connectivity; and Bahari Mezani, which provides solar-powered cold storage and logistics to reduce fish spoilage and post-harvest losses along the Kenyan coast.

The Nigerian finalists are Ruralpass, which provides identity verification and risk assessment tools that help banks and insurers serve smallholder farmers, and BetaLife, which connects hospitals, blood banks and donors to improve the delivery of blood and medical supplies.

Uganda&#8217;s Kyuka Ventures, the remaining finalist, converts plastic waste into clean LPG cooking fuel.

&#8220;What stands out about this year&#8217;s TOP10 is the range of founders and ideas,&#8221; said Krista Meinarde, head of investor-startup relations at Latitude59. &#8220;Put them on one stage, and you get a real picture of where African tech is heading, and these are the teams to watch.&#8221;

Before the final, the startups will get pitch training and mentoring from pitch coach Gleb Maltsev.

The winner will receive a &#8220;golden ticket&#8221; to Tallinn, Estonia. The prize fast-tracks them into the top 30 of the Latitude59 2027 Pitch Competition and covers their return trip. It also gives them the chance to meet investors and potential partners in Estonia&#8217;s startup ecosystem.
