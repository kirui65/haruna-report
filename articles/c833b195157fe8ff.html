"THE YUNGES": How much Isaya Yunge's customised Mercedes G-Wagon costs

When Tanzanian tech entrepreneur Isaya Yunge pulled up to his wedding celebrations in Mwanza, it wasn&#039;t just the occasion that got people talking.

A convoy of black Mercedes-Benz G-Wagons, led by one carrying a custom plate bearing the words "THE YUNGES" alongside the Tanzanian flag, made the kind of entrance that few could ignore.

Yunge had flown into the city on a private aircraft before switching to the vehicles for the drive to the festivities.

The cars in the convoy were customised G63 models, though the exact year of manufacture and confirmed ownership of the vehicles have not been publicly established.

Based on Kenyan market listings, a 2023 or 2024 Mercedes-AMG G63 carries a price tag of between KSh 35 million and KSh 45 million, depending on the specification and import route.

At a mid-range estimate of KSh 40 million, a single unit costs roughly the same as a luxury apartment in Nairobi. When you factor in a full convoy, the figures multiply quickly.

Under the bonnet, the G63 packs a 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8 engine producing 577 horsepower.

The machine accelerates from zero to 100 km/h in around 4.4 seconds, features three lockable differentials, twin 12.3-inch displays and a Burmester premium sound system, making it one of the more recognisable symbols of high-end motoring on the continent.

The personalised plate attracted nearly as much attention as the vehicles themselves.

If registered as a customised number plate in Tanzania , the fee would be approximately KSh 250,000 for three years, with an additional KSh 25,000 charged for a special registration number.

However, if the plate was a novelty item produced specifically for the wedding, the cost would drop to a few thousand shillings at most.

It is not yet clear which applies here. It is worth noting that standard Tanzanian number plates already carry the national flag as part of their design, meaning the flag visible on the plate does not confirm its registration status either way.

Yunge has not commented publicly on the vehicles or the cost involved, and no statement has been issued by his representatives.
