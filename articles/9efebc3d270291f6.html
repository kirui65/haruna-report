Students urged to seek help amid concerns over teenage mental health

WhatsAppFacebookX

High school students have been urged to speak out about mental health challenges and seek professional support amid growing concerns over teenage suicide and other forms of emotional distress.

Children officers from the State Department for Children Services cautioned learners against suffering in silence, urging them to seek guidance and counselling when confronted with stress, anxiety, peer pressure, low self-esteem and family-related challenges.

The officers spoke during an end-year mentorship conference at Enoomatasiani Girls High School in Kajiado North Sub-County, which brought together hundreds of Form Three students benefiting from the Jomo Kenyatta Foundation (JKF) Elimu Scholarship Programme.

Elizabeth Wawire, a Children Officer, said while scholarships had helped improve access to education for vulnerable learners, financial support alone was not sufficient to address the challenges they face.

She noted that some beneficiaries grapple with emotional and social difficulties that require mentorship, counselling and parental support.

Wawire said the mentorship programme, implemented in collaboration with JKF, provides an opportunity to equip learners with life skills, strengthen their resilience and guide them through challenges associated with adolescence.

She also called on parents and guardians to pay closer attention to their children&#8217;s emotional well-being, particularly during school holidays when learners spend more time at home.

Wawire urged parents to create supportive environments where teenagers can freely discuss their challenges without fear of judgement, adding that listening to children and monitoring changes in their behaviour were important in identifying distress early.

Another Children Officer, Billy Adera, challenged learners to exercise responsibility in their use of technology and social media, warning that online interactions could expose them to exploitation and harmful content.

He cited cases of young people whose lives had been adversely affected by negative online experiences, urging students to be cautious about the information they consume and share.

Adera also warned against online exploitation, including blackmail involving intimate images shared or obtained without consent. He encouraged learners facing online harassment, threats or any form of abuse to report the incidents instead of suffering in silence.

Adera also advised students to seek assistance through the Child Helpline toll-free number 116 or WhatsApp number 0722 116 116.

Ashley Merit, a student at Ruthimitu Girls High School who attended the conference, urged fellow learners to embrace positive behaviour, remain alert to challenges affecting their well-being and seek help whenever necessary.

“Change begins with you. We must be mindful of our character; watch out for the challenges we face and never be afraid to speak up whenever we encounter difficulties. There is help available, and we should not suffer in silence,” she said.

The conference underscored the importance of mentorship, guidance and counselling in helping adolescents navigate social and emotional pressures, build resilience and make responsible decisions.
