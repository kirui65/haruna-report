Transport CS Chirchir Announces Tougher Vehicle Inspection, Enforcement on Kenyan Roads

Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir has outlined plans to overhaul motor vehicle inspections in Kenya, introducing automated testing facilities and digital systems as part of a broader push to make roads safer across the country.

Chirchir made the announcement on Thursday, 26 November, at the Africa Road Safety Conference, which was convened by KCB Group and the Global Road Safety Partnership.

The CS said the government aimed to cut road fatalities by more than 30% over the next five years, with a longer-term goal of reducing deaths and injuries by at least 50% by 2030. He reaffirmed the Ministry of Roads and Transport&#039;s commitment to achieving those targets through a range of interventions.

He added that enforcement on the roads would be intensified, with authorities cracking down on speeding, drunk driving, dangerous overtaking, overloading and reckless driving. Technology will play a central role in that effort.

Chirchir also announced the establishment of the Kenya Road Assessment Programme (KenRAP), designed to identify high-risk sections of road and prioritise remedial work at accident blackspots.

On public transport, he said operator accountability was being strengthened alongside professional driver training. More than 4,000 public transport personnel and drivers completed training in the past three months, according to the CS.

All these measures fall under the KSh 30 billion Road Safety Action Plan covering 2024 to 2028, which also addresses motorcycle safety and improved enforcement capacity.

The announcement arrives against a contentious backdrop. Earlier this year, the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA) had proposed rules requiring private vehicles older than four years to undergo annual inspections covering brakes, tyres, steering systems and other safety-critical components.

In July, however, the Senate annulled those rules, raising concerns that the implementation window was too short and that existing inspection facilities were insufficient to handle the volume of private vehicles. Senators also warned that the shortage of inspection centres could leave motorists vulnerable to harassment by enforcement officers.

Chirchir&#039;s latest statements suggest the government is pressing ahead with its road safety agenda despite that legislative setback, with a greater emphasis on automated infrastructure to address the capacity concerns that derailed the earlier proposals.
