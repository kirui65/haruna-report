Several governments and local authorities across Europe, North America, and Asia are running financial incentive schemes designed to draw new residents, remote workers, and skilled professionals into areas struggling with population decline.

The programmes vary significantly in size and structure, but most share a critical limitation: the money does not come with a visa or immigration status. Applicants must typically already have the legal right to live and work in the country before they can qualify.

Portugal&#039;s Emprego Interior MAIS programme pays KSh 538,000 (€3,759.91) to workers who relocate to designated interior territories for permanent employment, self-employment, or business creation.

The base payment rises by 20% for each additional household member, plus KSh 115,300 (€805.70) towards removal costs. Third-country nationals with legal Portuguese residence are eligible.

Switzerland&#039;s Alpine village of Albinen in the canton of Valais offers KSh 3.87 million (CHF 25,000) per adult and KSh 1.55 million (CHF 10,000) per child.

A family of two adults and two children could collectively qualify for KSh 10.8 million (CHF 70,000).

Applicants must be under 45, purchase a qualifying primary home worth at least KSh 31 million (CHF 200,000), and commit to residing in the village for a minimum of 10 years. Foreign nationals must already hold a Swiss C residence permit.

Italy&#039;s Sardinian regional government provides grants of up to KSh 2.15 million (€15,000), capped at 50% of eligible costs, for the purchase or renovation of a first home in municipalities with populations below 5,000.

Japan&#039;s regional relocation programme offers up to KSh 816,500 (¥1 million) to households leaving the Tokyo metropolitan area, with the scheme primarily targeting people who have previously lived or worked in Tokyo.

In the United States, Ascend West Virginia pays selected remote workers KSh 1.56 million ($12,000) in monthly instalments over two years but limits eligibility to US citizens and lawful permanent residents.

Tulsa Remote in Oklahoma offers KSh 1.3 million ($10,000) to remote workers who relocate to the city; since 2018, more than 4,000 workers have participated. Both programmes require existing US work authorisation and do not sponsor visas.

Across all eight programmes, the financial support is intended to supplement a relocation that the beneficiary already has the legal right to make, rather than to serve as a route into the country.

In other news, Immigration New Zealand will remove 17 occupations from its Green List Work to Residence pathway starting March 8, 2027, redirecting affected trades and technician roles to the Skilled Migrant Category (SMC).

Thirteen occupations, including motor mechanics, welders, metal fabricators, automotive electricians and building associates, will move to the Trades and Technician pathway, while crane operators, telecommunications technicians, paving plant operators and pressure welders will enter the Skilled Work Experience pathway.

The new routes require applicants to meet higher experience, qualification and wage thresholds, including at least four years of relevant experience and 18 months earning NZ$35 per hour for the Trades and Technician pathway, or five years of experience and two years earning NZ$38.50 per hour for the Skilled Work Experience route.

However, workers who began accumulating qualifying experience before March 8, 2027, may continue counting it towards the existing 24-month residence requirement, while four special education teaching roles will move to the Straight to Residence pathway.
