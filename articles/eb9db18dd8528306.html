Video: UDA MP ranks opposition leaders, names one he considers most suitable

Molo Member of Parliament Kuria Kimani, who also chairs the National Assembly&#039;s Budget Committee and serves as a United Democratic Alliance (UDA) member, has publicly ranked key opposition figures on their suitability for the presidency, singling out Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka as the most relatable candidate among the current crop of contenders.

Speaking in an interview with Obinna, Kimani described Kalonzo as experienced, diplomatic, and notably non-confrontational, qualities he said would make it easier for ordinary Kenyans to accept him as a national leader.

The MP was more sceptical about former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang&#039;i, arguing that he has no meaningful electoral experience and would struggle to mobilise grassroots support.

Kimani challenged whether Matiang&#039;i could even name a thousand people he knows personally across the country, saying that precise, name-by-name voter mobilisation is a core skill in Kenyan politics, one he said Matiang&#039;i has never had to develop independently because the state machinery did it for him during his time in government.

On Linda Mwananchi Movement (LMM) leader Edwin Sifuna , Kimani acknowledged that he has some practical experience of running a campaign, calling it a foundation others in the opposition lack.

Sifuna is currently in the United States to meet and rally Kenyans in the diaspora behind him in the upcoming General Election.

Regarding former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua , Kimani acknowledged his strength as a mobiliser but pointed to what he described as a critical and self-inflicted setback.

"If you take Rigathi Gachagua, good mobiliser, but he made one mistake. He allowed himself to be impeached. And therefore in our constitution, if you are impeached, you can never run for office," Kimani said.

Gachagua was impeached by Parliament in October 2024, a move he has since contested through the courts.

When asked directly whether the 2027 election is already settled, Kimani was blunt, suggesting that any serious presidential aspirant should shift their focus to 2032, a clear signal of his confidence that President William Ruto will secure a second term.
