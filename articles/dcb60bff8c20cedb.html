Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga has edged out Kirinyaga Governor Ann Waiguru in a closely contested national poll ranking Kenya&#039;s most influential woman leaders.

The November 2026 survey by Mwelekeo Insights placed Wanga first with 40.3% of the responses, equivalent to 27,066 votes, narrowly ahead of Waiguru, who garnered 39.4%, representing 26,482 votes.

The difference was just 584 responses, or 0.9 percentage points, highlighting the close contest between the two governors.

Former Health Cabinet Secretary Susan Nakhumicha came a distant third with 2.0%, attracting 1,319 responses, while Samburu West MP Naisula Lesuuda finished fourth with 1.7% (1,157 responses).

Machakos Governor Wavinya Ndeti followed closely in fifth position with 1,154 responses, also representing 1.7%.

Businesswoman Agnes Kagure ranked sixth nationally, receiving 1,066 responses (1.6%), followed by Suba North MP Millie Odhiambo with 1,027 (1.5%).

Former Kitui Governor Charity Ngilu secured 916 responses (1.4%), while nominated Senator Catherine Omanyo received 903 (1.3%).

Other notable figures included Law Society of Kenya President Faith Odhiambo with 828 responses, former Meru Governor Kawira Mwangaza with 809 and former presidential candidate Martha Karua with 808. All three recorded 1.2%.

Nakuru Governor Susan Kihika and businesswoman Wangui Ngirici each received less than 1%, while Chief Justice Martha Koome attracted 332 responses (0.5%). Esther Passaris, Esther Muchemi and Beatrice Elachi completed the list.

Further down the rankings, Fatuma Achani received 753 responses (1.1%), followed by Susan Kihika with 599 and Wangui Ngirici with 585, both recording 0.9%.

Cecily Mbarire garnered 540 responses (0.8%), Tabitha Karanja 502 (0.7%), while Chief Justice Martha Koome received 332 (0.5%).

Esther Passaris recorded 192 responses (0.3%), Esther Muchemi 104 (0.2%) and former Nairobi Speaker Beatrice Elachi finished last with 78 responses (0.1%).

According to Mwelekeo Insights, the poll ran from November 18 to November 21, 2026, attracting 67,220 responses from participants across the country.

It featured 21 women drawn from different areas of leadership, including governors, legislators, judicial officials, business leaders and former government officials.

However, the organisation clarified that the exercise was an open online poll in which participants voluntarily submitted their preferences. It was not a statistically representative survey of the Kenyan population, and no conventional margin of error applies.
