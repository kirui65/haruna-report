Ivy Njeri Maingi: The future of digital loyalty will be built on everyday value

WhatsAppFacebookX

The way Kenyans use digital services is changing. From ordering meals and groceries to booking rides and making payments, more of our everyday activities are moving onto digital platforms. But as these services become part of our daily routines, consumer expectations are changing too. Convenience is no longer enough. People want to know that the services they use regularly are giving them something meaningful in return.

This presents an interesting opportunity for businesses operating in Kenya&#8217;s digital economy. The next phase of growth will not simply be about getting more people to download an app or try a service once. It will be about earning a place in their everyday lives and giving them good reasons to keep coming back. One model gaining relevance in this conversation is the subscription service.

Subscriptions are changing the relationship between businesses and consumers. Rather than treating every purchase as a separate transaction, they create an ongoing relationship in which customers pay for access to a set of benefits over time. For businesses, this creates an opportunity to build loyalty. For consumers, it should mean greater convenience, more predictable value and a better experience.

But there is an important distinction, a subscription is only as good as the value it delivers. Consumers are becoming more deliberate about the digital services they pay for. They will question another monthly charge if the benefits are unclear or difficult to use. Businesses therefore have to move beyond simply packaging services together and demonstrate how a subscription makes everyday life easier or more affordable.

Kenya&#8217;s digital landscape provides a strong foundation for this shift. According to the Communications Authority of Kenya&#8217;s Q3 FY 2025/26 sector statistics, active mobile subscriptions reached 84.1 million, while mobile broadband consumption stood at 800 million GB during the quarter. The Media Council of Kenya&#8217;s Navigating the Digital Reality 26 report also found that 45.8% of respondents paid to access Kenyan digital content. While this is not a measure of subscription uptake across the economy, it points to a growing familiarity with paid digital experiences. The opportunity now is to make those experiences more useful and relevant to how people live.

At Bolt, this thinking informed the introduction of Bolt Plus in Kenya. We looked at how customers already use our platform: taking a ride to work, ordering lunch, buying groceries or making a last-minute change to their plans. These are not occasional activities for many of our customers. They are part of everyday life.

We asked ourselves how we could make those interactions more rewarding, particularly for people who use Bolt regularly. The answer was to bring together benefits that customers can use across rides and food and retail delivery in one subscription service.

For KES 280 a month, Bolt Plus subscribers receive 10% cashback on every eligible ride, free delivery on eligible food and retail orders, priority driver matching, faster food delivery and three free ride cancellations each month. New subscribers also get their first month free, with an annual subscription option available.

The thinking is not complicated. A customer takes a ride and earns cash back towards their next trip. They order food or groceries and save on eligible delivery fees. When demand is high, priority matching can make getting a ride more convenient. When plans change, they have more flexibility. These are small moments individually, but together they can make a meaningful difference to the overall customer experience.

This is also where subscriptions can play a role in shaping digital habits. When a service consistently delivers value, customers have more reason to return to it. Over time, the platform becomes a more familiar and useful part of how they organise their day. That is the kind of loyalty businesses should be working towards: not simply repeat transactions, but a relationship built on usefulness, reliability and trust.

For businesses, this requires a different approach to customer retention. Loyalty cannot be assumed because someone has downloaded an app or subscribed to a service. It has to be earned continuously. That means listening to customers, understanding how their needs evolve and making sure the benefits offered remain relevant.

It also means being transparent about what customers are paying for. Subscription services should not rely on complicated terms, hidden conditions or benefits that are difficult to access. The value proposition should be easy to understand, and the experience should live up to the promise.

There is a wider opportunity for Kenya&#8217;s digital economy here. As more services move online, businesses can think beyond individual transactions and build connected experiences around the needs of their customers. Mobility, food, grocery shopping and delivery may serve different needs, but they are all part of how people manage their everyday lives.

Ultimately, the success of subscription services will not be measured by how many people sign up alone. It will be measured by whether customers continue to find value in them.

At Bolt, we see Bolt Plus as one way of putting that principle into practice. We want customers to feel that the more they use the services they already rely on, the more value they get in return. Because lasting loyalty is not built by asking customers to come back. It is built by giving them a reason to.

Ivy Njeri Maingi, General Manager, Bolt Food Kenya
