Kenya woos foreign investors to partner with Kenya on development projects

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya is courting international investors to bring capital, technology and expertise to fund development, as the government leans on private businesses to complement stretched public resources.

The push was the main theme of Equity Group&#8217;s five-day Trade and Investment Roadshow. The event opened in Nairobi on November 25 and has brought together more than 650 investors, business leaders, policymakers and development partners from 54 countries.

Prime Cabinet Secretary and Foreign and Diaspora Affairs Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi said Kenya wants investors who bring more than money.

&#8220;Kenya welcomes you, bringing not only capital, but also technology, expertise, market knowledge and confidence in the possibilities of our people,&#8221; said Mr Mudavadi.

&#8220;Join us as partners in building competitive and sustainable projects that generate attractive returns while creating jobs, expanding opportunities and delivering lasting benefits for our economy and society.&#8221;

Equity Group Chief Executive James Mwangi said the private sector&#8217;s role in financing development has long been underestimated.

&#8220;Businesses are creating thousands of jobs, generating wealth and contributing billions in taxes. The private sector is well positioned to work alongside governments in financing development, driving economic growth and creating opportunities for our people,&#8221; said Dr Mwangi.

Kenya Private Sector Alliance (KEPSA) Chief Executive Carole Kariuki said local businesses bring established supply chains, entrepreneurial expertise and manufacturing capability to partnerships with foreign investors.

She said such partnerships could help Kenyan firms reach wider markets. She also called for a predictable policy environment to attract investment in sectors such as e-mobility, agro-processing and digital services.

Mary Peschka, the International Finance Corporation&#8217;s regional director for Eastern Africa, said investors need mechanisms that reduce risk before they commit capital for the long term.

&#8220;Through risk-sharing solutions, sound financial structures and local-currency financing, we can help give investors the confidence to commit capital for the long term,&#8221; said Ms Peschka.

She identified regulatory reforms, a pipeline of bankable projects and stronger regional supply chains as key to drawing investment.

The roadshow, which ends on November 29, is connecting investors with government officials and Kenyan companies to identify partnerships in manufacturing, agriculture, financial services and other productive sectors.

The initiative is part of Equity Group&#8217;s Africa Recovery and Resilience Plan, which aims to link African businesses with capital, technology, markets and expertise.
