Nairobi runs on borrowed money: CoB flags Sidian Bank overdraft averaging KSh1.6B a month

WhatsAppFacebookX

Nairobi County is financing its monthly wage bill through a rolling bank overdraft — and the Controller of Budget says the arrangement has cost taxpayers millions in charges and penalties.

Nairobi County has been maintaining a monthly overdraft facility with Sidian Bank of Kenya averaging KSh1.6B to cover staff salaries, with an outstanding balance of KSh1.54B recorded as at 31 March 2026, the Controller of Budget has flagged in a report raising fresh concerns about the county&#8217;s chronic dependence on short-term borrowing to meet its most basic financial obligations.

Controller of Budget Margaret Nyakang&#8217;o&#8217;s report reveals that the County Executive has relied on the Sidian Bank overdraft facility specifically to cover personnel emoluments, with the outstanding balance standing at KSh1.54B by 31 March 2026. The county also carried an additional overdraft of KSh214.51M with Co-operative Bank.

Nyakang&#8217;o confirmed the arrangement before the Senate Standing Committee on Information Communication and Technology on Monday, noting that the commercial bank had confirmed the facility enabled the county to settle salary payments up to October 2026. She further recommended integrating gratuity into the payroll system and ensuring timely submission of monthly salary requisitions — basic financial hygiene that the county has consistently struggled to maintain.

Beyond the overdrafts, Nairobi still carries a long-standing KSh4.5B loan from Kenya Commercial Bank secured in 2010 under the defunct Nairobi City Council, which remains classified under trade payables — a debt so old it predates devolution, yet one that continues to weigh on a county that spent KSh32.12B on development and recurrent programmes in the reporting period, representing 136 per cent of funds actually released by the Controller of Budget.

That a city generating billions in revenue each year cannot pay its workers without a rolling commercial overdraft is not a fiscal footnote. It is an indictment — and with Nairobi&#8217;s 2027 election politics already heating up, the question of who bears responsibility for the financial mess at City Hall is about to become very uncomfortable for everyone inside it.
