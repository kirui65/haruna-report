"One day at a time": Baba Talisha undergoes transplant to save his left eye

Kenyan influencer Baba Talisha shared a deeply personal update on Saturday, November 21, 2026, revealing he was undergoing a cornea transplant at a hospital in Kenya in a bid to save his remaining good eye.

In the post shared on Instagram , Baba Talisha disclosed that an aggressive infection, one that doctors have struggled to identify and bring under control, had caused his cornea to perforate, forcing his medical team to recommend the surgery as an urgent intervention.

He had previously revealed that he lost the ability to see in his right eye, a development that sent shockwaves through his online following.

Now, with pressure in his right eye remaining dangerously high despite medical efforts to lower it, and with his left cornea perforated, the situation has taken a more critical turn.

His doctors advised that the transplant was necessary not only to protect his left eye but also to stabilise his condition ahead of a planned trip to India, where he is expected to undergo further investigations and access more specialised care.

Baba Talisha has also reached out to Kenyans to support him financially as the cost of treatment both locally and abroad continues to mount.

Despite the weight of the situation, the influencer struck a hopeful tone in his message, saying he was taking the journey "one day at a time, with hope and gratitude for everyone walking beside us."

The post, which was shared while wearing what appeared to be an LCIF Lions Clubs International Foundation SightFirst-branded item, also hinted at more details to come, with the caption cutting off mid-sentence, leaving followers eager for his next update.

Baba Talisha&#039;s wife, Miss Wanjey, recently revealed during a TikTok livestream that a parasitic amoeba linked to contaminated water and dust embedded in Baba Talisha&#039;s cornea caused total vision loss in his eye.

She noted that they were awaiting lab results sent to India before starting new treatment, planning a second opinion at Kikuyu Hospital, and considering a corneal transplant if medication fails.

While hoping he regains most of his sight, Miss Wanjey stepped in to manage his business communications as he deals with light sensitivity and recovery.
