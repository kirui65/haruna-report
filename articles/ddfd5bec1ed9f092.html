Vision Impact Project expands services to include ear and hearing care

WhatsAppFacebookX

A four-year project targeting over 336,000 people in Kakamega County has incorporated ear and hearing health services in its expanded health interventions.

The Vision Impact Project (VIP) Scale-Up, implemented by Operation Eyesight Universal in partnership with the County Government of Kakamega, targets to reduce avoidable blindness and visual impairment while increasing access to quality eye, ear and hearing care.

The second phase of the project, which is being funded by Christian Blind Mission (CBM), will run from 2026 to 2029, building on the first phase implemented in seven counties between 2022 and 2025.

Speaking during the Vision Impact Project Disability Stakeholder Forum, at a hotel in Kakamega town, Operation Eyesight Universal Program Coordinator Susan Anjichi said the new phase has expanded its scope to incorporate ear and hearing health, which was not covered in the previous phase.

She said under the project a situation analysis to establish the extent of ear and hearing health needs in the county will be conducted to guide on the necessary interventions.

Anjichi noted that a Rapid Assessment of Avoidable Blindness (RAAB) survey conducted in 2024 established that 1.6 per cent of people aged 50 years and above in Kakamega are blind, while 17.6 per cent are living with visual impairment.

She said most cases of blindness are avoidable, with cataracts, uncorrected refractive errors and glaucoma identified among the major causes.

&#8220;The survey showed that there is a significant need for eye health services in Kakamega County, and most of the blindness is avoidable,&#8221; she said.

Anjichi urged residents, particularly those aged 40 years and above, to undergo regular eye examinations to enable early detection and treatment of eye conditions, noting that glaucoma could progress without noticeable pain.

The program targets to screen 336,763 people over the four-year implementation period, comprising 187,534 community members and 149,229 school-going children.

Community and school screening activities will be undertaken in Malava, Mumias East, Navakholo, Shinyalu and Ikolomani sub-counties, in collaboration with the county government and other implementing partners.

Anjichi said community members will benefit from free screening, treatment, reading spectacles and cataract surgeries, with the project targeting 1,712 cataract operations and approximately 16,000 reading spectacles.

In schools, learners requiring prescription spectacles would receive them to address refractive errors and prevent visual impairment that could affect their learning.

&#8220;When we go to schools, we target every child, including the teachers and other workers in the school. We provide free screening and treatment, and learners who require spectacles are supported,&#8221; she disclosed.

She said the project is also investing in human resource development through training healthcare workers, including supporting one audiologist to undertake a higher diploma course at the Kenya Medical Training College (KMTC).

Other capacity-building interventions include advanced training for ophthalmic clinical officers in cataract surgery, training of a low vision therapist and supporting healthcare workers to attend specialized short courses.

The program will also procure eye and ear diagnostic equipment and support the establishment of additional eye health services at sub-county facilities to improve access to specialized care.

Currently, eye health services in the county are largely centralized at Kakamega General Hospital, with the project seeking to take services closer to communities.

According to Anjichi the project will work with Community Health Promoters, teachers and healthcare workers to strengthen early identification, treatment and referral of eye and ear conditions.

She said: &#8220;We work within the existing government structures because our aim is to strengthen the health system while increasing access to quality services. We do not have our own parallel system,”.

“The project will further support the county in strengthening health information systems, establish an eye health working group and develop an eye and ear health strategic plan,” she added.

Anjichi said Operation Eyesight would also collaborate with the National Council for Persons with Disabilities (NCPWD) to support disability assessments and improve referral pathways for persons with disabilities.

Kakamega Association of the Deaf Chairman, Abel Ouma commended the project implementers for incorporating ear and hearing health in its second phase but sought clarification on whether funds have been allocated for hearing aids and how they will be distributed to beneficiaries.

In response, Anjichi said the project has made budgetary provisions for hearing aids, noting that lessons from an earlier pilot program in Meru County have informed better planning for their provision and maintenance.

She further explained that hearing aids require proper fitting, regular adjustments and replacement batteries, adding that the project has since adopted a structured approach to ensure beneficiaries receive appropriate support.

&#8220;We have learnt that it is not just about buying hearing aids and giving them to people. They require proper fitting, adjustments and maintenance, including replacing batteries,&#8221; she said noting that although the project might not reach every child requiring hearing aids, it has made specific allocations to support their provision.

Through the interventions, more children and adults are expected to benefit from early detection, specialized treatment and rehabilitation services for eye and hearing conditions.
