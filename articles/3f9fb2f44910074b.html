Kenya Met Predicts Rain in 30 Counties, Warns of Cold Nights Below 10°C Over Next 7 Days

The Kenya Meteorological Service Authority (KMSA) has released its weekly weather outlook for the period spanning November 24 to 30, 2026, projecting continued rainfall across 30 counties alongside sharply contrasting temperature extremes between the country&#039;s highlands and arid north.

KMSA published the forecast on Monday, November 23, 2026, via WhatsApp.

The authority indicated that wet conditions are set to persist across a wide swathe of the country.

Counties expected to receive rainfall include Kakamega, Bungoma, Nandi, Homa Bay, Migori, Kisii, Kericho, Nyamira, Nyandarua, Laikipia, Nyeri, Murang&#039;a, Kiambu, Nairobi, Kirinyaga, Embu, Tharaka Nithi, Meru, Isiolo, Kitui, Makueni, Machakos, Kajiado, Taita Taveta, Tana River, Garissa, Lamu, Kilifi, Kwale, and Mombasa.

The list spans the western highlands, the Mount Kenya region, Nairobi, and much of the Coast, suggesting the long rains season is maintaining its reach across a large portion of the 47 counties.

Residents in highland areas face notably cold nights during the forecast window. Minimum temperatures below 10°C are expected in parts of Meru, Tharaka Nithi, Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Murang&#039;a, Nyandarua, Laikipia, Nakuru , Elgeyo Marakwet, Trans Nzoia, Taita Taveta, and Kajiado Counties.

At the other extreme, the country&#039;s arid and semi-arid northern and coastal zones are forecast to record daytime highs exceeding 35°C.

Parts of Samburu, Turkana, Marsabit, Wajir, Garissa, and Lamu counties are expected to bear the brunt of intense afternoon heat through the week.

The divergence underscores Kenya&#039;s wide climatic range, where temperatures across the country can differ by more than 25°C on the same day depending on elevation and geography.

KMSA urged the public to stay informed and prepared ahead of the week&#039;s conditions.

Meanwhile, farmers in Limuru and Karatina are using sandbags, trenches, Napier grass , and bamboo to protect their land from forecast El Niño rains.

After losing crops and fertile topsoil in the 2024 floods, farmers are turning to low-cost methods to limit damage.

The floods killed more than 230 people, and forecasters expect November to bring peak rainfall in several parts of the country.
