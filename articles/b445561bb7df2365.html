From Free Clinic Visits to 2.5 Million Housing Jobs: Ruto Outlines Sweeping Economic Plan at Joyful Women’s 17th Anniversary

President William Ruto has unveiled a sweeping package of financial, healthcare, educational, and employment interventions targeting Kenyan women and youth during the 17th-anniversary celebrations of the Joyful Women Organisation (JoyWo) at State House, Nairobi. Addressing a massive gathering where women comprised roughly 95 percent of attendees, the Head of State praised First Lady Rachel Ruto for organizing women across 45 counties and building thousands of businesses.

President Ruto noted that while he struggled to mobilize women in a single constituency during his time as a Member of Parliament, the First Lady had successfully scaled the grassroots movement nationwide, prompting him to declare, "When an organization has brought together thousands of women, built thousands of businesses, and supported thousands of families, I confirm without fear of any doubt that what a man can do, a woman can do better."

Central to the President’s address was a comprehensive overhaul of maternal healthcare financing under the Social Health Authority (SHA) to curb maternal and neonatal mortality. Under the new universal health coverage framework, all prenatal clinical visits are fully funded by the government, with mothers encouraged to attend a minimum of four—and ideally six to seven—clinic visits prior to delivery.

Furthermore, the administration has increased normal delivery reimbursements sixfold from KSh 2,500 under the defunct NHIF to KSh 15,000, while assisted deliveries, including Cesarean sections, have risen from KSh 11,000 to KSh 30,000. Postnatal care coverage has also been extended from 42 days to six months (180 days). Explaining the rationale behind these reforms, President Ruto stated, "What we did is we thought about how to support our mothers, and in our design of the social universal health program, we did three things to support our mothers because they carry the heaviest responsibility."

To address high mortality rates in under-resourced regions, the government partnered with the Buffett Foundation and county governors to invest KSh 1.5 billion in equipping 638 health facilities across 26 high-burden counties. President Ruto reported that these targeted interventions have already lowered Kenya's maternal mortality ratio from 355 deaths per 100,000 live births down to 283, moving closer to the international benchmark of 70.

The President revealed that he personally monitors daily maternal and child birth statistics submitted by county and national hospitals up to 11:00 p.m. every night, citing a recent day where nine mothers died across seven specific referral and county hospitals out of over 300,000 deliveries. Emphasizing accountability, Ruto remarked, "We report every day because we want to make sure that we understand the reasons why so that we can deal with those reasons, and progressively we are making progress in saving mothers."

Beyond maternal care, President Ruto highlighted broader healthcare reforms anchored on accessibility, affordability, and sustainability. The government has transitioned Community Health Promoters (CHPs) from unpaid volunteers into trained, stipended frontline workers who will receive upgraded mobile diagnostic kits in December to deliver primary care directly to households.

Simultaneously, medical supply chains and SHA funds have been restructured to flow directly from Nairobi to dispensaries and health centers, bypassing county headquarters to eliminate delays. With 33.7 million Kenyans already registered on the SHA platform—and full state sponsorship provided for indigent citizens—the President urged total registration to prevent families from selling land or livestock to pay medical bills, noting, "We want to make sure that we do not have another situation where our children, our elders, and our mothers are forced to sell their property just to pay hospital bills."

Turning to the high cost of living and household food security, the President detailed agricultural subsidies and value-chain reforms aimed at lowering consumer prices while boosting farmer earnings. By digitizing input distribution to eliminate middlemen, the government reduced the cost of a bag of fertilizer from KSh 7,000 to KSh 2,500, helping push the retail price of maize flour (unga) down from KSh 250 to KSh 150.

Cash crop farmers have also recorded substantial gains, with coffee prices rising from KSh 60–70 per kilogram to KSh 120–130, sugarcane prices increasing from KSh 2,042 to KSh 5,500 per tonne alongside historic first-time bonuses, and tea earnings stabilizing. Justifying these interventions, Ruto said, "When the price of food is high, the mother is affected, the family is affected, and the nation is affected, which is why we put in place deliberate agricultural programs."

In the education sector, the government has moved to relieve parents of the financial burden of paying Board of Management (BoM) teachers and funding school infrastructure. Over the past four years, the Teachers Service Commission (TSC) has absorbed thousands of parent-paid teachers onto the public payroll, with an additional 20,000 BoM teachers slated for recruitment this coming December.

Working alongside the National Government Constituencies Development Fund (NG-CDF), the state has also constructed thousands of new classrooms over three years and streamlined the Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC) following stakeholder consultations. Explaining the policy shift, the President noted, "As a government, we identified education as a place where we can lift the heavy burden on parents and mothers by taking over the payment of teachers who were previously paid by parents."

Higher education financing is also undergoing legislative overhaul to guarantee full state funding for students from low-income households across universities, Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) institutes, and Kenya Medical Training Colleges (KMTC). President Ruto announced that Parliament is finalizing legislation under the new student-centered funding model to ensure no Kenyan student is ever sent home due to unpaid fees, making admission and progression dependent solely on intellectual capacity rather than parental wealth.

Speaking on the impact of this policy on single and married mothers alike, Ruto declared, "Going forward, the education of our students in TVETs, KMTCs, and universities will no longer depend on the ability of the parent to pay; it will depend on the intellectual capacity of the student to learn."

On housing and domestic job creation, the President revealed that the Affordable Housing Program has scaled up to 300 active construction sites nationwide, including dedicated hostels for university, TVET, and KMTC students to guarantee safe accommodation, particularly for female learners. The housing ecosystem currently employs 1.1 million young Kenyans—including architects, engineers, masons, plumbers, carpenters, and electricians—who were previously unemployed three years ago.

Supervised by Lands and Housing Cabinet Secretary Alice Wahome, the program is projected to expand to 2.5 million jobs within the next two years, with local youth given first priority at every project site. Defending the initiative against critics, Ruto stated, "One of our main objectives when designing the housing program was how we would create employment for our young people, and today we have 1.1 million young people working in that program."

The Head of State also outlined rapid expansion in the digital economy, where 350,000 youth are currently employed in Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) firms and online platforms. Working with ICT Cabinet Secretary William Kabogo and Members of Parliament, the government is rolling out nationwide last-mile fiber optic connectivity and establishing Jitume digital hubs in every ward to train youth for remote international jobs.

This infrastructure expansion aims to grow Kenya’s digital workforce from 350,000 to 1.5 million young people over the next two to three years. Urging mothers to guide their children toward these opportunities, Ruto said, "Our intention is to create an ecosystem where, once we complete last-mile fiber optic connectivity and ICT hubs across the country, we will have 1.5 million young people working in digital jobs."

To curb recruitment scams in foreign labor markets, the government has centralized overseas job placements through the National Employment Authority (NEA) portal (neaims.go.ke) and a USSD platform (*384*4#). Over the past three years, the bilateral labor migration program has placed 705,000 young Kenyans in jobs across Germany, the United States, the United Arab Emirates, and other countries, with plans to unlock up to 2 million global opportunities.

Successful applicants are now guaranteed expedited passport processing within three days, alongside a government-backed air ticket financing mechanism that beneficiaries repay once they begin earning abroad. Highlighting the transparency of the system, Ruto remarked, "There used to be a lot of fraud in the past, but now we have taken control of that sector as government through the National Employment Authority so our youth are not conned."

For young entrepreneurs with limited formal education, the administration is scaling up the NYOTA business grant program, which provides KSh 50,000 in non-repayable start-up capital to youth aged 35 and below. Having already disbursed funds to 130,000 beneficiaries across all wards during Phase One, the government—through Principal Secretary Susan Mang'eni—is preparing to launch Phase Two to onboard an additional 200,000 youth, split equally on a 50-50 gender basis.

Dismissing critics who claim the grant amount is too small, President Ruto pointed to grassroots women entrepreneurs as proof of its viability, stating, "There are wealthy people who ask what 50,000 shillings can do, yet for ordinary Kenyans and the mothers gathered here, 50,000 shillings is enough capital to start and run a real business."

Simultaneously, the government has ring-fenced KSh 250 billion in public tenders under the Access to Government Procurement Opportunities (AGPO) program for women, youth, and persons with disabilities. Through a simplified mobile portal (*254#), the state is onboarding 600,000 women and youth by offering free procurement training, certification, complimentary business registration, and Local Purchase Order (LPO) financing so suppliers can fulfill state contracts without capital constraints. Explaining the end-to-end support structure, the President noted, "Once we award you that tender to supply the government, we will also look for financing for you so that you have the money required to supply the goods and get paid."

In a major boost to micro-credit access, President Ruto announced an immediate KSh 5 billion capital injection into the Hustler Fund to eliminate liquidity bottlenecks for its 4.5 million regular borrowers. Additionally, disciplined borrowers who have maxed out their personal loan tiers of KSh 10,000 to KSh 20,000 are being graduated to a bridge loan product with credit limits of up to KSh 150,000.

Responding directly to requests from JoyWo table-banking groups for larger collective credit lines, the President directed the Hustler Fund management and Principal Secretary to design a dedicated KSh 300 million group-lending facility. Outlining the directive, Ruto said, "I am asking the management of the Hustler Fund to design a product of nearly 300 million shillings that will be made available to organized groups like those here so they can lend to members."

Concluding his address, the President appealed to parents and leaders to protect young Kenyans from political manipulation, fake news, and disinformation that could incite unrest. He stressed that Kenya’s youth must be nurtured through education, formal employment, and enterprise rather than exploited as political tools during election cycles.

Thanking the women for their prayers, resilience, and vital role in sustaining families, President Ruto closed with a stern warning against political exploitation, declaring, "Our children should never be available for manipulation by anybody, and our children should never be used by anybody as political tools to further anybody's political agenda because they are a national asset."
