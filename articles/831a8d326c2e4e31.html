Okoth Obado: Ex-Kenyan governor jailed for 25 years for killing pregnant girlfriend

WhatsAppFacebookX

A former Kenyan governor has been jailed for 25 years for the murder of a university student who was seven months pregnant with his child.

The High Court on Friday sentenced Zacharia Okoth Obado, the former governor of Migori County, along with two former aides, over the 2018 killing of Sharon Belyne Otieno.

The investigation established that an "intimate relationship" had existed between Obado and Otieno, which "subsequently led to a pregnancy with the deceased that the governor was unhappy about".

Both Obado and Otieno&#x27;s legal teams said they would appeal against the sentence.

His former personal assistant, Michael Oyamo, was also sentenced to 25 years in prison, while former county clerk Caspal Ojwang&#x27; Obiero received an 18-year sentence.

The court found that Obado had provided the motive and financial resources for the murder, while Oyamo coordinated its execution and Obiero provided logistical support and helped conceal the crime.

Judge Cecilia Githua said Obado&#x27;s absence from Migori at the time of the killing did not absolve him of responsibility.

She rejected the prosecution&#x27;s request for the death penalty, as well as Obado&#x27;s plea for a non-custodial sentence.

Otieno, 26, was killed between 3 and 4 September 2018 after leaving a hotel in Rongo, south-western Kenya, with journalist Barrack Oduor.

She had contacted Oduor to discuss her relationship with the then-governor. The two were abducted shortly afterwards.

Oduor managed to escape by jumping out of a moving vehicle. His account of the abduction became a crucial lead in the investigation.

Otieno&#x27;s body was later found near Kodera Forest.

A post-mortem examination found that she had died from strangulation and excessive bleeding caused by multiple stab wounds.

The court also established that Obado was the biological father of her unborn child.

Obado and his co-accused were convicted of murder on 23 July 2026, following a lengthy trial in which prosecutors called 42 witnesses and presented 81 exhibits.

On Friday, the court declined to award compensation to Otieno&#x27;s family, saying there was insufficient evidence to establish the financial losses they had claimed.

Before sentencing, Otieno&#x27;s mother had appealed to the judge not to send Obado to prison, saying his imprisonment would amount to "another death in the family".

She urged the court to consider a non-custodial sentence, arguing that Otieno&#x27;s three children, from different relationships, needed his support.

But the judge said the family&#x27;s circumstances could not override the court&#x27;s responsibility to impose an appropriate punishment.

Otieno&#x27;s family, through their lawyer Antony Opondo, told Kenya&#x27;s Daily Nation that they would file an appeal seeking Obado&#x27;s acquittal.

Otieno&#x27;s murder sparked protests and renewed public debate about violence against women in Kenya.

A national survey found that at least 34% of women had experienced physical violence in 2022.

The case has also drawn attention because it is rare for such senior political figures to face public criminal trials in Kenya.

Obado served two terms as governor of Migori County in western Kenya, switching allegiance between a smaller political party and the region&#x27;s dominant opposition movement.

He later backed President William Ruto and formally joined his ruling party in 2023, becoming a regional organiser.
