Nairobi Firm Lists 33 Cars for Auction, Shares Prices From KSh 167,000 to KSh 2.6m

A Nairobi law firm has invited prospective buyers to submit competitive offers for 33 motor vehicles being disposed of through a private treaty sale, with reserve prices starting at KSh 167,000 and rising to KSh 2,668,000.

Onyango & Tarus Advocates, whose offices are located at The Bloom, KMA Centre in Upper Hill, Nairobi, issued the notice on behalf of the sellers.

All 33 vehicles are being sold on an "as is where is" basis, meaning buyers will take ownership of each unit in its current condition, without recourse to the sellers.

The catalogue includes a broad mix of vehicle types, saloon cars, station waggons, SUVs, pickup trucks and commercial vehicles, manufactured between 2000 and 2017.

Toyota models make up a large share of the list, with the Probox, Crown Royal, Premio, Allion, Harrier, Noah and Fielder all featuring.

Other manufacturers represented include Nissan, Honda, Subaru, Daihatsu, Isuzu, Mazda, Mercedes-Benz, Ford and Volkswagen.

The most affordable entry on the list is a 2012 Subaru Forester S/Tb 2.0X S15 Auto, registration number KCX783C, with a reserve price of KSh 167,000.

It is available for viewing at Capital Diamond in Thika .

At the upper end, a 2017 Volkswagen Arteon carries the highest reserve price of KSh 2,668,000 and can be inspected at Naivasha Storage.

Offers must be submitted through the Eezy Cars website at www.eezycars.co.ke, with additional enquiries accepted at sales@eezycars.co.ke or via telephone on 0783 616458 and 0709 335005.

Prospective buyers should note that the sale will close on December 1, 2026.

Buyers are advised to check the condition of the motor vehicles before making a decision because they will be sold on an as-is-where-is basis.

In other news, the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) announced plans to auction unclaimed goods held at customs warehouses in Eldoret , Naivasha, Lodwar and Nakuru following gazette notices published on November 6, 2026.

Items listed include hundreds of used smartphones, laptops, perfumes, drums of ethanol, construction materials, empty beer crates, ceramic tiles and sugar, with owners given 30 days from the notice to clear their consignments.

The public auctions are scheduled for December 28–31, 2026, through KRA’s online bidding platform, while prospective buyers can physically inspect the goods at their respective storage locations on December 23 and 24.

The authority cited Section 42 of the East African Community Customs Management Act 2004, which allows it to treat goods that have overstayed in customs warehouses as abandoned and dispose of them through public auction.
