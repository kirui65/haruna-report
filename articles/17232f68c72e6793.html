Nigeria Internet Registry Proposes Domain Name Integration with Corporate Affairs Commission

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Nigeria Internet Registration Association is seeking a partnership with the Corporate Affairs Commission to link business registration with .ng domain names. The initiative aims to boost local digital identity adoption, reduce foreign exchange outflows from overseas hosting services, and enhance data sovereignty. NiRA noted that Nigeria currently has a low adoption rate compared to regional peers like Kenya and South Africa, and the proposal would allow new businesses to secure domain names matching their registered names, similar to existing models in other countries.

Nigeria’s internet registry is seeking a partnership with the Corporate Affairs Commission to provide new businesses with corresponding .ng domain names , in a move aimed at expanding local digital identities, reducing foreign exchange spending and keeping more business data within the country.

The Nigeria Internet Registration Association, which manages the country’s .ng domain, said the proposal would allow businesses registering with the CAC to secure a domain name matching their registered business names. The initiative is expected to address the low adoption of Nigeria’s country-code domain despite the country’s large population and growing digital economy. President of NiRA, Adesola Akinsanya, disclosed the proposal after a meeting with the Registrar-General of the CAC, Hussaini Magaji, SAN.

Akinsanya said many Nigerian businesses relied on foreign domain names and overseas hosting services, resulting in recurring payments to providers outside the country. He said the payments, although relatively small for individual businesses, accumulated across the economy and contributed to foreign exchange outflows. Akinsanya also raised concerns about the location of business data, noting that information hosted on foreign servers could be subject to the laws and regulatory requirements of the countries where the servers were located.

NiRA’s figures show that Nigeria had more than 268,000 active .ng domain names in October 2026, a relatively small number compared with its population of more than 242 million. The country had 996 country-code domains per million people, compared with 1,962 in Kenya and 21,407 in South Africa, highlighting the gap in local domain adoption. South Africa has about 1.4 million domains for a population of 65 million.

Its company registry, the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission, has operated a registration-linked domain initiative with the . ZA Domain Name Authority since 2017. NiRA recorded 98,285 registrations across the .ng domain space in 2025 through 168 accredited registrars and more than 500 resellers. The association had also reduced domain prices by about 40 per cent in 2022 to encourage adoption.

Under the proposed Nigerian arrangement, the CAC portal would connect to accredited domain registrars through a shared management platform, allowing businesses to obtain domain names corresponding to their registration details. Akinsanya said the arrangement would give businesses a recognised online identity linked to their CAC registration numbers and encourage more companies to establish their digital presence within Nigeria.

“This partnership is about giving every Nigerian business a trusted home online, here in Nigeria. It will contribute to our digital economy, help us keep new businesses and their data within our borders, and reduce the pressure on foreign exchange that comes from renewing foreign domain names every year,” he said. The Registrar-General of the CAC said the proposed partnership could also create an opportunity to align annual business filings with the renewal of domain names.

The discussions come as NiRA pursues measures to increase adoption of the country’s internet domain. The National Information Technology Development Agency approved the association’s 2026 expansion plan, while NiRA digitally signed the .ng registry zone with Domain Name System Security Extensions in February 2026. In April, the association called for an executive order mandating the use of .ng domains for government communications, public institutions and official digital engagements.

NiRA and the CAC are expected to hold another meeting before the end of the year, with a target of signing a memorandum of understanding.
