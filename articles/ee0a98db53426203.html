Australia Introduces Stricter Rules for Foreign Workers' Travel Visas From November 30

Australia will apply tighter scrutiny to Bridging Visa B (BVB) applications linked to employer-sponsored visa pathways from November 30, 2026.

This will require applicants to provide specific evidence justifying their overseas travel rather than relying on general statements.

The change affects foreign workers awaiting decisions on the Skills in Demand visa (Subclass 482), the Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186), and the Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional visa (Subclass 494), as well as family members included in those applications.

A Bridging Visa A allows a holder to remain lawfully in Australia while a substantive visa is being processed, but it lapses the moment the person departs the country.

A BVB is the only bridging visa that permits a holder to leave and return within an authorised travel window.

The underlying legal standard has not changed. Clause 020.212(2)(d) of the Migration Regulations 1994 already requires that an applicant&#039;s reasons for leaving and re-entering Australia be "substantial".

What shifts from November 30 is how rigorously that standard will be applied. Immigration lawyers describe it as a move away from a permissive, near-automatic process towards an individualised assessment tied to the specific trip being proposed.

Travel validity periods will now reflect the stated purpose, the anticipated departure and return dates, and the applicant&#039;s individual circumstances, rather than being granted for extended, open-ended periods as has often been the practice.

Vague justifications such as a general holiday or a family visit are unlikely to satisfy the threshold without supporting documentation.

Applicants should prepare travel itineraries, expected travel dates, a detailed explanation of why the trip is necessary, and corroborating evidence.

Depending on the purpose, this could include medical records, business invitations, conference registrations, or documentation related to family events.

The BVB application fee currently stands at AUD 575 (approximately KSh 48,000) for most applicants, following a significant increase from AUD 190 on July 1, 2026.

There are no published processing-time standards, and immigration advisers recommend lodging applications between four weeks and three months before the intended departure.

Lodging an application only a few days before travel carries the risk of it not being processed in time.

Applications already lodged before November 30, 2026 will continue to be assessed under the existing approach.

Applicants with pending applications are not required to submit additional information unless the department formally requests it.

This change is distinct from a separate measure that took effect on November 1, 2026, which removed BVB eligibility entirely for Protection Visa (Subclass 866) applicants.

Workers with pending employer-sponsored applications should confirm their current bridging visa status before booking travel, avoid non-refundable arrangements until a BVB has been granted, and note that departing on a Bridging Visa A without a valid BVB will cause the visa to lapse and may prevent re-entry.

Sponsoring employers should also account for the new framework when planning for business travel or staff absences during the holiday season.

In other news, Immigration New Zealand will remove 17 occupations from its Green List Work to Residence pathway from March 8, 2027, shifting affected trades and technician roles to the Skilled Migrant Category (SMC).

Thirteen occupations, including motor mechanics, welders, metal fabricators, automotive electricians and building associates, will move to the Trades and Technician pathway, while crane operators, telecommunications technicians, paving plant operators and pressure welders will enter the Skilled Work Experience pathway.

The new routes require applicants to meet higher experience, qualification and wage thresholds, including at least four years of relevant experience and 18 months earning NZ$35 per hour for the Trades and Technician pathway, or five years of experience and two years earning NZ$38.50 per hour for the Skilled Work Experience route.

However, workers who began accumulating qualifying experience before March 8, 2027, may continue counting it towards the existing 24-month residence requirement, while four special education teaching roles will move to the Straight to Residence pathway.
