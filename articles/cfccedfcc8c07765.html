KRA Explains Reverse Invoicing for Kenyan Farmers to Meet eTIMS Requirements

The Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) unveiled a reverse invoicing arrangement under which factories and other agricultural produce buyers can generate electronic tax invoices on behalf of small-scale farmers, removing the need for individual farmers to operate their own invoicing systems.

KRA Commissioner for Micro and Small Taxpayers George Obell announced the arrangement at the KRA Citizen Assembly, a taxpayer town hall held at Murang&#039;a University on Tuesday, November 17, 2026.

Obell said the arrangement addresses a persistent challenge in agricultural supply chains where large processors deal with numerous smallholder suppliers who may lack the equipment, internet connectivity, or technical know-how to issue eTIMS invoices independently.

Under the arrangement, an approved buyer such as a dairy processor documents the transaction directly within eTIMS, capturing details including the quantity of produce received and the agreed price.

A dairy factory buying milk from smallholder farmers, for instance, can generate an invoice reflecting each delivery without requiring each farmer to maintain a separate invoicing system.

This benefits buyers as well, since the generated invoices serve as valid documentation to support expense claims when filing tax returns.

Before any buyer can use the system, KRA requires them to complete a Know Your Customer process and obtain explicit approval from the authority.

Farmers must give informed consent before invoices can be raised in their name, and they receive notifications by SMS or email each time a transaction is recorded.

Farmers can also independently monitor and validate invoices through their eTIMS dashboard.

Buyers are required to capture the farmer&#039;s KRA PIN, the quantity supplied, and the transaction price, and must maintain a full audit trail of all records.

KRA also stipulates that buyers must absorb the operational costs of the reverse invoicing functionality rather than passing them on to farmers.

The arrangement is not entirely new. KRA introduced reverse invoicing on December 27, 2024, initially capturing transactions worth KSh 800 million.

At that point, approximately 800 buyers had used the system to generate around 4,500 invoices for small suppliers.

The renewed push comes after KRA issued a public notice on November 10, 2025, confirming that from January 1, 2026, declared income and expenses in tax returns would be validated against eTIMS records.

That requirement exposed a documentation gap for businesses buying produce from farmers unable to issue compliant electronic invoices.

Reverse invoicing is positioned as one of several solutions, alongside eTIMS Lite services accessible through eCitizen and USSD, designed to draw informal and small-scale suppliers into the electronic tax framework.

Obell encouraged farmers encountering difficulties with the process to contact KRA offices or reach out through the authority&#039;s official communication channels for assistance.
