Organisation takes anti-GBV campaign to communities affected by violence

WhatsAppFacebookX

Youth for a Sustainable World (YSW) has joined community organisations, health officials, police officers and other partners in marking the launch of the 16 Days of Activism against gender-based violence (GBV), calling for community action, support for survivors and access to justice for victims.

Speaking during the launch at Mukuru kwa Njenga estate, Nairobi County, Christabel Were, a Projects Officer at YSW supporting programme implementation, said the event was organised to remember women and girls who have experienced violence, honour survivors and raise awareness about the need to end GBV.

Ms. Were said the choice of venue, located next to the Mukuru dumpsite, was deliberate, given the history of violence against women in the area, including the recovery of women&#8217;s bodies (suspected victims of femicide) from the dumpsite in July 2024.

She noted that the event provided an opportunity for the community to reflect on the experiences of victims and survivors while renewing its commitment to fighting violence against women and girls.

“Today is basically about remembering these women, celebrating them, but also calling the community to action,&#8221; she said.

According to Ms. Were, YSW has been working in Mukuru for several years, through organised youth groups and existing community structures to reach young people and promote awareness of gender-based violence and sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR).

The Projects Officer explained that the organisation works with peer educators aged between 10 and 24 years through its youth empowerment centres to reach young people within the community.

They also collaborate with community health promoters and sub-county health teams to mobilise residents, provide information and link survivors to essential support services.

Ms. Were emphasised the importance of strengthening partnerships with law enforcement agencies, particularly police gender desks, to ensure survivors receive assistance and can pursue justice.

She observed that while reporting and awareness efforts are important, access to justice remains a significant challenge for many survivors.

Through its initiatives, it seeks to support survivors to navigate through legal processes and be able to access justice.

“We want these survivors to get justice. The call is usually to support these survivors so that they are able to navigate the legal processes and actually get justice,” she said.

During this year&#8217;s 16 Days of Activism, YSW plans to intensify its awareness campaigns across the 13 counties where it operates through community events, webinars, digital platforms and partnerships with other stakeholders.

In Nairobi, the organisation has identified Embakasi South and Embakasi East as key areas of focus, with activities aimed at mobilising communities and strengthening the fight against GBV.

Ms. Were urged young people and community members to speak out against gender-based violence, support survivors and actively participate in efforts to prevent further cases.

She said ending GBV requires collective responsibility, noting that raising awareness and encouraging young people to use their voices can help trigger meaningful action.

“The power is usually in their hands. We need to rally young people to have that voice and urgency to speak about these issues. The more we speak about it, the more we bring awareness, and the more we can get action taken,” she said.

YSW called on communities, partners and relevant authorities to work together to ensure that survivors are supported, perpetrators are held accountable and women and girls can live free from violence.

Nyambura Kahanyah, the facility in-charge and Manager at Kware Dispensary, said most cases reported at the facility involve physical violence affecting both men and women, while sexual violence also accounts for a number of cases handled.

Ms Kahanyah said financial challenges were among the major factors forcing survivors, particularly women, to remain in abusive relationships.

She observed that some women continue living with abusive partners because they depend on them financially and lack alternative sources of income to support themselves and their families.

“For a woman to stay in a marriage that is not working and where she is being violated, sometimes it is because she does not have support from outside. She continues staying with the person violating her because of the financial support she receives,&#8221; she said.

The dispensary manager noted that economic empowerment initiatives were essential in helping survivors become financially independent and make informed decisions about their safety and future.

She said many cases of GBV go unreported because survivors fear the consequences of reporting individuals who provide for their household needs.

&#8220;So many cases are not reported. How do you report the person who is supporting you in the house?&#8221; she posed.

She explained that the facility handles basic medical services before referring survivors requiring specialised attention to higher-level facilities, including Tumaini Clinic in Mukuru.

Ms Kahanyah added that the survivors seeking to pursue legal action are also guided to the police station&#8217;s gender desk to facilitate reporting and access to relevant support services.

She emphasised the importance of coordination between healthcare providers and law enforcement agencies to ensure survivors receive appropriate medical attention, counselling and assistance in pursuing justice.

Ms Kahanyah commended the partnership between Kware Dispensary, Youth for a Sustainable World (YSW) and the Sisbro of Zion Foundation for supporting survivors, particularly those facing financial difficulties.

She said partners have been instrumental in providing transport assistance for survivors requiring referrals to health facilities, counselling, mentorship and follow-up for survivors, helping them recover and rebuild their lives.

Kahanyah called on the government to strengthen the capacity of police officers through specialised training on gender-based violence and the appropriate handling of survivors.

“Uniformed officers should be equipped with the skills to receive complaints sensitively, protect survivors&#8217; dignity and guide them through the reporting and justice processes.”
