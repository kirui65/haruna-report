MSEA advances grassroots industrialisation through common user machines

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Kariobangi Centre of Excellence, MSEA&#8217;s model manufacturing and incubation hub, continues to advance grassroots enterprise development through access to productive infrastructure. Today, the Centre hosted an economic empowerment forum during which 44 registered youth and women groups from Mathare Constituency received Common User Machines to strengthen their enterprise operations and productivity.

The forum was presided over by the Principal Secretary, State Department for MSMEs, Hon. Susan Auma Mang’eni, alongside Mathare Constituency MP, Hon. Tom Aluoch, MSEA Board Chairman, Dr. James Mureu, Board Members and Director General/CEO, Mr. Henry Rithaa, bringing together over 600 Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs), including members of registered groups and associations.

In her address, the PS underscored the critical role of MSEs in Kenya&#8217;s economy, noting that they account for approximately 98% of businesses in the country. She highlighted the Government&#8217;s commitment to expanding economic opportunities for youth through programmes such as the National Youth Opportunities Towards Advancement (NYOTA), whose second phase is expected to extend support to more young entrepreneurse across the country.

She also encouraged MSEs to leverage the Kenya Jobs and Economic Transformation (KJET) Project, which strengthens MSME clusters through capacity building and co-investment support along priority value chains. Additionally, she sensitised participants on the ongoing AGPO training programme, accessible through *254#, which equips participants with the knowledge and certification needed to access government procurement opportunities. She urged youth and women to take advantage of the programme and explore available financing opportunities through the Hustler Fund, Uwezo Fund, Women Enterprise Fund and Youth Enterprise Development Fund.

Speaking during the forum, MSEA Director General/CEO, Mr. Henry Rithaa, encouraged the beneficiary groups to make productive use of the equipment, noting that access to appropriate machinery is essential in strengthening enterprise capacity, improving productivity and creating employment opportunities. He further emphasised the importance of organised groups in leveraging shared infrastructure to grow sustainable businesses.

The 44 beneficiary groups received a range of equipment, including car wash machines, welding machines, sewing machines, generators and woodworking machines. The equipment will support various enterprise activities, enhance production capabilities and open up additional income-generating opportunities for youth and women within Mathare Constituency.

Through Centres of Excellence and enterprise development programmes, MSEA continues to bring productive infrastructure, business support and economic opportunities closer to MSEs, advancing inclusive growth, job creation and the development of a competitive MSE sector.

Present at the forum were representatives from Uwezo Fund, Women Enterprise Fund, the National Government Administration and other stakeholders.
