Sports Department Warns Sh8.38 Billion Budget Shortfall Could Jeopardise AFCON 2027 Preparations

State Department for Sports says it needs Sh10.78 billion in recurrent funding but has received only Sh2.39 billion as Kenya prepares for major international sporting competitions.

Kenya’s preparations to host the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) and the 2028 World Athletics Championships could face financial challenges after the State Department for Sports revealed an Sh8.38 billion funding shortfall in its proposed 2027/2028 budget.

Principal Secretary Elijah Mwangi raised the concern before the National Assembly Sports and Culture Committee, chaired by Dan Wanyama, while presenting the department’s budget proposals.

Mwangi said the department requires Sh10.78 billion to meet its recurrent expenditure obligations in the 2027/2028 financial year but has been allocated only Sh2.39 billion under the Budget Policy Statement (BPS).

The allocation leaves a deficit of Sh8.38 billion, which the PS warned could undermine the department’s ability to implement critical programmes and meet its financial obligations.

Mwangi attributed the increased budgetary requirements partly to preparations for hosting major international sporting competitions.

According to the PS, AFCON 2027 preparations require Sh3.55 billion, while preparations for the 2029 World Athletics Championships require approximately Sh3.9 billion.

He told MPs that the proposed funding would also cater for essential recurrent expenses needed to sustain departmental operations and deliver its core programmes.

“The higher requirement in FY 2027/28 are for preparation to host AFCON 2027 which is Sh3,546,789,165 and the 2029 World Athletics Championships which is Sh3.9 billion,” Mwangi told the committee.

The department's total resource requirement for the 2027/2028 financial year stands at Sh42.01 billion, comprising Sh10.78 billion for recurrent expenditure and Sh31.23 billion for development programmes.

However, the Budget Policy Statement provides an allocation of Sh27.75 billion, leaving the department with an overall funding gap of approximately Sh14.26 billion.

The shortfall raises questions about the government's financial preparedness to deliver the infrastructure, logistics and administrative requirements associated with hosting major international sporting events.

During the committee proceedings, Wanyama urged the department to ensure that available resources are fairly distributed, particularly in settling outstanding debts owed to sports federations and other creditors.

He also called for the completion of sports academies being established across constituencies, noting the importance of investing in grassroots talent development.

The committee chairperson observed that funding national teams remained one of the primary responsibilities of the Sports Fund but expressed concern over the financial difficulties facing several federations.

“The issue of funding of national team was one of the priority things that the sports fund must do. So far we are doing well but federations have issues that you have quite a number of debts to pay to them and they are having a lot of problems,” Wanyama said.

Meanwhile, the State Department for Youth Affairs and Creative Economy, led by Principal Secretary Jacob Fikirini, raised concerns over the lack of funding for film location mapping in the 2027/2028 financial year.

Fikirini told MPs that the project remained ongoing and was expected to be completed by June 2027.

The department further reported receiving a recurrent allocation of Sh974.95 million in the current financial year, against expenditure of Sh400.99 million, representing an absorption rate of 41 percent.

Separately, Principal Secretary Ummi Bashir of the State Department for Culture, Arts and Heritage asked the committee to recommend additional funding to support departmental programmes and responsibilities.

The department cited inadequate funding for key priorities among its semi-autonomous government agencies, including the National Lottery Board, Kenya National Library Service and National Heroes Council.

It said additional resources were necessary to enable the institutions to implement planned activities and fulfil their respective mandates.
