27 years on, family seeks justice over alleged police killing

WhatsAppFacebookX

Dominic Kioko, grandson of the late Dominic Musomba Wambua, during an interview on November 22, 2026, in which he called for investigations into his grandfather's death in a 1999 police shoot-out to be reopened.

Twenty-seven years after Dominic Musomba Wambua was killed in a violent shoot-out, the pain of losing him has not faded, nor has his family's quest for answers.

What began as an apparently ordinary Saturday morning on April 24, 1999, ended in tragedy when Wambua, then 38, was caught up in an exchange of gunfire between police and suspected bank robbers shortly after arriving in Nairobi's city centre to run personal errands.

His death not only cut short his life but also disrupted the future of his five children, who lost their sole breadwinner. The effects of the loss have since extended to another generation, with his grandchildren growing up without knowing him.

Dominic Kioko, grandson of the late Dominic Musomba Wambua, called for investigations into his grandfather's death in a 1999 police shoot-out to be reopened.

Now, a new generation is determined to keep the family's quest alive. His grandson, Dominic Kioko, wants the circumstances surrounding his grandfather's death investigated and those responsible held to account.

“We, the grandchildren, have lived knowing that our grandfather was innocently killed at the hands of the police,” says Kioko, 21.

He was not yet born when the incident happened but says that has not diminished his determination to pursue the matter. The family is now seeking the intervention of the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (Ipoa) to reopen investigations and establish what happened.

Kioko says previous attempts to seek redress through government institutions have yielded little.

“Despite the years that have passed, our wounds are still fresh. I have visited the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights (KNCHR), but I have not received the help we need. No one was arrested, the government has never explained what happened, and our demand for an inquest has gone unanswered,” he says.

Beyond the loss of their grandfather, Kioko says the family continues to grapple with the economic consequences of his death, particularly the disruption of his children's education and livelihoods.

“His children did not get the education they should have because his life was cut short. We have never received compensation or even an official communication from the government,” he says.

He is also troubled by the circumstances surrounding Wambua's death, particularly an additional bullet wound he says was discovered on the body.

“It is very sad that people entrusted with protecting lives could be responsible for someone's death. Where did the additional bullet come from? We need to know what happened after he was shot,” he says.

In pursuit of redress, Kioko approached KNCHR and was advised to submit a claim to the Panel of Experts on Compensation of Victims of Human Rights Violations, including those arising from demonstrations and public protests. He says the panel provided a claim form, which he completed and returned.

Documents seen by the Nation show that Kioko submitted the claim as Wambua's representative, with authorisation from his widow, Rose Nduku.

The claim identifies officers from the Kenya Police Flying Squad and Quick Response Mobile Unit as responsible for the killing. It lists the harm suffered as death, torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment, loss of livelihood, economic loss and psychological trauma.

In his account, Kioko states that Wambua was shot in the chest but remained alive when placed at the back of a police Land Cruiser. He alleges that his grandfather died while in police custody on the way to hospital.

He further states that the body was taken to City Mortuary, now Nairobi Funeral Home, with an additional bullet wound to the mouth or head.

The claim describes Wambua as the family's sole breadwinner, saying his death deprived his widow and children of income, school fees and basic support. It also states that Nduku and the children have continued to suffer psychological trauma, while their education and upkeep were adversely affected.

In a letter dated September 29, 2026, addressed to the KNCHR chairperson, Kioko formally sought intervention over what he describes as his grandfather's extrajudicial killing.

He refers to the incident involving the Housing Finance Company of Kenya (HFCK), now HF, Gill House branch robbery, which was reported by the Daily Nation and documented by the Kenya Human Rights Commission (KHRC).

According to the letter, KHRC had previously urged the Commissioner of Police to investigate the killing, take action against those responsible and ensure the family was compensated.

Kioko further alleges that the police file disappeared at Vigilance House, no inquest was conducted, no officer was charged and the family received no compensation.

He is asking KNCHR to help reopen the file, provide the family with a copy of the commission's earlier correspondence to the Attorney-General and assist in tracing police records, including the Occurrence Book (OB) number. He has also requested assistance in securing pro bono legal representation to pursue a High Court petition.

“The worst part of this case is not merely the loss of life but the absence of justice. I refuse to allow 26 years of unanswered questions to be met with silence,” he says.

On the fateful day, April 24, 1999, Wambua had left his colleagues at Nairobi Bottling Company in Industrial Area at about 8.30am on, intending to travel to his rural home in Kibwezi after running personal errands in town.

About half an hour later, suspected robbers raided the HFCK Gill House branch and escaped with Sh3.5 million, triggering a shoot-out with police officers.

The robbery began shortly after 9.00am when two armed men, posing as customers, entered the banking hall, confronted a cashier and ordered everyone to lie down before taking the money. Officers from the Flying Squad and Quick Response Mobile Unit arrived shortly afterwards, prompting an exchange of gunfire.

During the confrontation, one of the robbers was shot and wounded but was pulled back to his feet by a colleague who fired at the officers as they dragged him towards a getaway vehicle. The gang sped towards the nearby Kenya Railways station as the gunfight continued.

Wambua was reportedly hit by a bullet during the second exchange of fire. Newspaper accounts at the time quoted witnesses as saying he was shot in the chest near the bank entrance, staggered onto the pavement and lay writhing in pain before police officers placed him in the back of their Land Rover.

His photograph, showing him seated in the vehicle with his jacket soaked in blood, appeared in newspapers the following day.

His brother, Wambua Musyoki, and colleagues recognised him in the photograph and began searching police stations and hospitals. Their search ended at City Mortuary, where they found his body bearing an additional gunshot wound to the mouth that had shattered his brain.

The discovery raised questions about when and how he sustained the fatal injury, given that witnesses had reportedly seen him alive after being shot in the chest and placed in the police vehicle.

Police, however, maintained that Wambua was among six gang members involved in the robbery. The account was disputed by witnesses, including bank guard Ronald Simiyu, who said he saw only two armed men enter the premises, while others may have been waiting outside in the getaway vehicle.

The conflicting accounts of his involvement in the robbery and the circumstances surrounding his death remain central to the family's demand for an explanation.
