Experts Raise Alarm Over Lenient Penalties in Proposed Pesticide Bill

WhatsAppFacebookX

NAKURU, Kenya Nov 24 – Organic farming lobby groups and environmental and agricultural experts have raised concerns over proposed penalties for pesticide-related offences, warning that the fines and prison terms outlined in the Pest Control Products Bill, 2025, may not be strong enough to deter offenders.

The stakeholders argue that the proposed fines are disproportionately low compared to the profits made from importing and selling hazardous, banned and counterfeit pesticides, raising questions about the effectiveness of the legislation in protecting public health and the environment.

The Bill, which is awaiting the Committee of the Whole House stage in the National Assembly, proposes fines of up to Sh50,000 or imprisonment for six months, or both, for providing false information during product registration.

Similar penalties apply to unauthorised research and certain violations involving pesticide transportation.

The highest penalty for importing, selling or using unregistered pest control products is a fine of Sh1 million or imprisonment for two years, or both.
