Former Migori County Governor Zacharia Okoth Obado has been sentenced to 25 years in prison for the 2018 murder of university student Sharon Belyne Otieno, bringing a dramatic turn to an eight-year legal battle that has drawn widespread attention in Kenya.

Obado was sentenced on Friday, November 27, 2026, by High Court Judge Cecilia Wairimu Githua at the Milimani Law Courts in Nairobi, Kenya after being found guilty of murder in July.

His former personal assistant, Michael Juma Oyamo, also received a 25-year prison sentence, while former Migori County Clerk Caspal Ajwang Obiero was sentenced to 18 years.

In her sentencing remarks, Justice Githua described Obado as the principal architect of the criminal enterprise that led to Sharon’s death, saying he supplied the motive and financial resources used to facilitate the killing.

The judge found that the three accused acted jointly with others who were not before the court, with each playing a distinct role in the plan that resulted in the murder.

Obado, however, has filed a notice of appeal challenging both his conviction and the 25-year sentence. His lawyer, Senior Counsel Kioko Kilukumi, submitted the notice on Friday, indicating that the former governor intends to contest the High Court’s July 23 decision before the Court of Appeal.

The appeal means the case, which began in 2018, is set to enter another legal phase.

Obado’s journey to political prominence began far from the corridors of county power.

Born in 1957, he trained as an educationist and worked as a teacher before venturing into business, particularly sugarcane farming. His professional background included teaching at Rapogi High School and Taranganya Boys High School.

He later became involved in the sugar industry and served as chairman of the Kenya Sugar Board, a position that helped establish his influence among farmers and business stakeholders in the region.

His entry into politics followed an unsuccessful attempt to win the Uriri parliamentary seat in 2007.

Obado subsequently shifted his ambition to the governorship of Migori County ahead of Kenya’s 2013 General Election. After falling out with the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) during the party’s nomination process, he contested under the People’s Democratic Party (PDP).

He defeated ODM candidate Edward Oyugi to become Migori’s first governor following the introduction of devolved government.

In 2017, Obado returned to ODM, secured its nomination and won re-election, defeating former Cabinet minister Ochilo Ayacko.

His two terms in office placed him among the influential political figures in western Kenya. But his political career was later overshadowed by the murder investigation involving Sharon Otieno.

Sharon, a 26-year-old Rongo University student, was seven months pregnant when she was killed in September 2018.

The High Court established that she was in an intimate relationship with Obado and that he was the biological father of her unborn child.

The case attracted national attention after Sharon’s disappearance and subsequent discovery of her body. The prosecution alleged that her murder was connected to the relationship and the circumstances surrounding her pregnancy.

Evidence presented during the trial included testimony from 42 witnesses and 81 exhibits.

Obado, Oyamo and Obiero denied the murder charges when they were arraigned in October 2018.

The proceedings continued for years, with the trial affected by legal applications, procedural delays and the COVID-19 pandemic.

On July 23, 2026, Justice Githua convicted the three men, finding that they had acted together in a common criminal plan that led to Sharon’s death.

The court subsequently considered their individual roles, the aggravating circumstances of the offence and the mitigation presented before imposing the sentences on November 27.

In explaining the sentence, Justice Githua said Obado’s role was central to the murder plot. She found that he provided the motive, instigated the killing and supplied financial resources to facilitate its execution.

Oyamo, the former personal assistant, was found to have played a role in operationalising and facilitating the plan, while Obiero provided logistical assistance.

The court held that the aggravating circumstances outweighed the mitigating factors presented by the convicts.

Sharon’s family had asked the court to consider a non-custodial sentence for Obado, citing his financial support for her children. The judge rejected that request, warning that financial assistance to a victim’s family should not become a means of avoiding imprisonment for a serious crime.

The court also declined to award compensation to the family, saying there was insufficient evidence to determine the extent of the loss and damage for that purpose.

Obado’s notice of appeal was filed shortly after the sentencing. His lawyer also requested certified copies of the proceedings, judgment and sentencing decision to facilitate the preparation of the appeal.

The former governor is challenging both the July conviction and the prison term imposed on him.

Sharon’s family has also expressed dissatisfaction with the sentences and indicated its intention to challenge the punishment, arguing that the outcome did not fully address its expectations of justice.
