17 Mastercard Foundation Scholarships Open for Kenyans to Apply Ahead of 2027 Cycle

The Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program has opened applications for the 2027 academic year across 17 partner universities.

This offers Kenyan students access to fully funded study opportunities at institutions spanning Africa, Europe, and North America.

The programme is among the most comprehensive scholarship offerings available to African students, typically covering full tuition, accommodation, meals, learning materials, a monthly living stipend, and, in many cases, airfare, visa fees, and health insurance.

At the University of Cambridge, for instance, the package extends to a laptop, books, and medical examinations. Ashesi University similarly covers all fees alongside socio-economic transition support.

The core eligibility requirements are largely consistent across partner institutions.

Applicants must be African citizens ordinarily resident in Africa and must demonstrate significant financial need, a strong academic record, and a history of community leadership or service.

Individual universities impose additional conditions. Cambridge requires a completed bachelor&#039;s degree and a focus on climate resilience and sustainability and excludes MBA, MCL, MFIN, and MACC courses.

Sciences Po requires a bachelor&#039;s degree from an approved institution and does not accept candidates who already hold or are currently pursuing a master&#039;s degree.

McGill University places particular emphasis on significant barriers to education, whether financial, social, personal, health-related, or practical.

The 17 partner institutions span five continents, and each runs its own admissions and scholarship process independently.

The Mastercard Foundation itself does not accept applications and charges no fees.

The most pressing deadlines for 2027 include Ashesi University in Ghana on November 16, 2026; Sciences Po in Paris on November 23 for early decision applicants and December 14 for final applicants; African Leadership University in Kigali on November 30; Carnegie Mellon University Africa on December 15 for early applicants; and the University of Cambridge on December 5 or January 5, 2027, depending on course funding requirements.
