How Hackers Allegedly Stole 740GB of Sensitive Data From Kenya's eCitizen Portal

WhatsAppFacebookX

The government has urged Kenyans to change their eCitizen passwords and watch out for fraudsters following reports that hackers stole 740GB of sensitive records from the state portal.

In a statement on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, the State Department for Immigration and Citizen Services warned that criminals could exploit news of the alleged breach to target users through fake phone calls, text messages, emails and links.

"Fraudsters may use them to target the public Dont share your password or one-time PIN (OTP) with anyone. eCitizen will never ask you for them," the department said.

Officials advised anyone who reuses their eCitizen password on other websites to switch to a strong, unique combination immediately. They also asked users to report suspicious account activity and rely only on official channels for updates rather than spreading unverified claims.

The warning comes after cybersecurity monitoring firm VECERT Analyser reported that a group calling itself ByteToBreach had breached the platform. The attackers claimed to have extracted 600GB of personal identification and verification documents, 100GB of government database records and 40GB of computer source code.

According to technical details from the report, the hackers gained initial access by exploiting a flaw in eCitizen's file-upload system. That vulnerability allowed them to run commands on the servers using a webshell, a malicious script that gives attackers remote control over a web server.

The intrusion also allegedly reached federated identity systems, the shared login infrastructure that lets Kenyans access multiple connected agencies using a single eCitizen account. Because of this link, the breach potentially exposed integrated entities including the Social Health Authority (SHA), the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) and the National Police Service.

The State Department, which falls under the Ministry of Interior and National Administration headed by Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen, said security teams are investigating the reports to determine the extent of the damage and whether any data actually landed in unauthorized hands.

"The State Department for Immigration and Citizen Services is aware of reports circulating online on unauthorised access to data on the eCitizen platform. The office is treating these reports seriously and the technical and security teams are investigating to establish the extent of the incident," the department said.

Despite confirming the security probe, the ministry maintained that the incident has not disrupted public access to the central government services gateway.

"We are working with the relevant government agencies to respond to the incident. eCitizen services remain available, and the public can continue to access government services as usual," the statement added.

The department promised to release further updates once technical teams conclude their investigation.
