Joy as teacher celebrates graduation in hospital

WhatsAppFacebookX

Joyce Owenda Omondi, a Bachelor of Education Arts in English Literature graduate of Kaimosi Friends University, celebrates her graduation at Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Teaching and Referral Hospital in Kisumu after missing the university's graduation ceremony due to illness.

On Friday, as scores of graduands streamed into Kaimosi Friends University, Vihiga, ready for the much-awaited conferment, Ms Joyce Owenda Omondi was not one of them.

Instead, the newly minted English and literature teacher sat on her hospital bed at the Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Teaching and Referral Hospital, waiting for her family members.

A few minutes to noon, her parents, three sisters, a brother, in-laws and several nephews and nieces arrived at the hospital.

The nurses allowed Ms Owenda to step out of Ward Eight, which had now become her temporary home. She was dressed in her graduation gown which her elder brother, Mr Benedict Wanga, had brought for her.

“My brother had gone an extra mile, he travelled to my university and collected my graduation gown. This was a whole surprise,” she said.

For hours, the family members sang, danced, laughed and shared a meal with Ms Owenda. Thereafter, the family members gave their speeches. Ms Owenda, 24, also shared her experience at the hospital.

Before leaving, the family members shared a cake, held prayers, and took photographs.

“I am very happy. This is something I really wanted despite the sickness. My family members made the day beautiful,” a beaming Ms Owenda said.

This, however, was not the kind of graduation she had hoped for after completing her final exams in April this year.

Ms Owenda said she joined university in 2021, but deferred her studies to the following year due to lack of school fees. She made friends with other students with similar fees problems.

“By the time we got to our fourth year, we would jokingly refer to ourselves as fifth year students because we had all deferred our studies,” she said, adding that they had planned to take a group photo during their graduation to remind them of a journey of resilience.

“Graduation was a big deal to me; not even once did I ever think of missing out for any reason,” Ms Owenda said.

However, in October she started feeling sickly, with symptoms including dizziness, loss of appetite and fatigue.

Her brother, Mr Wanga, said the symptoms had first appeared in 2025 but disappeared before she could be diagnosed.

When the symptoms reappeared, a month before graduation, Ms Owenda went to Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Hospital where she was told she had low blood supply.

Later on, further tests revealed she had Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), also known as Lupus, a chronic autoimmune disease in which the body’s immune system attacks healthy tissues.

She, however, did not lose hope; each time a doctor came to check up on her, she would ask if there was any possibility of attending the graduation ceremony.

The doctors, however, said it was risky for her to step out of the hospital unless an ambulance would drop her at the institution, stay by in case of an emergency, and later bring her back to the facility.

The doctors and nurses had also hinted that the celebrations could as well be held at the facility.

Mr Wanga had gone ahead to consult with the hospital administration, who allowed them to hold the celebrations at the hospital.

“I was unable to join my friends to take our planned photos since they were at the graduation grounds while I stayed in hospital. I am, however, glad that I got to experience the joy of graduation,” Ms Owenda said.

Her brother, Mr Wanga, said what mattered to the family was to put a smile on his sister’s face. They plan to hold a thanksgiving ceremony when she leaves the hospital.

“The hospital put in place all the necessary arrangements to ensure the celebration was a success,” said Mr Wanga.

The doctors, Ms Owenda says, have not only been counselling and guiding her on how to manage the condition, including routine checkups.

The 24-year-old is now hopeful that if all goes well, she will be discharged next week.
