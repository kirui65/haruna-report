Trans Nzoia targets patients to help boost nutrition, food security

WhatsAppFacebookX

The County government of Trans Nzoia has kicked off initiatives aimed at improving nutrition, food security and patient well-being.

The initiatives being spearheaded by the Departments of Health and Agriculture will see patients trained on establishing kitchen gardens at home.

The trainings being conducted through the Kenya Livestock Commercialisation Project (KELCOP) will equip the patients with skills to establish low-cost kitchen gardens using locally available materials, enabling them to produce nutritious vegetables for their households.

Speaking during a sensitisation meeting, Trans Nzoia County Nutritional Coordinator Lydia Kimani said the collaboration between the two departments was helping address food insecurity and hidden hunger among vulnerable households.

&#8220;When we talk about food safety, we are looking at how food is produced from the farm to the market and eventually to the plates at home.

Through our partnership with the Department of Agriculture, we have received kitchen garden kits under the KELCOP project, including conical gardens, vertical gardens, tile gardens and raised gardens,&#8221; said Kimani.

She said the Agriculture Department had not only donated materials for establishing the gardens but had also provided experts to train healthcare workers and patients on food production techniques.

&#8220;This initiative is helping us address food security at the household level, especially hidden hunger caused by a lack of essential micronutrients. Vegetables are rich in nutrients that are important for good health, and we are grateful for this collaboration,&#8221; she said.

Kimani noted that while health workers educate patients on proper nutrition, agricultural officers complement the effort by teaching food production methods.

&#8220;They are experts in production. As we teach people about the foods they need to eat, the agriculture team teaches them how to produce those foods. They are also training families on rearing small livestock such as chickens, rabbits and dairy goats to improve access to eggs, meat and milk,&#8221; she said.

She revealed that the demonstration gardens established at the facility include innovations such as the use of discarded tyres to grow vegetables while promoting environmental conservation through recycling.

&#8220;The project has also taught us how to prepare soil and manure mixtures for optimum production. Today we have transferred that knowledge to cancer patients so they can establish similar gardens in their homes and strengthen nutrition security while undergoing treatment,&#8221; she added.

The nutrition coordinator said the programme would also demonstrate proper cooking methods to ensure vegetables retain their nutritional value.

&#8220;Today is harvest day, and we are preparing the vegetables. We shall also demonstrate how to cook them correctly so that nutrients are not lost during preparation,&#8221; she said.

Trans Nzoia County Agri-Nutrition Officer Rael Gitonga said the initiative had established demonstration plots in several health facilities across the county to teach patients and the healthcare providers practical food production skills.

&#8220;We are here together with the Ministry of Health at the Palliative Oncology Department of Mount Elgon Hospital, where cancer patients receive treatment. We have established demonstration plots in a number of health facilities to show communities how to set up kitchen gardens,&#8221; said Gitonga.

She noted that vegetables planted at the hospital three weeks ago were already ready for harvesting and consumption.

&#8220;Vegetables are highly nutritious and particularly important for patients battling cancer because they provide essential vitamins and help improve overall health,&#8221; she said.

The County Palliative Care Coordinator Emmanuela Busara Webale lauded the partnership, saying it had improved access to nutrition education and affordable vegetable production among patients.

&#8220;This collaboration has given our clients the opportunity to learn about the importance of nutrition and how they can easily grow vegetables at home at a very low cost while ensuring a steady supply throughout the year,&#8221; she said.

Webale observed that many palliative care patients frequently visit hospitals due to low immunity and the need for blood transfusions, particularly those undergoing chemotherapy.

&#8220;With these demonstration plots, patients can grow their own vegetables and improve their nutritional status. This can help reduce the number of hospital visits associated with low immunity and repeated blood transfusions,&#8221; she said.

The coordinator expressed optimism that beneficiaries would replicate the gardens at home for both household consumption and income generation.

Several cancer survivors who participated in the training welcomed the programme, describing it as timely and practical.

The beneficiaries noted that the gardens could be established using locally available materials and managed with minimal labour, making them suitable even for people undergoing treatment.

They appealed to the Ministry of Agriculture through the KELCOP project to support them with additional materials for establishing conical gardens, saying surplus produce could be sold to generate income for household upkeep and medical expenses.

The initiative forms part of ongoing multi-sectoral efforts by the Trans Nzoia County Government to promote nutrition-sensitive agriculture and improve the health outcomes of patients living with chronic illnesses.
