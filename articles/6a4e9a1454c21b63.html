Okoth Obado Case: Sharon’s Family to Appeal 25-Year Sentence, Wants Former Governor Freed

The family of slain university student Sharon Otieno plans to appeal the 25-year prison sentence handed to former Migori Governor Okoth Obado for her murder, seeking to have him acquitted.

The family&#039;s lawyer, Anthony Opondo, confirmed that they intend to challenge the sentence, which was delivered by the High Court in Milimani on Friday, November 27, 2026.

Obado&#039;s legal team has also announced plans to file a separate appeal against the decision, setting the stage for further legal proceedings in the high-profile murder case.

The family&#039;s decision comes after Justice Cecilia Githua sentenced Obado to 25 years in prison for the September 2018 murder of Sharon, a second-year Rongo University student who was seven months pregnant at the time of her death. A DNA test subsequently confirmed that Obado was the father of the unborn child.

The court also handed Obado&#039;s former personal assistant, Michael Juma Oyamo, a 25-year prison sentence, while former Migori County employee Caspal Ojwang Obiero was sentenced to 18 years for their roles in the killing.

The sentencing followed a judgment delivered on July 23, in which Justice Githua found all three men guilty of murder after considering evidence from 42 prosecution witnesses and 81 exhibits.

Okoth Obado sentenced to 25 years in prison over murder of Sharon Otieno

During sentencing, Justice Githua described Obado as the principal architect of the murder, saying he provided the motive, money and instructions that facilitated the killing.

She said Oyamo coordinated the murder while Obiero offered logistical support.

Before sentencing, Sharon&#039;s parents made an emotional appeal, asking the court to consider a non-custodial sentence for Obado. They said the former governor&#039;s family had approached them seeking reconciliation and that they had forgiven him.

Sharon&#039;s mother, Melinda Auma Rangili, told the court that her daughter&#039;s three children needed financial support and that allowing Obado to serve a non-custodial sentence would give him an opportunity to help provide for them.

She added that sentencing him to death or life imprisonment would feel like experiencing another death in her family. Sharon&#039;s father, Douglas Zakaria Otieno, also accepted reconciliation with Obado&#039;s family but expressed anger towards Oyamo and Obiero.

The court further declined to award compensation or restitution to Sharon&#039;s family, saying there was insufficient evidence to determine the losses they had presented.
