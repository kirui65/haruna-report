Here are the top Kenyan entertainment and media stories making waves this week.

Kenyan broadcasters NTV and KTN News have taken advantage of Citizen TV&#039;s new YouTube subscription charges to attract viewers seeking free livestreams.

Royal Media Services introduced a KSh 350 monthly fee on November 21 for audiences watching Citizen TV, Inooro TV and Ramogi TV through YouTube, while maintaining free access on other platforms. NTV welcomed viewers with a promotional message before KTN also highlighted its free news coverage.

Citizen TV journalist Stephen Letoo poked fun at rival broadcasters following a surge in their YouTube viewership after Royal Media Services introduced a KSh 350 monthly subscription fee.

His remarks, shared on Facebook using the hashtag #BeiNi350, attracted thousands of reactions.

The family of gospel DJ and MC Nicholas Mwangi, popularly known as DJ Nicks – The President, has broken its silence following his death on November 23, with a postmortem reportedly revealing an undiagnosed heart condition.

In a statement, relatives paid tribute to him for mentoring young creatives and making significant contributions to gospel entertainment through events and talent initiatives.

They also announced that he will be buried on December 2 in Kahuho, Nyeri County , following memorial events in Nairobi.

Influencer Nandwa Isaac has sparked online debate after sharing a screenshot comparing live YouTube audiences for Kenya&#039;s leading TV stations, with Citizen TV recording just 47 viewers against NTV&#039;s 61,000 and KTN News&#039; 53,000.

Kenyan TikToker Baba Talisha has claimed that the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) contacted him and the hospital treating him to verify documents linked to his medical fundraiser.
