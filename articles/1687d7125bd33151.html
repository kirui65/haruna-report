Stanbic Kenya Foundation opens 20-station computer lab in Baringo

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Stanbic Kenya Foundation has opened a 20-station computer laboratory at Cheberen Vocational Training Centre (VTC) in Baringo County, in partnership with the county government.

The lab was delivered under the foundation&#8217;s Future Ni Digital programme. It fills a hardware gap at the public institution: the county government had already posted a qualified information and communications technology (ICT) trainer to the centre, but the lack of computers limited practical lessons.

The facility will build hands-on digital skills, practical artificial intelligence (AI) literacy and e-learning resources into the curriculum for all enrolled students.

&#8220;A computer lab is a doorway. For a young person in Cheberen, these 20 computers mean learning, earning, and competing without having to leave home to do it,&#8221; said Billups, head of programmes at the Stanbic Kenya Foundation.

&#8220;Through Future Ni Digital, we are investing in Baringo&#8217;s young people because when they are digitally equipped, local enterprises expand, and the entire socio-economic landscape moves forward.&#8221;

The lab will serve ICT students as well as trainees in electrical installation, garment making and masonry. The aim is to help them run digital businesses, find clients online and take part in the gig economy without moving to towns and cities.

The initiative is in line with Kenya&#8217;s reforms to technical and vocational education and training (TVET), which aim to produce job-ready, self-reliant graduates who can support local industry and reduce youth unemployment.

Peninah Bartuin, Baringo County&#8217;s executive committee member for education, vocational training and library services, said the investment would be judged by its impact on young people&#8217;s lives.

&#8220;The success of this investment will not be measured merely by the number of computers installed, but by the tangible socio-economic transformation of the young people whose lives are altered through practical knowledge,&#8221; said Bartuin.

She said the partnership shows how collaboration can unlock potential across the county&#8217;s rural vocational training network.

Cheberen VTC was established in 2007. It offers competency-based training to young people from Cheberen, Saos, Tenges, Eldama Ravine and other parts of Baringo County.
