Ruto Warns Against 'Voting Bloc' Politics in Veiled Attack on Gachagua's Mt Kenya Mobilisation

WhatsAppFacebookX

President William Ruto has taken a veiled swipe at former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua over his political mobilisation in the Mt Kenya region, declaring that "no community should be reduced to a voting bloc." The remarks, reported from Nairobi, the Kenyan capital, on November 18, 2026, come as Gachagua intensifies his political activity across a region that has become the central battleground in the country's escalating succession politics.

The president's declaration, carried by Nairobi-based Capital FM, did not name his estranged former deputy directly, but the target of the criticism was clear: Gachagua, who served as Ruto's deputy before his removal from office through impeachment in 2024, has spent months building a political base in Mt Kenya and positioning himself as the region's principal political voice ahead of the next general election, due in 2027.

Ruto's framing strikes at the heart of Gachagua's political strategy. The former deputy president has cast himself as the authentic representative of Mt Kenya interests, and his mobilisation in the region has drawn large crowds and unsettled the ruling coalition's calculations. By arguing that no community should be reduced to a voting bloc, the president is challenging the premise that a single leader can claim ownership of an entire region's electoral loyalties.

The exchange is the latest flashpoint in a feud that has grown steadily more hostile since the two men parted ways. In recent weeks, Gachagua has attacked the administration's 'Usawa ni Sawa' slogan over what he described as 'tribal' patterns in state appointments, accusing the government of favoritism in how senior public positions have been distributed. He has also welcomed talks involving the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission, scheduled over the Smartmatic voter technology contract and allegations of biased national identity card issuance, issues he has pressed as evidence of threats to electoral integrity.

For Ruto, the Mt Kenya question carries particular weight. The region played a decisive role in delivering his victory in the 2022 presidential election, when Gachagua, then his running mate, helped consolidate support across the vote-rich counties. The president's decision to confront the voting bloc narrative directly signals that his camp now views his former deputy's mobilisation there as a serious political threat rather than a passing nuisance.

The veiled swipe also fits a broader pattern in the president's recent public engagements, which have combined attacks on his political rivals with defenses of his administration's record. Ruto has simultaneously been promoting his government's infrastructure and public service programs, telling Parliament that plans to dual the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway are at an advanced stage and announcing savings of Sh1.5 billion from the first local printing of national examination papers in 65 years, part of an effort to shore up his development credentials as the political temperature rises.

Attention now turns to Gachagua's response and to the next round of mobilisation in Mt Kenya, where the contest between the two men is set to sharpen as the country moves closer to the 2027 general election.
