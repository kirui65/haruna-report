Uganda tightens Ebola controls in 34 DRC border districts

WhatsAppFacebookX

The rules cover gatherings, returning travellers, aid workers and public facilities, while Kenya’s outbreak remains linked to one reported cluster.

Uganda has tightened controls in 34 high-risk districts bordering the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), requiring health ministry clearance for mass gatherings and 21 days of quarantine or monitored self-isolation for Ugandans returning from the DRC. The measures took effect immediately and are intended to reduce the risk of Ebola crossing the border.

The restrictions were set out in a public health advisory on November 19 by Ministry of Health Permanent Secretary Diana Atwine. Both Capital FM and The Star reported that Uganda’s National Task Force on Epidemic Preparedness and Response approved the measures after assessing the risk from the outbreak in the DRC. Atwine said authorities had seen continued movement across the border, including through unofficial crossing points. She also said earlier restrictions remain in force.

In the designated districts, local authorities are to monitor new arrivals and people moving into communities. Local Council chairpersons must be notified of those movements. People who cross illegally, and those who help them, may face legal action.

The gathering rule is more specific than a blanket ban. Concerts, festivals, political rallies and similar events cannot proceed in the districts without written clearance from the Ministry of Health. The Star’s headline describes the events as suspended, while its report, like Capital FM’s, says they require prior approval. Neither account says all gatherings have been prohibited outright.

Returning Ugandans must undergo health risk assessments before quarantine or monitored self-isolation. Humanitarian workers need clearance from the Director General of Health Services to cross the border, and moving bodies into or out of Uganda also requires government authorisation. Day scholars travelling between the two countries in the designated districts are barred from crossing; boarding students with possible exposure must complete 21 days in designated quarantine facilities before returning to school.

Airports, accommodation and public facilities are also covered. Entebbe International Airport has enhanced screening for arriving and departing passengers, and travellers must complete locator forms before boarding. Airlines and other transport operators risk sanctions if they knowingly carry people without proper authorisation or documentation. Hotels must keep guest registers and verify identification. Schools, health facilities, transport terminals, places of worship and other public facilities must provide handwashing facilities and follow infection-prevention measures. Radio stations in the affected districts must devote at least two hours a month to Ebola-prevention campaigns.

The accounts largely agree on the scope and terms of the restrictions. Capital FM gives extra detail on the instructions to local leaders and the monitoring of hotels and schools. The Star supplies the clearest account of the Kenyan outbreak: it reports that the first case was confirmed on October 6, after a traveller came through Uganda from the DRC; the patient died in Nairobi on October 5. The Star says Kenya recorded nine cases and two deaths , all linked to one transmission cluster, with no evidence of community transmission.

Capital FM says Kenya was approaching the end of its Ebola monitoring period, but the report provided cuts off before giving an end date or further details. The Star does not give a monitoring-period deadline. That leaves the status and timing of the end of monitoring unclear in these accounts.

Only two outlets in this coverage have reported the new measures, so the available reporting is narrow. Both describe an extensive set of controls, but neither says how long they will remain in force or how compliance will be enforced beyond the stated penalties and sanctions. Those details, and whether the rules will prevent further cross-border transmission, are still open.
