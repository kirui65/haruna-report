Kenya exploring AI application to improve healthcare delivery

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Ministry of Health is stepping-up efforts to integrate Artificial Intelligence into healthcare delivery, while strengthening doctors’ welfare and expanding access to HIV prevention services through strategic partnerships.

Principal Secretary (PS) for Medical Services, Dr Ouma Oluga, held a series of engagements at Afya House in Nairobi with representatives of global health organisations, technology experts and the Kenya Medical Practitioners, Pharmacists and Dentists Union (KMPDU), to explore ways of improving healthcare delivery and strengthening the country’s health system.

In a meeting with Ms. Anne Stake, Global Health Team Lead, at Anthropic BPC, the Ministry explored opportunities to harness artificial intelligence (AI) to improve clinical decision-making, disease surveillance, diagnostic efficiency and overall healthcare delivery.

The discussions come as Kenya accelerates its digital health transformation through the Digital Health Agency (DHA), which is spearheading efforts to strengthen digital infrastructure and equip healthcare workers with technology-driven tools to support more informed clinical decisions.

Oluga emphasised the importance of ensuring that AI applications in healthcare align with Kenya’s national AI in Health Strategy, particularly on data sovereignty, security and responsible technology use.

The engagement also highlighted the potential of AI to strengthen health systems while ensuring that emerging technologies are deployed in ways that protect patient information and support sustainable healthcare delivery.

Stake is in Kenya to engage healthcare workers and county health teams in Machakos and Nairobi as part of efforts to understand opportunities and challenges in applying AI to healthcare.

In a separate meeting, Oluga hosted KMPDU officials led by Secretary General, Dr Davji Atellah, to discuss doctors’ welfare and strengthen cooperation between the Ministry and the union.

The PS reaffirmed the Government’s commitment to constructive dialogue and sustained engagement with medical practitioners, recognising their critical contribution to healthcare service delivery.

The discussions focused on promoting the welfare of doctors and addressing issues affecting healthcare professionals, with both sides underscoring the importance of collaboration in improving the quality of health services.

Oluga also held talks with a delegation from the Elton John AIDS Foundation (EJAF) and Tiko Africa on strengthening sustainable HIV prevention programmes through increased national ownership, predictable financing and strategic partnerships.

He called for expanded access to HIV prevention services, particularly among populations at substantial risk, while urging development partners to align their support with national health priorities and strengthen existing health systems.

The meeting also examined emerging HIV prevention technologies, including long-acting injectable Lenacapavir, with Oluga stressing the need for scientific evidence, affordability, regulatory compliance and adequate health-system preparedness before their wider adoption.

The engagement explored opportunities to strengthen HIV prevention programmes, generate evidence and introduce innovative approaches to service delivery.

The delegation was led by EJAF Chief Executive Officer Anne Aslett and Tiko Africa Co-Chief Executive Officer Serah Malaba, alongside representatives from the two organisations.

The engagements reflect the Ministry’s broader focus on combining digital innovation, improved healthcare workforce welfare and strategic partnerships to build a resilient, accessible and sustainable health system.
