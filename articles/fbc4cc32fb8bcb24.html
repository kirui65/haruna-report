Kenya Unveils USD 692 Mn Circular Economy Strategy

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya has launched the National Circular Economy Strategy and Implementation Plan (CESIP) 2027–2032, aimed at transforming waste into an economic resource, reducing pollution and creating employment opportunities.

Launched on November 24, the five-year strategy seeks to mobilise over USD 692 million in investment by shifting from traditional waste disposal to a circular economy based on reusing, repairing, recycling and recovering materials.

The strategy prioritises five sectors: agriculture and food systems, plastics and packaging, electrical and electronic equipment, textiles and fashion, and mining, quarrying and construction. Environment Cabinet Secretary Deborah Barasa said the initiative would promote resource efficiency, sustainable production and consumption while creating investment opportunities and reducing environmental degradation.

Key measures include strengthening recycling infrastructure, enforcing Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), promoting waste segregation at source and encouraging the use of recycled materials through green public procurement.

County governments will work with the national government to improve waste collection, sorting and processing systems. The strategy also seeks to integrate informal waste workers into formal economic structures, improve their working conditions and expand employment opportunities for women and young people.

The government expects the initiative to stimulate local manufacturing, strengthen supply chains, reduce reliance on raw material imports and create opportunities for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises. Beyond economic benefits, the strategy aims to reduce pollution, minimise waste sent to dumpsites, conserve natural resources and support climate action.

However, its success will depend on effective implementation, sustained financing, strong government coordination and active participation from businesses, investors, consumers and waste workers.
