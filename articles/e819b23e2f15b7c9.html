Wangari and Thuku leave UDA for rival parties

WhatsAppFacebookX

Wangari joined Gachagua’s DCP while Thuku chose Ndindi Nyoro’s People’s Party, giving UDA two departures with different political destinations.

Two UDA figures left the party on November 20 , but they did not join the same rival: Gilgil MP Martha Wangari moved to Rigathi Gachagua’s DCP, while Chinga Ward MCA Kiruga Thuku joined Ndindi Nyoro’s People’s Party. The departures put fresh attention on UDA’s support in Central Kenya ahead of the 2027 election, though the available coverage is from TUKO alone.

Thuku, elected MCA for Chinga Ward in Nyeri County, previously chaired the UDA MCA Caucus and served on the party’s National Executive Committee. He has said he intends to contest the Othaya parliamentary seat in 2027. Nyoro welcomed him alongside Kiganjo/Mathari MCA Muthoni Kuruga, who is already in the People’s Party and is seeking the Nyeri Town seat.

Nyoro predicted UDA would be wound up within weeks, saying the exodus would grow. That is his forecast, not an established account of UDA’s future. TUKO’s report does not provide a response from UDA or evidence that the party is about to dissolve.

Wangari was received by Gachagua at his Wamunyoro residence in Nyeri. She is serving her second term as Gilgil MP. TUKO reports she was elected in 2017 on a Jubilee ticket, moved to UDA in February 2022 and retained her seat that August with 35,363 votes , against 19,764 for the Jubilee candidate. She also sits on the National Assembly’s Speaker’s Panel and had voted against Gachagua’s removal as Deputy President.

Gachagua said Wangari had a mandate from her constituents to join his party. He promised free and fair nominations and urged her to strengthen her grassroots support before primaries scheduled for March 2027 . TUKO counts Naivasha MP Jayne Kihara and Kipipiri MP Wanjiku Muhia among the UDA-elected MPs already in DCP, making Wangari the third in that group.

The reports’ central distinction is easy to miss if the departures are treated as one bloc: Wangari went to Gachagua, while Thuku went to Nyoro. The first TUKO report frames both moves as pressure on UDA in Central Kenya; its earlier report focuses on Wangari and DCP’s expanding ranks. The coverage also gives different names for Gachagua’s party: one report calls it the Democratic Change Party, while the other uses Democracy for the Citizens Party. The reports do not explain the discrepancy.

TUKO’s broader account lists other figures who have moved from UDA to DCP, including governors Irungu Kang’ata and Kiarie Badilisha, senator Tabitha Karanja, and the Nyeri County Assembly. It also says Gachagua received three legislators on November 17 and names other MPs who switched earlier in November. Those details describe a wider run of defections, but they do not establish how many members have left UDA nationally.

The reports do not quantify the effect of the departures on UDA’s membership, support or organisation in Central Kenya. Nor do they independently verify Gachagua’s claim that 60 UDA members in various roles have privately asked him to reserve places for them. That claim should be kept separate from confirmed public defections.

Gachagua has set December 31, 2026 as the deadline for defections into DCP, according to TUKO. The next concrete test will be whether more UDA members cross over before then, and whether Wangari and other DCP aspirants can secure the party nominations promised for March.
