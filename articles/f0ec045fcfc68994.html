Medical professionals sound alarm over antibiotic misuse, warn of rising drug resistance

WhatsAppFacebookX

NAIROBI, Kenya, Nov 20 &#8211; Medical and pharmaceutical professionals have raised concern over the growing misuse of antibiotics in Kenya, warning that failure to regulate the use of antimicrobial medicines could undermine treatment of common infections and expose the country to a major public health crisis.

The Kenya Medical Association (KMA), Kenya Dental Association (KDA) and Pharmaceutical Society of Kenya (PSK) are calling for stricter controls on the sale and prescription of antibiotics, alongside expanded surveillance and public education to contain antimicrobial resistance (AMR).

In a joint statement marking World Antimicrobial Resistance Awareness Week, observed from November 18 to 24, the three organisations warned that drug-resistant infections are increasingly threatening the effectiveness of medicines that have long been relied upon to save lives.

They said Kenya risked losing critical gains in healthcare if urgent measures were not taken to address the inappropriate use of antibiotics in hospitals, pharmacies, communities and the agricultural sector.

The professionals cited global estimates linking bacterial antimicrobial resistance to approximately 4.7 million deaths in 2021, with projections indicating that resistance could directly cause 39 million deaths worldwide between 2025 and 2050.
