Kenya, EU agree on AI, fibre and data adequacy roadmap

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya and the European Union (EU) have concluded their Digital Dialogue in Brussels with a joint communiqué and a roadmap for cooperation on digital infrastructure, artificial intelligence (AI), data protection, digital public infrastructure and online safety.

The talks took place on 23 and 24 November. They were led on the Kenyan side by Principal Secretary for ICT and the Digital Economy John Tanui and Kenya&#8217;s ambassador to the EU, Joash Maangi. The European Commission team was led by deputy directors-general Renate Nikolay of the Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology (DG CONNECT) and Myriam Ferran of the Directorate-General for International Partnerships (DG INTPA).

&#8220;The aim is to expand trade, attract investment and create jobs in Kenya,&#8221; said Tanui.

Kenya became the first African country to set up a formal Digital Dialogue with the EU in March 2026.

The Team Europe investment pipeline, which combines funding from the EU, its member states and their development finance institutions, includes 3,000km of aerial fibre. It also includes internet connectivity for 3,000 schools, health centres and digital hubs, and Wi-Fi for 400 remote communities.

The two sides are also discussing extending the Blue Raman subsea cable system to Kenya and Tanzania. Blue Raman links Europe and India through the Middle East and is backed by Google.

The EU will put €10 million (about US$11.6 million) behind EU–Kenya partnerships with the European AI Factories, the bloc&#8217;s network of AI supercomputing and support centres. The work will start with language diversity and agriculture.

Kenya and the EU are also exploring an open-access AI compute facility in Kenya, where startups, SMEs and researchers could build and export AI solutions.

Technical negotiations on mutual data adequacy are complete. Once adopted, Tanui said, it would be the first arrangement of its kind between the EU and an African country.

Adequacy decisions recognise that each side&#8217;s data protection rules offer equivalent protection. That allows personal data to flow across borders without extra safeguards, which could make it easier for European firms to process data in Kenya.

On trade, the European DIGITAL SME Alliance and Digital Transformation Centre Kenya will start a business-to-business matchmaking partnership to boost Kenya&#8217;s digital services exports and business process outsourcing (BPO) jobs.

The EU will also share lessons on its Digital Product Passport, which will require many products sold in the bloc to carry digital records of their origin, materials and sustainability. This could help Kenyan exporters meet EU market requirements.

The two sides will also strengthen cooperation on trusted digital identity, including interoperability with the EU Digital Identity Wallet, and on protecting children online.

Tanui said the next step is projects that connect communities, help Kenyan firms win business in Europe, bring in EU investment and create digital jobs for young people.
