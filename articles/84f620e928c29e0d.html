Zimbabwean Billionaire Seeks KSh 129b to Fund Chips in Kenya, Other African Markets

Cassava Technologies, the pan-African digital infrastructure company founded by Zimbabwean billionaire Strive Masiyiwa, is in private discussions with potential investors in the United States and the Middle East to raise up to KSh 129 billion (USD 1 billion).

As reported first by Bloomberg, the capital is intended to fund the purchase of Nvidia graphics processing units (GPUs), the semiconductors that power modern AI systems, and to strengthen the company&#039;s fibre-optic and data centre networks to meet rising demand for AI computing across Africa.

Should the raise succeed, it would add to an already substantial investment drive at Cassava. The company has committed to deploying 12,000 Nvidia chips across planned AI facilities in South Africa , Egypt, Kenya, and Nigeria as part of a wider KSh 93 billion (USD 720 million) programme to establish five AI computing hubs on the continent.

A facility in Cape Town is already operational, with additional locations planned in Johannesburg, Egypt, Kenya, Morocco, and Nigeria .

Separately, Cassava is partnering with Vodafone Business and Egyptian industrial group Elsewedy Electric on a KSh 129 billion (USD 1 billion) data centre development in Egypt, which will include the country&#039;s first sovereign AI data centre.

The project is expected to begin with 20 megawatts of capacity and scale to 200 megawatts over time, potentially creating around 200 direct jobs and 2,000 indirect ones.

Cassava holds the distinction of being Nvidia&#039;s first cloud partner based in Africa. Both Google and Nvidia are among its existing shareholders, connecting the company to two of the most influential players in global AI.

Masiyiwa, born in 1961, built his business empire through Econet Wireless after a prolonged legal fight to obtain a mobile operating licence in Zimbabwe.

Cassava Technologies was later established as a separate pan-African platform encompassing Liquid Intelligent Technologies, which operates more than 116,000 kilometres of fibre-optic network, and Africa Data Centres. Forbes estimates his net worth at $2.2 billion as of 2026.

The KSh 129 billion (USD 1 billion) figure represents a fundraising target, not money already secured, and the discussions may not result in a completed deal. If finalised, it would rank among the largest private capital raises for digital infrastructure in African history.

The fundraising effort comes as Nvidia navigates a period of internal turbulence.

Co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as president and chief executive on November 12, 2026, following revelations of a personal scandal.

CFO Colette Kress has assumed the role of interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement. More than 158,000 people, including over 7,500 self-identified Nvidia employees, signed an open letter calling for Huang&#039;s reinstatement by November 15, with Elon Musk among those publicly backing the campaign.

Nvidia has stated that its operations, customer commitments, and product roadmap will continue uninterrupted.

No African partner, including Cassava, has announced any project delays linked to the leadership transition.
