Nairobi forum puts Africa’s voice at centre of global AI governance debate

WhatsAppFacebookX

NAIROBI, Kenya, Nov 19 – Scholars and legal practitioners from several countries have called for stronger international cooperation on artificial intelligence (AI) and data governance, warning that the rapid growth of digital technologies risks deepening inequalities if countries and communities are excluded from shaping the rules.

The experts said fair digital governance must go beyond technological development to address data privacy, national sovereignty, accountability and equitable access to the benefits of the digital economy.

The concerns featured prominently at the inaugural Global Forum on Digital Intelligence, Rule of Law and Interdisciplinary Governance, hosted by the University of Nairobi’s Faculty of Law on November 17, bringing together participants from Kenya, China, South Africa, Japan, the United States and other countries.

Held under the theme &#8220; Law, Innovation and China–Africa Cooperation in the Intelligent Age ,&#8221; the forum examined how countries can develop common approaches to regulating emerging technologies while protecting individual rights and ensuring that digital partnerships deliver shared benefits.

A key focus was the need to give African countries greater influence over the rules governing data and artificial intelligence, particularly as cross-border digital trade and the use of personal information expand.
