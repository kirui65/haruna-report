Dubai-based businessman charged with defrauding a Nairobi trader Sh172m in gold racket

A Dubai-based businessman has been charged in a Nairobi Court with defrauding another businessman based in Nairobi of USD1,325,970 (Sh172.6million), claiming he would supply him with 210 kilogrammes of gold.

Maheshbhai Rameshbhai Bhadra denied the offence when he was presented before Senior Principal Magistrate Teresia Nyagena.

Mr Bhadra, who was arrested on November 11, 2026, at the Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA), Nairobi, after landing from Dubai, has been in prison custody pending determination of whether he will be freed on bond or not.

Ms Nyagena heard from a prosecuting counsel that Mr Bhadra is a flight risk, as it took police eight months to locate and arrest him.

Opposing the grant of bail, the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) Renson Ingonga told the court that the alleged offence is a transnational crime and “there is no guarantee the accused will turn up in court once released on bond.”

The prosecutor urged the magistrate to disregard averments by the defence that the prosecution would liaise with the Indian Embassy in Nairobi in the event the accused fails to turn up for trial.

“Although the accused is an Indian national, he is not a diplomat and the charge he is facing is criminal in nature and individualistic. The charge is not against all Indians. It is against an individual,” the prosecutor said.

The court was informed that the accused was apprehended upon arrival from Dubai, then detained as investigations went on.

The magistrate was further informed that the offence was committed in a progressive manner between November 2025 and June 2026.

Ms Nyagena was further asked to consider the plight of the victim who has allegedly lost USD ($) 1,325,970 (KSh172.6) in the fraudulent gold scheme.

The magistrate heard that the prosecution is ready to fast-track the hearing of the case.

The accused, through his lawyer, urged the court to free him on bond, saying “he should benefit from the rights guaranteed to foreigners by the Kenyan Constitution”.

Ms Nyagena was urged not to lose sight of the fact that “any suspect is presumed innocent until proven otherwise”. The court was urged to free the accused on reasonable bond but on stringent terms.

Ms Nyagena reserved her ruling on the bail application to November 26 2026.

Mr Bhadra is charged with obtaining through pretences contrary to section 313 of the Penal Code.

The court was told that on diverse dates between November 4, 2025 and June 12, 2026, at an unknown place, with intent to defraud, he obtained USD ($) 1,325,970 (Sh172.6m) from Abdullahi Yussuf Buthul, pretending that he was in a position to supply him with 210 kg of gold, a fact he knew to be false.

He denied the charge and was remanded in prison custody.
