Kenyan court declines to block Dangote’s 24.76% stake in pipeline company

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya’s High Court has declined to suspend transactions involving Nigerian billionaire Aliko Dangote’s 24.76 percent stake in Kenya Pipeline Company (KPC), leaving a legal challenge over the sale of shares by public institutions unresolved ahead of the company’s December 2 annual general meeting. Advertisement Advertisement Justice Patricia Nyaundi Mande, in directions issued on November 10, declined to certify the application as urgent or grant interim orders before hearing the other parties in a petition filed by human rights activist Francis Awino. “This is not an appropriate matter for the grant of ex parte orders,” the court stated. Read also: Dangote set for Kenya Pipeline board seat after acquiring 24.9% stake The judge directed the respondents to file their replies by November 27, with the matter scheduled for mention on November 30, two days before KPC’s annual general meeting. The decision leaves the requested interim restrictions unenforced but does not resolve the substantive allegations or establish whether the transactions complied with applicable laws and regulations. Advertisement Dangote’s investment vehicle is expected to continue preparations to nominate a director ahead of the shareholder meeting, a move that could result in the National Social Security Fund (NSSF) losing its representation on KPC’s board. The petition, filed by Awino, challenges the acquisition, holding and disposal of shares in KPC by public institutions and pension funds, seeking greater disclosure of the decisions and approvals behind the transactions. Advertisement The activist is asking the court to compel the institutions involved to provide details of the shares transferred, transaction dates, proceeds received, approvals obtained and the processes used to execute the deals. The requested documents include investment appraisals, board and trustee resolutions, fund-manager instructions, transaction agreements and payment records. Institutions identified in reports by Business Daily and The Star include the NSSF, Public Service Superannuation Fund, County Pension Fund, Lapfund and Laptrust, alongside pension schemes linked to Kenya Power, KPC and Kenya Ports Authority. Advertisement Advertisement The respondents include the National Treasury, Privatisation Authority, Capital Markets Authority, KPC, pension institutions and a Dangote-linked refinery company. At the heart of the dispute is whether the public institutions involved followed the required procedures in acquiring, holding and disposing of their shares in the pipeline company, and how proceeds from the transactions were accounted for. Read also: Dangote stake makes Kenya Pipeline fourth most valuable firm on NSE The court has not made any findings of wrongdoing against the institutions or parties involved, with the petition still awaiting substantive determination. Dangote’s investment in KPC Dangote acquired approximately 24.76 percent of KPC in October 2026 in a deal valued at about $327 million, giving his investment group a significant stake in infrastructure that connects Kenya’s coastal petroleum facilities to inland and regional markets. The acquisition forms part of his broader expansion into East Africa’s energy sector. KPC plays a central role in petroleum transportation and distribution in Kenya, making its ownership and governance structure significant to the region’s energy supply network. The pending petition seeks to scrutinise the public-sector shareholding arrangements that preceded the acquisition and the institutional decisions behind the transfer of the shares. Dangote’s broader energy plans in Kenya have also faced scrutiny over a separate proposed $16 billion refinery project in Lamu. In October, a Kenyan court lifted an earlier restriction on the project, allowing development to proceed while the legal dispute continued. Separately, a consumer group has sought details of Kenya’s proposed $500 million investment in the refinery following plans for East African governments to participate in the project. Read also: Dangote plans $1.5bn expansion of Kenya Pipeline network The refinery-related proceedings and investment questions are distinct from the KPC shareholding petition, which focuses on transactions involving shares in the pipeline company by public institutions and pension funds. The KPC case is expected to return to court on November 30, following the deadline for respondents to file their replies on November 27. The hearing schedule comes just ahead of the December 2 shareholder meeting. For now, the court’s refusal to grant immediate restrictions leaves preparations for the meeting in place, while the legality of the share transactions and the allegations raised by the petitioner remain unresolved.
