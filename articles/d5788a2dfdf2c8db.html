Gachagua skips Christmas as DCP machine shifts to top gear

WhatsAppFacebookX

Former Deputy President hands Methu the grassroots brief as Democracy for the Citizens Party races to open offices nationwide before 2027 primaries

Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua has assigned Nyandarua Senator John Methu the role of leading grassroots mobilisation for the Democracy for the Citizens Party, as Gachagua himself turns his full attention to preparations for the party&#8217;s primaries ahead of the August 2027 General Election.

The announcement, made on Tuesday, marked a deliberate shift in the operational structure of a party that has moved with striking speed since its formation. Methu, designated as the party&#8217;s Secretary General, had returned from a two-week break and was immediately handed one of the most demanding briefs in Kenyan opposition politics: opening DCP offices across all 47 counties and embedding the party in the grassroots networks that will ultimately determine whether Gachagua&#8217;s political project succeeds or stalls.

&#8220;This morning, I have welcomed Senator John Methu back to duty after a two-week rest,&#8221; Gachagua said. &#8220;Moving forward, the SG-designate will lead the team in officially opening all DCP party offices and driving grassroots political mobilisation, allowing me to focus on preparing for the party primaries.&#8221;

Methu&#8217;s response was characteristically direct. &#8220;Back to work,&#8221; he wrote. &#8220;A soldier reports to his commander after leave.&#8221; In four words, he captured something essential about the culture Gachagua is building inside DCP — disciplined, urgent and unapologetically militarised in its language of mission and sacrifice.

That sense of urgency is not performative. It reflects the brutal arithmetic of Kenyan electoral politics. The 2027 General Election is less than nine months away. Party primaries will precede it by several months, meaning the internal competition for DCP&#8217;s parliamentary and gubernatorial tickets is effectively already under way. For a party that is still establishing its physical infrastructure — offices, branch officials, registered members — the pressure to compress years of institution-building into months is immense.

Gachagua made clear that neither he nor his leadership team would be taking the festive season off. The party would observe only two days of rest — December 25 and 26 — before returning to the campaign trail. &#8220;Senator Methu, myself and our team will have no time for a Christmas break, except on 25th and 26th December,&#8221; he said. &#8220;The task ahead, to liberate our country from agents of corruption and impunity, leaves us no room for luxury.&#8221;

He went further, offering a promise that doubles as a campaign pledge: &#8220;My assurance is that, in December 2027, we will celebrate Christmas together and make up for the one we miss.&#8221; It is the language of a man who believes, with evident conviction, that the prize is within reach — and that the cost of reaching it is a holiday season spent in constituency meetings rather than around a family table.

The significance of this moment extends well beyond the internal mechanics of DCP. It speaks to the broader political realignment now reshaping Kenya&#8217;s electoral landscape in ways that have no precedent in recent memory. Gachagua&#8217;s removal from the Deputy Presidency through an unprecedented parliamentary impeachment in October 2024 was supposed to diminish him. It has not. If anything, it has clarified his purpose and sharpened his message, giving him the narrative of a wronged man fighting back — a story that resonates powerfully in the Mt Kenya region and is finding audiences beyond it.

DCP&#8217;s emergence has fundamentally complicated President William Ruto&#8217;s re-election calculations. The Mt Kenya vote bloc, which delivered Ruto his 2022 victory margin, can no longer be treated as a fixed asset. Gachagua is working methodically to peel it away, county by county, ward by ward, leveraging a combination of personal loyalty, regional grievance and an anti-corruption message calibrated to appeal to voters who feel the promises of 2022 have gone unfulfilled.

The decision to place Methu in charge of grassroots mobilisation is strategically sound. The senator brings credibility in the Mt Kenya North corridor, a network of relationships built over years in legislative politics and, critically, the energy of someone who appears genuinely invested in the project rather than merely attached to it for opportunistic reasons. His public response to the assignment — brief, soldier-like, devoid of self-promotion — will have been noted by party supporters as a sign of the seriousness with which DCP&#8217;s inner circle is approaching the task.

What DCP is attempting is, by any measure, ambitious. Building a party capable of mounting a serious presidential challenge in under two years requires not just enthusiasm but organisation: offices that function, branches that are legally compliant, members who are registered and motivated, candidates who are vetted and prepared, and a campaign infrastructure capable of reaching voters in every corner of a geographically and demographically diverse country. The opening of party offices across Kenya is not a ceremonial exercise. It is the foundation on which everything else rests.

The opposition landscape into which DCP is inserting itself is itself in flux. Former Prime Minister Raila Odinga&#8217;s death has reshuffled the political deck in ways that are still playing out, with his elder brother Oburu Odinga now leading the Orange Democratic Movement through a period of internal recalibration. Other opposition formations are watching the DCP surge with a mixture of interest and anxiety, calculating whether alignment or competition better serves their interests. Coalition conversations are happening, though few are conclusive. The shape of the opposition by the time campaigns formally open remains genuinely uncertain.

What is not uncertain is Gachagua&#8217;s intent. He has staked his political future, his reputation and apparently his Christmas on the belief that DCP can go the distance. The machinery is moving. The offices are opening. The Secretary General-designate has reported for duty.

Kenya&#8217;s most consequential election season in a decade has begun — and by all visible signs, the Democracy for the Citizens Party intends to be at its centre.
