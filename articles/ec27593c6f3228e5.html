KNH closes trauma centre front section for 120-day renovation

WhatsAppFacebookX

KNH says urgent care will continue through alternative access points, but reports do not identify those entrances or give a detailed completion date.

The front section of Kenyatta National Hospital’s International Trauma Centre is closed for renovation for about 120 days , but the hospital says patients needing emergency and trauma care will be directed to alternative access points within KNH.

The closure began at 5pm on Monday, November 23 , according to notices reported by the outlets. KNH said the work will upgrade the affected section as part of its infrastructure improvement programme, with the aim of providing a safer, more functional and efficient environment for patients, staff and other users. The hospital apologised for the short notice and asked patients, visitors and emergency service providers to follow signs and seek help from staff near the affected area.

This is a closure of the front section, not an announcement that the entire International Trauma Centre has shut. The distinction matters for people arriving for urgent care: KNH says services will continue through designated routes elsewhere within the hospital.

The five accounts agree on the central details: the section affected, the start time, the approximate duration, the renovation purpose and the plan to maintain emergency and trauma care through alternative access points. The Star, The Kenya Times and Kenyans.co.ke all identify Dr Joel Lessan , KNH’s acting chief executive officer, as the signatory or source of the notice. Tuko also attributes its account to a statement signed by Lessan. People Daily says Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale shared the notice, a detail not included in the other reports.

The wording on service continuity is not identical. Tuko says emergency services will remain “fully operational”; the other reports describe continuity of care through alternative entrances or access points. The common assurance is that care will continue, but the reports do not explain how patient flow, staffing or capacity will be managed while the front section is unavailable.

People Daily supplies more background on the International Trauma Centre, describing its role in specialised emergency care, poly-trauma management and emergency medical training. It also lists mass-casualty response and training as functions. That context is not present in the other accounts, which concentrate on the closure notice and practical directions for visitors.

Kenyans.co.ke is the only outlet to connect this work to a wider six-month renovation plan and to report recent work on three orthopaedic wards. It says the wards, with a combined 96-bed capacity , were completed on November 4 at an estimated cost of KSh102 million . That is useful context about other work at KNH, but the report does not establish that the trauma-centre renovation shares the same budget or project timetable.

Tuko is the only outlet to publish contact details for people seeking assistance: the hospital’s main lines and mobile numbers, as well as [email&#160;protected] . For patients navigating the closure, the reports’ consistent practical instruction is to follow on-site signage and ask staff for directions.

None of the reports gives a more precise completion date than approximately 120 days, or sets out the renovation’s cost, contractor or detailed scope. They also do not identify the alternative access points by name or location. KNH’s notice promises continuity and efforts to minimise disruption, but the coverage does not say how the hospital will measure whether those arrangements are working. Those are the details patients and emergency responders will need as the work proceeds.
