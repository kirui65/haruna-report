Mother gets court nod to sue hospital, doctor four years after daughter’s death

WhatsAppFacebookX

The High Court has allowed a mother to pursue a medical negligence claim against Nairobi Women’s Hospital and a doctor more than four years after her daughter died, giving her a chance to seek answers and accountability over the death.

Justice Stella Mutuku ruled that Shillen Musyimi Mwende can file a case against the hospital and Dr Peter Igogo outside the three-year period normally allowed for claims involving personal injuries and death caused by negligence.

Ms Mwende, who is acting on behalf of the estate of her late daughter Diana Mwikali, wants the two held responsible for alleged medical negligence linked to her daughter’s death on March 20, 2022.

The judge allowed her to proceed after she explained that she had first lodged a complaint with the Kenya Medical Practitioners and Dentists Council (KMPDC) and waited for the medical regulator to conclude its investigation before turning to the courts.

The council delivered its decision on September 4, 2024, but the time taken to determine the complaint meant that the three-year deadline for filing a civil claim was approaching.

Under the law, claims arising from personal injuries or death caused by negligence should generally be filed within three years. In Diana’s case, the deadline fell on March 20, 2025.

Ms Mwende argued that the delay was not deliberate, saying she had been waiting for the council to determine her complaint and that its findings were important to the civil case she intended to file.

She told the court that the regulator’s delay was beyond her control and that it would have been premature to begin a separate court process before receiving its decision.

In her application dated July 13, 2026, Ms Mwende asked the court to extend the time allowed for filing the claim and permit her to proceed despite the missed deadline.

She argued that there were sufficient reasons and exceptional circumstances to justify the delay, adding that denying her an opportunity to pursue the case would unfairly affect her efforts to seek compensation over her daughter’s death.

Her lawyer urged the court to allow the application, arguing that the hospital and the doctor would not suffer any unfair disadvantage if the case proceeded.

He told the court that, on the other hand, Ms Mwende stood to suffer prejudice if she was prevented from pursuing her claim simply because she had waited for the medical regulator to complete its process.

The application was filed without first requiring the hospital and the doctor to respond. Neither of the two opposed the request.

In allowing the application, Justice Mutuku said she had considered the reasons presented by Ms Mwende and the circumstances surrounding the delay.

The judge said the court had powers to make orders necessary to ensure fairness and prevent injustice.

“I have considered the application and the grounds in support,” Justice Mutuku said on November 18. “It is my considered view that it will be in the best interest of justice to grant the orders sought.”

The judge consequently allowed the application dated July 13, 2026, paving the way for Ms Mwende to formally file and pursue the medical negligence case.

However, the court attached a condition to the permission, directing that the plaint, which is the document setting out her claim against the hospital and the doctor, would be treated as properly filed only after payment of the required court fees.

Ms Mwende was given 45 days from the date of the ruling to pay the fees.

“Further, the Plaint dated July 16, 2026 shall be deemed as duly filed upon payment of requisite court fees, which should be done within 45 days from the date of this ruling,” the judge ordered.

Justice Mutuku further directed that the costs of the application would be determined together with the outcome of the main case.
