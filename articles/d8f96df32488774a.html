Kenya’s Athletics reputation on the line over increased doping cases

WhatsAppFacebookX

NAIROBI, Kenya, Nov 19 &#8211; Kenya&#8217;s historic opportunity to host the 2029 World Athletics Championships is facing an uncomfortable credibility test following a fresh wave of doping sanctions against three prominent athletes, including reigning world marathon champion Peres Jepchirchir.

The sanctions against Jepchirchir, world road running silver medallist Veronica Loleo and marathoner Ronald Korir have reignited concerns over Kenya&#8217;s persistent doping crisis, threatening to overshadow the country&#8217;s preparations for the first-ever World Athletics Championships to be staged on African soil.

When World Athletics awarded Nairobi the hosting rights, the decision was celebrated as a major endorsement of Kenya&#8217;s rich athletics heritage and its standing as a global distance-running powerhouse.

However, the latest doping cases have cast a shadow over that achievement, raising difficult questions about the country&#8217;s ability to protect the integrity of its athletes and deliver a championships worthy of its reputation.

For a nation preparing to welcome the world&#8217;s best athletes to the Moi International Sports Centre, Kasarani, in 2029, the timing could hardly be more troubling.
