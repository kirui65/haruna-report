George Ruto&#039;s popular John Wick-themed nganya, Baba Yaga, is undergoing major renovations at a local garage.

Fresh photos show the extent of the transformation, with significant changes to its front section.

The images, shared with TUKO.co.ke by matatu enthusiast Brian Musili, show Baba Yaga parked in a dusty garage, barely recognisable from the front.

The bumper, bonnet and grille have all been pulled off, leaving exposed wiring and bare metal in place of the sharp gold-and-black bodywork that made the vehicle a fan favourite.

The John Wick lettering on the windscreen and the orange Baba Yaga sticker remain intact, as do the rows of roof lights, with a technician in grey overalls visible beside the vehicle.

For anyone who has followed Baba Yaga&#039;s journey, the contrast with its former look is stark.

The nganya was previously known for its menacing black-and-bronze body kit, a grille with sharp vertical slats, sleek LED headlights, and three round red-rimmed lights running across the bumper.

Musili told TUKO.co.ke that Baba Yaga appears set for a major transformation, with technicians having already dismantled the front section fans knew and loved.

He drew comparisons to Matrix, rebranded as Zoro, another of George&#039;s matatus that returned with a fresh look after going in for repairs following an accident.

The nganya was involved in a crash earlier this month along the Ongata Rongai route, reportedly rear-ending fellow popular matatu Detroit after the two vehicles allegedly engaged in a road race .

The incident left Baba Yaga with heavy damage to its front bumper , bonnet, grille and left headlight area, while Detroit&#039;s rear section also took a serious hit.

No fatalities or official injuries were reported from the incident.

The following morning, the Baba Yaga crew, operating under Raptors and 98 Sacco, acknowledged the crash on Instagram without sugarcoating it.

It was not an isolated incident either. This crash was actually the second major accident involving Baba Yaga in roughly six weeks, suggesting the rebuild is as much about necessity as it is about giving the matatu a fresh identity.

TUKO.co.ke also highlighted George’s Mood matatu, which also recently underwent a major makeover at an industrial paint garage.

Its front was coated in lilac, while parts of the previous teal-and-green artwork remain visible.
