NCPWD, APDK And Kilimanjaro Blind Trust Partner To Expand Accessibility And Economic Inclusion

The National Council for Persons with Disabilities (NCPWD), the Association for the Physically Disabled of Kenya (APDK) and Kilimanjaro Blind Trust Africa (KBTA) have held discussions aimed at strengthening collaboration on accessibility, education, employment and economic inclusion for persons with disabilities.

The meeting brought together representatives of the three organisations, including NCPWD Chief Executive Officer Dr Michael Munene, Alex Kaluki of APDK and Sopang of KBTA. Discussions focused on identifying areas of cooperation that would improve access to assistive technology, educational opportunities and sustainable economic participation for persons with disabilities.

During the engagement, Sopang highlighted KBTA's role as a delivery partner across Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda and Malawi, supporting children with disabilities through education, employment preparation and eye-care initiatives. The organisation works with education stakeholders, including the Ministry of Education, teacher training colleges (TTCs) and technical and vocational education and training institutions (TVETs).

KBTA presented its work across 60 Kenyan special and integrated schools, comprising 42 primary schools and 18 secondary schools. Its interventions include providing assistive technologies, accessible learning materials and technical support to improve the participation of visually impaired learners in mainstream and special education institutions.

Among the technologies highlighted was the Orbit Reader 20, a portable refreshable Braille display that enables visually impaired learners to read and write digital content independently. The device has a 20-cell Braille display and supports access to electronic educational materials, helping learners participate more effectively in classroom activities.

Another technology showcased was the Orion TI-84 Plus talking scientific graphing calculator, designed to help visually impaired students access mathematical and scientific concepts. According to the meeting account, the initiative is currently benefiting approximately 1,500 students, with teachers also receiving training in equipment maintenance and repairs to support continued use.
