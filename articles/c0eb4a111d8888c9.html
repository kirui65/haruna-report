Kuna makosa: Treasury hits back at KSh 233 billion new foreign loans reports

The National Treasury has formally disputed a Daily Nation report that Kenya&#039;s foreign debt increased by KSh 233.4 billion over four months, arguing that the figure refers to signed loan agreements rather than money the government actually received.

Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi signed the rejoinder on November 23, 2026, the same day the newspaper published its article titled "Foreign debt up by Sh233bn in 4 months."

The report had drawn on the Treasury&#039;s statutory submission to Parliament covering external loans contracted between May 1 and August 31, 2026.

At the core of the Treasury&#039;s objection is the distinction between contracted loans and actual debt.

Of the seven loan agreements signed during the reporting period, only two had been disbursed by the time the report reached Parliament: KSh 44.1 billion from the World Bank&#039;s International Bank for Reconstruction and Development and KSh 51.6 billion from the International Development Association, bringing total disbursements to KSh 95.7 billion.

The remaining KSh 137.7 billion had not yet been released and could be drawn down progressively over several years depending on project conditions.

It further noted that Kenya repaid KSh 89.97 billion in maturing external loans during the same four-month window.

The Treasury also took issue with the description of the lenders.

Of the seven agreements, six were with multilateral development institutions, specifically the World Bank Group and the African Development Bank, while the seventh was a bilateral concessional loan from the Kingdom of Denmark.

It highlighted that KSh 132.9 billion, approximately 57% of the total, came from the International Development Association, which it described as one of the most concessional sources of development financing available globally.

Those loans carry an annual interest rate of 1.5%, include a grace period of roughly five years before principal repayments begin, and extend to repayment schedules running to 2050 and 2051.

On the broader debt trajectory, the Treasury noted that public debt denominated in foreign currencies dropped from 47.0% to 44.6% between June 2025 and June 2026, while the average time to maturity of total public debt extended from 8.3 years to 8.5 years.

Multilateral creditors now account for 55.3% of the external debt portfolio.
