Martha Karua biography: Career, politics and 2027 role

WhatsAppFacebookX

Former Justice Minister rebrands party to People’s Liberation Party and declares 2027 presidential bid while anchoring United Opposition unity talks ahead of the General Election

Martha Wangari Karua, Senior Counsel, former Cabinet Minister, and Party Leader of the People’s Liberation Party (PLP), has formally entered the race for State House ahead of the August 10, 2027 General Election. Born on September 22, 1957, in Kimunye village, Gichugu Constituency, Kirinyaga County, Karua has spent more than four decades in public life spanning the judiciary, private legal practice, parliamentary representation, ministerial portfolios, regional human rights advocacy, and national opposition leadership.

Karua began her primary education at Mugumo Primary School before attending Kabare Girls Boarding School and St. Michael’s Boarding School in Kerugoya. She pursued her secondary education at Kiburia Girls Secondary School, Ngiriambu Girls’ Secondary School, and Karoti Girls’ Secondary School, where she sat her East African School Certificate. She completed her Advanced Level studies at Nairobi Girls’ Secondary School before enrolling at the University of Nairobi, graduating with a Bachelor of Laws degree in 1980. In 1981, she earned a Postgraduate Diploma in Legal Practice from the Kenya School of Law and was admitted to the Roll of Advocates of the High Court of Kenya. She later attained a Master of Business Administration from the United States International University–Africa (USIU-Africa) to strengthen her executive and governance credentials.

Her professional career began on the bench in 1981 when she joined the judiciary as a magistrate, rising to the rank of Senior Resident Magistrate before leaving public judicial service in 1987. She subsequently established Martha Karua & Company Advocates, operating a private legal practice in Nairobi from 1987 to 2002. During the height of single-party rule under President Daniel arap Moi, Karua emerged as a prominent constitutional and human rights litigator, representing political detainees, journalists, and pro-democracy activists. Her work alongside the &#8220;Young Turks&#8221; and women’s rights groups during the push for multi-party democracy earned her national recognition, including being named Jurist of the Year by the International Commission of Jurists (Kenya Section) in 1999.

Karua’s elective political career commenced in the historic 1992 multi-party General Election when she won the Gichugu Constituency parliamentary seat on a Democratic Party (DP) ticket. She retained the seat across four consecutive terms, serving continuously in the National Assembly for two decades until 2013. Following the National Rainbow Coalition (NARC) victory in 2002, President Mwai Kibaki appointed her Minister for Water Resources Management and Development, where she spearheaded sweeping reforms in water governance and service delivery. In 2005, she was appointed Minister for Justice, National Cohesion and Constitutional Affairs, a portfolio she retained in the Grand Coalition Cabinet formed after the contested 2007 election.

During her tenure at the Justice Ministry, Karua was a central figure in legislative and institutional reform initiatives, though she resigned from Cabinet in April 2009, citing frustrations over executive interference and the appointment of judges without her consultation. Having assumed leadership of NARC–Kenya in 2008, she contested the presidency for the first time in 2013, later running for the Kirinyaga gubernatorial seat in 2017. In May 2022, she made political history when former Prime Minister Raila Odinga selected her as his running mate under the Azimio la Umoja–One Kenya Coalition, becoming the first woman nominated as deputy presidential candidate on a major Kenyan ticket.

Ahead of the 2027 election cycle, Karua has reshaped her political vehicle and regional profile. In early 2025, she officially rebranded NARC–Kenya into the People’s Liberation Party (PLP), launching grassroots mobilisation drives across the country and pledging to serve a single five-year term if elected to demystify executive power. Beyond domestic politics, she has remained active in East African legal advocacy, notably serving as lead counsel for detained Ugandan opposition figure Dr. Kizza Besigye—a role that saw her denied entry and deported from Uganda in June 2026.

On November 27, 2026, Karua officially launched her second presidential bid in Nairobi under the banner &#8220;Simama na Martha&#8221;. Her campaign platform centres on seven pillars: economic reforms and auditing public debt, accountable leadership, affordable healthcare, universal free public education, protection of human rights, strengthening independent institutions, and safeguarding devolution. While positioning herself as a reformist contender to challenge President William Ruto, Karua remains a key principal within the United Opposition alongside leaders such as Kalonzo Musyoka, Rigathi Gachagua, and Eugene Wamalwa, maintaining that she will back a single coalition flag bearer selected through a transparent mechanism to unseat the Kenya Kwanza administration.
