10 Most Financially Stable SACCOS to Trust with Your Savings, New Report

The Sacco Societies Regulatory Authority (SASRA) has published its SACCO Supervision Annual Report 2025 , released on October 6, ranking Kenya&#039;s Deposit-Taking Saccos (DT-Saccos) across a range of financial health indicators.

The report measures five core metrics: Core Capital to Total Assets (CCA), Core Capital to Total Deposits (CCD), Institutional Capital to Total Assets (ICA), external borrowing levels, and Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratios. Based on these indicators, the following ten institutions stand out as the most financially stable.

Winas DT-Sacco leads the rankings, posting a CCD ratio of 84.94% against the regulatory minimum of 8%, alongside a CCA of 42.22%, zero external borrowing, and an NPL ratio of just 1.61%.

Kenya National Police (KNP) DT-Sacco, the third-largest by total assets, follows closely with a CCA of 35.67%, an ICA of 30.11%, no external debt, and an NPL of 2.33%.

Transnation DT-Sacco ranks third, meeting all capital thresholds comfortably with a CCA of 21.10% and an NPL of 2.90%, while Hazina DT-Sacco records a CCA of 16.15% and an NPL ratio of just 2.00%.

Sheria DT-Sacco, which serves Kenya&#039;s legal fraternity, maintains a CCA of 18.17% and zero external borrowing, with NPLs at 2.80%.

Newfortis DT-Sacco distinguishes itself with the lowest NPL ratio on the entire list at 0.48%, supported by a CCA of 13.48% and no external debt.

Imarisha DT-Sacco records a CCA of 17.49% and an NPL of 1.53%, with minimal external borrowing of 2.44%.

Mwalimu National DT-Sacco, the largest Sacco in Kenya by total assets, improved its NPL ratio to 2.42% in 2025, reinforcing confidence in the teachers&#039; institution. Its CCA stands at 13.96% and ICA at 9.89%.

Stima DT-Sacco, the second-largest, holds a CCA of 19.22% and an ICA of 12.84%.

Its NPL ratio of 6.48% is higher than others on the list, though the institution carries negligible external borrowing at 1.39%.

Unison DT-Sacco rounds out the top ten with a CCA of 16.15%, zero external debt, and an NPL of 2.16%.

A consistent pattern across the top performers is the near-total absence of external borrowing, with most institutions relying entirely on member deposits.

The report also highlights that NPL ratios below 3% serve as a reliable indicator of responsible lending practices.

SASRA&#039;s data further underscores that financial stability does not correlate directly with size, as smaller institutions such as Winas and Newfortis outperform some of the sector&#039;s largest players on key capital and asset-quality metrics.

SASRA warned that SACCO members should look beyond high dividends and consider their institution’s financial health .

The regulator cautioned that excessive payouts could weaken a SACCO’s ability to lend, withstand unexpected losses and protect members’ savings.
