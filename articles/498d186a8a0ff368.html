Rama Afullo: Kenyan Behind a $9 Million Venture Bringing AI Computing to Satellites

WhatsAppFacebookX

A team of engineers working out of Nairobi and California is helping power a space software startup that has raised more than $9 million (about Ksh1.17 billion) to process satellite data directly in orbit.

Founded in 2024 by 34-year-old Kenyan technology engineer Rama Afullo, San Francisco-based Satlyt builds artificial intelligence (AI) software that lets satellites analyze information in space rather than beaming raw data down to Earth.

Satellites gather critical data for farming, environmental monitoring, disaster response, and security. However, limited onboard computing power forces operators to transmit massive amounts of raw information to ground stations for analysis, a process that is often slow and expensive.

Satlyt’s software solves this bottleneck by running on computing hardware already installed in existing satellites. It allows satellites to process workloads in orbit and communicate as a network, sending back finished insights to operators, companies, and governments.

The system also allows third-party software developers to deploy applications in space without the cost of building or launching their own satellites. For sectors like agriculture, faster orbital processing could help farmers spot crop stress much earlier.

Before launching Satlyt with backing from venture capitalists, Afullo spent years at major global technology firms. He worked as a Global Product Manager for SpaceX’s Starlink program, where he led efforts to expand global connectivity and customer engagement.

Prior to SpaceX, he spent five years at Google as a Cloud Architect and Solutions Consultant, later managing AI startups. Early in his career, Afullo became one of Tesla’s first three employees in Africa, serving as Telco Business Lead to launch the Powerwall and build energy and telecom partnerships across emerging markets.

Afullo’s path to space technology began at home. His father is an electrical engineering professor who researched microwave and satellite technology, while his mother is a computer scientist.

Raised across Kenya, Botswana, and South Africa, Afullo earned a mechanical engineering degree from the University of Cape Town. He later moved to the United States to complete a Master of Science in Space Systems Engineering at Johns Hopkins University's Whiting School of Engineering.

Today, as Satlyt's chief executive officer, Afullo splits his engineering operations between Nairobi and California, establishing himself as a prominent voice on orbital computing, intersatellite networks, and AI autonomy.
