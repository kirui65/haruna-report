Gachagua Welcomes Three UDA MPs To DCP, Targets 60 MPs By 2027

Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua has intensified his political mobilisation in the Mount Kenya region, welcoming three Members of Parliament who have defected from President William Ruto's United Democratic Alliance (UDA). The lawmakers are Gatundu South MP GG Kagombe, Laikipia West MP Wachira Karani and Nyeri Woman Representative Rahab Mukami, who joined DCP during a meeting at Gachagua's Wamunyoro residence in Nyeri County on Tuesday, November 17, 2026.

Gachagua described the defections as a significant development in Mount Kenya politics, welcoming the three legislators into his party and inviting other political leaders dissatisfied with their current political arrangements to join DCP. He declared that the party would accommodate new members without imposing conditions on those seeking a political home.

"I welcome all leaders who wish to join our party unconditionally."

The former Deputy President announced that DCP's parliamentary membership had increased from 19 to 25 members following the latest political developments. He expressed confidence that the party would expand its representation to 60 Members of Parliament by 2027, positioning DCP as a major political force ahead of the next General Election.

"I have no doubt that we will have 60 MPs by next year."

He further announced that DCP's political recruitment window would remain open until December 31, 2026, giving elected leaders, politicians and prospective candidates an opportunity to join the party before the deadline. Gachagua used the slogan green to symbolise the party's political identity, describing it as a representation of prosperity and future leadership.

Addressing a delegation from Keharu in Kiharu Constituency, Gachagua urged residents to organise their political activities under DCP, describing the area as an important centre of Mount Kenya politics because of its historical association with the Kikuyu community. He said political decisions made in Keharu and Murang'a County carried considerable influence across the wider Mount Kenya region.

"Keharu is very important in the politics of Mount Kenya."

Gachagua welcomed the introduction of a prospective parliamentary candidate from the area, describing him as an engineer and son of Matangi. He promised that DCP would conduct transparent and competitive nominations in March 2027, insisting that no candidate would receive preferential treatment regardless of their political connections.

"We will have free and fair nominations."

He also announced that the party had established a National Elections Board and a dispute resolution tribunal to independently manage nominations and address disputes arising from the electoral process. According to Gachagua, party coordinators would not participate in selecting candidates, leaving the responsibility entirely to registered party members and voters.

"We have no preferred candidate; let the people decide."

On national opposition politics, Gachagua said Kenyans were increasingly demanding a single presidential candidate to challenge President Ruto in the 2027 General Election. He disclosed that he had held discussions with Kalonzo Musyoka, Eugene Wamalwa, Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang'i as part of efforts to consolidate opposition political interests.

"The best formula is one single presidential candidate."

Gachagua rejected suggestions that he was contributing to divisions within the opposition, describing himself as one of the leading figures committed to bringing different political leaders together. He said his immediate priorities were developing DCP into a strong political organisation and negotiating an opposition arrangement capable of challenging the incumbent administration.

The DCP leader also addressed preparations for the 2027 elections, revealing that he had contacted the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) chairman, Erastus Ethekon, regarding consultations with political stakeholders. He criticised the proposed meeting schedule of approximately three hours, arguing that electoral preparedness required a full day of discussions to address concerns raised by different political formations.
