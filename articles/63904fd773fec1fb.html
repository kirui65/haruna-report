VetLab’s Aloha Fiesta: Golf, celebration and a captain’s farewell

WhatsAppFacebookX

Outgoing Captain Raj Shah hands over to Eric Mugo at VetLab Sports Club&#8217;s Aloha Captain&#8217;s Prize Fiesta 2026, closing a year of service with laughter, golf, and gratitude.

VetLab Sports Club in Nairobi marked the close of its captaincy year with the Aloha Captain&#8217;s Prize Fiesta 2026, a two-day celebration of golf, friendship, and leadership held on Friday and Saturday at the club&#8217;s course along Kapenguria Road, Loresho Ridge.

The weekend drew together members, guests, sponsors, and friends of the club for an event that managed to be simultaneously competitive and deeply communal — the kind of gathering that reminds you why golf, at its best, is as much about the people around the course as the shots played on it.

Friday set the tone. Golfers completed their rounds before the evening gave way to a country-themed party that filled the club with music, laughter, and conversation. Members and guests leaned into the theme, and the atmosphere carried the easy warmth of a community that has spent a year building something together.

Saturday brought the golf back to the fore, but the defining moment of the weekend had little to do with scorecards. Outgoing Captain Raj Shah formally handed the captaincy to his Deputy Captain, Eric Mugo, in a ceremony that acknowledged a year of service and signalled the beginning of a new chapter for the club&#8217;s leadership.

Shah, who has served as captain for the past year, spoke with characteristic understatement about his time in the role. &#8220;I play golf for fun,&#8221; he said, with the quiet confidence of someone who has never confused the game&#8217;s rewards with its purpose. &#8220;The win will come on its own.&#8221;

His reflections on the sport itself carried a broader wisdom. &#8220;Golf is a game of patience because it really tests your patience,&#8221; Shah observed. &#8220;The game is growing, with more juniors taking part in it.&#8221; It was a remark that pointed beyond the weekend&#8217;s celebrations toward something he clearly cares about — the future of the game in Kenya, and the importance of bringing younger players into the fold.

For Mugo, the handover marks the beginning of a captaincy that follows a year spent at close quarters with the role he is now inheriting. The transition carries the advantage of continuity — a deputy who already knows the club&#8217;s culture, its members, and the demands of the position is better placed to build on what his predecessor established than to start from scratch.

The Aloha Captain&#8217;s Prize Fiesta also served as a moment of institutional recognition for the partners and sponsors who keep club activities possible. Citizen TV and Citizen Digital were among the media partners supporting the celebrations — a reflection of the growing visibility of Kenyan club golf beyond its traditional constituency.

What made the weekend memorable, however, was not the sponsorship or the scoreboards. It was the texture of it — the country outfits on Friday evening, the shared meals, the conversations between rounds, the applause when Shah reflected on his year and the warmth with which Mugo received the captaincy. These are the moments that define a club&#8217;s character and determine whether its members return not simply to play golf, but to belong to something.

VetLab Sports Club, tucked along Loresho Ridge in a city that rarely slows down, offered its community exactly that over two days in November. A place to compete, yes — but more importantly, a place to gather, to celebrate, and to pass the torch with the kind of grace that makes an institution worth serving.

As Shah put it, the win comes on its own. What you build in the meantime is what endures.
