Dubai Introduces New Social Media Ban Affecting Thousands of Foreign Teachers

Dubai has banned education professionals in its private sector from using personal mobile phones, private WhatsApp accounts and personal social media profiles to communicate with students, requiring all contact to be conducted through institution-approved platforms.

The Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA) announced the restrictions on November 13, 2026, as part of a new policy framework for safeguarding and learner protection in Dubai&#039;s private educational institutions.

The restrictions cover all adults working within KHDA-licensed and permitted institutions, including teachers, teaching assistants, coaches, volunteers and third-party contractors.

Foreign nationals form a large portion of this workforce, with Dubai&#039;s private education sector drawing educators from the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, South Africa , India, the Philippines, Egypt and Jordan, among other countries.

The rules do not constitute a general ban on teachers using social media in their personal lives.

The restrictions specifically target communication between staff and learners through unofficial or private channels.

Under the framework, institutions must ensure all teaching-related communication takes place through approved devices and platforms, giving administrators oversight of staff-learner interactions.

Schools must also prohibit personal or secretive relationships with learners and enforce documented supervision arrangements for any one-to-one meetings.

Additional conduct requirements include prohibiting staff from accepting unauthorised gifts, ensuring interactions take place in visible environments where possible, maintaining professional boundaries in both physical and virtual settings, and observing institutional rules on photography and digital content involving students.

KHDA has granted institutions a phased implementation period of up to 12 months to review existing safeguarding policies, train staff and put the required oversight systems in place.

Requirements designed to provide immediate protection to learners are not subject to this transition window.

Institutions must also strengthen their safeguarding governance structures by appointing a Safeguarding Governor at board level alongside a Designated Safeguarding Lead or Child Protection Coordinator responsible for oversight and reporting.

The framework applies to virtual learning environments as well as on-site activities, meaning online interactions are subject to the same safeguarding standards.

All personnel must acknowledge their institution&#039;s Code of Conduct during induction, and the provisions also extend to learners aged 18 and above, with procedures adapted to adult consent and confidentiality requirements.

KHDA has indicated it will track compliance through its quality assurance activities and may take regulatory action against institutions that fail to meet mandatory requirements.

The new policy forms part of a broader push to strengthen learner protection across Dubai&#039;s private education sector, with the authority citing risks such as cyberbullying and online exploitation as key concerns driving the reforms.

In other news, Dubai introduced Law No. 4 of 2026, which took effect on August 26 , banning families and individual workers from sharing the same residential units and restricting shared accommodation to designated areas.

The regulations require at least five square metres of bedroom space per occupant, one bathroom for every four residents and separate bedrooms with private bathrooms for each household in family accommodation.

Violations attract fines ranging from AED 500 (approximately KSh 17,650) to AED 500,000 (KSh 17.6 million), with repeat offences potentially doubling the maximum penalty to AED 1 million, alongside possible licence cancellations and utility disconnections.

The changes could affect thousands of Kenyans among the estimated 30,000 to 50,000 living in Dubai, although existing shared housing remains permitted if it meets the new standards, with property owners given until August 26, 2027, to comply with the licensing requirements.
