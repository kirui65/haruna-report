Africa Uncensored&#039;s investigative documentary, &#039;Beg, Borrow, Steal: Kenya&#039;s Budgeted Corruption and The China Connection&#039;, produced by veteran journalist John-Allan Namu, has been taken down following a court order obtained by individuals and entities named in the film.

Namu confirmed the development on Thursday, November 26, explaining that parties adversely mentioned in the exposé had secured an order from the court restraining publication of the documentary. He noted that although the order was not formally served on his team, they independently verified its authenticity before deciding to comply.

The seasoned investigative journalist clarified that the decision to pull the documentary down was neither an admission of guilt nor a surrender in the fight against corruption.

The film investigated allegations that more than KSh 30 billion intended for major Kenyan infrastructure projects was diverted through shell companies, questionable financial transactions, irregular tax arrangements and dubious contractors.

Its central inquiry was whether Kenya received full value for the billions borrowed to build roads and other infrastructure, or whether portions of those funds were siphoned into private networks.

The investigation drew on whistleblower accounts, banking records and tax investigations. It scrutinised two Chinese companies and their financial relationships with Kenyan businesses, raising questions about whether the government received the services and materials for which payments were made.

Among the key projects examined was the Nairobi Western Bypass, financed through a loan of approximately KSh 23.4 billion (USD 181 million) from China. The documentary questioned the value Kenyans received relative to what was borrowed and spent on the project.

The Kipevu Oil Terminal in Mombasa was also under the spotlight, with the film highlighting a figure of approximately KSh 21.5 billion in its examination of alleged financial irregularities connected to the project.

The documentary presents investigative findings and allegations; it does not constitute a court conviction of any person or company mentioned in it.

Last month, Namu called out a section of Kenyan podcasters for accepting large sums of money from politicians and passing it off as journalism.

Speaking at the OAT Media Forum, he warned that the trend is earning the media industry the label &#039;Githeri media&#039;.

The Africa Uncensored founder urged journalists to guard the profession and stamp out the culture of brown envelope.
