Wetangula declares 2032 presidential bid after Ruto’s second term

WhatsAppFacebookX

The National Assembly Speaker says decades of legislative and administrative experience make him the right successor to President William Ruto.

National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetangula has declared his intention to contest the presidency in 2032, announcing his ambition publicly on Sunday and urging Western Kenya to unite behind him in pursuit of a seat the region has long coveted.

Wetangula, who entered Parliament in 1992 and has served across successive administrations in senior legislative and cabinet roles, argued that his record positions him as the natural successor to President William Ruto upon completion of a second term. &#8220;I have the energy, experience and commitment required to serve this country, build on the progress President Ruto has initiated and safeguard the gains made under his leadership,&#8221; he said.

The Speaker grounded his argument in Kenya&#8217;s presidential history, noting that every occupant of State House since independence has arrived there through sustained political experience rather than overnight ambition. He used that logic to dismiss first-time Members of Parliament entertaining 2027 presidential ambitions, telling a rally at Jepkoyayi Grounds in Hamisi Constituency, Vihiga County: &#8220;There is no miracle in winning the presidency in Kenya. Kenyans have always put their trust in experienced politicians.&#8221;

Wetangula stopped short of distancing himself from the current administration, explicitly backing Ruto&#8217;s 2027 re-election before staking his own claim for what comes after.

For Western Kenya, the declaration is both a rallying call and a long-range political promise — one the region will now watch Wetangula work to keep.
