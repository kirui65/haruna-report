Reprieve for western Kenya cane farmers as Ksh1.2 Billion is dished out

WhatsAppFacebookX

Western Region farmers are all smiles after the much-anticipated Ksh1.2 billion bonus was released by President William Ruto.

The bonus is aimed at benefiting farmers affiliated with public sugar companies across the sugarcane belt.

The President was speaking at Nzoia Sugar Factory in Kanduyi Constituency, Bungoma County, where he presided over this historic milestone.

Farmers who supply cane to the factory received Ksh195 million, marking a significant step towards ensuring that they enjoy benefits similar to those of growers of other crops.

The President further revealed that, starting next week, farmers supplying Mumias Sugar Factory in Kakamega County would also receive their bonuses, with cane growers at Chemilil and Muhoroni in Kisumu County and Sony Sugar in Migori County following suit.

“We promised bonuses, and our competitors thought it was just another hearsay story, but today they are dumbfounded as we have fulfilled our pledge to transform sugar farming in many ways.”

President Ruto stated that, following the leasing of public factories to the private sector, farmers were now being paid every week after delivering their cane and no longer had to wait for years to receive their hard-earned dues.

He added that employees were also being paid regularly at the end of every month.

The Rai Group, which manages Nzoia Sugar Factory, has invested Ksh1.9 billion in renovating the factory, the nucleus estate and irrigation infrastructure covering 3,600 acres of sugarcane.

It has further paid Ksh688 million in taxes to the Government of Kenya.

To revitalise the sugar sector, the Kenyan government has written off Ksh117 billion in debt owed by sugar factories and ensured that farmers have access to affordable, subsidised fertiliser at Ksh2,000 for a 50kg bag.

Following the reduction in fertiliser prices, the country&#8217;s sugar production has increased significantly and is expected to hit 900,000 metric tonnes this year, with more efforts being put in place to reach 1.2 million metric tonnes and make Kenya self-sufficient in sugar production.

The country currently has a deficit of 200,000 metric tonnes, which usually creates an opportunity to import industrial sugar that sometimes ends up in the local market, flooding it and affecting local production.

Nzoia Sugar Company in Bungoma County disbursed Ksh195,652,462 in sugarcane bonus payments to eligible farmers supplying the factory, equivalent to Ksh597.10 per tonne of cane delivered.

Ruto said the issuance of the bonuses fulfilled the government&#8217;s commitment to ensuring that sugarcane farmers benefited from the improved performance of the leased sugar factories.

The disbursement of the bonuses confirms part of the government&#8217;s broader efforts to revitalise the domestic sugar industry, enhance factory performance and improve returns to sugarcane growers.

Kenya&#8217;s sugar production has recorded significant growth in 2026, with the industry projected to achieve production exceeding 900,000 tonnes by the end of the year, marking a major milestone in strengthening domestic production capacity and reducing reliance on imports.

Since it was privatised, Nzoia Sugar Company has received 327,671.18 tonnes of sugarcane from 13,946 farmers during the reporting period.

The bonus recognises farmers&#8217; contribution to the factory&#8217;s operations and underscores the importance of translating improved factory performance into tangible economic benefits for sugarcane growers.
