Over One Million Candidates Complete 2026 KCSE Examinations Countrywide

WhatsAppFacebookX

The 2026 Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) Examinations came to a close, today, with a record 1,048,355 candidates registered across 10,693 examination centres countrywide having sat for their respective papers, thus, marking the first time in the history of a national examination that candidature surpassed the one million marks.

Education Cabinet Secretary (CS), Julius Migos Ogamba, said the milestone reflected the growing number of learners progressing through the country&#8217;s education system, with the government deploying a Multi-agency coordination framework, to facilitate the administration of examinations on an unprecedented scale.

Speaking during an inspection and monitoring exercise on the final day of the examinations in Sosiot Town, Belgut Sub-county in Kericho, Ogamba said the exercise had been conducted successfully despite flooding, insecurity and logistical challenges, that threatened to disrupt examination activities in several parts of the country.

The Cabinet Secretary disclosed that the government had effectively facilitated the participation of candidates in affected regions through emergency interventions, maintaining that no candidate had missed an examination paper because of the disruptions.

He identified flooding in Tana River, Kitui, Turkana, West Pokot, Wajir and Mandera counties as one of the major challenges encountered during the examination period, with damaged and inaccessible transport routes, complicating the delivery of examination materials.

Ogamba mentioned few isolated insecurity incidents in Moyale, Isiolo, Dadaab in Garissa County, Kapedo and Arror in Baringo and Turkana Counties, which necessitated additional security arrangements, to safeguard candidates and examination personnel.

To overcome the difficulties, the government deployed helicopters and alternative means of transport to deliver examination materials, to inaccessible areas, while some candidates were temporarily relocated to safer locations, to enable them to sit their papers.

The Cabinet Secretary attributed the successful administration of the exercise to the coordinated efforts of officers from the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Interior and National Administration, the Teachers Service Commission and other government agencies involved in examination management.

He disclosed that examination materials had been distributed daily through 646 designated distribution centres countrywide, with security personnel and education officials working together to facilitate timely delivery to examination centres.

“The dedication of our officers has been very pivotal to the success of this exercise, especially in view of the challenges that we have faced this season,” Ogamba said.

During his visit to Belgut Sub-county, the Cabinet Secretary witnessed the opening of the Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC) examination container at the Deputy County Commissioner&#8217;s office in Sosiot Town and the distribution of sealed examination materials to examination centre managers.

He later proceeded to Kabianga Boys Senior School, where he monitored the administration of the final KCSE examination papers to candidates.

Turning to the next Phase of the national examination process, Ogamba announced that an estimated 34,170 examiners, would undertake the marking of KCSE answer scripts across 41 designated marking centres, with the Ministry targeting the release of results in January 2027.

He said the processing of the 2026 Kenya Junior School Education Assessment (KJSEA), had been given priority to facilitate the release of results in December 2026 and the subsequent placement of learners into Grade 10 in senior schools before January 2027.

The Cabinet Secretary disclosed that 13,484 examiners were already marking KJSEA structured questions at 24 marking centres, while multiple-choice questions were being processed concurrently through machine scoring.

He further indicated that the 2026 Kenya Primary School Education Assessment (KPSEA) outcomes would be released in January 2027, to provide feedback on learners&#8217; academic achievement as they transition to Grade 7 in junior school.

The Cabinet Secretary explained that candidates attaining university entry qualifications would have opportunities to pursue degree programmes, while others could advance their education through Technical and Vocational Education and Training institutions, the Kenya Medical Training College and other training institutions.

Ogamba said the Competency-Based Education system was designed to recognize individual abilities by integrating academic achievement with practical skills and talent development, enabling learners to pursue pathways suited to their strengths.

On school financing, the Cabinet Secretary announced that the government was preparing to review capitation rates for primary, junior and senior schools, which have remained unchanged since 2015, to address funding shortfalls affecting learning institutions.

He disclosed that the Ministry of Education would establish a team to examine the existing capitation framework and recommend adjustments, aimed at ensuring schools receive adequate resources to implement their academic programmes.

On budgetary allocations, Ogamba disclosed that government funding for the education sector had increased from approximately Sh500 billion in 2022 to Sh784 billion in the 2026/2027 Financial Year.

He further indicated that the allocation was projected to rise to nearly Sh850 billion in the 2027/2028 Financial Year as the government continues to expand educational infrastructure and strengthen institutional capacity.

Regarding the implementation of Competency-Based Education, Ogamba said the government was undertaking the construction of 1,600 laboratories in senior schools to strengthen practical learning and support the transition of learners to Grade 10.

He explained that the laboratory programme formed part of wider infrastructure investments involving additional classrooms and hostels, to increase the capacity of learning institutions as enrolment continues to rise.

Kericho County Commissioner, Omari Ali, who accompanied the Cabinet Secretary during the inspection, wished the candidates success and encouraged them as they prepare for the next stage of their education.

Also present were Kericho County Director of Education, Julius Ngoneshi and other senior national government, education and security officials.
