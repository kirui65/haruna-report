Wetang’ula sends CCTV phone footage for committee review

WhatsAppFacebookX

Chege says the phone was hers; Wetang’ula has sent the footage to a committee that he says he will not chair.

National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetang’ula has referred CCTV footage of a colleague’s missing phone to the Powers and Privileges Committee after nominated MP Sabina Chege said the phone was hers and challenged him either to identify the person shown or have it returned. Wetang’ula will not chair the review, although he is the committee’s substantive chair.

The phone went missing during a confrontation in Parliament on June 8, 2023 , when Chege’s removal as Deputy Minority Whip was being contested. Chege told the House on Wednesday that she realised it was gone after Kisumu Town West MP Rozaah Buyu picked up her handbag during the commotion. She said she later tracked the phone from Parliament towards Donholm but never recovered it.

The accounts differ over what Wetang’ula said the footage showed. On Tuesday, he described seeing an MP pocket a colleague’s phone and said the legislator denied taking it when confronted with the recording. The Standard and The Star both carried that version. On Wednesday, after Chege identified herself as the owner, Wetang’ula said the footage showed a member picking up a phone from the seat where it had fallen. He maintained that he had said “taking”, not “stealing”.

Capital FM puts the shift in wording at the centre of the story: Wetang’ula had said he watched the recording with Majority Leader Kimani Ichung’wah and other MPs, but did not name the person. Tuko reports that his Wednesday wording referred to a male member . None of the reports identifies the MP, and the footage has not been made public in the accounts provided.

Wetang’ula said he would appoint another member to lead the committee’s review and bring a report to the House. The review is meant to establish what the footage shows; whether it can identify the person remains open. The reports give no date for the committee’s work or its report.

Mumias East MP Peter Salasya objected to the way the matter had become public, not simply to the allegation itself. Tuko and People Daily quote him warning that disclosures about internal disputes risk damaging Parliament’s reputation and turning embarrassing incidents into public entertainment. Salasya said the incident could be genuine while questioning whether a public disclosure was the right way to handle it.

The coverage also carries different details about the 2023 disorder. Tuko says seven MPs were suspended and Chege remained in her position after a court order. Capital FM recounts Chege’s account that she was caught up in a row involving Suba South MP Millie Odhiambo and Buyu. Tuko’s account of Wednesday’s exchange instead identifies Odhiambo as the MP who dismissed Chege’s concerns, and describes the original incident as involving Suba North MP Millie Odhiambo. The reports therefore differ on Odhiambo’s constituency; they do not explain the discrepancy.

The Star and People Daily add a separate episode that prompted Wetang’ula’s original remarks. Embakasi West MP Mark Mwenje asked him to review CCTV after social media claims that Mwenje had played video games during a sitting. Wetang’ula said he believed Mwenje was not responsible. The Star gives more detail about Mwenje’s concern that the allegation had reached his family and children. The reports do not establish who was playing the game.

What is now clear is that Chege has publicly claimed the missing phone and asked for an answer, while the Speaker has put the footage on a committee’s agenda. The MP’s identity, the phone’s whereabouts and the committee’s findings are still unresolved.

The Powers and Privileges Committee is to review the CCTV footage and report to the House.

The review may clarify what the footage shows and whether the person who picked up the phone can be identified.
