Kalonzo takes his 2027 campaign to the pews and the people of Mwala

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Wiper leader&#8217;s Machakos County tour blends faith, constitutional messaging, and grassroots mobilisation as the race for State House moves into its most consequential phase.

Wiper Party leader Kalonzo Musyoka carried his 2027 presidential campaign into Machakos County at the weekend, addressing residents of Mwala Constituency and attending Sunday worship at AIC Mbaikini in Wamunyu Ward in a visit that combined grassroots political outreach with a carefully framed constitutional message.

The former Vice-President received a warm reception in Wamunyu, where he addressed residents and laid out the ideological foundation on which he intends to contest the August 2027 General Election. His message was pointed and deliberate. &#8220;The people of Mwala have made a commitment to stand for a better Kenya — a nation founded on constitutionalism, the rule of law, justice and respect for the rights and freedoms of every citizen,&#8221; Kalonzo told the gathering. &#8220;We want a nation where the constitution is supreme, where institutions serve the people, where justice is accessible to all, and where no Kenyan is above the law.&#8221;

The declaration — &#8220;It is liberation time&#8221; — that closed his public address served as both a campaign slogan and a signal of intent. For Kalonzo, who has contested the presidency three times and watched victory slip away on each occasion, the framing of 2027 as a moment of constitutional reckoning rather than a simple electoral contest reflects a strategic repositioning. He is not merely running against a candidate; he is running against what his campaign characterises as institutional decay and constitutional subversion.

The Sunday church service added a different but complementary dimension to the visit. Joining worshippers at AIC Mbaikini, Kalonzo used the occasion to anchor his political mission in faith and communal responsibility. &#8220;Our faith reminds us that, in every season, we must seek God&#8217;s wisdom and remain steadfast in our duty to one another,&#8221; he said. &#8220;Let us continue to pray for our families, our communities and our country, asking God to guide our leaders and grant Kenya peace, unity and a just future.&#8221; He drew on Psalm 33:12 — &#8220;Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord, the people he chose for his inheritance&#8221; — to underscore his message.

The visit was not a solo endeavour. Kalonzo was accompanied by a delegation that underlined the breadth of support he is assembling in the region. Democratic Party leader Justin Muturi, Wiper Party Foundation National Chairman Jamleck Kamau, Machakos Deputy Governor Francis Mwangangi, Machakos Women Representative Joyce Kamene, Machakos Senator Agnes Kavindu, Members of Parliament Julius Mawathe and Makali Mulu, and Wiper Party Foundation spokesperson Ndegwa Njiru were all present — a show of force that the Wiper leader&#8217;s campaign will hope translates into organised electoral infrastructure as the race intensifies.

The political significance of Machakos County in the 2027 contest cannot be overstated. The Ukambani region — comprising Machakos, Kitui, and Makueni counties — delivered a substantial bloc of votes for Kalonzo in previous elections, and consolidating that base while reaching beyond it remains central to any realistic path to State House. A weekend that moves between a public rally and a church service in a single constituency reflects the rhythm of a campaign that understands the terrain: in Ukambani, as in much of rural Kenya, political persuasion happens as much in places of worship and community gatherings as it does on the rally ground.

The timing of the Mwala visit is also instructive. Kenya&#8217;s political calendar is accelerating. With the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission under sustained opposition pressure over electoral preparedness, voter registration timelines, and results transmission architecture, the window for meaningful pre-campaign positioning is narrowing. Opposition leaders who fail to build visible, organised grassroots structures in this period risk arriving at the campaign season with name recognition but without the organisational machinery that converts votes into a result.

Kalonzo&#8217;s constitutional framing — supremacy of law, independent institutions, justice for all — is calibrated to appeal beyond his traditional Ukambani base to the reform-minded urban and middle-class voters who swung behind the Gen Z protest movement in 2024 and have remained politically restless since. Whether that appeal translates into a broadened coalition or remains a regional consolidation exercise will be one of the defining questions of the 2027 opposition contest.

The Wiper leader goes into 2027 as a seasoned, battle-tested political figure who understands both the possibilities and the cruelties of Kenyan presidential politics. He has been this close before — close enough to know how little separates ambition from arrival. This time, he is making the case not simply that he deserves the presidency, but that Kenya&#8217;s democratic institutions demand the kind of leadership he represents.

Whether Kenyans agree will be settled in August. For now, Kalonzo is in the constituency, in the church, and in the conversation — and that, in the long march to State House, is exactly where a candidate needs to be.
