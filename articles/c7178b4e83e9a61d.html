Hollywood actor Jonathan Majors got candid about the state of his life when he first met actress Meagan Good, admitting he was struggling financially and felt he had very little to offer her.

Majors made the admission during a conversation with media personality Chude Jideonwo at the 15th Africa International Film Festival (AFRIFF) in Lagos, Nigeria , in early November.

The discussion touched on his relationship with Good and the turbulent period that defined it.

The actor did not hold back as he described the difficult circumstances surrounding their early relationship.

His finances were strained, and the kind of attention his name was attracting at the time was far from flattering.

He went on to admit that despite feeling he had nothing to give, Good chose to stay.

The remarks pointed to a painful chapter following his 2023 arrest and misdemeanour assault and harassment conviction involving a former partner.

The fallout cost him significantly in Hollywood, most notably his role as Kang the Conqueror in the Marvel Cinematic Universe after the studio cut ties with him.

Good stood by him throughout that period, attending court proceedings with members of her family. The two began dating in 2023 and tied the knot in March 2025.

The Lagos visit was not purely personal. Majors also took part in the second edition of the AFRIFF Film and Content Market (AFCM), a platform where filmmakers, investors and entertainment industry leaders came together to discuss financing African productions and expanding their global reach.

Representatives from MBO Capital, Logical Pictures Group, Filmhouse Group and several other organisations involved in production and distribution were among those present for the roundtable discussions.

Majors described the experience as transformative, saying it reconnected him with the reason he pursued acting in the first place.

He also expressed a clear desire to be part of building that future.

Other industry leaders also highlighted challenges facing African cinema, particularly funding, infrastructure and technical expertise.

Adekunle Adebiyi, Executive Director of Investments at MBO Capital, argued that African filmmakers needed more locally sourced financing to maintain control over their stories.

He warned that relying heavily on foreign funding could influence the narratives eventually told on screen.

Frédéric Fiore, CEO of Logical Pictures Group, stressed that African film productions needed to generate commercial returns to attract investors.

He also highlighted gaps in post-production facilities, citing a Senegalese production that had to complete its post-production work in France.

Meanwhile, Joël Chikapa-Phiri of Known Associates Group called for greater investment in technical skills, questioning whether the continent had enough trained colour graders and visual effects professionals to support its growing film industry.

AFRIFF founder Chioma Ude also emphasised the need for strong African institutions, investment and infrastructure to help locally produced stories compete internationally.

The investment discussions included MBO Capital, Logical Pictures Group, Filmhouse Group and other financial and entertainment organisations.

Participating creative businesses were seeking between $250,000 and $5 million in funding.

Although Majors did not announce a specific African film project or financial investment, his participation highlighted his interest in connecting with filmmakers on the continent and supporting African storytelling.
