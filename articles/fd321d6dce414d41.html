UK Launches KSh 6.8 Billion Platform to Help Kenyan Businesses Find Investors, Fund Jobs

The UK government has unveiled a new pan-African investment platform called Forge Africa, headquartered in Kenya, designed to connect promising businesses with long-term investors and support job creation across the continent.

UK Minister for Africa Kirsty McNeill made the announcement on November 19, 2026, during a visit to Octavia Carbon&#039;s Project Hummingbird in Gilgil.

This is a facility where Kenyan engineers use geothermal energy from the Rift Valley to capture carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and store it permanently underground. Early UK support helped Octavia Carbon raise around USD 10 million and create more than 100 skilled jobs .

Backed by £40 million (KSh6.81 billion) in UK investment, Forge Africa will initially support 100 businesses in attracting investors willing to commit for the long term.

The platform will launch in Kenya before expanding to other African markets.

Separately, in Nairobi, the minister launched the next phase of the Kenya Catalytic Jobs Fund, which links researchers, entrepreneurs, investors and government institutions to help Kenyan ideas mature into viable businesses.

The new phase will focus on technology, artificial intelligence, biosciences and women-led enterprises. By March 2029, it aims to mobilise £50 million (KSh8.51 billion) in financing for more than 500 start-ups.

British High Commissioner to Kenya Matt Baugh said the two platforms would help Kenyan businesses move "from the lab to the market, and from start-up to scale-up."

During her Rift Valley visit, McNeill also toured the UK-backed Globeleq geothermal plant at Menengai, where underground steam supplies clean power and skilled employment.

Kenya is Africa&#039;s largest producer of geothermal energy, with more than 90% of its electricity generated from renewable sources.

UK-Kenya trade is currently valued at £2.1 billion (KSh357.42 billion) annually, and more than 150 British-linked companies support over 250,000 jobs across Kenya.

The visit falls under the framework of the UK-Kenya Strategic Partnership covering 2025 to 2030, signed by former UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer and President William Ruto in July 2025.

Successful applicants will receive full tuition fees, return airfare, a monthly living allowance, a warm clothing allowance and coverage of the Immigration Health Surcharge.

Eligible candidates must be Kenyan citizens or permanent residents, hold at least an upper second-class honours degree or an equivalent qualification, demonstrate financial need and meet other residency requirements, with no age limit.

Applications must be submitted through the CSC Central portal by December 8, 2026, and successful scholars will be required to return to Kenya within one month of completing their studies.
