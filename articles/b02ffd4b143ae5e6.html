Karauri Fires Back at Gachagua Over Claims He Was Brought to Split Mt Kenya Votes in Nairobi

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kasarani Member of Parliament Ronald Karauri has hit back at former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua after the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) leader accused him of entering the Nairobi gubernatorial race to divide Mt Kenya votes in the capital.

In a statement on Monday, November 16, Karauri dismissed the allegations, maintaining that Nairobi residents were capable of independently choosing their preferred leaders ahead of the 2027 General Election.

The MP challenged Gachagua to stop blaming other political players whenever his political calculations failed to work out, insisting that Nairobi voters were not easily swayed by political influence.

"A bad dancer always blames the music, and when the music changes, they blame the dance floor. Dear Riggy G, why are you nervous? Don't always look for someone to blame whenever your political calculations don't add up," Karauri stated.

He further argued that Nairobi residents understood the challenges facing the city and would make independent decisions on who should lead them.

"Nairobi voters are not sheep waiting to be herded by whoever shouts the loudest. They are intelligent people who understand their city, their problems and the kind of leadership they deserve.

"They will decide who represents them, and as far as I am concerned, Nairobi has already found its Captain," he added.

Karauri's response followed remarks by Gachagua, who claimed that President William Ruto had facilitated the Kasarani MP's entry into the Nairobi gubernatorial contest to split support among Mt Kenya voters in the capital.

Speaking during a political engagement, Gachagua questioned whether residents from the Mt Kenya region would allow what he described as a political strategy to divide their votes.

"President Ruto has looked for Karauri and brought him to Nairobi so that he can divide the Mt Kenya votes; will you allow it?" Gachagua posed.

The exchange comes days after Karauri announced his decision to join the ruling United Democratic Alliance (UDA), where he was received by National Assembly Majority Leader Kimani Ichung'wah and party Secretary General Hassan Omar.

In announcing his move, the legislator said he had settled on UDA after considering different political options as he prepares to contest the Nairobi gubernatorial seat in 2027.

"Today, I take a new political step by joining the United Democratic Alliance (UDA) as I pursue my ambition to serve Nairobi as Governor in 2027. I looked at all the options and chose where I believe I can deliver for Nairobi," Karauri said.

The MP also expressed confidence in his ability to bring together Nairobi's diverse communities around a common development agenda.

His entry into the Nairobi gubernatorial race adds another dimension to the political contest for the county's top seat, with questions of party support, community voting patterns and competing political interests increasingly featuring in the campaigns ahead of the 2027 elections.
