Edwin Sifuna Breaks Silence on Babu Owino Fallout, Defends How He Became Linda Mwananchi Party Leader

WhatsAppFacebookX

Edwin Sifuna explains how he became Linda Mwananchi party leader, challenging Babu Owino's claims that an earlier leadership agreement was changed.

Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna has broken his silence on the growing leadership tensions within the Linda Mwananchi movement, revealing how he emerged as party leader and presidential candidate despite an earlier political arrangement that allegedly favoured Embakasi East MP Babu Owino.

In an interview with Citizen Radio, Sifuna rejected accusations that he had betrayed his political allies, insisting that the decision to appoint him was reached collectively after months of negotiations involving leaders seeking a common political vehicle.

His explanation comes after Babu publicly claimed that the original agreement was for Sifuna to become the movement's presidential candidate while he assumed the position of party leader.

The Embakasi East MP alleged that the arrangement subsequently changed without his knowledge, with some members insisting that whoever sought the presidency should also control the party.

However, Sifuna has now offered a different account, shifting attention to the negotiations that preceded the formation of their political outfit and the disagreements that emerged over party ownership and leadership.

The dispute threatens to expose deeper divisions within a movement that has increasingly positioned itself as an alternative political force following disagreements with the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM).

According to Sifuna , the disagreements started after the leaders were pushed out of ODM and began searching for a political vehicle through which they could pursue their ambitions.

He said the initial understanding was that members would abandon their individual political parties and consolidate their interests under one organisation.

The Nairobi Senator described the arrangement as an attempt to establish a common political home capable of accommodating all the leaders.

He disclosed that five politicians presented their existing political parties for consideration. They included Babu Owino, Caroli Omondi, Godfrey Osotsi, George Natembeya and Caleb Amisi.

The group subsequently established a subcommittee comprising the owners of the respective political parties.

Its responsibility was to submit party registration documents to lawyers for an assessment of their structures, ownership, registration status and legal compliance.

After evaluating the available options, Sifuna said the leaders agreed to settle on the Equitable Party as their common political vehicle.

He cited Caroli Omondi as one of the politicians who abandoned his own party to join the arrangement.

Former Meru Governor Kawira Mwangaza and Mavoko MP Patrick Makau also joined the political arrangement.

The Senator's account suggests that the initial negotiations were not simply about deciding who would occupy senior positions but also about identifying a legally suitable organisation that could accommodate the movement's political ambitions.

The dispute gained momentum after Babu claimed that he had initially agreed to allow Sifuna to become the movement's presidential candidate while he took charge of the party.

According to the Embakasi East MP, that understanding was later altered when some members argued that the presidential candidate should automatically become party leader.

The change reportedly caught him by surprise, raising questions about whether the movement's senior politicians had honoured their original political agreements.

However, Sifuna challenged the suggestion that he had manipulated the negotiations or deliberately sidelined Babu.

He maintained that the leadership structure was agreed upon collectively after the group settled on the Equitable Party.

Addressing the controversy, Sifuna defended his appointment as both party leader and presidential candidate, questioning the logic of trusting him with the country's highest office while denying him control of the political outfit.

“Is it possible you trust me to be president of the country but you cannot trust me to be party leader of a party?” he posed.

The Senator insisted that his colleagues had participated in the discussions and that the decision was not an individual undertaking.

He also rejected suggestions that the arrangement was designed to diminish Babu's political influence within the movement.

However, Sifuna acknowledged that some leaders later reconsidered their positions after recognising the political advantages of retaining control of their individual parties.

He said the group accepted their decision and allowed them to continue leading their respective political outfits.

“So we agreed. If you must be party leader so people enter your party, it's okay. You can be party leader,” he explained.

The remarks expose a fundamental disagreement within Linda Mwananchi over whether political unity should take precedence over individual control of party structures.

For politicians preparing for the 2027 General Election, party ownership carries significant strategic importance, particularly when negotiating nominations, controlling grassroots networks and determining who occupies influential positions.

Despite the public disagreements, Sifuna dismissed reports suggesting that his relationship with Babu, Siaya Governor James Orengo, Godfrey Osotsi and other members of the movement had collapsed.

He maintained that none of the leaders had personally confronted him over allegations of betrayal.

“When you talk about rumours, especially these rumours I see on social media, Babu himself or Orengo or Caleb hasn't said that Sifuna has wronged him anywhere,” he said.

The Senator challenged commentators to distinguish between disagreements over political arrangements and a complete breakdown in personal relationships.

He also urged his colleagues to concentrate on building the political movement rather than allowing leadership disagreements to dominate its public image.

“The hope that Kenyans have placed in Linda Mwananchi does not allow me to have side shows,” he said.

Sifuna further disclosed that the group had conducted more than 100 meetings in an effort to persuade its members to consolidate their political interests under one party.

His remarks suggest that the negotiations have been considerably more complicated than the public disagreements indicate.

However, the competing accounts from Sifuna and Babu leave unanswered questions about the precise terms of their original agreement and how the final leadership arrangement was reached.

With the 2027 elections approaching, the movement faces a difficult test of whether its leading politicians can reconcile their individual ambitions and establish a unified political structure.

The bigger question is whether Linda Mwananchi can survive the struggle for party control long enough to become a credible national political force.
