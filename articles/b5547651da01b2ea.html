"I'll make sure illness is not a sentence to poverty" - President Ruto highlights Kenya's state of health care

WhatsAppFacebookX

President William Ruto took time to explain the state of healthcare in the country during his fourth State of the Nation Address in Parliament on Wednesday, November 18.

In his address, the Head of State noted that the Kenya Kwanza Administration has come-up with healthcare reforms through a system of shared health financing, rather than falling entirely on individuals and their family, to address the cost involved in medical treatment.

The President stated that so far, 33.7 million Kenyans had registered with the Social Health Authority (SHA), comparing this to the National Hospital Insurance Fund (NHIF) in 2022, where approximately 8 million Kenyans were beneficiaries.

&#8220;Under the former NHIF, only 8 million Kenyans were registered. Today, under SHA, that number stands at 33.7 million. That is 25.7 million more Kenyans brought into the national health financing system. Millions who were previously outside its protection, for whom illness could mean selling their land, exhausting their savings or sinking into debt,&#8221; President Ruto stated.

He further stated that across the country, 53, 465 patients are currently receiving cancer treatment, 24,468 patients are receiving dialysis, while 89 patients have undergone kidney transplant under SHA.

At the same time, he acknowledged that SHA has been facing challenges that the government is working hard to address.

&#8220;The transition to SHA had initial challenges and we recognised the legitimate concerns of Kenyans. We have consistently addressed those concerns because health protection must be experienced by citizens in practice as it&#8217;s promised in policy,&#8221; the President said.

Ideally, the President emphasised that his administration is working to progressively and eventually remove the crushing burden of medical expenses from Kenyan households, protect family savings and ensure that illness does not become a sentence to poverty.

The President went ahead and gave a testimony of a lady known as Veronica who is a beneficiary of SHA. He mentioned that by her contributing Ksh840, the government was able to cover her medical bill that exceeded KSh966,00.

The Head of State, reiterated that the insurance fund has enabled patients to gain access to health care without over burdening their families.
