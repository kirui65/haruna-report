Canada has opened its doors to skilled workers from Kenya and other countries, with the country&#039;s immigration authority identifying seven occupational categories where it urgently needs talent to plug gaps in its labour market.

The Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) made the announcement on Monday, November 16, through its official account on X, calling on experienced professionals abroad to consider joining the Canadian workforce.

The seven categories listed under Canada&#039;s Express Entry system cover a broad range of professions.

Medical doctors, researchers, and senior managers who already have Canadian work experience are among those being targeted, alongside health care and social services professionals.

The IRCC also indicated it is actively seeking tradespeople and educators, as well as professionals working in science , technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) fields.

Transport sector professionals form another priority group, while workers with French-language proficiency round out the seven categories, reflecting Canada&#039;s sustained effort to maintain and grow its bilingual character.

Express Entry is Canada&#039;s flagship points-based immigration system, designed to fast-track the selection of skilled foreign nationals for permanent residency. Candidates are assessed on factors including age, education, work experience, and language skills, with higher-scoring profiles receiving invitations to apply for permanent residence.

The latest category-based selection rounds signal that Canada is not only looking to manage general immigration volumes but is specifically targeting professionals whose skills align with current economic needs.

For Kenyan professionals in health care, engineering, education, or the trades, the announcement represents a direct recruitment signal from one of the world&#039;s most sought-after immigration destinations.

In other news, Canada’s Rural Community Immigration Programme offers skilled workers and international graduates a pathway to permanent residency through employment in rural communities.

The programme was made permanent, giving participating communities a long-term way to attract foreign workers and address labour shortages.

It also creates opportunities for eligible newcomers seeking to settle and work in smaller Canadian communities.
