Hussein Dado Leads Early Tana River Governor Poll

WhatsAppFacebookX

A Mizani Africa phone poll puts former governor Hussein Dado at 53% for Tana River&#x27;s open 2027 seat, though 22.6% of surveyed voters remain undecided.

Former Tana River County Governor Hussein Dado holds a wide early advantage in the contest for the county's top seat ahead of Kenya's 2027 General Election, according to a Mizani Africa survey released on November 25, 2026.

The poll placed Dado at 53 percent support among respondents asked whom they would back if the election were held today, more than four times the share recorded by second-placed Said Hiribae at 12.9 percent. Still, 22.6 percent of respondents said they remained undecided, leaving a substantial swing bloc unaligned more than 20 months before polling day.

Behind Dado and Hiribae, the remaining field polled in single digits in Mizani Africa's snapshot. Nuh Nassir drew 7.3 percent support, followed by Hero Said at 2.3 percent and Hassan Morowa at 1.9 percent, pointing to heavy early consolidation behind the former county chief alongside deep voter hesitation.

Kenya's county governors are elected by simple plurality under Article 180 of the Constitution, running alongside a nominated deputy governor rather than facing a 50-percent runoff threshold. In Mizani Africa's snapshot, the four trailing contenders combined drew 24.4 percent, meaning Dado's 53 percent score exceeds all named rivals and the 22.6 percent undecided group individually, though coalition realignments and undecided turnout still leave the contest fluid.

Under Article 180(7) of the Constitution of Kenya, county governors are barred from holding office for more than two consecutive terms, turning the 2027 contest into an open succession race whenever a two-term incumbent steps aside. In an open field, early name recognition often favours a former governor such as Dado before party nominations and coalition alignments settle the final ballot.

Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) 2022 county data underline how concentrated Tana River's electorate is compared with larger urban counties. Of Kenya's 22,102,532 registered county voters in 2022, Tana River accounted for 141,096 voters across three constituencies: Garsen with 55,453 (39.3 percent of the county total), Bura with 45,678 (32.4 percent), and Galole with 39,965 (28.3 percent).

Within the six Coast counties, IEBC 2022 records show Tana River holds the second-smallest electorate, trailing Mombasa (641,913), Kilifi (588,602), Kwale (328,253), and Taita Taveta (181,827), and standing ahead of only Lamu (81,453). Because total county registration sits near 141,000 voters, modest shifts in turnout or local endorsements across Garsen, Bura, and Galole can swing final margins faster than in populous coastal cities.

Those constituency weights mean a candidate who wins decisive backing in Garsen and Bura can build a commanding county-wide lead even before Galole breaks. Conversely, with 22.6 percent of polled voters undecided, a coordinated shift across any single constituency or late coalition endorsement could alter the gap markedly once formal campaigns begin.

Mizani Africa conducted fieldwork between November 2 and November 12, 2026, using mobile telephone interviews carried out daily between 9am and 5:30pm. Out of 1,800 targeted registered voters aged 18 and above drawn from the 2022 IEBC register, 1,507 completed interviews, giving an 83.7 percent response rate and a reported margin of error of plus or minus 2.5 percentage points at a 95 percent confidence level.

Early polls measure current name recognition rather than guaranteed Election Day turnout, especially before party primaries test rival coalitions and the Election Campaign Financing Act, 2013, shapes disclosure requirements for county contenders. The next meaningful tests of Tana River's race will be updated voter-register figures from the IEBC, formal candidate clearances, and independent surveys capturing post-2022 voters across all three constituencies.
