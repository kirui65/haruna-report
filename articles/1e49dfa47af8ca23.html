Kenya’s female-led venture capital firm closes $26m fund to support African fintech startups

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya-based, pan-African female-led venture capital firm First Circle Capital has closed its Africa Fund I at $26 million, securing capital to invest in early-stage fintech startups developing the next generation of financial products, services and technology infrastructure across the continent.

The fund will back startups at the pre-seed and seed stages, focusing on six key areas: insurance technology (insurtech), alternative lending models, financial interoperability and infrastructure, climate fintech, fintech software-as-a-service (SaaS), and regulatory technology (regtech).

First Circle Capital, which specialises in African fintech and has offices in Nairobi, Kampala, Casablanca and Cape Town, was founded by Selma Ribica, a former M-Pesa executive and fintech investor; Agnes Aistleitner, a serial entrepreneur with experience in emerging markets; and Robert Smith, a former strategy professional with a background in technology entrepreneurship in East Africa.

The firm&#8217;s investment strategy, which it describes as &#8220;FinTech 2.0&#8221;, seeks to build on the first generation of African fintech companies that transformed payments and lending by backing businesses addressing broader gaps in financial services.

These include expanding access to credit and insurance, strengthening financial infrastructure, improving digital business operations and enhancing regulatory compliance.

The firm is also placing greater emphasis on AI-native fintech startups, which integrate artificial intelligence, automation and data-driven operations into their businesses from inception. According to First Circle Capital, these technologies could reduce the cost of delivering financial services, improve product efficiency and enable companies to serve more customers.

&#8220;We are particularly excited by the emergence of AI-native fintech companies. Founders now have the opportunity to build financial businesses around automation, intelligence, and software from day one, fundamentally changing the economics of delivering financial services across African markets,&#8221; said Selma Ribica, Co-Founder and Managing Partner at First Circle Capital.

&#8220;We want to identify those businesses at the earliest stages and work closely with their founders as they scale.&#8221;

Africa Fund I has a pan-African investment mandate, targeting fintech opportunities in established and emerging markets, including Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa, Egypt, Morocco, Uganda, Ghana and Francophone Africa.

Investors in the fund include the International Finance Corporation (IFC), Bpifrance, MSMEDA, Axian, the Dutch Good Growth Fund, Stellar Foundation and FSD Africa. The fund brings together development finance institutions, institutional investors and strategic partners to support the firm&#8217;s investment strategy.

First Circle Capital has already invested in 18 companies across African markets, building a portfolio spanning payments, financial wellness, alternative lending, insurtech, climate finance, fintech SaaS, commerce enablement, card infrastructure and fraud prevention.

Some of its portfolio companies include South Africa-based TurnStay, which develops cross-border payment infrastructure for global travel merchants; Morocco&#8217;s WafR, which digitises transactions and payments through neighbourhood retailers; Scale, a Card-as-a-Service infrastructure provider; and Orca Fraud, which uses artificial intelligence to help financial institutions prevent fraud.

Other investments include Opareta, which provides software and data solutions for managing mobile money agent networks; Balad, an Egyptian regulated remittance infrastructure provider facilitating international money transfers; and Power, which offers earned wage access solutions to major African banks.

Beyond providing early-stage capital, First Circle Capital says it works closely with portfolio companies to help them secure follow-on funding, attract strategic investors, establish commercial partnerships and pursue business development opportunities. Several of its portfolio companies are now progressing towards Series A funding rounds.

Agnes Aistleitner, Co-Founder and Partner at First Circle Capital, said significant gaps remain in Africa&#8217;s financial services industry despite the growth of fintech businesses over the past decade.

&#8220;Despite the extraordinary growth of fintech across Africa over the past decade, significant gaps remain in key verticals such as financial access, interoperability, digital infrastructure, and the availability of specialised financial products,&#8221; she said.

Aistleitner added that the fund would support founders developing solutions to make payments cheaper, expand access to credit and insurance, strengthen fraud prevention and risk management, and improve financial operations through software.

She also pointed to opportunities in sectors such as agriculture and energy, where specialised financial solutions could address longstanding financing challenges.

Robert Smith, Partner and Head of Operations at First Circle Capital, said the firm&#8217;s ambition is to back companies that become central to the delivery of financial products across African markets.

&#8220;Our ambition with Africa Fund I is to build a portfolio of companies that become fundamental to how financial products are delivered across Africa,&#8221; Smith said.

He added that the next generation of African fintech businesses would combine local market knowledge with advanced technologies, including AI-native solutions, to improve the accessibility and economics of financial services.

With its $26 million fund now closed, First Circle Capital is positioning itself to back early-stage founders seeking to address structural challenges in Africa&#8217;s financial services sector, while supporting the development of technology-driven businesses across the continent.
