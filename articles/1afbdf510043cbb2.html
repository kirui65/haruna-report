World Toilet Day: Sanitation must become an affordable investment for every household

WhatsAppFacebookX

Access to a safe toilet is fundamental to public health, dignity and well-being. While Kenya has made progress in expanding access to improved sanitation, disparities in household circumstances, infrastructure and affordability mean that more needs to be done to extend these gains to every community.

The 2025–2026 Kenya Mini Demographic and Health Survey shows that 76 per cent of households have access to improved sanitation facilities. However, the proportion stands at 63 per cent in rural areas, compared with 93 per cent in urban areas. The survey also indicates that 45 per cent of the population has access to at least basic sanitation services, while 7.4 per cent still practise open defecation.

These findings underscore the importance of sustaining investments in sanitation while addressing the different needs of households. Access to an improved toilet is an important step, but safely managed sanitation also requires hygienic and durable facilities supported by appropriate waste containment and management systems.

The Ministry of Health&#8217;s Costed National Sanitation and Hygiene Roadmap 2023–2030 provides an indication of the investment required. It estimates that a basic G1 sanitation unit, designed to provide a clean and flyproof toilet, costs between KES 10,000 and KES 12,000 in hardware, depending on county conditions. A more durable individual G2 toilet with safe waste containment costs between KES 25,000 and KES 30,000.
