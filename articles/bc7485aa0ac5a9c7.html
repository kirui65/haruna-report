Bayern vs Ajax match set to unlock business opportunities for Kenyan SMEs

WhatsAppFacebookX

Visitors who fly in for a match rarely fly straight out. Lungaho has pointed to Kenya's attractions, from the Mara to the beaches

A full stadium creates demand far beyond its gates. Tony Lungaho has named the sectors that stand to gain from Talanta&#8217;s opening day. Here is what 5 January could mean for each.

When FC Bayern Munich and AFC Ajax open the Raila Odinga International Stadium at Talanta Sports City on 5 January, the turnstiles will not be the only place money changes hands. Tony Lungaho, CEO of the AFCON PAMOJA 2027 Local Organising Committee, highlights that hotels, transport, restaurants, retail, tourism operators and local suppliers, as well as media, technology and creative businesses.

Not every fan is local. Almost 10 per cent of tickets sold so far went to buyers outside Kenya, Lungaho says, from as far as Kazakhstan and Colombia. The two clubs have also asked for 5,000 tickets for their travelling supporters. Every one of those visitors needs a bed.

With gates opening at 11:00 and an evening kick-off, 5 January is a full day out. Expect fans to meet for a meal on the way in and to keep celebrating, or commiserating, long after the final whistle.

Getting a stadium crowd to Ngong Road and home again is a business in itself. Routes and road closures will come from the responsible agencies, so operators should watch for their notices.

A food festival is part of the programme. With no re-entry for this match, fans inside will be eating and drinking there for the day. Details of the vendors will be announced through official channels.

Sound and staging crews, lighting technicians, security and cleaning firms, stewards and ticket scanners: a stadium opening with a concert attached needs all of them.

Lungaho has described an A-list concert as part of the day, and he hints at a line-up of around 15 local and international artists. He sees Talanta as a home for concerts and live entertainment, part of Kenya&#8217;s creative economy. Behind every act is a team: DJs, dancers, stylists, photographers, videographers and content creators.

Bayern and Ajax have almost half a billion followers between them, Lungaho says, and both have loyal supporters&#8217; groups in Kenya. Expect demand for jerseys, scarves and anything in team colours.

Visitors who fly in for a match rarely fly straight out. Lungaho has pointed to Kenya&#8217;s attractions, from the Mara to the beaches, as part of the pitch for sports tourism, and the LOC is targeting at least 500,000 international visitors between January and July 2027. His summary of the ambition: &#8220;If we make it possible, the world will come to Africa.&#8221;

One match will not transform Nairobi&#8217;s economy on its own. But as a first test of whether a Kenyan stadium can pull in visitors, spending and repeat events, 5 January matters, and AFCON PAMOJA 2027 follows from 19 June to 17 July.
