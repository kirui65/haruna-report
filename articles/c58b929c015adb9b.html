Kenyans Could Soon Convert Petrol Cars Into Electric Vehicles Without Buying New Ones

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Ministry of Transport is exploring new electric vehicle retrofitting technology that will allow Kenyan motorists to convert their petrol and diesel cars into electric vehicles without buying new ones.

This proposal emerged on Wednesday, November 18, following high-level discussions between Transport Principal Secretary Joseph Mbugua and Euro Retrofits Mobility President Emmanuel Flahaut. During the meeting, Flahaut demonstrated how electric vehicle retrofitting works and outlined strategies for introducing the green mobility solution to the Kenyan market.

The conversion process removes a vehicle&#8217;s traditional internal combustion engine and replaces it with an electric motor and battery pack. By upgrading existing cars to run on electricity, Kenyan drivers can bypass the prohibitive cost of spending millions of shillings on brand-new electric vehicles.

Flahaut informed ministry officials that the retrofitting technology adapts easily across multiple transport categories, including motorcycles, private cars, buses, delivery vans, trucks, and other commercial fleets. Depending on the vehicle class and the complexity of the engineering required, technicians can complete a full conversion in anywhere from a single day to three weeks.

Ministry officials noted that adopting EV retrofitting will extend the lifespan of existing vehicles while shielding motorists from volatile petrol and diesel prices.

Principal Secretary Mbugua added that transitioning older vehicles to electric power will significantly curb air pollution, particularly in congested urban centers where thousands of fuel-powered vehicles emit daily exhaust fumes. These talks align with Kenya&#8217;s broader national push to accelerate electric mobility adoption and transition away from fossil fuel transport.
