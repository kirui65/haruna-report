Uhuru's former top bodyguard lands new job in fresh police changes

Senior Assistant Inspector-General Pepita Ranka.

A senior police officer who headed the Presidential Escort under former President Uhuru Kenyatta has been given a top posting at police headquarters, even as changes are made to an elite Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) unit that has been under public scrutiny.

Senior Assistant Inspector-General Pepita Ranka, who led the Presidential Escort during Mr Kenyatta's presidency, has been promoted from regional police commander to Director of Logistics at Vigilance House, the police headquarters.

He takes over from Peter Ndung'u, who retired from the police on November 13, 2026.

Deputy Inspector-General Eliud Lagat, who spoke at a farewell party for Mr Ndung'u at the Police Grounds in South C, praised him for his leadership, professionalism and contribution to strengthening logistical support for policing operations.

Director of Criminal Investigations (DCI) Mohammed Amin.

At the DCI, director Mohammed Amin has made changes to the Operations Support Unit (OSU).

He has appointed David Chebii as the unit's deputy head. Mr Chebii has been the Tana River County DCI boss and previously headed the Parklands-based Anti-Counterfeit unit, which was disbanded when President William Ruto took office.

Mr Amin has also directed that all of the unit's operations move to DCI headquarters on Kiambu Road. The unit is currently based at the Old Nairobi area offices, which also serve as the Nairobi County Traffic Headquarters. The move appears aimed at rebranding and reorganising the unit.

The current head of the OSU, Zachary Kariuki, is serving on contract after reaching the mandatory retirement age of 60.

Mr Amin created the OSU after President Ruto ordered the disbandment of a former police unit, the Special Crimes Prevention Unit (SCPU), following accusations that its members were involved in criminal activities.

On September 13, 2022, soon after taking office, Dr Ruto promised that his administration would not entertain secret police units such as the DCI Special Service Unit. He said the police would operate independently and would not be used to harass people with divergent views.

The SCPU was linked to the disappearance and killing of two Indian nationals who were part of Mr Ruto's presidential campaign team, and of a taxi driver who was driving them when they were abducted in Nairobi.

Mohammed Zaid Sami Kidwai, Zulfiqra Ahmed Khan and Nichodemus Mwania Mwange went missing on July 23, 2022. The two Indians were part of a five-man team that arrived in the country as tourists on April 16 and visited several places in the Rift Valley and Nairobi.

Mohamed Zaid Sami and Zulfiqar Ahmed Khan who were killed between July 22 and 23, 2022.

Police investigations led by Mr Amin, who was then head of the Police Internal Affairs Unit, and CCTV footage placed at least 28 DCI officers from the Special Service Unit at the centre of the abduction and disappearance of the three men.

"I have directed that immediate measures be undertaken to disband the Special Services Unit in the DCI and with urgency investigations must commence so that those found culpable are held accountable," President Ruto said in his Mashujaa Day speech.

After the SCPU was disbanded, Mr Amin created the OSU. The unit's focus came under sharp scrutiny at the height of the 2024 anti-Finance Bill protests.

It has been scrutinised further after some of its members were accused of abductions, including the attack on Standard Group editor Alex Kiprotich.

On July 9, former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua claimed at a press conference in Nairobi that he knew of a secret police unit attached to the National Intelligence Service (NIS) that was responsible for the abduction and killing of peaceful protesters. He claimed that the covert unit was assembled while he was still in government and was highly trained for covert operations.

The government has denied that any police squad is involved in extrajudicial killings. Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen has said that all police operations are conducted in accordance with the law.

"In the event any police officer is accused of unlawful use of force, they will face the law," he said.
