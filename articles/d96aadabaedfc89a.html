KENYAN EX-GOVERNOR OBADO SENTENCED TO 25 YEARS FOR PREGNANT STUDENT’S MURDER

WhatsAppFacebookX

The High Court also handed prison terms to two former aides of the governor

A former governor of Migori County in Kenya, Zacharia Okoth Obado, has been sentenced to 25 years’ imprisonment for his role in the murder of 26-year-old university student Sharon Belyne Otieno, who was seven months pregnant with his child.

The High Court also handed prison terms to two former aides of the governor over the September 2018 killing. Michael Oyamo, Obado’s former personal assistant, was sentenced to 25 years, while former county clerk Caspal Ojwang’ Obiero received an 18-year jail term.

According to the court’s findings, Obado provided the motive and financial resources for the murder, Oyamo coordinated the operation, while Obiero assisted with logistics and efforts to conceal the crime.

Delivering the judgment, Justice Cecilia Githua maintained that Obado’s absence from Migori County at the time of the killing did not remove his responsibility for the crime.

The judge rejected the prosecution’s request for the death penalty and also dismissed Obado’s appeal for a non-custodial sentence.

Evidence presented during the trial established that Obado and Otieno were involved in an intimate relationship which resulted in her pregnancy. The court further established through evidence that the former governor was the biological father of the unborn child.

Otieno was murdered between September 3 and 4, 2018, after leaving a hotel in Rongo, southwestern Kenya, alongside journalist Barrack Oduor.

Reports indicated that Otieno had contacted Oduor to discuss her relationship with the then-governor. However, the two were abducted shortly after leaving the hotel.

Oduor managed to escape by jumping out of the moving vehicle in which they were being transported. His account of the incident subsequently provided investigators with a crucial lead, while Otieno’s body was later recovered near Kodera Forest.

A post-mortem examination revealed that she died from strangulation and severe blood loss resulting from multiple stab wounds.

Obado and his co-defendants were convicted of murder on July 23, 2026, following a prolonged trial during which the prosecution presented 42 witnesses and 81 exhibits to establish their involvement.

Before the sentencing, Otieno’s mother appealed to the court to spare Obado a prison term, arguing that his incarceration would amount to another loss for the family.

She requested a non-custodial sentence, explaining that Otieno’s three children, from different relationships, required his financial and parental support.

However, Justice Githua held that the family’s circumstances could not outweigh the need to impose an appropriate punishment for the offence.

The court also declined to grant the family compensation, citing insufficient evidence to substantiate the financial losses presented.

Meanwhile, the family, through its lawyer, Antony Opondo, told Kenya’s Daily Nation that it intended to appeal the judgment, seeking Obado’s acquittal. The legal teams representing the former governor and his co-defendants also indicated plans to challenge their respective sentences.

The killing generated widespread outrage in Kenya, sparking protests and renewed discussions about violence against women and the accountability of influential political figures.

The case also drew considerable public attention because prosecutions involving senior political figures are relatively uncommon in the country.

Obado served two terms as governor of Migori County in western Kenya. Throughout his political career, he moved between political parties before eventually aligning with President William Ruto’s administration.

He formally joined Ruto’s ruling party in 2023 and later became a regional organiser.

His sentencing brings a significant development in a murder case that has remained before the courts for years since Otieno’s death in 2018.
