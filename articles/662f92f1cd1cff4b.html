"Now we are stuck": Kenyan Prince's 2 cars sink at Diani Beach

Kenyan forex trader and social media personality Raymond Omosa, popularly known as Kenyan Prince, turned a fun coastal outing into an expensive headache on Sunday, November 15, after driving two of his luxury cars onto the sand at Diani Beach.

The vehicles in question were his signature yellow Lamborghini Urus and a grey Mercedes-Benz GLE. What started as a moment to show off the cars to friends at the Coast ended with both machines buried wheel-deep in the sand, unable to move.

"We drove onto the beach to show off, and now we are stuck," Kenyan Prince told his TikTok followers in footage he posted himself that evening.

In the clips, Kenyan Prince appeared shirtless in patterned swim shorts as he surveyed the situation.

The videos showed tyres spinning helplessly, flinging sand in all directions while the cars sank further with each attempt to accelerate.

He and his friend Mushkerr, along with others in their group, tried pushing the vehicles free but had no success. The spinning only deepened the holes beneath the wheels.

Pointing to the Lamborghini, he noted the suspension had lifted off the ground entirely. "The Lambo&#039;s suspension is even floating, clearly showing that it is in the air. It is tough. Anyway, pray for us," he said.

After inspecting the tyres and the underside of one of the cars, he raised concerns about the damage caused. "You see, the spade has even destroyed my car&#039;s tyres, and those tyres to replace are around Ksh400,000. And you can see there is even a scratch," he said.

He concluded that only specialised recovery equipment would do the job. "The only way that car has to be lifted is with a winch. That is the easiest way," he added.

The timing of the incident added an extra layer of irony. Just hours before the cars got stuck, Kenyan Prince had posted a YouTube video from Mombasa pushing back against claims that his Lamborghini had been repossessed by auctioneers after footage of a Lamborghini Urus on a vehicle carrier had circulated online.

"Now we are here. You can see the Lambo was brought here safely, and I wonder if people still believe that it had been auctioned," he said in that video.

The day before, on Saturday, November 14, he had already drawn attention at a Mombasa petrol station after accelerating the Lamborghini at high speed near a crowd, nearly knocking over a bystander who had to leap out of the way.

As of Monday morning, Kenyan Prince had not shared any update confirming whether the two cars had been successfully winched out or what the full extent of the damage turned out to be.

Reactions online were largely amused, with many Kenyans treating the situation as a classic case of showing off gone wrong.
