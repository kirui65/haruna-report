Nairobi - Stakeholders from Kenya&#039;s hospitality, entertainment , labour and transport sectors have sounded the alarm over the proposed Alcoholic Drinks and Drug Control Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, warning that several of its provisions could destroy tens of thousands of jobs and drive consumers towards illicit alcohol.

The Bill, published in the Kenya Gazette Supplement on September 18, 2026, proposes raising the legal drinking age from 18 to 21 years, banning alcohol sales in restaurants and certain hospitality establishments, and introducing transport restrictions that critics say would penalise motorcycle riders who earn a living moving licensed goods.

Michael Kiragu Muthami, Chairman of the Pubs, Entertainment, Restaurants Association of Kenya (PERAK), said the proposed amendments could force roughly 10,000 outlets to shut down, putting approximately 30,000 direct jobs at risk and generating an estimated annual economic loss of KSh 8.46 billion.

He rejected the proposed drinking age increase outright.

He also challenged the rationale for barring restaurants from holding alcohol licences, asking: "Which restaurant can you go to and not be served?"

Sava Wycliffe, Secretary-General of the Kenya Hotels and Allied Workers Union (KHAWU), echoed those concerns, warning that raising the drinking age would trigger redundancies across the sector.

Wycliffe called on Parliament to delete the clause restricting alcohol sales in hospitality establishments and replace it with targeted safeguards focused on responsible service and compliance.

Boda boda operators also pushed back against provisions that would restrict the transportation of alcoholic drinks between 6am and 6pm, with Kenya Riders and Owners Bodaboda Sacco Chairman Elly Kegode warning that motorcycle riders depend on distribution work for their income. He also challenged a proposed KSh 10 million fine or imprisonment for those found transporting alcohol.

Student representative MalcomX from Multimedia University raised a different dimension, arguing that raising the drinking age to 21 could block younger students in hospitality courses from accessing industrial attachments and jobs. He also cautioned that tightening formal access without addressing demand could push young people towards illicit products.

All stakeholders said they support eliminating illicit alcohol and protecting minors, but urged Parliament to craft regulations that strengthen the formal sector rather than undermine it.
