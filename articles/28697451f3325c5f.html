The Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) has opened applications for its annual Internship Programme.

The regulator is calling on recent graduates to apply for placements across nine functional departments before the December 10, 2026 deadline.

The programme targets Kenyan citizens aged between 21 and 29 who hold at least a first degree from a recognised university in fields such as Finance, Economics, Accounting, Law, Information Technology, Human Resources, Statistics, Social Sciences, or related disciplines.

Holders of a master&#039;s degree in a relevant field are also eligible.

Applicants must have graduated within the last 24 months from the closing date of the advertisement.

Crucially, candidates who have already completed another internship or accumulated workplace experience directly related to their area of study since graduating are not eligible to apply.

Each applicant is required to submit a recommendation or reference letter from their learning institution, alongside certified copies of academic certificates and transcripts.

Successful candidates must also hold valid medical insurance for the full duration of the programme, whether through SHIF or another reputable provider.

Placements are on offer in Finance, Research, Banking and Payment Services, Communications, Human Resources, Internal Audit, General Services, Legal Services, and Information Technology.

Interested candidates must apply online through the portal at careers.jkuates.co.ke , completing the programme application form in full and attaching all required documents.

The CBK has stated that late or incomplete applications will not be accepted under any circumstances, and only shortlisted candidates will be contacted.

The internship runs for one full year and must be completed without a break.

The CBK emphasised that the programme does not carry a guarantee of employment upon completion, though graduates who finish the programme are free to apply for any positions the Bank advertises subsequently.

The CBK described the programme&#039;s objective as developing young talent capable of taking up roles within Kenya&#039;s broader banking and financial sector, while also providing participants with practical workplace exposure, the chance to refine their career goals, and an opportunity to build professional networks.

The Bank noted it is an equal opportunity employer and specifically encouraged women, persons living with disabilities, and candidates from marginalised communities to submit applications.

It warned that any form of canvassing, whether direct or indirect, would result in automatic disqualification.

In other news, the Public Service Commission (PSC) plans to expand its Public Service Internship Programme from approximately 8,000 to 50,000 positions starting January 2027, in what would be its largest intake increase.

PSC Chairperson Francis Meja announced the plan during a capacity-building forum in Mombasa on November 2, 2026, urging state agencies and public universities to declare available vacancies and commit to mentoring interns.
