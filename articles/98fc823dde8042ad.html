Burundi bars opposition leader Agathon Rwasa from presidential race

WhatsAppFacebookX

The commission says Rwasa falsely registered as an independent; he has two days to challenge the ruling before the Constitutional Court.

Burundi’s electoral commission has rejected Agathon Rwasa’s application to contest next year’s presidential election, removing the country’s main opposition figure from the race unless he succeeds in a court challenge. President Évariste Ndayishimiye is seeking a second term for the ruling CNDD-FDD party, which is widely considered the favourite.

Both Citizen Digital and The Standard report that the commission disqualified Rwasa because he applied as an independent while remaining a member of a political party. It described the application as containing a “false declaration”. The outlets quote the commission’s notice: “Consequently, the Independent National Electoral Commission rejects his candidacy.”

The commission approved seven of 10 applicants . The rejected applicants have two days to challenge the decision before the Constitutional Court, and the final list of candidates is due on December 6. Neither report says whether Rwasa has filed an appeal or gives a specific date for a court hearing.

The coverage is strikingly alike: both accounts carry the same explanation for the rejection, the same appeal window, and the same background on the election and Rwasa’s political history. The Standard says the commission acted on Friday; Citizen Digital’s report was published shortly after midnight on Saturday. Beyond that timing detail, neither offers a materially different account of the decision.

Both reports also carry the reaction of Pacifique Nininahazwe, an exiled civil society activist, who called the disqualification “no surprise” and said it was part of a process to neutralise Ndayishimiye’s main rival. That is Nininahazwe’s assessment, not a finding by the commission. The reports do not include a response from Rwasa or the government to that accusation.

The limited coverage is worth noting: only these two outlets have reported the decision in the material available, and their accounts are closely matched. The shared reporting establishes the commission’s stated reason and the available appeal route, but offers little independent detail about how the decision was reached or how Rwasa plans to respond.

Rwasa, a former militia leader who fought in Burundi’s civil war, lost to Ndayishimiye in the 2020 election. Opposition figures called that vote an “electoral charade”. Ndayishimiye has led the country since June 2020, following the death of his predecessor, Pierre Nkurunziza, who ruled for 15 years.

The reports place the candidacy decision against a record of disputed electoral conditions. The CNDD-FDD won all 100 seats in the 2025 National Assembly elections, which the accounts say also saw irregularities. Opposition parties have also argued that the 100 million Burundian franc ($33,000) deposit paid by Ndayishimiye to submit his own candidacy papers is designed to keep them out of the contest.

The wider stakes are serious. The reports cite Burundi’s history of political crises and a civil war that killed about 300,000 people between 1993 and 2005. They also note that, in October, the UN Human Rights Council expressed concern over an increase in extrajudicial killings and enforced disappearances.

For now, the immediate questions are procedural: whether Rwasa challenges the decision, and whether the Constitutional Court overturns it before the final candidate list is published. The coverage does not establish whether the commission’s ruling will face a wider legal challenge or how many of the other rejected applicants will appeal.

Rejected applicants can challenge the commission’s decision before the Constitutional Court.
