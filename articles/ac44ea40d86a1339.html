Kirinyaga County expands healthcare infrastructure

WhatsAppFacebookX

At least 23000 Kirinyaga County residents are set to benefit from improved access to healthcare following the launch of three projects targeting maternal and child health, outpatient services and primary healthcare delivery.

Governor Anne Waiguru launched the projects, comprising the groundbreaking of a Maternal, Newborn and Child Health (MNCH) Unit at Kiamutugu Hospital and the opening of Kiandieri and Mathia dispensaries, in an initiative aimed at reducing the distance residents travel to access medical services.

The projects are expected to improve access to affordable healthcare while reducing the financial burden on households that have been spending money travelling to distant health facilities for treatment.

Speaking during the launch, Waiguru said the investments aimed at ensuring residents received quality medical services closer to their homes, irrespective of their economic circumstances.

&#8220;The projects will bring healthcare services closer to the residents, ensuring that they access quality healthcare when they need it, regardless of their location or economic circumstances,&#8221; she said.

She noted that the three facilities would expand access to maternal and child health services, outpatient care, preventive healthcare, laboratory services and essential medicines.

One of the major investments is the construction of the MNCH unit at Kiamutugu Hospital, which is being undertaken through a partnership between the county government and the M-Pesa Foundation.

Upon completion, the new unit will increase the hospital&#8217;s bed capacity from seven to 37, providing an additional 30 beds for mothers and newborns requiring medical attention.

The facility will have an operating theatre, newborn unit, antenatal ward, mother and child welfare clinic, laboratory and pharmacy, enabling the hospital to provide comprehensive maternal and newborn healthcare services.

The expanded capacity is expected to strengthen the hospital&#8217;s ability to manage deliveries, maternal complications and newborn conditions while improving access to specialised services within the county.

Waiguru said the project was part of broader efforts to improve maternal and newborn healthcare, building on investments already made at Kerugoya County Referral Hospital and South Ngariama Hospital.

The county has established a specialised MNCH department at Kerugoya County Referral Hospital and operationalised a fully-fledged maternity wing at South Ngariama Hospital.

It has also incorporated modern MNCH units into the ongoing upgrading of Kianyaga and Kimbimbi hospitals, whose construction works are 95 per cent complete.

The Governor said sustained investment in maternal healthcare had contributed to a reduction in maternal and newborn deaths, with maternal mortality declining from 88 deaths per 100,000 live births in 2017 to 49 deaths per 100,000 live births. Neonatal mortality has also dropped from 26 to 11 deaths per 1,000 live births.

She said the county was committed to further reducing preventable maternal and newborn deaths through improved infrastructure, skilled healthcare personnel, specialised care and efficient referral systems.

Waiguru also highlighted the importance of partnerships between government and the private sector in accelerating healthcare development and improving service delivery, citing the near-completion of Mutithi Maternity Wing.

Meanwhile, the opening of Kiandieri and Mathia dispensaries is expected to address the challenge of accessing basic medical services among residents in the surrounding communities.

Some residents previously walked as far as seven kilometres to reach the nearest health facility, a situation that has particularly affected expectant mothers, children and families seeking routine medical attention.

The two dispensaries will offer antenatal and postnatal care, immunisation, family planning and child welfare clinics. Residents will also access cancer screening services, HIV testing and treatment services, tuberculosis screening and care, laboratory investigations and pharmaceutical services.

The availability of these services at the community level is expected to improve access to disease screening, diagnosis and treatment while reducing the need for residents to travel to distant hospitals for medical attention.

Waiguru said expanding primary healthcare facilities was essential in reducing pressure on referral hospitals while strengthening preventive and promotive healthcare at the community level.

The County Government has previously operationalised several dispensaries and laboratories across Kirinyaga, including Kavote, Mucagara, South Ngariama, Matandara, Riakithiga, Kamwana, Kiamwathi, Kianjiru, Umoja, Kiaumbui, Joshua Mbai, Kiandai and Kiandagae.

In addition to expanding primary healthcare, the county is strengthening its referral system through the upgrading of Kimbimbi and Kianyaga hospitals to Level Four facilities, with ongoing equipping.

To ensure the expanded infrastructure is supported by adequate personnel, Waiguru said the county had deployed 116 additional healthcare workers during the current financial year.
