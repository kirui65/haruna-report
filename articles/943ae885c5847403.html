Kenya, Aga Khan University explore partnership to advance Universal Health Coverage

WhatsAppFacebookX

Health Cabinet Secretary (CS) Aden Duale has held a strategic engagement with the leadership of Aga Khan University (AKU) at its global headquarters in Karachi, Pakistan, to explore practical areas of collaboration in advancing Kenya’s Universal Health Coverage (UHC) agenda.

The meeting, hosted by AKU Global President and Vice-Chancellor Dr. Sulaiman Shahabuddin, Aga Khan Development Network (AKDN) Kenya Diplomatic Representative H.E. Amin Mawji, and senior university faculty, focused on translating the longstanding Kenya–AKDN partnership and existing cooperation agreements into measurable improvements in healthcare delivery for Kenyans.

In his remarks, the CS acknowledged the contribution of the Kenya–AKDN development cooperation framework, which has extended healthcare services to hundreds of thousands of people.

He also noted the significant role played by Aga Khan hospitals in Kenya through their participation in Social Health Authority (SHA) financing arrangements, supporting access to quality healthcare through publicly funded schemes.

The discussions further explored a joint research and innovation framework covering health-system implementation, noncommunicable diseases, maternal and newborn health, and digital health.

Both sides underscored the importance of ensuring that research undertaken in Kenya complies with national legislation, secures local ethical approval, involves Kenyan co-investigators, and delivers meaningful value to the communities concerned.

On strengthening service delivery, the engagement identified opportunities to improve clinical referral systems, enhance patient follow-up in their respective home counties through Community Health Promoters (CHPs), and expand specialist outreach to underserved communities.

“The partnership will also explore supporting public health facilities to strengthen quality assurance and patient-safety practices in line with internationally recognised accreditation standards,” added Duale.

Equally, the two institutions also examined opportunities to strengthen biomedical engineering capacity for the maintenance of public health equipment and advance interoperable digital health systems aligned with the Digital Health Agency (DHA).

“Such integration will support more efficient verification of claims, minimise rejections, and help address processing backlogs within the Social Health Authority (SHA),” the CS assured.

Meanwhile, AKU’s international expertise, research capacity and established healthcare network present an important opportunity to strengthen Kenya’s health system, enhance the national Community Health Promoter (CHP) network and translate successful innovations into scalable interventions and evidence-based national policies.

The CS was accompanied by Kenya’s High Commissioner to Pakistan, Lt Gen (Rtd) Peter Njiru; Dr Ahmed Mohammed, Chief Executive Officer of the Pharmacy and Poisons Board (PPB), and other senior government officials.
