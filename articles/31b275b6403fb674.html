Kenya and DRC Agree to Restore Ambassadorial Ties After Two Years of Diplomatic Fallout

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya and DRC have agreed to restore ambassadorial relations after years of diplomatic tensions linked to the M23 conflict.

Kenya and the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) have agreed to restore full ambassadorial relations after more than two years of diplomatic tensions that strained ties between the neighbouring East African Community (EAC) members.

President William Ruto announced the breakthrough on November 22, 2026, following a Quadripartite Political Summit in Oyo, Republic of the Congo, where he held discussions with DRC President Felix Tshisekedi and other regional leaders.

The agreement provides for the restoration of diplomatic representation at ambassadorial level, the resumption of the Joint Grand Commission for Cooperation and regular consultations between the two governments.

The development marks a significant step towards repairing relations that deteriorated after Kinshasa accused Nairobi of allowing M23 rebel leaders to operate politically from Kenya.

The diplomatic fallout resulted in the recall of envoys, disagreements over ambassadorial appointments and an attack on the Kenyan Embassy in Kinshasa.

Ruto said the two countries had committed themselves to fully normalising relations and rebuilding cooperation across several areas of mutual interest.

"We committed to working towards fully restoring diplomatic relations, including restoring representation at ambassadorial level, resuming the Joint Grand Commission for Cooperation and strengthening regular diplomatic consultations," Ruto stated.

Under the agreement, the DRC will also restore its ambassadorial representation in Nairobi, addressing the diplomatic imbalance that followed the deterioration of relations.

The diplomatic crisis between Nairobi and Kinshasa emerged amid escalating tensions over the conflict in eastern DRC, where government forces have been fighting the M23 rebel movement.

Relations deteriorated after the DRC government accused Kenya of providing a platform for M23 leaders, allegations that Nairobi faced amid its diplomatic and regional engagement efforts.

Kinshasa subsequently recalled its diplomatic officials from Nairobi, although its envoy briefly returned in mid-2024.

However, the return did not result in a complete restoration of ambassadorial relations between the two countries.

The disagreement continued to affect diplomatic representation and cooperation, creating uncertainty over the future of bilateral relations.

In January 2025, the diplomatic dispute escalated after rioters attacked and looted the Kenyan Embassy in Kinshasa.

The protesters accused Kenya of involvement in the conflict in eastern DRC, further complicating efforts to repair relations between the two governments.

The incident prompted the Kenyan government to condemn the attack and demand action over the safety of its diplomatic personnel and property.

The disagreement also affected Kenya's efforts to establish full diplomatic representation in Kinshasa.

In early 2025, Ruto appointed Peter Tum as Kenya's ambassador to the DRC.

However, the Congolese government withheld accreditation, preventing Tum from formally taking up the position as Kenya's confirmed top diplomatic representative in the country.

The latest agreement now provides an opportunity to resolve the outstanding diplomatic issues and restore normal government-to-government engagement.

The Oyo summit brought together Ruto, Tshisekedi, Republic of the Congo President Denis Sassou N'Guesso and Togo President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé.

The discussions focused on rebuilding diplomatic relations, strengthening economic partnerships and addressing regional security challenges.

Kenya and the DRC also agreed to deepen cooperation in trade, economic development, security and cultural exchange.

Ruto said the two governments had committed to working together to promote regional peace, stability and integration.

"We also agreed to deepen cooperation in trade, economic development, security and culture while working together to advance regional peace, stability and integration," he added.

The discussions come at a time when both countries have an interest in strengthening economic and political cooperation within the East African Community.

The DRC joined the regional bloc in 2022, creating opportunities for expanded trade, investment and cross-border economic activities.

Kenya has maintained strategic economic and diplomatic interests in the Congolese market, particularly in trade, banking, logistics and regional infrastructure.

Restoring ambassadorial relations could therefore help remove diplomatic obstacles affecting cooperation between the two countries.

The resumption of the Joint Grand Commission for Cooperation will also provide a formal platform for government officials to address bilateral challenges, review existing agreements and identify new areas of partnership.

However, the success of the agreement will depend on whether both governments follow through on their commitments, particularly the restoration of diplomatic representation and regular consultations.

For Nairobi and Kinshasa, rebuilding trust will be critical after years of disagreements over regional security and the conflict involving M23 in eastern DRC.

The latest breakthrough offers both countries an opportunity to move beyond the diplomatic fallout and strengthen their relationship as members of the East African Community.
