KAA Seeks Public Views on Proposed 2026 Airport Charges, Sets December Deadline

Kenya Airports Authority (KAA) has opened consultations with business organisations and the public over proposed changes to commercial charges at airports across the country.

The authority convened a high-level roundtable in Nairobi on Thursday, November 26, bringing together representatives of ten business member organisations to discuss the proposed Commercial Charges Order, 2026.

KAA Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer Moses Wekesa chaired the meeting, which attracted leaders from the cargo, logistics, horticulture, manufacturing and aviation sectors.

The consultations are intended to gather views from businesses that rely on airport services before the proposed charges are finalised.

The Shippers Council of Eastern Africa and the Kenya International Freight and Warehousing Association (KIFWA) represented the logistics and freight sectors.

The Fresh Produce Exporters Association of Kenya (FPEAK), Kenya Flower Council (KFC) and Fresh Produce Consortium of Kenya represented fresh produce and flower exporters, many of whom transport their goods through Jomo Kenyatta International Airport.

Other participants included the Kenya Association of Manufacturers (KAM) and Nyamita Miraa Traders.

The aviation sector was represented by the Kenya Association of Air Operators (KAAO), the Airline Operators Committee (AOC) and the International Air Transport Association (IATA).

KAA has invited members of the public and other stakeholders to review the draft Commercial Charges Order, 2026, which is available on the authority&#039;s official website.

Written submissions will be accepted until December 8, 2026. The authority has also scheduled regional public barazas for December 9 as part of efforts to extend the consultation beyond Nairobi.

The proposed order outlines commercial fees applicable at KAA-managed airports. Its provisions are particularly relevant to cargo owners, freight forwarders, warehouse operators, airlines and exporters who depend on airport facilities and services.

The authority is seeking feedback from these groups to inform the final document before it comes into effect.
