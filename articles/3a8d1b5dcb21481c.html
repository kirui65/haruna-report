Opposition within opposition? How third power bloc is taking shape ahead of 2027 elections

WhatsAppFacebookX

From left: Jubilee leader Fred Matiang’i, People’s Party of Kenya leader Ndindi Nyoro, People’s Liberation Party chief Martha Karua, and Siaya Governor James Orengo.

For some time now, the country has been treated to two main political factions; one supporting President William Ruto and his re-election bid, and another, an amalgamation of his opponents.

However, a new centre is slowly emerging that appears to be splintering from the second grouping with the potential to significantly redraw the political landscape ahead of the polls on August 10.

The growing partnership between Jubilee presidential candidate Fred Matiang’i, People’s Party of Kenya leader Ndindi Nyoro, People’s Liberation Party chief Martha Karua, and Siaya Governor James Orengo, is one of the most significant developments in the opposition’s succession politics so far.

Jubilee Party leader Dr Fred Matiang’i joined by Ndindi Nyoro, Martha Karua and Siaya Governor James Orengo amongst other leaders during a joint People's Tour in Kisii town on November 15, 2026.

Their joint political activities, which have culminated in a high-profile tour of the Gusii region, have fuelled speculation that the four are forming a powerful political bloc that could influence the direction of the opposition and the selection of its presidential candidate.

On Sunday, November 22, 2026, the leaders are expected to attend a service at Kiang’ombe Anglican Church in Ms Karua’s home county of Kirinyaga, before holding a series of rallies and political engagements as they step up their efforts to consolidate support across the country.

While the leaders have publicly presented their collaboration as a strategy to increase the opposition’s national presence, insiders claim that the alliance was formed in response to the “arrogance” of certain opposition leaders, who, they say, could jeopardise the opposition’s collective quest for the presidency.

Dr Fred Matiang’i and Rigathi Gachagua during the United Opposition's tour of Kisumu.

The former Deputy President and Dr Matiang’i have in recent days traded barbs, further exposing the widening political fault lines within the opposition over its leadership and strategy ahead of the 2027 elections.

Wiper Patriotic Front spokesperson Ndegwa Njiru has cautioned that the opposition coalition remains fragile, urging its principals to treat one another with respect and rein in their supporters to avoid undermining the partnership.

“Those joking around with this relationship must understand that it is very delicate and should be treated with the utmost respect. It is important that you rein in your supporters and ensure they respect other presidential candidates,” Mr Njiru told the opposition principals.

According to experts, the implications of the Matiang’i-Nyoro-Karua-Orengo alliance must be considered in light of Kenya’s constitutional requirement that a presidential candidate has to secure 50 per cent plus one of all votes cast in order to be declared the winner in the first round.

Therefore, the emerging political partnership could have a broader significance: its influence could affect how votes are distributed among competing presidential candidates, potentially making it difficult for any one candidate to reach the constitutional threshold.

If the alliance attracts a significant proportion of voters who would otherwise support different opposition groups, it could lead to a fragmented presidential contest and increase the likelihood of a second round.

Siaya Governor James Orengo at his Ugenya home in Siaya County, on February 27, 2026.

Advocate Chris Omore says that this developing alliance is important because it could complicate the arithmetic of the presidential contest by preventing any candidate from achieving the required number of votes to win in the first round.

“This alliance is crucial because it has the potential to deny any presidential candidate the 50 per cent-plus-one threshold. Its impact will be felt in how votes are distributed across the political formations, particularly if it operates independently of the other opposition groups,” said Mr Omore.

Article 138(4) of the Constitution provides that a presidential candidate must receive more than half of all votes cast in the election and at least 25 per cent of the votes cast in each of more than half of the 47 counties. This translates to at least 24 counties. Article 138(5) further provides that where no candidate meets these two conditions, a fresh election must be held within 30 days, involving the candidate who received the greatest number of votes and the one who received the second-greatest number.

The Constitution thus provides for a two-candidate run-off rather than allowing a candidate to win simply by polling more votes than their competitors.

Vihiga Senator Godfrey Osotsi, a member of Edwin Sifuna’s Linda Mwananchi Movement has expressed confidence that the opposition will eventually rally behind a single presidential candidate ahead of the 2027 General Election, drawing parallels with the 2002 political alliance that ended Kanu’s 24-year rule.

He maintained that despite the current realignments and competing interests, opposition leaders would eventually overcome their differences and settle on one candidate to challenge President Ruto.

“The opposition will have one candidate like in 2002 because the concerns are the same. We are confident we shall have a 2002 moment,” Mr Osotsi said.

But despite his explanation, everything points to the fact that all is not well within the united opposition, with some members feeling sidelined because they do not have as high a number of registered voters from their own backyards as others do. It is this feeling that is said to fuel the need for a third force: an alternative alliance.

The Matiang’i-Nyoro-Karua-Orengo partnership stands out, with analysts noting that the four leaders have raised concerns over the management and direction of the opposition, suggesting that they could be charting an independent political path outside the existing power matrix.

Dr Matiang’i has, however, dismissed suggestions that the emerging partnership represents a breakaway faction or an attempt to undermine other opposition leaders, insisting that the arrangement is part of a collective strategy to strengthen the coalition.

Speaking to Sunday Nation, the former Interior Cabinet Secretary said opposition leaders had agreed that politicians should organise separate activities across the country instead of concentrating their mobilisation efforts in one geographical area.

“We all agreed in opposition that everybody moves around. We need to create teams to cover the whole country,” he said.

He suggested that the growing speculation surrounding the leaders’ movements was being fuelled by unnecessary political interpretations.

“People just over-read things,” he said. “We all understand the country. We must all move, at some point sit and sort out the underlying issues.”

Jubilee Party leader Fred Matiang’i addresses residents of Kware in Ongata Rongai, Kajiado County on October 25, 2026.

Dr Matiang’i’s presidential ambitions have increasingly gained visibility through his engagements with leaders from different political backgrounds.

Pundits say that the former Interior CS brings national government experience, having served in several Cabinet portfolios under former President Uhuru Kenyatta. His supporters see his administrative experience and national profile as important assets in a contest against an incumbent seeking a second term.

However, his political challenge is transforming that profile into a broad electoral coalition capable of attracting support beyond his Gusii backyard.

“Nyoro’s involvement is particularly significant because it gives the emerging partnership a foothold in the politically influential Mt Kenya region,” says political analyst Geoffrey Mboya.

“Karua, on the other hand, brings a different proposition to the emerging alliance. The former Justice Minister and longtime reform campaigner has maintained a national political profile built around governance, constitutionalism and accountability. She has also previously vied for the presidency and was Raila Odinga’s running mate in the last election, making her participation in the partnership important in the management of competing ambitions,” says Mr Mboya.

During the Gusii gathering, Ms Karua sought to demonstrate that individual aspirations would not stand in the way of a collective political project.

“We are here with Dr Fred Matiang’i, Ndindi Nyoro and James Orengo. Many of us, including myself, have presidential ambitions. However, if it is agreed that Dr Matiang’i should be the flag bearer, I will step aside and support him,” she said. She extended the same commitment to Mr Nyoro, saying she would support him if the opposition settled on his candidature.

Her remarks were an important political signal at a time when the opposition’s biggest challenge could be managing the competing ambitions of its leading figures.

Governor Orengo’s appearance alongside Dr Matiang’i, Ms Karua and Mr Nyoro in Gusii, pundits say, also carries significance beyond a routine political engagement.

Mr Orengo had previously aligned himself with Nairobi Senator Sifuna’s Linda Mwananchi Movement. However, his recent political activities, including the acquisition of the Liberal Democratic Party and public endorsement of Dr Matiang’i in Kisii, have placed him increasingly within the emerging partnership.

During the Gusii rally, Mr Orengo openly urged the community to support Dr Matiang’i’s presidential ambitions, invoking the political legacy of Raila and his father, Jaramogi Oginga Odinga.

“I thank the Abagusii people for consistently voting for the late Raila Odinga. And not only Raila, but also the late Jaramogi Oginga Odinga. Please understand that if you vote for President Ruto to return to office, Raila will not be pleased wherever he may be. However, if you vote for your son, Matiang’i, he will be very happy,” he told residents.
