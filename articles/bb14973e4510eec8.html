MSEA backs digital push to help Kenyan MSMEs expand online markets

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Micro and Small Enterprises Authority (MSEA) has stepped up efforts to boost digital entrepreneurship following the launch of a new programme aimed at helping small businesses harness online platforms to grow their enterprises and reach wider markets.

The initiative, dubbed TikTok/International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) Digital Commerce Labs (DCL), seeks to equip entrepreneurs, particularly women and young people, with practical digital skills to improve their online visibility, attract customers and increase sales.

The programme was officially launched by MSEA Director of Business Development, Marketing and Trade, Dr Caroline Kaua, alongside the Head of Presidential Special Projects and Creative Economy, Dennis Itumbi.

The launch attracted representatives from the private sector, business associations, digital creators and entrepreneurs, including partners such as TikTok, ICC Kenya, the Kenya National Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KNCCI) and the Kenya Private Sector Alliance (KEPSA).

Speaking at the event, Itumbi applauded TikTok and ICC Kenya for introducing the initiative, noting that entrepreneurs also need to understand how to safeguard their innovations, business ideas and intellectual property as they expand into the digital space.

He further urged TikTok to explore the possibility of introducing TikTok Shops in Kenya, saying the platform could create additional opportunities for businesses to sell their products online and reach customers beyond the country’s borders.

On her part, Dr Kaua reaffirmed MSEA’s commitment to strengthening small businesses through digital transformation, improved market access and initiatives that enhance their competitiveness.

She pointed to opportunities for integrating the programme with existing government interventions, including the National Youth Opportunities Towards Advancement (NYOTA) programme, Kenya Jobs and Economic Transformation (KJET) project and Uwezo Fund.

Kaua also proposed leveraging MSEA’s registration framework through the Registrar of Micro and Small Enterprises to bring more businesses on board and widen the programme’s reach.

She challenged entrepreneurs to move beyond simply establishing an online presence and focus on converting digital engagement into tangible business results. This, she noted, requires creating engaging content, understanding consumer preferences and building customer confidence to turn online interactions into actual purchases.

Implemented in collaboration with ICC Kenya, the Digital Commerce Labs will provide entrepreneurs with a broader learning platform through practical training, strategic partnerships and engagement with digital content creators.

The initiative comes amid growing opportunities for small businesses to use digital platforms to promote their products, connect with consumers and explore markets that would otherwise be difficult to access.

MSEA said it remains committed to supporting enterprises to embrace innovation, strengthen their operations and take advantage of emerging opportunities in the digital economy.
