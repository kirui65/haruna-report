"It's immoral": Governors reject PS Oluga's order to remove hospital paybills

Nairobi - The Council of Governors (CoG) has come out strongly against a directive issued by the Principal Secretary for Medical Services, Ouma Oluga, instructing county health facilities to deactivate their Paybill numbers, describing the order as unconstitutional and a direct assault on the autonomy of county governments.

In a statement released on Monday, November 23, the CoG acknowledged the broader goal of shielding patients from double charges on services fully funded by the Social Health Authority (SHA), but argued that the PS&#039;s directive failed to account for the realities facing county facilities on the ground.

The Council noted that SHA coverage does not extend to every Kenyan or to the full range of healthcare services offered at county facilities.

Certain services fall outside the authority&#039;s approved benefits package, and delayed, rejected, or insufficient reimbursements from SHA have continued to place serious financial strain on county health facilities across the country.

Removing Paybill numbers, the CoG warned, would strip county governments of a lawful mechanism for recovering costs and sustaining day-to-day service delivery.

The Council consequently directed all health facility managers to disregard the PS&#039;s instruction entirely, maintaining that county governments must retain the right to collect payments for services not covered or not yet reimbursed under the SHA framework.

The CoG framed the standoff not merely as an administrative dispute but as a constitutional matter, arguing that the directive by Oluga cuts across the devolved functions assigned to county governments under Kenya&#039;s constitution.

The Council&#039;s position is that decisions over cost-recovery mechanisms within county health facilities fall within the mandate of county governments, and cannot be overridden by a directive from a national government official.
