Relief for Nairobi Residents as Mbagathi Adds More Diabetes Clinic Days

NAIROBI, Kenya- Nairobi residents seeking diabetes care have received a boost after Mbagathi County Referral Hospital expanded its dedicated clinic from two days a week to five.

The move comes as the hospital, Nairobi City County and Novo marked World Diabetes Day with free screening and health education services targeting up to 400 community members.

Mbagathi’s diabetes clinic will now run from Monday to Friday and serves between 250 and 300 patients every month.

Residents who attended the World Diabetes Day event were offered free diabetes, hypertension, eye, foot and HbA1c checks, alongside HIV and tuberculosis screening.

The event was held under the theme, Early Action on Diabetes, highlighting the importance of knowing one’s status before complications develop.

Participants also received nutrition education and practical physical activity sessions led by a physiotherapist.

Dr Erica Koima, Mbagathi Hospital’s Medical Superintendent, said the extended clinic hours would ease access to care for patients who previously had to wait for limited clinic days.

The clinic operates through Mbagathi’s Centre for Driving Change in Diabetes, established under the iCARE partnership involving the hospital, Nairobi City County and Novo.

Walk-in patients can access services, while eligible Type 1 diabetes patients below 25 receive insulin support.

Jakob Mollerup, Novo’s General Manager for Middle Africa, said more than 800,000 people in Kenya are living with diabetes, with over half believed to be unaware of their status.

He said early testing, treatment and quality care were crucial in helping patients avoid severe complications.

Novo said its iCARE programme has set up more than 92 diabetes centres across Kenya, trained over 3,500 health workers and supported more than 6,100 people to access affordable insulin.

Nairobi County Director of Medical Services Dr Moses Owino said the fight against diabetes should continue long after World Diabetes Day.

People living with diabetes also shared their experiences, encouraging others not to fear diagnosis or abandon treatment.

Eric Omondi , who has lived with Type 1 diabetes for 23 years, said people diagnosed with the condition could still lead fulfilling and productive lives with the right support.

Health experts urged Kenyans to seek testing, especially if they experience increased thirst, frequent urination, unusual tiredness, unexplained weight loss or blurred vision.

They also encouraged people living with diabetes to remain physically active, eat healthy foods, take prescribed medication and attend regular clinic visits.
