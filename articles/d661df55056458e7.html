"Not limited": Kuwait threatens deportation of Kenyans, others working as guards

Kuwait has warned building guards and caretakers, including Kenyan nationals, that they face administrative deportation and a ban on re-entry if they fail to report illegal or suspicious activity on the properties they oversee.

The warning was issued on November 13, 2026, by a security source and published by the Arab Times.

It fundamentally reframes the guard&#039;s role, stating that their responsibility "is not limited to protecting the property but also includes reporting violations and illegal activities occurring within the premises under their supervision."

What raises the stakes considerably is the regional dimension of any deportation.

The security source explained that "GCC security coordination allows information concerning deported individuals to be registered and shared among Gulf Cooperation Council countries," meaning those placed on deportation lists "may consequently face restrictions on entering other GCC countries."

For Kenyan workers, this is not an abstract threat. Kuwait deported 610 expatriates over five days in June 2026 for violating residency and labour laws, with those individuals banned from re-entering Kuwait for life and other GCC states for five years.

A separate incident saw 20 Asian expatriates arrested in a betting den referred for deportation under equivalent conditions. A single infraction in Kuwait can now close doors across Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Oman, and Bahrain.

Kenya is among the countries whose nationals work in Kuwait&#039;s security sector. By 2025, more than 300,000 Kenyans were employed across Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the UAE, with additional thousands in Kuwait, Oman, and Bahrain, working in domestic services, hospitality, construction, and security.

The building guard role sits at the intersection of property management and law enforcement.

Guards who fail to report a betting den, an unlicensed business, or a residency violation on their premises can now be treated as complicit, regardless of personal involvement.

The November 13 warning coincided with a dawn raid in Jleeb Al-Shuyoukh, where Kuwaiti detectives arrested 14 Asian expatriates at a betting den, all referred to the Administrative Deportation Department.

Kuwait, a country of 4.9 million people, the majority of them foreign nationals, has been intensifying crackdowns on labour and residency violations. The Interior Ministry stated in June 2026 that accountability "targets the worker and the employer alike."

Building guards are advised to familiarise themselves with Kuwaiti reporting channels and escalate any suspected illegal activity promptly. Maintaining records of such reports, including dates, times, and the authorities contacted, adds a layer of protection.

Ensuring that residency and work permits remain valid is equally critical, since the ongoing security campaigns target immigration violations alongside criminal conduct.

For Kenyan households that rely on Gulf remittances, a deportation means an immediate loss of income and the additional costs of repatriation, making compliance not just a legal obligation but a financial necessity.
