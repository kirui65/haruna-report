Catholic Officials Who Allow Politicians to Speak in Church to Be Fired, Archbishop Muheria Says

Nyeri Catholic Archbishop Anthony Muheria has threatened to dismiss church officials who allow politicians to address congregations during services, warning that anyone facilitating political speeches will immediately lose their position.

Muheria issued the stern warning amid growing concerns over politicians turning religious gatherings into campaign platforms ahead of the 2027 General Election.

Speaking in Nyeri, the archbishop said the punishment would apply to all church officials, including coordinators, secretaries, catechists and other people entrusted with managing church activities.

He warned that anyone who handed a politician the microphone to address worshippers would face immediate dismissal once the matter was reported to him.

He added that the church would organise elections to replace officials removed for violating the directive.

The archbishop also condemned politicians who engage in heated exchanges and use obscene language inside churches, arguing that such behaviour undermines the sanctity of places of worship, particularly when children are present.

In a stern warning delivered in Kiswahili, Muheria insisted that churches belonged to God and should not be turned into political battlegrounds.

He further instructed worshippers to resist attempts by politicians to deliver political speeches during services, urging them to make noise and ululate whenever a politician is handed the microphone.

Muheria maintained that political leaders had other avenues to communicate their agendas, including public rallies and other political gatherings, instead of using churches to advance their ambitions.

The warning follows a nationwide directive issued by the Kenya Conference of Catholic Bishops (KCCB) on Thursday, November 12, prohibiting politicians from delivering political addresses in Catholic churches ahead of the 2027 General Election.

The bishops maintained that churches should remain places of prayer, unity and spiritual nourishment where Kenyans from different political backgrounds can worship without being subjected to partisan campaigns.

They also reminded politicians that they had several alternative platforms to reach voters, including the media, public barazas and roadside rallies.
