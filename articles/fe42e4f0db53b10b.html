Gachagua Announces December Political Direction for Mt. Kenya and Endorses Gelonga Mkunji for Embu Governor

Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua addressed congregants during a church service at Wonders of God Church in Runyenjes, Embu County, where he outlined a major political realignment for the Mount Kenya region ahead of the upcoming general election. Gachagua welcomed Runyenjes Member of Parliament Muchangi Karemba back to the regional fold, noting that Karemba had listened to the sentiments of his constituents on the ground and decided to abandon President William Ruto's political camp. Emphasizing that all politics is local, Gachagua praised Karemba, alongside lawmakers Kagombe and Gathoni Wamuchomba (referred to as Mkami), for resonating with the desires of their electorate rather than remaining tied to political arrangements in Nairobi.

"What a clever leader does, you listen to your people and these are your people. All politics is local hapa mlimani," Gachagua stated.

Gachagua announced that the Mount Kenya region will make its definitive political decisions for the next election during the upcoming Christmas festivities, between December 23 and Boxing Day on December 26. He explained that residents working in Nairobi and elsewhere will travel home to consult with their families and agree on preferred candidates for every elective seat, from the presidency down to Members of County Assemblies. Ahead of the holiday break, Gachagua pledged to issue a clear political direction on December 22 or 23, asking residents to deliberate on his guidance over Christmas and report back with any proposed adjustments by December 27 or 28.

"So promise before we break for Christmas, we shall give political direction for this region so that tukikula Christmas tukubaliane vizuri," he said.

Reflecting on past political alliances, Gachagua used the metaphor of hiring a vehicle for a wedding only to be abandoned along the way to describe the region's fallout with President Ruto. He declared that the Mount Kenya electorate has resolved never again to rely on another leader's political vehicle, announcing that they now have their own green-colored party, the Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP), which he described as the color of restoration and prosperity. Dismissing rival political outfits—including Ruto's yellow party and smaller parties being introduced in Runyenjes and Manyatta to confuse voters—Gachagua insisted that the region's electorate is astute enough to recognize and stick to its own political vehicle.

"Sisi mlimani tumeamua hakuna siku tutaenda harusi na gari ya mtu mwingine. Sisi tuko na gari yetu sasa ya DCP," Gachagua declared.

The former Deputy President strongly criticized President Ruto's narrative of national equality, branding it a deceptive tactic reminiscent of the 2007 "41 against 1" political mobilization. Gachagua accused the Head of State of tailoring false narratives to different communities—telling Luo voters that previous regimes sidelined them in favor of Mount Kenya, while feeding similar lines to the Somali and Luhya communities. Pointing to the tea sector in Runyenjes and Manyatta, Gachagua argued that Mount Kenya farmers received meager bonuses while Ruto directed KSh 3.7 billion in taxpayer-funded price stabilization money exclusively to tea zones in his Rift Valley backyard, including Bomet, Kericho, and Nandi, leaving out Kiambu, Murang'a, Kirinyaga, Nyeri, Embu, Meru, and Tharaka Nithi.

"Amesema hiyo pesa ipewe watu ya kwao watu ya Bomet, watu ya Kericho, watu ya Nandi. Kiambu, Murang'a, Nyeri, Embu, Meru hakuna chochote," Gachagua noted.

Further challenging the administration's claims of fairness, Gachagua highlighted alleged nepotism in public service appointments and state corporations, claiming the only "equality" achieved under Ruto's government is the uniform deduction of civil servants' payslips. He alleged that out of 5,000 police recruits admitted at the Kiganjo Police Training College, 3,200 came from the President's community while all other Kenyan communities shared the remaining 1,800 slots. He similarly claimed that 27 out of 33 Officers Commanding Stations (OCS) in Nairobi hail from one community, and accused the leadership of the Kenya Electricity Generating Company (KenGen) of prioritizing relatives, warning that such exclusion amounts to a declaration of political war against Mount Kenya.

"Sasa mkisikia hiyo usawa ni sawa mjue ni vita na Mlima Kenya. Naweka soko na wewe ujue tuko na date na wewe next year," he warned.

Turning his focus to county leadership, Gachagua launched a scathing attack on Embu Governor Cecily Mbarire, expressing regret for having campaigned for her election and apologizing to Embu residents. He accused the governor of abandoning her county duties to spend months at State House, UDA headquarters, and across Rift Valley counties like Kericho and Nandi strategizing for Ruto's re-election. Gachagua alleged that while local hospitals in Embu lack medicine, roads remain stalled, and a promised water project in Karurumo has failed to materialize, Mbarire was busy distributing mattresses, hospital blankets, gas cylinders, and sacks of money to voters during a by-election in Mbeere North (referred to as Walokalau).

"Hakuna kitu inaendelea Embu. Hospitali hazina dawa. Barabara zimekwama. Pale mbele there is nothing. You are not available," Gachagua said.

Gachagua also faulted Governor Mbarire for failing to champion the interests of Embu tea farmers despite the pressing economic challenges facing the sector. He revealed that he confronted the governor over the 0.8 percent levy imposed on tea and the KSh 12 billion owed to the Kenya Tea Development Agency (KTDA) after the government allegedly failed to honor its fertilizer subsidy pledge, forcing farmers to shoulder the cost through deductions. According to Gachagua, Mbarire dismissed his concerns, stating that her primary focus was securing President Ruto's re-election so that he could reward her with a government job even if Embu voters rejected her.

"Akasema mimi wacha kwanza nifanye kazi ya rais. Rais akirudi mimi sina shida, rais atanipatia kazi," Gachagua recounted.

In light of what he termed a betrayal by the incumbent governor, Gachagua endorsed a young aspirant, Gelonga Mkunji, to unseat Mbarire in the upcoming gubernatorial race. He praised Mkunji for his integrity and loyalty to the community, revealing that the young leader had turned down a KSh 30 million bribe allegedly offered by President Ruto's camp to support Gachagua's impeachment. Urging Embu residents not to dismiss Mkunji because of his youth, Gachagua argued that energetic young leaders who cannot be bought are what the region needs to drive development, while telling Mbarire she was free to relocate to Ruto's Sugoi home.

"Huyu kijana alitiwa pesa na Kasongo 30 million anitoe kwa ofisi akasema hataki. Kama alikataa pesa akula anaweza kula pesa ya watu?" Gachagua asked.

Addressing social issues affecting the youth in Mount Kenya, Gachagua vowed to reinstate the strict crackdown on illicit alcohol and second-generation brews that he spearheaded while in office. He accused the current administration of deliberately flooding the region with lethal brews such as "Katuringui" to destroy young men, challenging the President to distribute the same alcohol in his own home region if he truly believed in equality. Gachagua promised that within ten months of taking power, his administration would ban toxic brews, reduce the number of bars in shopping centers so that butcheries, hotels, and shops outnumber drinking dens, and enforce historical drinking hours starting after work.

"By mwezi ya 10 hiyo gadhuri ngui na hiyo pombe ingine yote tutapiga marafuku. Hatutakubali na hata Mkunji nimemwambia mambo ya vilabu lazima ipunguzwe," he pledged.

Shifting to electoral preparedness, Gachagua issued stern demands to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) chairperson Erastus Ethekon and the commissioners, dismissing vocal government-allied youths as "spoiled brats." He demanded an immediate timeline for the audit of the voters' register to expunge names of deceased persons—citing remarks by Hassan Omar—as well as alleged foreign voters registered from Uganda and Somalia. Gachagua also raised alarm over the expired Smartmatic elections management system contract, questioning how the commission plans to conduct mass voter registration in February without a new system in place.

"Tunauliza wewe chairman wa IEBC tunataka kujua the audit of the voters register itafanywa lini, ndio tutoe majina ya ile watu walikufa," Gachagua demanded.

Gachagua further accused IEBC chairperson Ethekon of taking instructions from Interior Principal Secretary Raymond Omollo, alleging discriminatory practices in the issuance of national identity cards and voter registration. He claimed that while mobile registration units are taken door-to-door in President Ruto's strongholds and regions like North Eastern and Nyanza—with children as young as 14 allegedly receiving IDs within two days—young people in Mount Kenya face delays of up to eight months and are forced to travel to constituency headquarters. Gachagua warned the IEBC chair against allowing Ruto to use him to rig the election, insisting that presidential results must be announced at the constituency level without interference from a national tallying center at Bomas.

"By January you either talk to the leaders or you talk to the people directly, and I want to assure Ethekon don't underrate the determination of Kenyans," he warned.

Responding to President Ruto's recent remarks in Parliament criticizing regional voting blocs, Gachagua mocked the Head of State and told him to save his tears for next year. He confirmed that Mount Kenya has resolved to vote as a unified bloc to remove Ruto from office, uniting voters who previously backed Ruto, those who supported Raila Odinga, undecided voters, and newly registered youth into a formidable eight-million-strong voting bloc. Beyond the sheer numbers, Gachagua emphasized a second, more decisive strategy: achieving a voter turnout of at least 96 percent across the mountain to guarantee Ruto's defeat.

"Tumeamua we are voting as a block na that block ni ya kufukuza wewe uende nyumbani that we have decided so don't panic," Gachagua declared.

Outlining the broader national coalition taking shape against the government, Gachagua asserted that the anti-Ruto wave extends far beyond Mount Kenya. He projected that the opposition will sweep Nairobi's 2.7 million votes, dominate Western Kenya through leaders like Eugene Wamalwa (referred to as Sivuna), secure Kisii, capture the Coast region through Mohamed Ali (Moha), and lock down the Lower Eastern bloc under Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka. With such nationwide consolidation, Gachagua predicted that Ruto's support in Mount Kenya would plummet to between 12 and 14 percent, leaving the opposition with an overwhelming 86 percent victory.

"Si mlima tumeamua, the whole country imeamua. Nairobi 2.7 million yote ni ya kupeleka Kasongo nyumbani," he asserted.

To achieve the targeted 96 percent voter turnout, Gachagua unveiled an elaborate grassroots mobilization plan for election day, August 10. He instructed vehicle owners and boda boda riders to dedicate their transport services to ferrying elderly women and voters to polling stations, adding that even hospitalized patients and individuals in police custody—noting that disgruntled police officers whose pay and insurance were cut are sympathetic to their cause—would be facilitated to cast their ballots. Crucially, Gachagua issued a directive to mothers and local leaders to collect and safely keep the original national ID cards of young men struggling with alcoholism, giving them photocopies for daily use until the morning of the election so no vote is lost.

"All their original IDs must be collected and kept in safe custody na watolewe photocopy watembee nayo, alafu hiyo original tutaitoa asubuhi tarehe 10 mwezi wa nane," he instructed.

While welcoming back Mount Kenya politicians who had previously aligned with President Ruto, Gachagua drew a clear moral line on forgiveness and political rehabilitation. He stated that legislators like Karemba who merely took money from Ruto have been forgiven by the community, provided they return home and seek fresh approval from voters. However, he declared that leaders who allegedly masterminded the storming of churches—citing an incident at Christ the King—or shed the blood of protesters during the Gen Z demonstrations and the Mbeere North by-election would never be forgiven, comparing Ruto's recent actions to the 2007 Kiambaa church tragedy.

"Lakini this time again we cannot forgive him. Tulimsamehe mara ya kwanza. Si ameua Gen Z? Si amesema apigwe risasi mguu? Hatuwezi msamehe," Gachagua stated.

Gachagua also mocked Ruto's planned visit to Embu during the Christmas season, welcoming him to bring sacks of money just as he allegedly did during tours in Kisii and Busia. He urged Embu residents to take the money if offered, noting that taxpayers deserve a share of public funds, while reminding them that taking the cash does not obligate them to vote for the administration. Simultaneously, he ridiculed Public Service Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku (referred to as Rukoo), accusing him of trying to divide the Kikuyu, Embu, and Meru communities—who have been inseparable since the colonial era—before attempting to run for Nairobi Governor where 800,000 Kikuyu voters reside.

"The Kikuyus, the Merus and the Embu are inseparable. Hata wakati ya mkoloni pass ya kutembea ilikuwa ni ya Mkuyu, Mwembu na Mumeru," Gachagua emphasized.

According to Gachagua, senior Mount Kenya ministers serving in Ruto's cabinet have already sensed the impending political defeat and are scrambling to secure county seats as fallback options. He named Lands Cabinet Secretary Alice Wahome as preparing to run for Murang'a Governor, Trade Cabinet Secretary Lee Kinyanjui eyeing the Nakuru gubernatorial seat, and Geoffrey Ruku targeting Nairobi, alleging that they are merely draining resources from the President while positioning themselves for post-2027 survival. Gachagua advised Embu gubernatorial hopeful Gelonga Mkunji to carefully manage Ruku should he return to local politics after failing in Nairobi, ensuring he is never placed in a position where he can divide the Mount Kenya community.

"Hawa watu wote ya mlima wako na Kasongo wamesikiza ground wananyonya yeye tu wanaimba pesa ya kwenda kusimama," he remarked.

Concluding his address on a personal note, Gachagua recounted how President Ruto allegedly bribed Members of Parliament to impeach him, expressing deep gratitude to Runyenjes MP Muchangi Karemba for refusing the money and voting against his ouster. Noting that Embu hosted his last public function as Deputy President and his first after leaving office, Gachagua pledged to camp in the county during the election period—even if it meant sleeping in his car or bathing from a basin—to ensure DCP sweeps all seats from Governor to MCAs. He announced a meeting the following week at Monyoro for all DCP aspirants to prepare for free and fair party primaries in March, urging candidates to rally behind women voters before the church service concluded with a fundraiser.

"Governor hapa atakuwa wa DCP, senator wa DCP, women rep wa DCP, wabunge wote wanne DCP, MCAs wote 20 of them," Gachagua concluded.
