Ruto and Tshisekedi agree to restore Kenya-DRC diplomatic ties

WhatsAppFacebookX

The Oyo summit produced a pledge to restore ambassador-level ties, but the reports give no timetable for putting the agreement into effect.

Kenya and the Democratic Republic of the Congo agreed to restore diplomatic representation at ambassador level, resume their Joint Grand Commission for Cooperation and hold regular consultations, President William Ruto said after meeting Félix Tshisekedi in Oyo on Sunday.

The agreement came at a four-country summit in the Republic of the Congo, bringing together Kenya, the host country, the DRC and Togo. The dispute between Nairobi and Kinshasa had strained relations since late 2023, when the DRC objected to the launch in Nairobi of the Alliance Fleuve Congo, a political-military coalition linked to the M23 rebellion. Kinshasa recalled its ambassador; Kenya said it could not restrict political expression.

Ruto and Tshisekedi also committed to cooperation in trade, security, economic development and culture , according to The Star’s account of the post-meeting statement. Ruto said the engagement would be guided by dialogue and mutual respect. The leaders were hosted by Republic of the Congo President Denis Sassou-N’Guesso, with Togo’s Faure Gnassingbé participating as a regional mediator.

The first reports, filed before the summit, described its purpose as expected rather than settled. The Standard and People Daily said the leaders were due to discuss diplomatic ties, regional security, peacebuilding and economic cooperation. People Daily noted that a detailed official agenda had not been publicly released at that point.

TUKO likewise reported before the meeting that the specific agenda was undisclosed, and said the full attendee list had not been officially confirmed when it published. The Standard and People Daily named all four leaders expected to attend. The Star’s later report supplied the outcome those earlier accounts could not: Ruto and Tshisekedi had agreed on practical steps to restore government-to-government ties.

The Star’s earlier report and The Standard both set out the roots of the falling-out. They cited the December 2023 AFC launch in Nairobi and a further deterioration after a gathering of Congolese opposition figures in Nairobi in October 2025. People Daily also identified former DRC president Joseph Kabila among those involved in the later gathering. Kenya’s defence, as reported by The Star, was that it had not participated in the event and could not bar political expression.

The Star offered the clearest account of the economic stakes: eastern DRC uses the Port of Mombasa and the Northern Corridor as a trade route, while Kenyan financial institutions also have business interests in the DRC. That makes the restoration of diplomatic channels relevant beyond the two governments’ political dispute.

The Standard placed the summit within a broader sequence of regional diplomacy, recalling an Oyo meeting in 2022 and a Quadripartite Summit in Luanda in 2023. It also described Gnassingbé as the African Union-appointed mediator in the eastern DRC conflict. The Star’s account said he mediated the talks, while noting that the AU appointed him to the eastern DRC peace process in April 2025.

Ruto’s travel interrupted a Western Kenya development tour. The Kenya Times gave the most detailed itinerary, listing engagements in Busia and Bungoma before his departure and saying the tour’s second phase was scheduled for November 28 to December 1 . People Daily and TUKO reported that he left via Kisumu International Airport; TUKO characterised the departure as secretive, while the other accounts focused on the summit and its diplomatic purpose.

What remains open is how quickly the promised ambassadorial appointments and cooperation commission will be put in place. The reports give no timetable, and the agreement itself does not settle the wider dispute over political activity linked to armed groups. The next test is whether the leaders’ commitment to consultation produces regular contact and changes in how Nairobi and Kinshasa handle those disagreements.

The second phase of Ruto’s Western Kenya tour is scheduled to resume.
