Govt urges cooperative societies to embrace reforms to drive growth

WhatsAppFacebookX

The government has called on cooperative societies across the country to embrace reforms, digitalisation, professional management and innovation to strengthen their contribution to Kenya&#8217;s economic growth and wealth creation.

Principal Secretary (PS) for the State Department for Cooperatives, Patrick Kilemi, said cooperatives must move beyond traditional savings mobilisation and agricultural produce aggregation to become commercially competitive enterprises capable of investing, processing, manufacturing and accessing local and international markets.

Speaking during the Fifth Annual Cabinet Secretary&#8217;s Consultative Forum for Cooperatives and Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) held from November 16 to 20, 2026, at Sawela Lodge in Naivasha, Kilemi said cooperatives were central to the government&#8217;s Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda (BETA).

He noted that the cooperative movement had the membership base, reach and institutional capacity to mobilise domestic resources, attract investment, create employment, promote financial inclusion and connect millions of Kenyans to economic opportunities.

Kilemi challenged cooperative leaders to rethink their business models and embrace modern approaches that would enhance productivity, competitiveness and sustainability. In his view, the success of the cooperative movement should not be measured by the number of registered societies but by the economic value they create and the impact they have on the lives of their members.

Kilemi said significant progress had been made on the Cooperatives Bill, 2024, which is expected to strengthen governance, accountability and supervision within the sector. Preparatory work on the regulations required to support its implementation is also underway.

In the Saccos subsector, the government is advancing proposals for a Central Liquidity Facility and shared services framework aimed at strengthening financial stability, institutional resilience and operational efficiency.

Furthermore, the Kenya Cooperative Transformation Strategy has progressed, with its first draft undergoing stakeholder consultations to provide a long-term roadmap for modernising and improving the competitiveness of cooperative enterprises.

On digital transformation, Kilemi said the implementation of the Cooperative Management Information System was at an advanced stage, adding that the system would improve transparency, accountability, data management, registration and supervision of cooperative societies.

In the agricultural sector, the government continues to position cooperatives at the centre of strategic value chains, including coffee, dairy, livestock and cotton, with emphasis on increasing production, processing, value addition and market access.

Kilemi emphasised that the ongoing reforms were not intended merely to improve the administration of cooperative societies but to fundamentally change how they conduct business and contribute to the national economy.

The PS urged cooperative leaders and members to view the reforms as opportunities to address longstanding institutional weaknesses, strengthen their organisations and unlock new economic opportunities.

He called on cooperatives to invest in modern processing facilities, develop their own brands, establish strategic partnerships and explore international markets to ensure members benefit from greater value addition.

Saccos were also challenged to embrace technology, strengthen their financial systems and expand their role in supporting enterprise development through competitive financial services.

According to Kilemi, professionalisation, digitalisation, good governance, innovation and strategic partnerships would be critical in building strong and sustainable cooperative institutions.

Kilemi reaffirmed the government&#8217;s commitment to creating an enabling policy, legal and institutional environment to support the transformation of the cooperative movement.

He urged cooperative leaders to take ownership of the reform agenda and work closely with the government and development partners to reposition the sector as a major driver of economic growth.

Besides, he noted that the cooperative movement had played a significant role in Kenya&#8217;s economic development for generations but needed to adopt greater ambition, innovation and competitiveness to remain relevant in the changing business environment.

The Principal Secretary called on stakeholders to work collectively in implementing the reforms, stressing that the future of the sector would depend on their willingness to embrace change and move beyond traditional business practices.

Finally, he urged the cooperative movement to actively participate in shaping the country&#8217;s economic future rather than remain spectators to the ongoing transformation.

The forum brought together stakeholders in the cooperative and MSME sectors to deliberate on reforms, challenges and opportunities for strengthening the sectors&#8217; contribution to Kenya&#8217;s economic transformation.
