Kenya Airways Issues Early Warning as Fuel Costs Surge, Earnings Decline

Kenya Airways has issued a formal profit warning to investors, disclosing that its earnings for the financial year ending 31 December 2026 are expected to drop by at least 25% compared to 2025 figures.

The announcement, signed by Board Chairman Kiprono Kittony on 26 November 2026, cited soaring fuel costs linked to the ongoing Middle East conflict and widespread supply chain disruptions as the primary drivers of the anticipated decline.

The warning was issued in line with Kenya&#039;s Capital Markets (Securities) (Public Offers, Listing and Disclosures) Regulations, 2023.

The Board acknowledged that passenger demand across the aviation sector remains robust, but said external pressures have continued to weigh heavily on the airline&#039;s performance.

Fuel costs at Kenya Airways jumped 72% in the first half of 2026, with acting CEO George Kamal stating that fuel now represents up to 50% of total operating costs.

The International Air Transport Association projects jet fuel will average USD 152 (approximately KSh 19,600) per barrel in 2026, nearly 70% above the 2025 average of USD 90 (approximately KSh 11,600).

African carriers face an additional burden, paying around 17% more than the global average owing to limited local refining capacity, transport costs, and taxation.

The Middle East conflict has also forced airlines to reroute flights around affected airspace, stretching journey times and burning more fuel on connections between Africa, Europe, and Asia.

Kenya Airways did attract additional passengers redirected away from Gulf transit hubs, with load factors reaching as high as 99% on select routes, but the sharp rise in operating costs offset much of that revenue gain.

The 2026 warning builds on a troubled recent history. In 2025, the airline posted a net loss of KSh 17.2 billion, reversing a net profit of KSh 5.4 billion recorded in 2024, which had been the carrier&#039;s first annual profit in over a decade.

Total income fell to KSh 161.5 billion in 2025, down from KSh 188.5 billion the previous year.

Much of that decline stemmed from the temporary grounding of three Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner aircraft due to supply chain difficulties, which cut capacity by 18% and reduced passenger numbers by roughly 13%.

The first half of 2026 showed no meaningful recovery. Although revenue grew 9% to KSh 81 billion, the airline still posted a loss after tax of KSh 16.1 billion, compared to KSh 12.2 billion over the same period in 2025.

Finance costs climbed 11.1% to KSh 12.4 billion, the basic loss per share reached KSh 2.94, and negative equity widened to KSh 132.1 billion from KSh 118.3 billion, with total liabilities rising 6% to KSh 315.3 billion.

Looking ahead to 2027, the airline said it would prioritise restoring its full fleet, reducing costs, conserving cash, and completing a capital-raising exercise designed to stabilise its balance sheet and support long-term growth.
