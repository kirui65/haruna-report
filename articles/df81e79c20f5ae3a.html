EU Pays KSh 190k Per Month to Kenyans to Study Master's in Europe: How to Apply

The European Union is offering Kenyan graduates a fully funded opportunity to pursue a master&#039;s degree at universities across Europe, with a monthly stipend of €1,400 (approximately KSh 196,000) for the duration of their studies.

The Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters (EMJM) scholarship programme, which covers tuition fees, health insurance, and contributions towards travel and relocation costs, is among the most comprehensive study-abroad funding options available to Kenyan students.

Applications for the 2027–2029 intake are currently open.

Rather than a single university or programme, Erasmus Mundus is a collection of over 200 joint master&#039;s programmes, each delivered by a consortium of at least three universities across three European countries.

Scholars earn joint or multiple degrees and complete their studies in different countries, gaining international academic exposure.

The monthly €1,400 stipend is paid for every month of study, whether the programme runs for 12, 18, or 24 months.

Over a two-year master&#039;s, that amounts to €33,600 in living support. The package also includes a full waiver of tuition and participation fees, health insurance, and a contribution towards travel and installation costs.

Students should, however, budget separately for visa fees and initial accommodation deposits, as the first stipend instalment may not arrive before those early expenses fall due.

There is no single centralised application. Each consortium runs its own process through its own portal, so applicants must apply directly to the programmes they wish to join.

The starting point is the official Erasmus Mundus catalogue , which lists every funded programme alongside its academic focus, participating universities, and application deadline.

Experts advise shortlisting three to five programmes and submitting multiple applications, as competition is intense.

Deadlines for the 2027 intake vary by programme. Neurasmus, which focuses on neuroscience, closes on December 1, 2026.

EMMIR, covering migration and intercultural relations, has a deadline of December 20, 2026.

GLOBE, focused on biodiversity and conservation, closes on January 4, 2027, while InterMaths, spanning mathematics, physics, and engineering, closes on February 17, 2027.

Applicants must hold a bachelor&#039;s degree or a recognised equivalent by the time of application.

Those who have previously received an Erasmus Mundus scholarship for a master&#039;s or doctoral programme are not eligible to apply again under the same scheme.

Each consortium sets its own academic requirements, including minimum grade thresholds, discipline prerequisites, and English proficiency conditions.

Standard application documents include academic transcripts, degree certificates, a curriculum vitae, a motivation letter, and two or three academic references.

Some programmes additionally request a research proposal, a portfolio, or evidence of relevant work experience.

Kenyan graduates who have previously won the scholarship stress that academic excellence, while helpful, is not the only deciding factor.

One past recipient noted that "you don&#039;t have to be a first-class student to secure scholarships like the Erasmus," pointing instead to a well-tailored motivation letter and credible academic references as the most critical elements.

Starting the application process at least three to four months before the relevant deadline is strongly advised.
