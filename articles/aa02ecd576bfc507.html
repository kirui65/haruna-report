Australia to Block Kenyans, Other Visitors From Shifting to Work, Student Visas in New Law

Australia is set to close a widely used migration loophole that allowed foreign visitors, including Kenyans, to switch to student, work, or other substantive visas without leaving the country.

Under the Migration Amendment (Preventing Further Stay for Visitor Visas) Regulations 2026, the "No Further Stay" condition, formally known as condition 8503, will become mandatory on all Subclass 600 Visitor visas, Subclass 601 Electronic Travel Authorities (ETA), and Subclass 651 eVisitors for applications lodged on or after November 28, 2026.

Previously, the condition was discretionary on many visitor streams and could not be applied at all to the ETA or eVisitor categories.

A person whose visa carries condition 8503 cannot lodge a valid application for most substantive visas while physically in Australia.

The only exception is a protection visa. In nearly all other cases, including those seeking to study, work, or apply for a partner or skilled visa, the applicant will be required to leave Australia and submit their application from outside the country.

Visas granted before November 28, 2026, are not affected, and applications submitted before the cutoff date will be processed under the previous rules.

The Australian government says the aim is to preserve visitor visas for genuine short-stay purposes and to curb what it describes as "visa hopping," where individuals enter as visitors and subsequently apply for successive temporary visas to remain in the country.

The measure forms part of a wider package of migration reforms, including student visa changes introduced in October 2026, all aimed at reducing net overseas migration.

Separately, from November 30, 2026, the Department of Home Affairs will apply a more rigorous, case-by-case assessment to Bridging Visa B applications linked to employer-sponsored visas.

This will cover the Skills in Demand (Subclass 482), Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186), and Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Subclass 494) streams.

The legal threshold remains the same, requiring applicants to demonstrate "substantial reasons" for travelling abroad, but the standard will be enforced more strictly, with shorter travel windows tied to stated purposes and specific dates.

General references to holidays or family visits may no longer be sufficient without supporting documentation. The application fee stands at AUD 575, equivalent to approximately KSh 48,000, following a rise from AUD 190 on July 1, 2026.

For Kenyans intending to visit Australia, the central takeaway is that a visitor visa can no longer serve as a first step toward study or employment in the country.

Those with plans to work or study should apply for the correct visa before departing Kenya.

For those already in Australia on a Bridging Visa A awaiting an employer-sponsored visa decision, leaving the country without a valid Bridging Visa B will cause the visa to lapse and may bar re-entry.

Waivers of condition 8503 remain available but are granted only where compelling and compassionate circumstances arose after the visa was issued and were beyond the applicant&#039;s control.
