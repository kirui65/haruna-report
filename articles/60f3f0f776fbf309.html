Martha Karua declares 2027 presidential bid

WhatsAppFacebookX

People&#8217;s Liberation Party leader Martha Karua has formally entered the 2027 presidential race, anchoring her campaign on economic accountability, healthcare, education and the restoration of constitutional order.

Martha Karua, leader of the People&#8217;s Liberation Party, on Friday declared her candidature for the presidency of the Republic of Kenya in the 2027 General Election, delivering a sweeping policy address in Nairobi that placed women at the centre of her campaign coalition while extending her appeal to every Kenyan household struggling under the weight of a deepening economic and governance crisis.

The declaration, made on Friday, 27 November 2026, was framed not as a moment of personal ambition but as a response to what Karua described as a profound crisis of leadership and a dangerous deficit of public trust — one that she argued was being felt in markets, hospitals, schools and homes across the country.

&#8220;Today, our country is facing a profound crisis of leadership and a deep deficit of public trust,&#8221; she told those gathered. &#8220;This crisis affects every household. It hurts our children, our families, our men and women, and threatens our collective future.&#8221;

Karua opened her address with a direct appeal to women, whom she identified as her primary constituency — not because she intended to exclude others, but because she said she understood, from lived experience, the burdens women carry as caregivers, providers and community anchors. The vision she set out, however, was explicitly national in scope: a government that serves every Kenyan regardless of gender, region or political affiliation.

Her economic platform is built around a central image — a mother at a market stall with a thousand shillings, buying essentials, calculating the fare home and quietly returning the sugar to the shelf. It is, Karua argued, the most honest description of what macroeconomic failure actually looks like at household level, and it is the standard against which she intends to hold herself accountable.

&#8220;Public money must serve the public,&#8221; she said. &#8220;Every shilling must count.&#8221;

On the economy, Karua committed to fair and responsible taxation that does not crush households or stifle enterprise, prompt payment of suppliers and pending bills, a crackdown on corruption and wastage, and a sharp prioritisation of essential services — healthcare, education, infrastructure and food security — over what she characterised as unnecessary government expenditure. For farmers, she cited the specific example of potato growers selling below the cost of production on harvest day, their bargaining power gone, their next planting season in jeopardy. Her government, she said, would work with county governments and farmers&#8217; organisations to improve access to quality inputs, storage, irrigation and reliable markets, while pushing for greater local value addition across agricultural commodities including tea, coffee and horticulture.

For young people, the commitment is to close the gap between training and employment through expanded apprenticeships and structured partnerships between technical institutions and private-sector employers. For small businesses and traders, it is predictable taxation, transparent licensing and a government that actually pays its bills on time.

The transparency pillar of her platform is specific and verifiable. She pledged that if ten million shillings were allocated to build a road in any ward in Kenya, any citizen would be able to establish how much was approved, who received the contract, what was delivered, how much was paid and who signed off on the work — through digital platforms and accessible public noticeboards. The same accountability framework, she said, would apply to hospitals, schools, water projects and every publicly funded programme.

On healthcare, Karua was direct: no parent should take a sick child to a public hospital and return home with a prescription they cannot afford to fill, and no family should lose a loved one because essential medicines are unavailable. She committed to progressively realising universal healthcare free at the point of treatment, backed by adequate budgetary allocations, timely payments to health facilities and fair remuneration for health workers. On education, she committed to universal free public education, progressively extended from pre-primary through to university, with a funded implementation plan and a review of the competency-based curriculum in genuine consultation with teachers and parents.

The human rights section of her declaration was among its most pointed. Karua committed to strengthening the institutional response to domestic violence, sexual abuse and femicide, demanding prompt investigations, evidence handling, survivor protection and accountability for perpetrators. She pledged full implementation of the constitutional two-thirds gender principle in government appointments. And she was unequivocal on security: there would be no tolerance for abductions, extrajudicial killings or enforced disappearances under her administration. Security agencies, she stated plainly, exist to protect citizens, not to terrorise them.

On devolution, she committed to equitable, predictable and timely disbursement of funds to county governments, published transfer schedules, and an active strengthening of the Equalisation Fund to address persistent service inequalities. On the Constitution, she was equally plain — court orders would be obeyed, parliamentary oversight would be respected without interference, and the rule of law would apply equally to everyone, including those occupying the highest offices in the land.

In a move designed to signal that her accountability commitments are immediate rather than aspirational, Karua outlined four specific deliverables for her first 100 days in office: a published statement of national debt and pending bills; a register of stalled national projects showing amounts spent and work outstanding; a funded timetable for settling arrears owed to hospitals and schools; and the launch of project-level expenditure records accessible to ordinary citizens. On the hundredth day, she said, she would report publicly on progress against each commitment, name who was responsible for delays and state when outstanding work would be completed.

Karua has previously served as a Member of Parliament for Gichugu constituency, as Minister for Justice and Constitutional Affairs under President Mwai Kibaki, and as running mate to Raila Odinga in the 2022 presidential election. She resigned from the Ministry of Justice on principle — a decision she referenced in her declaration as formative to her understanding of what genuine public accountability requires.

Her entry into the 2027 presidential race reorders the emerging field and adds a voice with an unusually detailed — and testable — policy platform to what has, until now, been a contest defined more by political realignment than by programmatic substance.

For the mother quietly returning the sugar to the shelf at the market stall, the question Karua posed is direct: does the person asking for your vote manage public money with the same discipline, care and sacrifice that you apply to yours?
