Mary Wahogo urges political leaders to preach peace ahead of 2027 elections

WhatsAppFacebookX

Ambassador Mary Wahogo has called on political leaders, the youth and women to take an active role in promoting peace and preventing violence as Kenya approaches the 2027 General Election campaign period.

She warned that reckless political utterances, ethnic mobilisation and incitement could trigger violence, putting lives at risk and undermining national cohesion.

Amb. Wahogo also urged politicians to exercise restraint in their public statements and avoid language that could fuel tribal animosity, confrontations or other forms of violence.

Speaking at Hennessis Hotel along Forest Road in Nairobi, Wahogo conveyed her condolences to families who have lost loved ones through violence, condemning all forms of violence that threaten the lives and safety of innocent people.

Her remarks came as thousands of women joined the UN Women team in paying tribute to women and children who have lost their lives in violent incidents.

Amb. Wahogo, who serves as Kenya Head of the International Justice, Peace and Anti-Corruption Council under the Euracia Chamber of Commerce, said women and children were particularly vulnerable during periods of instability and often suffered the greatest consequences of political and communal conflicts.

She challenged young people to resist attempts by politicians and other influential individuals to recruit them into violent activities, urging them instead to become champions of peace, national unity and the protection of lives and property.

She said the youth had an important responsibility in safeguarding the country&#8217;s future and should use their energy and influence to encourage peaceful coexistence rather than engage in confrontations driven by political interests.

Amb. Wahogo also called on women across the country to take a leading role in peacebuilding by mobilising communities, promoting dialogue and discouraging violence at the grassroots level.

She observed that women had a special responsibility to speak out against violence because they and their children frequently bear the heaviest burden when conflicts erupt, including losing family members, livelihoods and homes.

Her appeal comes amid growing efforts by national institutions, civil society organisations and international partners to prevent election-related violence ahead of the 2027 General Election.

On November 19, UN Women, in partnership with the Embassy of Ireland and the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, announced an eight-month programme aimed at protecting women and strengthening their participation in electoral peace and security in Mombasa and Kilifi counties. The initiative is being implemented by Human Rights Agenda (HURIA).

The National Cohesion and Integration Commission has also warned that political violence, public mistrust and online incitement pose significant threats to peaceful elections in 2027.

The commission has called for responsible political leadership, stronger early-warning mechanisms and greater involvement of young people in preventing electoral violence.

Separately, women leaders under the Kenyan Women for Peace initiative have committed to strengthening women&#8217;s participation in peacebuilding, dialogue and conflict prevention ahead of the elections.

Amb. Wahogo said Kenya could only safeguard its democratic progress through collective responsibility, urging political leaders, women and young people to put the country&#8217;s stability above partisan interests.

She called for peaceful political competition, respect for human life and responsible leadership, saying no political ambition should come at the expense of innocent lives.

She urged Kenyans to learn from past experiences of violence and work together to ensure that the 2027 electioneering period does not expose families and communities to unnecessary suffering.
