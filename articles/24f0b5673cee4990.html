Null and void: Court declares 2% deduction from SHA claims unconstitutional

The High Court in Vihiga has declared the 2% Health Information Management Service (HIMS) charge levied on healthcare facilities processing claims through the Social Health Authority (SHA) unconstitutional, citing a failure to adequately involve the public before the deduction was introduced.

Justice Reuben Nyakundi delivered the virtual ruling on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, finding that while the law empowers the Digital Health Agency (DHA) to collect fees for services it provides, the government had not demonstrated that Kenyans were specifically consulted on the 2% rate, the maximum deduction ceiling, or the methodology used to arrive at it.

The charge, provided for under Regulation 11(2) and the Third Schedule of the Digital Health (Data Exchange Component) Regulations, 2025, requires healthcare facilities to remit 2% of approved claims, with a cap of KSh 5,000 per claim.

The court acknowledged that Section 48(1)(c) of the Digital Health Act, 2023, grants the DHA authority to charge for its services, but held that this statutory basis does not exempt the government from meeting constitutional obligations when fixing the actual amount.

The provision was found to contravene Articles 10(2)(a) and 201(a) of the Constitution, which require public participation in governance and openness in public finance, as well as Section 5 of the Statutory Instruments Act.

Justice Nyakundi also questioned whether the government had sufficiently explained the rationale behind settling on 2% and how the funds collected relate to the actual costs of running the digital health infrastructure. The court found that evidence of stakeholder engagements and public notices submitted by the government did not demonstrate meaningful engagement on the specific percentage-based deduction.

Despite declaring the charge unconstitutional, the court did not immediately nullify it. It suspended the declaration of invalidity for 120 days, giving the government a window to address the identified defects through a lawful process, thereby avoiding disruption to healthcare facilities and SHA claim processing during that period.

The court rejected the petitioners&#039; request that the government reimburse healthcare providers for past deductions, noting that the fees had been collected under a published regulatory instrument in connection with services actually rendered.

The petition was filed by Dr Magare Gikenyi Benjamin, Eliud Karanja Matindi and Busia Senator Okiya Omtatah, who challenged the legality, implementation and financial accountability of the HIMS utilisation fee. In August 2026, the court had declined to suspend the deductions at the interim stage after the petitioners&#039; application was found to lack a properly filed supporting affidavit.

Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale had previously defended the charge, maintaining it was legally provided for, capped at KSh 5,000 per transaction, and intended to support Kenya&#039;s digital health infrastructure.
