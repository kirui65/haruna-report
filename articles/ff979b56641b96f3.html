Ichaweri vs Othaya: Comparing the Rural Homes of Uhuru Kenyatta and Mwai Kibaki

Two of Kenya&#039;s most prominent former presidents both chose to return to their rural roots after leaving State House, yet a closer look at their respective compounds reveals just how differently each man settled into life after power.

Uhuru Kenyatta&#039;s base at Ichaweri in Gatundu South, Kiambu County , and the late Mwai Kibaki&#039;s ancestral home in Othaya, Nyeri County, have become talking points for Kenyans curious about what presidential retirement actually looks like on the ground.

The historical weight behind each property is hard to ignore. Ichaweri has been a Kenyatta family stronghold since founding president Jomo Kenyatta returned there following his release from colonial detention, and the compound has expanded considerably since those days.

Othaya carries its own significance as Kibaki&#039;s ancestral home and the place where he was eventually laid to rest.

One compound houses a former president who is very much still active in public life. The other is a quieter, more reflective space preserved by a family honouring a late leader&#039;s memory.

The difference in feel between the two properties is striking. Ichaweri is ringed by high perimeter walls fitted with electric fencing, with both private guards and state security personnel controlling all access.

Anyone attempting to film the premises from outside is turned away. The access road leading to the compound was tarmacked after 2022, a practical gesture marking the return of a sitting former head of state.

Othaya feels considerably more understated. A group of TikTok adventurers who rode past the property recently managed to capture footage of a large green and black gate with a distinctive roofed design, neat cabro paving and a guard&#039;s chamber.

Thick vegetation and a green perimeter fence offer the compound its privacy, but the overall impression is far removed from a fortress.

Ichaweri functions as more than just a residence. Uhuru has used the compound to receive diplomats, African Union envoys and delegations connected to peace processes on the continent.

The property also includes the St John the Baptist Chapel, consecrated in December 2023 by Archbishop Philip Anyolo and notable for its woodwork and stained-glass windows.

The interior of the Othaya home remains largely unseen. The TikTokers who filmed the gate did not gain access beyond the perimeter, so tall banana trees rising above the fence and yellow flower bushes along the road are about as much as the public has been able to observe.
