Kipchoge Tells Kenya to "Dream Big" and Chase Africa's Second Marathon Major

WhatsAppFacebookX

Eliud Kipchoge, Kenya's two-time Olympic marathon champion, has called on the country to "dream big" and pursue Africa's second Marathon Major, The Star reported. The Nairobi-based daily said the distance-running great wants the World Marathon Majors, the sport's premier series of city marathons, to stage a race on Kenyan soil.

Kipchoge is the most decorated marathon runner Kenya has produced. Born in Kapsabet in Nandi County and long based at a training camp in Kaptagat in the Rift Valley, he became the first person to cover the marathon distance in under two hours when he ran 1:59:40 at the INEOS 1:59 Challenge in Vienna in 2019, a run staged behind rotating teams of pacemakers that was not eligible for official record purposes. His official world record of 2:01:09, set at the Berlin Marathon in 2022, stood until fellow Kenyan Kelvin Kiptum clocked 2:00:35 at the Chicago Marathon in 2023.

The World Marathon Majors sits at the summit of the city marathon circuit. The series launched in 2006 with five founding races in Boston, London, Berlin, Chicago and New York, and grew to six when Tokyo joined in 2013. The Sydney Marathon became the series' seventh race in 2025, while South Africa's Sanlam Cape Town Marathon has been lined up as Africa's first entry in the fold. A second African slot is now the open question, and it is that prize The Star reported Kipchoge wants Kenya to claim.

The majors are the biggest races in the sport. The New York City Marathon, the largest marathon in the world, regularly records more than 50,000 finishers, while Boston, the oldest annual marathon, first staged in 1897, holds a field of about 30,000 runners who must post qualifying times to enter. Berlin's flat, fast course has been the setting for most of the men's world records of the past two decades. Champions at the majors race for the richest prizes in road running, and the series also crowns a season-long points champion, a title Kenyan runners have taken repeatedly.

Kipchoge's own record in the series stands apart. He has won nine major titles: the Chicago Marathon in 2014, the London Marathon in 2015, 2016, 2018 and 2019, and the Berlin Marathon in 2015, 2017, 2018 and 2022. He also claimed two Olympic titles, winning the marathon in Rio de Janeiro in 2016 in 2:08:44 and defending it at the Tokyo Games in Sapporo in 2021 in 2:08:38. Only two other men in Olympic history, Ethiopia's Abebe Bikila and East Germany's Waldemar Cierpinski, have won back-to-back marathon gold medals.

Kipchoge built his career on the track, winning the 5,000 metres world title in Paris in 2003 at the age of 18, beating Morocco's Hicham El Guerrouj and Ethiopia's Kenenisa Bekele, before taking Olympic bronze in Athens in 2004 and silver in Beijing in 2008. The tradition behind him runs deep. Kenyan men have won three of the last five Olympic marathons, with Sammy Wanjiru taking Beijing in 2008 in 2:06:32 and Kipchoge winning in Rio and Tokyo, while Peres Jepchirchir took the women's Olympic marathon title in Tokyo. Catherine Ndereba, a four-time Boston Marathon winner, led the country's women before them.

Kenya already holds a central place on the global marathon map. The high-altitude training towns of Iten in Elgeyo Marakwet County and Kaptagat draw professional runners from around the world, and the capital stages the annual Nairobi City Marathon on its streets. Kenyan runners have also supplied a steady stream of winners at the biggest city races from London to Boston to New York. No Kenyan race, however, currently sits in the majors series.

Only three men in history have broken 2:02 over the official marathon distance: Kipchoge, Kiptum and Ethiopia's Kenenisa Bekele. Two of the three are Kenyan, and the men's world record has been held by a Kenyan since Kipchoge broke it in Berlin in 2018, passing from his 2:01:39 that day through his 2:01:09 in 2022 to Kiptum's 2:00:35 in Chicago.
