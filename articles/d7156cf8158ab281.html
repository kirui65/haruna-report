Nakuru to offer free veterinary services during animal health field day

WhatsAppFacebookX

Nakuru County Government is set to conduct a free Animal Health Field Day aimed at improving animal health and welfare, preventing the spread of animal diseases, promoting responsible animal ownership and safeguarding public health.

The initiative will be implemented through the provision of essential veterinary services, bringing animal healthcare closer to residents, particularly pet owners and livestock keepers, while promoting preventive care and improving animal productivity.

The exercise, organised in collaboration with the Lions Club of Menengai, will be held at Baraka Estate Grounds in Rhonda Ward, Nakuru West Sub-County, on Thursday this week starting at 8:00 am.

According to Chief Officer for Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries and Veterinary Services, Dr. Michael Kiplangat Cheruiyot, the initiative is part of the county government&#8217;s commitment to improving animal health and ensuring residents have access to essential veterinary services.

“The initiative is part of our commitment to improving animal health and welfare while ensuring that residents have access to essential veterinary services,” said Dr. Cheruiyot.

He stated that by bringing these services closer to the people and offering them free of charge, the county government aims to encourage preventive animal healthcare, reduce the burden of animal diseases and promote responsible animal ownership.

Dr. Cheruiyot explained that during the exercise, free rabies vaccinations will be administered for dogs, cats and donkeys, while deworming services will be offered to dogs, cats, donkeys, sheep and goats.

In addition to that, castration services will also be available for dogs and cats.

The field day will further feature animal husbandry awareness sessions focusing on responsible dog keeping, proper animal care and disease prevention, with residents expected to benefit from information on practices that promote animal welfare and safer communities.

The Chief Officer noted that rabies prevention remained a critical component of animal healthcare, given the disease&#8217;s serious implications for both animal and human health.

He observed that rabies vaccination is an important preventive measure in protecting communities while encouraging residents to take advantage of this opportunity by bringing their animals for vaccination, deworming and other services.

Dr. Cheruiyot stated that the free Animal Health Field Day goal is to build healthier animal populations, protect public health and strengthen livestock productivity, which is an important pillar of household livelihoods and food security.

Rabies is a preventable but potentially fatal viral disease that can be transmitted from infected animals to humans, mainly through bites.

Vaccinating dogs and other susceptible animals is therefore an important strategy in reducing transmission risks and protecting communities.

Regular deworming is equally important in controlling internal parasites that can affect animal growth, health and productivity, while appropriate animal management practices help prevent diseases and improve animal welfare.

Beyond providing immediate veterinary interventions, the initiative highlights the importance of preventive animal healthcare in supporting the county&#8217;s agricultural development agenda.

Healthy livestock contributes to food security, household incomes and economic resilience, making investment in animal health an important aspect of improving the livelihoods of farming communities.

Through its Department of Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries and Veterinary Services, the county seeks to strengthen veterinary service delivery, enhance disease prevention and control, improve livestock productivity and promote responsible animal husbandry practices.

The collaboration with the Lions Club of Menengai also demonstrates the importance of partnerships between government and community organisations in complementing efforts to improve animal welfare and extend essential services to residents.

The County Government has urged residents to turn up with their animals and take advantage of the free services, noting that preventive healthcare and responsible animal ownership are essential in building healthier communities.
