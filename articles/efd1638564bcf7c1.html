Payaza secures Kenyan licence in a market Flutterwave struggled to enter

WhatsAppFacebookX

Payaza Africa Limited announced on X last Thursday that it has &#8220;secured regulatory approval from the Central Bank of Kenya to conduct payment services business in Kenya,&#8221; calling it &#8220;a major milestone&#8221; in its expansion across the continent and adding, &#8220;Kenya, we&#8217;re here. And we&#8217;re just getting started.&#8221;

A report from Condia provided additional details about the announcement, which was lacking in specifics. The new approval follows a remittance license that Payaza has held in Kenya for approximately a year.

This license will enable the company to operate directly in the Kenyan market instead of processing transactions through a sponsor bank. Neither of the accounts is issued by the Central Bank of Kenya.

Kenya&#8217;s central bank does not treat a Nigerian fintech&#8217;s claim to be licensed as self-evident. In July 2022, Governor Patrick Njoroge said publicly that Flutterwave and Chipper Cash were not licensed to operate as payment or remittance providers in Kenya and &#8220;should not be operating,&#8221; despite Flutterwave&#8217;s insistence that it had applied for a licence since 2019.

The dispute escalated well beyond a regulatory disagreement. A Kenyan court later froze more than $40 million held in Flutterwave accounts at three banks, acting on an Assets Recovery Agency request under the country&#8217;s anti-money-laundering law. As recently as mid-2026, Kenya&#8217;s central bank was still on record saying Flutterwave was not a licensed operator in the country.

Paystack&#8217;s experience in the same market was the opposite. The company announced in November 2022 that it had received a Payment Service Provider Authorisation directly from the Central Bank of Kenya, a straightforward approval that let it begin serving Kenyan businesses with no public dispute attached. So a Nigerian fintech clearing Kenya&#8217;s central bank is not, on this market&#8217;s own recent history, a given.

Paystack got through Kenya&#8217;s door without a fight. Flutterwave is still negotiating its way out of one. Where Payaza&#8217;s claim lands on that spectrum is, for now, something only Payaza has said.

Payaza is a Nigerian-founded payments company started in March 2022 by chief executive Seyi Ebenezer alongside co-founders Tochukwu Ekwonna, Philips Akinyele, Tolulope Atomori and Shola Asiru.

It has spent the past two years collecting licences well beyond Nigeria: a Payment Systems Operator licence in Ghana in February 2025, a similar licence in Uganda, a Money Services Business licence in the United States, and a Payment Service Provider licence in Canada as recently as February 2026.

Ebenezer has framed the Kenya move around the country&#8217;s informal economy, telling Condia that Kenya&#8217;s informal sector, &#8220;predominantly driven by MSMEs, accounted for 703,700 of the 782,300 jobs created in the country,&#8221; and that financial infrastructure should be &#8220;built to help them transact more efficiently, reach new markets, and grow.&#8221;

The first thing to watch is whether is whether the Central Bank of Kenya&#8217;s official directory of authorised payment service providers includes Payaza&#8217;s name. This directory is updated every few weeks, and it is this list, rather than a company&#8217;s social media post, that determines the status of payment service providers in Kenya.

The second is whether Flutterwave&#8217;s multi-year Kenya licensing fight ever resolves in the company&#8217;s favour, since its outcome is the clearest sign of how hard this specific regulator makes Nigerian fintechs work for an approval Payaza says it already has.
