Kenya Set To Sign Landmark Trade Agreement With Nigeria To Boost Technology Businesses

WhatsAppFacebookX

Kenya is among five African countries expected to enter into a major trade agreement with Nigeria aimed at expanding cross-border operations for technology companies and strengthening digital trade across the continent. The proposed deal will allow Nigerian technology businesses to access new markets, establish operations and serve customers in participating countries, including Kenya.

The agreement will bring together six African economies: Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, Rwanda, South Africa and Egypt. Its signing is expected later this month during a meeting of the Council of Ministers of the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) in Zimbabwe.

Nigeria&#8217;s Minister of Trade and Investment, Jumoke Oduwole, disclosed the development in an interview with Reuters , explaining that the five partner countries were selected following an assessment of markets where Nigerian digital businesses have significant growth potential.

According to Oduwole, the Nigerian government has already engaged regulatory authorities from the participating countries to identify barriers to cross-border business operations and establish a framework for greater cooperation. The consultations involved central banks, trade ministries and other institutions responsible for regulating digital businesses and facilitating trade. &#8220;We held a regulatory roundtable sandbox with those regulators, from their central banks to their ministries of trade to all the enablers,&#8221; Oduwole told Reuters .

The proposed agreement is expected to create opportunities for businesses operating in financial technology (fintech), digital payments, e-commerce and other technology-driven sectors. By improving regulatory cooperation and facilitating market access, the initiative seeks to make it easier for companies to expand their services beyond their domestic markets.

For Nigerian technology companies, Kenya and the other participating countries could provide access to new customers, business partnerships and investment opportunities. Similarly, Kenyan businesses could benefit from improved access to Nigeria&#8217;s large consumer market, potentially expanding opportunities for digital service providers, fintech firms and e-commerce platforms. The initiative could also strengthen commercial relationships between the participating countries by encouraging technology investment, promoting innovation and reducing some of the administrative and regulatory obstacles associated with operating across African borders.

The development comes against the backdrop of Kenya&#8217;s growing influence in Africa&#8217;s digital economy, particularly in e-commerce, financial technology and digital services. The United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD) has ranked Kenya as Africa&#8217;s third-largest e-commerce market, highlighting the country&#8217;s expanding online business ecosystem and increasing adoption of digital commerce.

Additionally, World Bank data identifies Kenya as the second-largest exporter of digital services in Sub-Saharan Africa. This position reflects the country&#8217;s growing capacity to provide technology-enabled services to international markets, supported by its established digital infrastructure, mobile financial services ecosystem and expanding technology sector. Greater access to Nigeria&#8217;s market could further strengthen Kenya&#8217;s digital businesses by creating opportunities for regional expansion, cross-border partnerships and increased revenue generation.

Despite the potential benefits, several details of the proposed agreement remain unclear. Oduwole did not disclose the specific provisions of the arrangement or explain whether it would immediately alter existing licensing, taxation or compliance requirements for foreign technology companies operating in the participating countries.

It is also yet to be established how the agreement will address differences in national regulatory frameworks, data protection requirements, digital payment regulations and other policies governing technology businesses across the six markets. The extent to which the proposed framework will simplify company registration, licensing and cross-border financial transactions will therefore become clearer once the agreement is formally signed and its implementation arrangements are announced.

Nevertheless, Nigeria says the initiative is part of a broader strategy to help African businesses expand across the continent rather than remain restricted to their domestic markets. &#8220;We&#8217;ve been pushing to support our businesses to do more on the continent. That&#8217;s the way we&#8217;re going to grow our economies,&#8221; Oduwole said.

The proposed agreement forms part of wider efforts under the African Continental Free Trade Area to deepen economic integration, increase intra-African trade and create a more favourable environment for businesses operating across national borders. Through the AfCFTA framework, African governments are seeking to reduce trade barriers, improve market access and encourage the movement of goods and services across the continent. Expanding cooperation in the digital economy is increasingly important as technology companies, online marketplaces and financial service providers play a growing role in economic development.

For Kenya, participation in the proposed arrangement could strengthen its position as a regional technology and digital services hub while providing local businesses with greater opportunities to compete in Africa&#8217;s largest consumer markets. If effectively implemented, the agreement could mark an important step towards building a more connected African digital economy, allowing technology companies to scale their operations, reach new customers and contribute to economic growth across the continent.
