Kicosca Games Promote National Unity

WhatsAppFacebookX

The 13th Edition of the Kenya Inter-Counties Sports and Cultural Association (KICOSCA) Games has continued to demonstrate the power of sports and cultural exchange in bringing Kenyans together, with public servants from across the 47 counties using the national event to strengthen friendships, celebrate diversity and promote national cohesion.

Hosted by West Pokot County, the week-long sporting extravaganza has brought together thousands of participants, officials and spectators in an atmosphere of friendly competition, cultural interaction and national unity.

Beyond the pursuit of trophies and medals, the games have provided a platform for workers from different counties, communities and cultural backgrounds to interact, appreciate one another and reinforce the shared identity of being Kenyan.

Bungoma Governor Kenneth Lusaka, who was the guest of the day&#8217;s activities, said KICOSCA had remained an important national platform for promoting unity since its establishment following the introduction of devolution.

Lusaka noted that although counties have different languages, traditions and cultural identities, sporting activities provide an opportunity for Kenyans to interact beyond their geographical and cultural differences.

He said the primary objective of the games should not be winning trophies but building lasting relationships and strengthening the bonds that hold the country together.

“The main aim is not necessarily to win, but to bond. These games bring people together from different counties, speaking different languages and practising different cultures. It is one way of fighting tribalism and promoting national cohesion,” said Lusaka.

He encouraged participants to use the opportunity to establish friendships and appreciate the country&#8217;s diversity, adding that they should return to their respective counties having made new friends and developed a stronger sense of national belonging.

“When you leave here, you should have made friends from other counties and understood that despite our differences, Kenya is one,” he added.

His sentiments were echoed by West Pokot Governor Simon Kachapin, who is hosting the 13th edition in his capacity as the patron of the whole event.

Kachapin expressed excitement over his county&#8217;s performance across the various sporting disciplines, saying the home teams had demonstrated determination and competitive spirit as they sought to defend the county&#8217;s sporting reputation.

He noted that hosting the national games was an opportunity for West Pokot to showcase its hospitality, cultural heritage and potential while creating economic opportunities for local residents.

The Governor said the influx of participants and visitors had stimulated business activities, particularly in hospitality, accommodation, transport, food vending and other small-scale enterprises.

He observed that such national events provide counties with an opportunity to open up their economies, attract visitors and promote destinations that may otherwise receive limited national attention.

Kachapin said the benefits of the tournament extended beyond the sporting arenas, as interactions among participants were helping strengthen relationships between county governments and communities across the country.

The Governor today hosted his Bungoma counterpart, Lusaka, at his official residence before the two leaders joined spectators at Talau and Kapenguria Boys Senior School to follow the football and volleyball encounters.

The visit highlighted the spirit of friendship and cooperation that the tournament seeks to promote among county governments and their leadership.

Meanwhile, competition has intensified as teams enter the decisive knockout stages, with hosts West Pokot among the counties making impressive progress.

Football attracted considerable attention on Day Four after West Pokot defeated Kilifi 3-0 at Makutano Stadium to secure a place in the men&#8217;s quarter-finals. Bungoma also progressed after edging Machakos 5-4 in a penalty shootout following a tightly contested encounter.

The men&#8217;s football quarter-final fixtures scheduled for Friday will see West Pokot face Bungoma, Kisumu take on Siaya, Kakamega meet Nyandarua, while Nairobi will battle Embu at different venues.

In women&#8217;s volleyball, West Pokot maintained its impressive run with a 3-1 victory over Nyandarua at Kapenguria Boys Senior School. The hosts won the opening two sets 25-18 and 25-18 before Nyandarua recovered to take the third set 28-26. West Pokot, however, regained control to win the fourth set 25-20 and advance to the quarter-finals.

Other teams that secured places in the last eight included Kakamega, Murang&#8217;a, Uasin Gishu, Bungoma and Nandi, following victories over their respective opponents.

Basketball has also provided entertaining encounters, with Nairobi narrowly defeating Kakamega 68-67 in the men&#8217;s category, while Kiambu registered a 70-26 victory over Uasin Gishu. Turkana also impressed with a 78-38 win against Narok.

In the women&#8217;s category, Vihiga defeated Kiambu 34-28, while Turkana overcame Meru 43-19. The women&#8217;s basketball quarter-finals will feature Kisumu against Narok, Nairobi against Machakos, Kisii against Turkana and Uasin Gishu against Vihiga.

Away from the competitive arenas, the cultural activities have offered counties an opportunity to showcase their distinct identities through traditional dances, music and other cultural presentations. These displays have provided an important reminder that the country&#8217;s diversity remains a source of strength and shared heritage.

The organisation of the games has also received positive feedback, with activities proceeding smoothly and scheduled competitions taking place without reported security incidents or major disruptions.

The venues have been adequately distributed across the host area, with participants able to access the different sporting facilities despite some distances between competition centres. The arrangements have enabled teams to participate in their respective disciplines while spectators move between venues to support their counties.

As the tournament progresses towards its conclusion, West Pokot is enjoying the opportunity to host the country and demonstrate its capacity to organise national events.

Ultimately, the significance of KICOSCA extends beyond the medals and championship titles. By bringing together public servants from all corners of Kenya, the games reinforce the spirit of devolution, encourage inter-county cooperation and promote a national identity that transcends ethnic and geographical boundaries.

For West Pokot, the event is not only a sporting milestone but also an opportunity to demonstrate that sports, culture and hospitality can serve as powerful instruments for economic development and national unity.
