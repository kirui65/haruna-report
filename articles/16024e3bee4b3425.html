George Ruto&#039;s prized matatu , Baba Yaga, is out of service after a collision that left it and a rival vehicle badly damaged.

Newly obtained footage, however, is raising uncomfortable questions about the events leading up to the crash.

The accident took place on the night of Friday, November 13, along Route 125, the Nairobi-Kiserian corridor that Baba Yaga operates under Ruto&#039;s 98 SACCO PSV line.

The John Wick-themed matatu, one of the more eye-catching vehicles in the fleet, reportedly ploughed into the rear of another well-known matatu on the route, Detroit.

A source familiar with the incident told TUKO.co.ke that the two vehicles had been competing on the road when the collision took place.

Images and footage captured at the scene told a stark story. Baba Yaga&#039;s front end bore the full force of the impact, with its bumper, grille and body panels caved in.

Debris and dislodged interior fittings lay scattered around the vehicle. Detroit did not escape unscathed either, with its rear section sustaining significant damage. No fatalities or official injury figures were reported in the initial coverage of the incident.

The crew running Baba Yaga under the Raptors banner addressed the situation on the matatu&#039;s official Instagram account the following morning, Saturday, November 14.

TUKO.co.ke has obtained video footage that purportedly captures events in the moments leading up to the collision.

In the clip, two Route 125 matatus appear to engage in a rat race, the kind of competitive speed duel that has long been a fixture of Nairobi&#039;s nganya culture.

The vehicles are seen driving in the wrong lane of a two-way road, overtaking other motorists at considerable speed.

What stands out beyond the driving itself is the behaviour on board. Some occupants are visible hanging out of the doors and windows, while others can be seen swaying as the matatus accelerate.

The footage does not capture the moment of impact itself, and while it appears to show risky behaviour shortly before the crash, it does not independently confirm the precise cause of the collision.

The video does, however, put a spotlight on the culture of rat racing on Route 125, and the safety risks it creates not just for passengers but for other road users as well.

Baba Yaga is among the more distinctive matatus associated with George and has built a following among fans of Nairobi&#039;s vibrant PSV scene.

Its crew&#039;s vow to return suggests repairs are already being planned, but the footage circulating online may yet attract attention from road safety authorities.
