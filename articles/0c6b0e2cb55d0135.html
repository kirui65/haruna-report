"I helped build it": Ruto breaks ground on KSh 32b plant set to create 10k jobs

Bamburi Cement broke ground on a new clinker production facility in Matuga, Kwale County , in a KSh 32 billion (USD 250 million) investment that will significantly expand Kenya&#039;s local cement manufacturing capacity.

President William Ruto officiated the ceremony Tuesday, November 24, alongside Cabinet Secretaries Hassan Joho and Salim Mvurya , Senate Speaker Amason Kingi, Kwale Governor Fatuma Achani, Bamburi Cement Board Chairman Dr John Simba, and Amsons Group Managing Director Edha Nahdi.

Once fully operational, the facility will lift Bamburi&#039;s annual clinker output from 1 million to 2.6 million tonnes, while cement production capacity is forecast to grow from 1.8 million to 4 million tonnes per year.

The project is expected to generate more than 10,000 direct and indirect jobs , with employment opportunities spanning logistics, quarrying and equipment maintenance.

Nahdi framed the investment as a long-term commitment by Amsons Group to Kenya&#039;s economy and the expansion of local manufacturing.

Dr Simba urged that the surrounding communities must share directly in the project&#039;s benefits, citing the Mwachome Village relocation programme as an example. Under that initiative, households displaced by the development have received support including financial literacy training to help them manage resettlement resources.

Governor Achani called for stronger participation by Matuga residents and local businesses in the construction and operational phases of the project, arguing that the community should be at the centre of the economic activity the plant will generate.

Bamburi signed an engineering, procurement and construction contract with China&#039;s Sinoma CBMI in December 2025 to build the plant, which is designed to reduce Kenya&#039;s reliance on imported clinker.

Event production firm Wave 360 Africa managed the full groundbreaking ceremony in Matuga, overseeing infrastructure, production, branding, protocol and on-ground logistics.

Managing Director Zedd Mukoya said the investment should open doors for young people in Kwale County.

The Matuga ceremony is the latest in a series of major infrastructure launches presided over by the President in recent months.

On September 30, Ruto broke ground on the KSh 2.2 trillion (USD 16 billion) Dangote East Africa Petroleum Refinery in Lamu, a facility designed to process up to 700,000 barrels of crude oil daily and expected to create more than 60,000 jobs.

That event was preceded by days of protests from residents raising concerns over compensation for land.

Four weeks before the Matuga event, on October 27, Ruto also officiated the groundbreaking of Devki Group&#039;s KSh 70 billion clinker and cement plant in Kitui.
