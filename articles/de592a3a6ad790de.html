IIHF ‘Learn to Play’ Program Lands in Nairobi to Fuel Kenya’s Ice Hockey Dreams

WhatsAppFacebookX

The International Ice Hockey Federation has brought its weeklong &#8220;Learn to Play&#8221; coaching seminar to Nairobi’s Panari ice rink, uniting 18 instructors from Europe and Africa to strengthen grassroots hockey in Kenya and accelerate the sport’s growth in non-traditional markets .

Marko Valenčič, an IIHF development and growth instructor for Europe and Africa, said the primary focus of the workshop is equipping coaches rather than directly training players .

&#8220;For us, it&#8217;s more working with the coaches, teaching them, and then they teach the kids,&#8221; Valenčič said .

The IIHF currently has 84 full and associate members worldwide .

While neighboring countries like Tanzania are not yet members, Kenya’s active affiliation and strong working relationship with the global body made Nairobi an ideal host .

Valenčič noted that the program carries a dual objective: preparing visiting participants to replicate the curriculum in their home countries, while raising the profile of ice hockey in Kenya .

Ultimately, the federation hopes the increased visibility will attract more youth to the sport and help the Kenya Ice Hockey Federation secure a full-size, regulation ice rink .

For visiting coaches from traditional hockey regions, Nairobi offers a striking contrast to European facilities .

Toms, a representative from the Latvian Ice Hockey Federation, described the Kenyan capital as a &#8220;very special place&#8221; and an unusual destination for the sport .

He said the weeklong exchange proves that ice hockey does not require a deep-rooted history to thrive .

&#8220;Even in such a country where there&#8217;s not a very big tradition for ice hockey, we can still make the game grow and attract new people to the game,&#8221; Toms said, adding that he hopes the program sparks a lasting love for hockey in Kenya .

Tamara, a representative from the Serbian Ice Hockey Association who has played for 10 years and helped establish Serbia&#8217;s Division 3A women&#8217;s national team, admitted she was initially surprised to learn Kenya had an ice hockey scene .

She pointed out the unique logistical hurdles at Panari, including a square rink without rounded corners, unfamiliar equipment cages, and a noticeably small Zamboni .

Despite lacking European infrastructure, Tamara said the local coaches and players impressed her on the ice .

&#8220;Even though you have these conditions as you have, I still saw a lot of potential in even the coaches,&#8221; she said . &#8220;You have to start somewhere, so I think it&#8217;s good.&#8221;

For Kenyan coaches on the ground, the IIHF’s hands-on guidance provides a critical blueprint for grassroots growth .

Abdul, a coach at Panari working with the Kenya Ice Hockey Federation, said the program has refined how local instructors communicate with young players and structure their sessions .

&#8220;It mainly helps on how well we are talking to the kids, how well we are giving them information, and how to engage them so that they love the game, and to give them a safe environment,&#8221; Abdul said .

Looking ahead, Abdul views the initiative as a stepping stone for both himself and the next generation of Kenyan athletes .

H is personal goal is to develop into an international-level coach—and to see local talent reach the sport&#8217;s highest stage .

&#8220;Seeing Kenyan players get drafted into the NHL,&#8221; Abdul said. &#8220;That&#8217;s my biggest dream.&#8221;
