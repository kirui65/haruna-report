"Move as fast": Payaza buys small businesses' platform to compete in Kenya

Payaza Africa has acquired Seesail Technologies Ltd, a Ghana-founded commerce platform serving small businesses, through its Shopaza product days after the Lagos-based payments company formally entered Kenya.

Financial terms of the transaction were not disclosed. The full Seesail team moves across to Shopaza, and Seesail co-founder and CEO Victor Blavo takes up the role of General Manager, Africa.

The acquisition did not come out of the blue. Seesail&#039;s merchants had already been processing payments through Payaza&#039;s infrastructure, giving Payaza&#039;s leadership an early vantage point on the team&#039;s performance before any ownership conversation began.

Payaza CEO Seyi Ebenezer described the deal as a conviction bet on a team that had achieved real traction with limited resources.

Payaza said every Seesail function joins Shopaza with its own budget from day one and the operational freedom to run independently, under guidance from the CEO.

The combined entity is designed to extend Shopaza&#039;s commerce infrastructure into access to finance and broader distribution for small and medium-sized enterprises across Africa.

Blavo built Seesail around a conviction shaped by his own family&#039;s experience. After his mother attempted to revive a restaurant business she had operated in Yaba, Lagos, following the family&#039;s relocation to Ghana, she found that affordable tools and financing were simply unavailable.

That gap became the founding premise of Seesail.

Before bootstrapping Seesail, Blavo accumulated experience across Nigerian, Ghanaian and international firms, including Chipper Cash.

As reported earlier on TUKO.co.ke, Payaza received authorisation from the Central Bank of Kenya on November 17, 2026, to operate as a payment service provider, landing in one of the continent&#039;s most active digital payments markets.

Founded in Lagos in 2022, the company now operates across 31 countries spanning Africa, North America, the United Kingdom, the United Arab Emirates and Oceania.

Kenya&#039;s payments landscape offers significant scale. Mobile money transactions reached KSh 8.7 trillion in 2024, a figure equivalent to more than half of national GDP.

Payaza&#039;s local focus centres on cross-border transfer solutions, merchant acquiring infrastructure and e-commerce tools for merchants and exporters.

The Seesail acquisition broadens the SME toolkit Payaza can deploy across Kenya, Ghana and other markets as it competes with regional players including Paystack, Cellulant and Moniepoint.
