Canada Raises Minimum Salary Rates for Foreign Workers Job Offers From January 2027

Canada has updated the wage data used to set minimum salaries for temporary foreign workers, following the annual Job Bank refresh published by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) on 18 November 2026.

The national average of occupational median hourly wages rose 3.7% to $35.39 (approximately KSh 5,500) from $34.13 (approximately KSh 5,300), tightening the floor that employers must meet when offering positions to workers under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program (TFWP).

Under the programme, employers applying for a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) must generally offer at least the new prevailing wage in the Job Bank defined as the higher of the Job Bank median for the specific occupation and economic region, or whatever the employer already pays comparable local staff in the same role.

Of 497 occupations with data available in both years, 414, or 83.3%, recorded higher median wages. Wages declined in 53 occupations and held steady in 30 others.

The typical change across occupations was 2.3%, roughly half the pace recorded in the previous year&#039;s update. Around one in five occupation-region pairings posted gains of 6% or more, compared with more than one in three a year earlier.

Regionally, every province and territory saw its average median rise.

Among the larger provinces, British Columbia recorded the highest average at $37.08 per hour (up 3.1%), followed by Alberta at $36.57 (up 2.5%) and Ontario at $35.61 (up 3.6%).

The territories posted the country&#039;s highest figures, with Nunavut reaching $46.77 and the Northwest Territories $45.32.

For individual workers, the national average carries limited practical weight. The figure that matters is the median tied to the precise occupation code and work location.

As an illustration, cooks in Toronto saw their median move from $18.00 to $18.56 per hour, while the same occupation in Ottawa rose from $19.00 to $20.00.

Early childhood educators in the Lower Mainland-Southwest region of British Columbia carry a median of $24.00, while banking and investment managers range from $47.12 in Winnipeg to $53.85 in the Hamilton-Niagara Peninsula.

Employers with existing TFW staff must review payroll against the updated figures and raise any wages that fall below the new medians by 1 January 2027.

Workers already earning at or above the new median require no adjustment. A protective floor also applies: wages cannot be reduced below the amount specified in the positive LMIA, even where a Job Bank median has decreased.

For new hires, the updated prevailing wage applies from the first day of employment, regardless of figures cited in earlier LMIA applications.

Offering below the prevailing wage triggers automatic LMIA refusal, with no appeal route for wage non-compliance.

The November 2026 update did not alter the thresholds separating the high-wage from the low-wage stream processing.

Those thresholds, last revised on 17 July 2026, stand at $36.92 per hour in Ontario, $37.50 in Alberta, and $38.40 in British Columbia.

Workers can verify their applicable median by looking up their National Occupational Classification code and region on the ESDC Job Bank.
