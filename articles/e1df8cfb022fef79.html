Central Papua HIV Treatment Reaches Just 21.5% of Diagnosed Residents

WhatsAppFacebookX

Central Papua health data through September 2026 show only 4,209 of 19,564 diagnosed residents living with HIV receive daily antiretroviral therapy.

Nearly four in five people diagnosed with HIV in Indonesia's Central Papua province are not receiving antiretroviral medication, provincial health officials disclosed on Wednesday, exposing a stark gap between case detection and sustained treatment across the remote highlands.

Speaking in the provincial capital of Nabire, Isak Waine, head of communicable disease prevention and control at the Central Papua Provincial Health Office, said program records through September 2026 counted 19,564 living residents who know their HIV-positive status. Only 4,209 of them, or 21.5% based on the province's published figures, were recorded as receiving antiretroviral therapy, leaving 15,355 diagnosed residents outside active treatment.

Provincial surveillance data show that drop-offs continue after patients begin medication. Among the 4,209 residents on antiretroviral drugs, 1,360 had undergone viral load testing through September 2026, representing 32.3% of treated patients, and 1,191 had achieved viral suppression.

Those figures carry a sharp split in clinical outcomes. While 87.6% of tested patients suppressed the virus to levels low enough to block sexual transmission under the Undetectable equals Untransmittable principle, they account for only 28.3% of people on medication and just 6.1% of all 19,564 diagnosed residents.

Central Papua's 21.5% treatment rate sits more than 45 percentage points below Indonesia's national treatment coverage. A June 2026 rapid risk assessment by Indonesia's Ministry of Health estimated 557,000 people living with HIV nationwide, with 71%, or 275,942 people, knowing their status and 75% of newly identified cases in the first half of 2026 starting antiretroviral therapy.

In an earlier June 2025 briefing on its 2030 HIV elimination target, the Ministry of Health reported that 67% of diagnosed residents nationwide were on antiretroviral treatment and 55% had reached viral suppression. Officials there classified Central Papua among 11 priority provinces holding 76% of Indonesia's cases and warned that, unlike elsewhere in Indonesia, transmission in Papua had reached the general population at an estimated 2.3% prevalence.

The national briefing also found that while HIV elsewhere in Indonesia remains concentrated in key populations such as sex workers and people who inject drugs, transmission in Papua has already spread into the general population. That broader epidemiological profile makes treatment interruptions far harder to contain across remote regencies.

Ministry of Health figures show why Central Papua remains a national priority. Indonesia ranks 14th globally in total people living with HIV and ninth in new infections, and infectious disease director Ina Agustina warned in June 2025 that rising sexually transmitted infections, including 23,347 syphilis cases and 10,506 gonorrhoea cases recorded the previous year, open further pathways for HIV transmission among working-age adults aged 25 to 49.

Across Central Papua, provincial authorities say strengthening service delivery inside hospitals and community health centres known as puskesmas is critical to keeping patients in care. Waine said the drive forms part of Governor Meki Nawipa and Deputy Governor Deinas Geley's health agenda covering HIV, tuberculosis, and malaria, while urging residents not to let fear or social stigma block early therapy. Waine also emphasised that the health office's goal extends beyond case detection: "Our target is not only finding new cases, but ensuring every person diagnosed with HIV receives ARV treatment, adheres to medication, and achieves suppressed viral load."

Waine said the health office is prioritising closer accompaniment immediately after diagnosis so residents start medication without delay. "Do not be afraid, do not be ashamed, and do not give up. HIV is not the end of life," he said, adding that regular therapy and viral load testing allow people living with HIV to "live healthy, productive lives, work, marry, and build families."

Because Central Papua's September report covers only diagnosed residents who are alive and registered in health records, it does not measure undiagnosed infections across highland districts. Closing the gap toward Indonesia's 2030 elimination goals will depend on expanding viral-load laboratory reach and sustaining monthly drug supply across regional hospitals and community health centres.
