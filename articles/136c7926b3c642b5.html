Ruto begins second Western tour phase with four-day project itinerary

WhatsAppFacebookX

The four-day itinerary includes projects across four counties, while the timing and amount of promised Mumias cane bonuses remain unspecified.

President William Ruto is due to begin the second phase of his Western Kenya tour in Busia on Saturday, November 28 , with a four-day programme of road, electricity, market and other project events running through December 1. The Star is the only outlet in the material provided to report the tour, so there is no broader outlet-to-outlet account to compare.

The second phase returns Ruto to Busia, Bungoma, Kakamega and Vihiga, after an earlier four-day visit to the same counties. According to the programme reported by The Star, Saturday’s engagements begin in Butula, Busia County, with the Butula-Nambale-Amukura Road and last-mile electricity connections for Butula, Matayos and Budalang’i. The itinerary also includes a market in Butula, then events in Webuye East, Bungoma, and Navakholo and Ikolomani in Kakamega.

There is a difference in how The Star describes the Saturday market stop. Its November 27 report calls it the Butula Economic Stimulus Programme Market; the November 28 report says Ruto will commission Bumala Modern Market. The reports do not explain whether these are two names for the same facility or separate events. The earlier report also lists a foundation stone for hostels at Butula Kenya Medical Training College, which does not appear in the later Saturday schedule.

The Star’s November 27 account sets out the wider route: Kakamega stops in Malava and Khwisero on Sunday, November 29; Mumias and Lugari in Kakamega, Kimaeti and Miyanga in Bungoma, and Emuhaya and Luanda in Vihiga on Monday, November 30; then Hamisi in Vihiga and Lurambi and Kakamega town on Tuesday, December 1.

The return also carries a commitment on sugarcane bonuses. After the first phase, Ruto said he would come back to pay bonuses to farmers supplying Mumias Sugar Company, with farmers linked to Chemilil, Muhoroni, Miwani and Sony factories expected to follow. The Star reports that the first phase ended with the launch of Sh1.2 billion in sugarcane bonuses and cheques worth Sh195 million for farmers supplying Nzoia Sugar Company. It does not give a date or amount for the Mumias payments.

The first phase included road launches, electricity connections and market events across the region. The Star’s longer account also records Ruto’s political attacks on former President Uhuru Kenyatta, former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua and opposition leaders, alongside his claim that every community deserves an equal share of development. He announced plans for 6,000 affordable housing units in Busia and pledged to make Malaba a smart city.

Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi said Ruto had fulfilled a Kenya Kwanza commitment to build 1,000 kilometres of roads in Western Kenya . The report gives no project-by-project tally or independent assessment of that claim.

The scheduled events are plans, not confirmed completions. The reports do not establish which projects are finished, their costs or delivery timelines. They also do not specify when Mumias farmers will receive bonuses, how much they will receive, or whether the other named factory suppliers have confirmed payment arrangements.

Coverage remains thin: both reports supplied here are from The Star. That limits what can be said about how other newsrooms assess the tour, its delivery claims or its political significance.
