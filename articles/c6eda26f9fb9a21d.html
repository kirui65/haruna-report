Kenya Loses Sh450 Billion Annually to Road Crashes, Chirchir Reveals

WhatsAppFacebookX

Road crashes are draining about Sh450 billion from Kenya's economy every year, roughly 5 to 8 percent of the country's Gross Domestic Product, as highway deaths continue to rise in 2026.

Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir disclosed the financial toll on Thursday, November 26, during the opening of the Africa Road Safety Conference 2026 in Nairobi. Official figures presented at the forum show fatalities reached 4,613 by November 16, 2026, up from 4,238 over the same period last year. The upward trend follows a jump in annual deaths from 4,748 in 2024 to 5,009 in 2025.

To reverse the losses, the government is rolling out a Sh30 billion Road Safety Action Plan. The strategy targets a 30 percent reduction in road fatalities by 2028 through legal reforms, automated policing, and infrastructure upgrades.

"The road safety challenge and experience in Kenya reflects the challenges associated with rapid urbanization, population growth and increasing motorization," Chirchir said.

A central pillar of the response is an ongoing review of the Traffic Act, Cap 403. Authorities aim to close existing legal loopholes and tighten accountability to match changing transport patterns and new vehicle technologies.

On the roads, the ministry is partnering with the National Police Service to expand technology-driven enforcement. Police are deploying speed cameras, automatic number plate recognition, and digital offense management systems to curb speeding, drunk driving, overloading, reckless driving, and unroadworthy vehicles.

Vehicle inspection centers are also shifting to automated testing and digital records to keep defective vehicles off the road.

For the public transport sector, where operator compliance currently stands at about 76 percent, more than 4,000 drivers and management staff have completed safety training over the past three months. The broader action plan also introduces specific reforms for public transport and motorcycle safety.

To address dangerous road designs, the government is setting up a road assessment program. The initiative will map high-risk corridors, prioritize known accident black spots, and require safety audits during infrastructure planning and funding.

Chirchir urged stronger political leadership, steady financing, and wider use of technology to shield vulnerable road users, including pedestrians, cyclists, and public transport passengers. Globally, road crashes claim roughly 1.2 million lives each year and leave millions more injured.

"Road crashes are not inevitable events. They are preventable tragedies. Every life lost is one too many," he said.

The two-day conference, running from November 26 to 27 at the KCB Leadership Centre, has drawn policymakers, government officials, development partners, and industry leaders. Delegates are examining ways to speed up the African Road Safety Action Plan for 2021–2030, which seeks to cut road deaths and injuries across the continent by at least 50 percent by 2030.
