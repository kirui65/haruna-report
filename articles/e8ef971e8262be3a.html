Kenya Plans Automated Vehicle Checks as Road Deaths Climb

WhatsAppFacebookX

Transport CS Davis Chirchir unveiled automated vehicle checks and a Sh30 billion safety plan as NTSA targets 300,000 licences amid rising road fatalities.

Roads and Transport Cabinet Secretary Davis Chirchir announced plans on November 26 to overhaul Kenyan motor vehicle inspections with automated testing centres, digital records, and expanded camera enforcement as road deaths rose nearly nine percent year-on-year.

Speaking at the Africa Road Safety Conference 2026 in Nairobi, Chirchir tied the reforms to Kenya's five-year Road Safety Action Plan for 2024 to 2028, valued at Sh30 billion. The government aims to cut fatalities by more than 30 percent over five years and halve road deaths and serious injuries by 2030.

The policy push coincided with an immediate festive-season crackdown by the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA). At Mlolongo in Machakos County on the same day, NTSA Director-General Nashon Odhiambo said the regulator plans to suspend at least 300,000 individual driving licences within a week over unpaid or uncontested instant fines.

Official figures presented by the Ministry of Roads and Transport show Kenya's road safety trajectory moving in the wrong direction. Annual fatalities increased from 4,748 in 2024 to 5,009 in 2025, a rise of 5.5 percent across 11,638 recorded crashes that also left 19,896 people with serious injuries.

By November 16, 2026, police and transport authorities had logged 4,613 road deaths, up from 4,238 during the same period in 2025. That represents 375 additional fatalities, or an 8.8 percent jump, ahead of December holiday travel when highway volumes typically surge.

Vulnerable road users, including pedestrians, motorcyclists, and passengers, accounted for 73 percent of crash victims in 2025, according to NTSA data. Chirchir estimated that road crashes drain approximately Sh450 billion from Kenya's economy each year, equivalent to between 5 percent and 8 percent of gross domestic product.

Under the ministry's Safe System framework, officials are pursuing a review of the Traffic Act, Cap. 403, alongside the Kenya Road Assessment Programme (KenRAP) to rank high-risk corridors and target accident blackspots during infrastructure planning. Over the past three months, the ministry also trained more than 4,000 public transport managers and drivers.

On the enforcement side, Chirchir said authorities are deploying speed cameras, automatic number plate recognition, and digital offence management with the National Police Service. NTSA surveillance already flagged 1.7 million vehicle and driver infractions over the preceding 30 days, with 740,000 speeding offences making up roughly 44 percent of all logged violations.

NTSA has already suspended 15,000 licences over the past month for speeding and reckless driving. Drivers affected by the new 300,000-licence suspension wave will have to pass a mandatory driving retest before returning to the road. Separately, the regulator plans to suspend inspection certificates for 25,000 commercial and public service vehicles caught speeding despite mandatory speed limiters, citing suspected device tampering.

Whether automated inspections will reach private motorists smoothly depends on resolving legal resistance that stalled earlier rules this year. In July and September 2026, the Senate formally annulled three statutory instruments gazetted by the ministry in February: the Traffic (Motor Vehicle Inspection) Rules, 2026, the Traffic (School Transport) Rules, 2026, and the NTSA (Operation of Commercial Vehicles) Regulations, 2026.

Those rules would have mandated annual inspections for private vehicles older than four years at fees between Sh1,000 and Sh2,000, alongside GPS telematics on school buses. Senators cited inadequate public participation and short compliance windows under the Statutory Instruments Act, while private school operators warned that scarce inspection bays in remote counties would penalise rural transport providers.

Funding remains the second decisive constraint. In his conference remarks, Chirchir acknowledged that achieving the five-year fatality reduction target depends on securing adequate financing and coordinated implementation across agencies. With the Senate blocking unconsulted rules and existing speed governors bypassed on thousands of vehicles, the ministry faces both a legislative test in redrafting Cap. 403 regulations and an operational test on highways.

The immediate test begins over the next seven days as NTSA executes its licence suspensions while Parliament awaits revised Traffic Act proposals from the ministry.
