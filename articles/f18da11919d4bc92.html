The Sacco Societies Regulatory Authority (SASRA) has published its Sacco Supervision Annual Report 2025 , revealing the full ranking of Kenya&#039;s largest deposit-taking SACCOs by total assets and exposing a sharp concentration of wealth among the top institutions.

Mwalimu National DT has held on to its position as Kenya&#039;s largest SACCO, recording total assets of KSh 76.31 billion in 2025, up from KSh 68.89 billion the previous year.

Stima DT, however, has mounted a serious challenge, expanding its asset base from KSh 66.51 billion to KSh 75.27 billion, leaving only a KSh 1 billion gap between the two giants.

An interesting distinction separates the two: Stima DT carries the larger loan book at KSh 57.49 billion, compared to Mwalimu National&#039;s KSh 56.98 billion, suggesting Stima is the more active lender despite sitting in second place overall.

Kenya National Police DT occupies third position with KSh 66.40 billion in assets, while Harambee DT holds fourth place at KSh 41.29 billion.

Below Harambee, a gap of approximately KSh 6.7 billion separates the top four from the rest of the rankings, with Tower DT entering at fifth place with KSh 34.56 billion.

Unaitas (6th) and Imarisha (7th) both recorded growth, posting assets of KSh 29.60 billion and KSh 28.14 billion, respectively. Afya DT, ranked eighth, was the only institution in the top 10 to record a decline, with assets slipping from KSh 22.79 billion to KSh 21.95 billion.

United Nations DT (9th) and Hazina DT (10th) rounded out the top ten at KSh 19.67 billion and KSh 18.71 billion.

Positions 11 through 20 form a tightly contested band, with all institutions holding assets between KSh 14 billion and KSh 18 billion. Mentor DT leads this group at KSh 18.47 billion (11th), followed by Gusii Mwalimu at KSh 18.40 billion (12th) and Transnation DT at KSh 17.95 billion (13th). Winas (14th), Apstar DT (15th), Invest and Grow (16th), Imarika (17th), and Bandari DT (18th) range from KSh 16.90 billion down to KSh 15.07 billion.

Qona DT (19th) and Boresha DT (20th) close out the list at KSh 14.80 billion and KSh 14.70 billion, respectively, meaning any SACCO outside the top 20 would need to surpass that KSh 14.70 billion threshold to break into the elite tier.

The 2025 data signals that Kenya&#039;s cooperative sector is increasingly dominated by its largest players, with the top 20 SACCOs collectively holding well above half a trillion shillings in combined assets, raising the bar for smaller institutions hoping to compete at the highest level.
