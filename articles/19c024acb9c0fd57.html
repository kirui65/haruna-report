UK Opens Seasonal Work Visa for 41,000 Farm Jobs With KSh 2,270 Wages Per Hour

The United Kingdom has reopened its Seasonal Worker visa programme, allowing foreign nationals, including Kenyans, to take up temporary agricultural roles lasting as long as six months.

The scheme targets labour shortages on British farms during peak harvesting periods and the Christmas poultry season, and it operates exclusively through a network of government-approved scheme operators.

Two sectors qualify under the programme. Horticulture positions span a wide range of produce, from soft fruits such as strawberries and orchard crops to mushrooms, cut flowers, and tree nurseries.

The poultry route covers twelve specific roles, including butcher, poultry processor, poultry catcher, and food operative, though it is restricted to workers arriving between October 2 and December 31 each year, with applications closing no later than 15 November.

For the 2026 and 2027 seasons, the annual quota stands at 41,000 slots for horticulture and 1,900 for poultry.

The scheme has been extended through 2029 to give growers and operators greater planning certainty.

Applicants must pay a £340 (approximately KSh 61,200) visa fee and demonstrate access to at least £1,270 (approximately KSh 227,700) in savings held for a minimum of 28 consecutive days unless their sponsor certifies maintenance.

No immigration health surcharge applies given the visa&#039;s duration. Workers are bound to the job listed in their certificate of sponsorship, cannot access public funds, and may not bring family members.

The financial appeal for Kenyan workers is considerable.

The UK national minimum wage for agricultural workers aged 21 and over is £12.71 per hour, equivalent to roughly KSh 2,270, compared with Kenya&#039;s general labour minimum wage of approximately KSh 15,200 per month.

Farm-provided accommodation is typically deducted at around £77 (KSh 13,800) per week.

The Kenyan government has promoted the route through the Mkulima Majuu programme, with the State Department for Diaspora Affairs partnering HOPS Labour Solutions and the Youth Enterprise Development Fund to recruit from agricultural colleges and polytechnics, including Bukura Agricultural College and Sigalagala National Polytechnic.

The scheme&#039;s structure poses documented risks. Because workers are tied to a single sponsoring operator and their accommodation depends on that employer, labour rights researchers have consistently flagged the power imbalance as a vulnerability.

A 2026 academic study concluded that the Seasonal Worker visa&#039;s design "compounded by the effects of the cessation of EU Freedom of Movement led to heightened risks of labour exploitation" and noted that workers had paid fees to secure placements.

Advocacy groups warn that recruitment-related debts, combined with the single-employer tie, heighten the risk of modern slavery.

Upfront costs remain a practical barrier. Beyond the visa fee and savings requirement, applicants must fund their own airfare.

Kenyans seeking a placement should approach only licensed scheme operators, which issues the certificate of sponsorship required to submit an online application. Any agent promising a visa without that document should be treated as a fraud risk.
