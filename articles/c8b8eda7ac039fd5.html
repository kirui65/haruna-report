Uasin Gishu youth agri-preneurs embrace technology to transform smallholder farming

WhatsAppFacebookX

More than 100 young agricultural entrepreneurs in Uasin Gishu County are being equipped with modern farming technologies, innovations and management practices in an initiative aimed at transforming smallholder agriculture, closing extension service gaps and strengthening food security.

The youth, trained under the National Agricultural Value Chain Development Project (NAVCDP), are expected to become a critical link between farmers and agricultural service providers by taking modern farming knowledge and solutions to the grassroots.

With one agri-preneurs deployed in each of the county&#8217;s 110 sub-locations, the initiative seeks to ensure farmers, particularly those in remote areas, have easier access to agricultural information, quality farm inputs, mechanization services, financial products and reliable markets.

The five-day training, which brought together the agri-preneurs, focused on technologies, innovations and improved field management practices across five priority value chains: coffee, avocado, dairy, potatoes and poultry.

County NAVCDP Project Coordinator Cosmas Langat said the programme was designed not only to improve agricultural productivity but also to empower young people to establish sustainable enterprises while providing essential services to farmers.

Langat noted that the agri-preneurs model, which has been rolled out nationally over the past year, was gaining momentum, with Uasin Gishu deploying 110 trained youth to serve farmers across all its sub-locations.

&#8220;We want, apart from extending agricultural technologies to farmers, to also impart the agri-preneurs with knowledge and skills that will enable them to link farmers with input suppliers, markets and other service providers while earning from the programme,&#8221; he said.

He explained that the initiative was intended to move farmers beyond subsistence production by exposing them to improved farming methods and commercial opportunities that could increase household incomes.

According to Langat, the youth would play a significant role in identifying farmers&#8217; needs, providing technical guidance and facilitating connections with various players in the agricultural value chain.

The programme also presents an opportunity for young people to tap into agribusiness as a source of employment and income rather than viewing agriculture solely as a traditional farming activity.

Francis Ndegwa, who supports data and digital agriculture under NAVCDP, said the training was part of a broader capacity-building programme designed to equip the agri-preneurs with practical knowledge that they would subsequently pass on to farmers.

Ndegwa said the trained teams were expected to cascade the knowledge to the next level of agri-preneurs, who would then engage farmers across the five supported value chains.

He expressed optimism that the adoption of the technologies and recommended practices would translate into improved farm management, increased production, higher incomes and enhanced food security.

The training also seeks to strengthen the delivery of agricultural extension services at a time when government capacity to employ and deploy sufficient extension personnel has become increasingly constrained.

Ndegwa observed that agri-preneurs were emerging as an alternative channel for reaching farmers with timely technical information and practical solutions.

&#8220;Extension services have gone down, and that is why agri-preneurs are coming in to fill that gap,&#8221; he said.

Beyond offering technical advice, the youth are expected to establish linkages between farmers and providers of agricultural inputs, mechanization, markets, financing and insurance services.

Through these partnerships, farmers will be able to access essential services more conveniently, while the agri-preneurs will generate income through commissions and other business opportunities arising from the services they facilitate.

The model is also expected to promote digital agriculture through farmer registration, profiling, data collection and the use of digital advisory platforms to improve decision-making at the farm level.

By connecting smallholder farmers to input suppliers, financial institutions, SACCOs, aggregators and produce off-takers, the agri-preneurs will help strengthen the entire agricultural value chain and create opportunities for more organised commercial farming.

The initiative comes at a time when the agricultural sector continues to face challenges including limited access to extension services, rising production costs, climate-related risks and difficulties in accessing reliable markets.

Uasin Gishu County Director of Agriculture and Agribusiness, Reuben Seroney challenged the agri-preneurs to take advantage of the knowledge and skills acquired during the training to reach as many farmers as possible and help transform agricultural production.

Seroney urged the youth to be proactive in translating the training into practical interventions at the grassroots, noting that their role would be critical in supporting farmers to adopt modern farming technologies and improve productivity.

He said strengthening agricultural production and food security remained a key priority under the Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda (BETA), which identifies agriculture as one of its key pillars.

Seroney encouraged the agri-preneurs to embrace their responsibility as frontline agricultural service providers by ensuring farmers benefit from the innovations, technologies and management practices introduced through the programme.

Through the deployment of youth agri-preneurs, NAVCDP seeks to address some of these challenges while creating a new generation of agricultural service providers capable of sustaining their businesses through the services they offer.

For Uasin Gishu, a county whose economy is largely anchored on agriculture, the success of the programme could have far-reaching implications for both rural livelihoods and the wider food production system.

With 110 agri-preneurs serving as the project&#8217;s grassroots link, the expectation is that improved farming knowledge, technology adoption and stronger market linkages will help farmers produce more, earn better returns and contribute to the country&#8217;s food security.
