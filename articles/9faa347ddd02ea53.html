Health experts converge in Makueni to learn on maternal health

WhatsAppFacebookX

At least 18 health experts gathered at the Mother and Child Hospital within Makueni County Referral Hospital (MCRH) to learn about Simulation Based Training (SBT), a model that enhances the skills of health workers providing maternal health in health facilities.

The experts present included those from the Ministry of Health, the Global Finance Facility (GFF), the Child and Adolescent Health team, the World Bank, Jacaranda Health, N360, the Lwala Community Alliance and the Beginnings Fund.

The one-day intensive training was conducted at MCRH in Wote Town, Makueni Sub-county, on Thursday.

The training team at the Mother and Child Hospital shared their experience of implementing the safer Births Bundle of Care (SBBC), where they focused on how health workers use simulation tools and regular drills to practise response to emergencies during childbirth and in the first minutes of a newborn&#8217;s life.

Addressing the media, the Ministry of Health (MoH) Maternal Health Lead Dr Loise Nyanjau said that they had come to learn about SBT, a model that enhances the skills for health workers in attending to pregnant mothers and newborn babies in our health facilities across the county.

Consequently, Dr Nyanjau said that it was imperative to scale up the model to all other health facilities in the country so that they can improve the knowledge and skill level of health workers in a bid to save pregnant mothers with their unborn babies.

“Today we are privileged to be here in Makueni County to learn about simulation-based training, which is a model that enhances the skill of our health workers so they can deliver quality services in the health facilities,” said Dr. Nyanjau.

“So, we are here as a team and have learned a lot. We have seen how health workers at MCRH do practice drills that enhance retention of their skills. We need to scale up simulation model training in the country so that we improve the knowledge and the skill level of health workers to enable them to save our mothers and newborns as they get services at the health facilities in the country,” stated Dr Nyanjau, who is also leading the experts to the country.

On his part, Makueni County Governor Mutula Kilonzo Jr said that the hospital has equipment that health workers can use in detecting the kind of problem to address in pregnant mothers and their unborn babies.

In this regard, Mutula underscored the need for the same technologies and equipment to be taken to far-flung counties like Mandera, Garissa and all other counties in the country, with a view to reducing maternal deaths in Kenya.

He further disclosed that his government took an initiative to take doctors for training in Manyara in the neighbouring Tanzania to acquire skills so that they get the knowledge to operate the equipment to save mothers and their unborn babies.

“When I travelled to Cape Town in South Africa, I met a development partner that had the latest technology. It came with equipment that can help health workers to know what they can do quickly so that they can save the life of a child,” said Mutula.

“The current statistics show that Kenya&#8217;s maternal mortality ratio stands at 355 deaths per 100000 live births in our health facilities. This education will help reduce many diseases or things that many do not know about, like cerebral paralysis,” stated the governor.

Mutula reiterated that his administration’s policy is to ensure that pregnant mothers coming to the hospital after nine months should go home happy and in good health together with their newborns.
