Visa has named former Safaricom executive Esther Masese Waititu as its Senior Vice President and Group Country Manager for Southern and Eastern Africa.

She has been handed responsibility for the payments company&#039;s footprint across 24 markets in the region. The announcement was made on November 19, 2026.

Waititu stepped down from Safaricom on July 31, 2026, with her resignation formally taking effect on August 1, 2026.

At Safaricom, she had served as Chief Financial Services Officer from February 2023, overseeing M-Pesa&#039;s payments, credit, savings, investments, and insurance divisions.

Working from Johannesburg, Waititu will engage clients, partners, governments, and internal Visa teams to advance digital payments across Southern and Eastern Africa.

Visa described the region as among the world&#039;s most dynamic and fast-growing payment markets.

Her appointment follows more than two decades of experience in African banking and financial services.

Before Safaricom, she served as Director of Corporate Banking at KCB Bank Group between 2021 and February 2023.

Prior to that, she spent a decade at Standard Bank Group and Stanbic Bank from 2011 to 2021, holding positions including Head of Client Coverage, Executive for Strategy Development, and Regional Head of Transactional Products and Services for East Africa.

Earlier roles included stints at Standard Chartered Bank and Commercial Bank of Africa, which subsequently merged into NCBA Group.

She holds a Master of Business Administration from the University of Liverpool and a Bachelor of Business Administration from the University of Eastern Africa, Baraton.

The appointment coincides with an active period of investment by Visa on the continent. The company opened its first African data centre in Johannesburg in July 2025, backed by a reported R1 billion investment over three years, supporting digital wallets, e-commerce, and card-based transactions.

Visa also plans to open additional offices in Angola, Uganda, and Mauritius before the end of 2026, adding to existing operations in South Africa , Kenya, Ethiopia, and Tanzania.

The company has further launched Visa Destinations in South Africa and expanded the Visa Accept product in Kenya, targeting small businesses and merchants.

Waititu&#039;s background in mobile money and corporate banking positions her directly within Visa&#039;s broader strategy of deepening ties with mobile money operators, fintechs, and financial institutions across Africa.

In other news, Stanbic Bank Kenya appointed Michael Mutiga as its new Chief Executive Officer, effective August 1, 2026.

Mutiga joined the bank from Safaricom, where he served as Chief Business Development and Strategy Officer, bringing more than 20 years of experience across banking, telecommunications and digital financial services.

His career includes over 15 years at Citi, where he held senior corporate banking and corporate finance positions in Nairobi and Johannesburg, as well as an earlier role at Barclays.
