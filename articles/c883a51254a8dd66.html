The ex-Homa Bay MCA who testified in Obado case then died mysteriously

WhatsAppFacebookX

A portrait of former Kanyadoto MCA in Homa Bay, Lawrence Obonyo Owuor, during his burial ceremony in 2024.

He was among the witnesses whose testimony helped shape the prosecution’s case in the murder of Sharon Otieno and her unborn baby.

But former Kanyadoto MCA in Homa Bay, Lawrence Obonyo Owuor, alias Mulla, would not live to see the trial reach its conclusion, dying months after appearing in court in a case that continues to attract public attention.

Years after serving as a MCA, he became the 28th prosecution witness in the murder trial arising from the killing of the Rongo University student and her unborn child.

Mr Owuor testified in the case against former Migori Governor Okoth Obado, his personal assistant Michael Oyamo and former county clerk Caspal Obiero. His evidence described how he became involved in efforts to address Sharon’s grievances during her pregnancy.

The official record of the 2013 General Election lists Owuor as the elected MCA for Kanyadoto Ward in Homa Bay County. Running on a Labour Party of Kenya (LPK) ticket, he won 1,956 votes (38.6 percent), of valid votes cast.

By September 2018, when Sharon was abducted and killed, Mr Owuor had left his MCA position.

He was arrested during investigations and detained for 14 days. Prosecutors told the court he had been mentioned by other suspects and witnesses and could assist detectives. His detention was an investigative measure.

Testifying in July 2023, Mr Owuor told the High Court that he first met Sharon at a hotel in Homa Bay town on July 20, 2018.

A portrait of former Kanyadoto MCA in Homa Bay, Lawrence Obonyo Owuor, during his burial ceremony in 2024.

She approached him while he was having tea, introduced herself and sought financial assistance, saying she knew him because he had served as an MCA.

He said he met her again the following day and gave her Sh1,500. Sharon asked him to recommend a journalist who could publicise her situation and draw attention to her grievances with Obado, whom she said had impregnated her.

Owuor arranged a meeting between Sharon and a journalist at Tourist Hotel in Homa Bay on July 25, 2018. The journalist testified under protection using the pseudonym XYZ. He told the court Sharon described her relationship with the governor, her pregnancy and financial difficulties, and shared photographs, WhatsApp exchanges and audio recordings.

Mr Owuor said he became an intermediary in efforts to address the dispute. He told the court that Sharon sought a house, a car and money for upkeep, and that he relayed her requests to the governor.

According to his testimony, Obado proposed buying a plot and building Sharon a house estimated to cost between Sh3 million and Sh4 million. Owuor said Sharon accepted, provided the house was properly furnished.

He also described subsequent meetings and the movement of money intended for Sharon’s support. The court heard that Obado admitted using Mr Owuor as an intermediary to channel funds to Sharon, in addition to his personal assistant. The former governor acknowledged that an arrangement to acquire a plot and build a three-bedroom house had not been fulfilled before Sharon’s death.

Owuor’s evidence formed part of the prosecution’s wider case, which included the journalist’s testimony, forensic examinations and phone records. His account helped explain how Sharon’s concerns reached people around the former governor in the weeks before she was killed.

After completing his testimony, Mr Owuor returned to Homa Bay, as he was not under any witness protection as XYZ. In May 2024, he collapsed in Homa Bay town and was taken to Homa Bay County Referral Hospital, where he was pronounced dead on arrival, according to his family. He was 60.

His sudden death reportedly shocked relatives, who said he had appeared well before collapsing.
