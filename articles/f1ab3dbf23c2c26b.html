Kenya and Switzerland deepen bilateral climate partnership at COP 31

WhatsAppFacebookX

Environment, Climate Change and Forestry Cabinet Secretary, Dr. Deborah Barasa has reaffirmed Kenya’s commitment to operationalizing the landmark Kenya–Switzerland Article 6 Implementing Agreement during high-level bilateral engagements at the Swiss Delegation Office on the sidelines of COP 31.

Following the Agreement’s signing in Bern in May 2025 and its entry into force in June 2026, both nations are moving decisively from policy to implementation to unlock high-integrity carbon markets that drive climate action and economic growth.

This strategic partnership is projected to deliver 5 million tonnes of CO₂ equivalent in mitigation outcomes while mobilizing USD 520 million in direct investments, generating USD 200 million in carbon revenues alongside USD 21.2 million in Designated National Authority (DNA) fees, and creating approximately 115,000 green jobs.

Further, momentum on the ground is already building, with eight projects registered across electric mobility, biogas, solar water pumping, and clean cooking.

Notably, four of these initiatives have already received Letters of Approval from Kenya’s DNA, including the recent issuance of a revised Letter of Approval for Roam Electric in October 2026, underscoring the Government’s commitment to facilitating implementation within its existing legal framework.

During the engagements, Dr. Barasa emphasized regulatory predictability and investor confidence, pointing to the Government’s formal communication to Switzerland on 6 th , November 2026 that confirmed Kenya’s authorization pathway.

“Under the Climate Change Act (Cap. 387A) and the Carbon Markets Regulations, 2024, Kenya remains committed to authorizing eligible projects, applying corresponding adjustments, and recognizing the international transfer and use of verified Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs),” the CS reaffirmed.

Furthermore, she noted that the mitigation volumes for activities with Letters of Approval have already been factored into Kenya’s carbon budget under the Kenya Guide for Strategic Engagement in Carbon Markets (2026).

While both countries issue their own independent authorizations under their respective domestic laws, Kenya has proposed establishing a Joint Technical Working Group—bringing together representatives from Switzerland, NEMA, the Climate Change Directorate, and the Ministry’s Legal Unit, to swiftly address any emerging technical or regulatory challenges.

Alongside this, Dr. Barasa called for expedited Swiss reviews and authorizations for projects that have already fulfilled Kenya’s domestic regulatory requirements to fast-track financial closure and investment mobilization.

Meanwhile, the Ministry remains steadfast in working closely with Switzerland to champion transparent, high-integrity carbon markets that deliver tangible climate resilience, green investments, and sustainable development for all Kenyans.
