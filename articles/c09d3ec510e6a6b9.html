Nzoia Farmers Benefit from Sugar Mill Revival, KSh 195m Bonus Payout

Sugarcane farmers in Bungoma County are reaping the rewards of a revived Nzoia Sugar Company, with President William Ruto on Monday handing out bonus cheques worth KSh195 million to growers supplying the factory.

The event, held at the Nzoia Sugar Company grounds in Kanduyi Constituency, marked a visible milestone in the government&#039;s push to breathe new life into Kenya&#039;s struggling sugar industry.

The Bungoma payment is part of a broader KSh1.2 billion bonus distribution earmarked for farmers across the Western sugar belt, with payouts to Mumias, Chemelil, Muhoroni, Miwani and Sony sugar factory farmers set to follow.

President Ruto credited the Rai Group, the private investor that took over operations under a leasing arrangement, with rescuing a factory he said had been on the verge of collapse just three years ago.

Managing Director Tejveer Rai confirmed that the company had paid KSh1.6 billion to farmers during the year, with an additional KSh150 million channelled to cane harvesters. The firm also remitted KSh688 million in taxes to the national government and Bungoma County, alongside KSh639 million in lease and concession fees.

Ruto was clear that the leasing deal does not transfer ownership of either the factory or its nucleus estate, stressing that both assets remain the property of the people of Bungoma.

For Ferdinand Mahanu, a farmer from Tembelela Village in Mukembe Location, sugarcane growing has anchored his family&#039;s livelihood for more than 20 years, paying school fees, improving their home and covering daily needs.

He recalled how delayed payments and earnings settled in percentages under the old management had dented farmer morale and squeezed household incomes.

The arrival of improved cane varieties and consistent payment schedules has renewed his confidence in the crop. Mahanu also pointed to a ripple effect beyond the farm gate, noting that the upturn in agricultural activity is generating work for young people and women in harvesting, haulage and other parts of the supply chain.

On the broader picture, the President said national sugar output had climbed from 790,000 metric tonnes the previous year and was on track to reach 900,000 metric tonnes in 2025. The government&#039;s longer-term target stands at 1.2 million metric tonnes, the threshold it considers sufficient for the country to meet its own sugar needs.
