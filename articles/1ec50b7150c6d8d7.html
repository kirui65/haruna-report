Gachagua’s green wave: How DCP is reshaping Kenya’s political map ahead of 2027

WhatsAppFacebookX

From Gilgil to Laikipia, a steady stream of legislators, county assemblies and grassroots leaders is rewriting Kenya&#8217;s political landscape — and Rigathi Gachagua is betting it all on March.

Gilgil Member of Parliament Martha Wangari crossed the floor to the Democracy for the Citizens Party on Friday, joining a growing exodus of legislators abandoning the ruling United Democratic Alliance as former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua&#8217;s opposition movement accelerates its march towards the August 2027 general election with a confidence that is becoming increasingly difficult for the political establishment to dismiss.

Wangari, a member of the National Assembly&#8217;s Speaker&#8217;s Panel and one of the more recognisable faces in the Mount Kenya parliamentary bloc, formalised her defection at Gachagua&#8217;s Wamunyoro residence in a ceremony that drew considerable attention — not least because she was joined by Naivasha MP Jayne Kihara and Kipipiri MP Wanjiku Muhia, two more legislators adding their weight to what DCP is calling a green wave sweeping across central Kenya and beyond.

&#8220;The people are supreme,&#8221; Gachagua said, framing Wangari&#8217;s move not as a personal political calculation but as the execution of a popular mandate. &#8220;She has listened to them and acted on their wishes.&#8221; He assured her of free and fair nominations ahead of party primaries scheduled for March next year, a promise he has now repeated to every defector who has crossed to DCP — and there have been many.

The numbers are beginning to tell a story. DCP now counts 26 Members of Parliament among its ranks, a tally that would have seemed fanciful when Gachagua was impeached in October 2024 and left office in circumstances that many of his allies described as a politically engineered removal. That the same man is now hosting legislators, county assembly delegations and thousands of grassroots leaders at his private residence, week after week, represents one of the more remarkable political rehabilitations in recent Kenyan history.

Friday&#8217;s events at Wamunyoro went beyond the parliamentary defections. The entire Nyandarua County Assembly formally joined DCP, giving Gachagua a complete sweep of that county&#8217;s devolved legislative arm. More than 1,000 grassroots leaders attended to present Kiharu parliamentary aspirant Mark Njung&#8217;e, who joined other candidates seeking DCP tickets for 2027. The entire Jubilee leadership structure from Kiambu County, led by chairman George Maara, also defected — a symbolically charged move given Jubilee&#8217;s historical dominance in the region and its current association with former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang&#8217;i, whom Gachagua identified as a figure with whom DCP maintains a cordial relationship, particularly on the question of identifying a joint presidential candidate.

That detail deserves scrutiny. Gachagua was careful to note that while DCP and Jubilee had agreed on cooperation at the presidential level, they would compete independently in all other positions — a tactical arrangement designed to maximise voter turnout and what he called &#8220;vote protection.&#8221; It is the language of a politician who has studied the architecture of Kenyan electoral politics carefully and understands that coalition-building at the top means little without grassroots mobilisation at the bottom.

The aspirants arriving at Wamunyoro reflect that understanding. Pastor Victor Mwangi Kanyari, who has settled on a DCP ticket for the Kasarani parliamentary seat, was welcomed alongside John Lemuna, a Laikipia North parliamentary aspirant who arrived accompanied by a ward-level MCA candidate from Segera. These are not headline figures. They are the foot soldiers of any serious electoral machine, and their presence signals that DCP&#8217;s recruitment is operating not merely at the level of optics, but at the granular level where elections are actually won and lost.

The political context surrounding all of this is significant. President William Ruto, who shared power with Gachagua for two years before orchestrating his removal, now faces a resurgent former ally who has positioned himself as the authentic voice of the Mount Kenya community and the broader opposition. The relationship between the two men has deteriorated publicly and bitterly. Ruto has accused Gachagua of extortion. Gachagua has accused Ruto of purchasing crowds and running a government without genuine popular support. The mutual antagonism has moved well beyond political rivalry into something that resembles a personal reckoning, and both men appear to understand that August 2027 will be its final arbitration.

Gachagua&#8217;s warnings to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission against vote manipulation — issued during the same week as the Wamunyoro events — are consistent with a campaign strategy that anticipates a contested electoral environment and seeks to place accountability markers early. By raising the spectre of rigging before the campaign has formally begun, he frames any future electoral dispute in terms favourable to his narrative.

Whether the green wave translates into actual votes remains the central question. Kenya&#8217;s political history is littered with movements that commanded impressive pre-election momentum and fell short when it mattered. The March party primaries will be the first real test of DCP&#8217;s organisational depth, and Gachagua&#8217;s repeated promise of free and fair nominations will face its most credible examination then. If the primaries are chaotic, exclusionary or perceived as predetermined, the coalition he is building could fracture before it reaches the general election.

For now, however, the momentum is undeniable. Legislators, county assemblies, grassroots networks and parliamentary aspirants from Gilgil to Laikipia are making the same calculation: that the political future, at least in this particular corner of Kenya, is green. Whether that calculation proves correct will define not only Gachagua&#8217;s legacy, but the shape of Kenya&#8217;s sixth administration.

The count is growing, the primaries are approaching, and the man at the centre of it all is receiving visitors at Wamunyoro — patient, methodical, and showing no sign of slowing down.
