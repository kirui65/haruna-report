IEBC flags 94 parties over nomination rules, but leaves election door open

WhatsAppFacebookX

The IEBC has asked the flagged parties to amend their rules, but says the first review does not automatically bar them from the 2027 polls.

Ninety-four of 105 political parties that submitted nomination rules to the IEBC failed its first compliance review ahead of the 2027 General Election. Only 11 passed. The commission says the other parties can amend their rules and resubmit them; the finding does not automatically bar them from the election.

The IEBC reviewed rules submitted by parties intending to take part in the August 10, 2027 polls. Kenyans.co.ke reported that submissions were due by November 6, 2026, under timelines set in Gazette notices. The commission assessed party election boards, candidate eligibility and nomination procedures, alongside party lists, internal dispute resolution, electoral conduct and safeguards against malpractice.

The parties named in both reports include the United Democratic Alliance (UDA), Orange Democratic Movement (ODM), Maendeleo Chap Chap, KANU and Third Way Alliance. Capital FM also named Azimio la Umoja One Kenya Coalition Party, the Democratic Party of Kenya and Democratic Action Party-Kenya among those needing amendments. Kenyans.co.ke reported that Chama Cha Kazi, the Equitable Party, Rigathi Gachagua’s Democracy for the Citizens Party (DCP) , David Maraga’s United Green Movement and Tujibebe Wakenya Party were also flagged.

The 11 parties the IEBC found compliant include Wiper Patriotic Front, Kenya Moja Movement Party, Jubilee, Safina, the Mwananchi Party, Liberal Democratic Party, Grand Dream Development Alliance, Democratic National Alliance Party, Chama ya Mapatano of Kenya, Vision for Development Alliance and Forward Kenya Party. Capital FM calls one of them the “Grand Dream Development Party”; Kenyans.co.ke gives its name as “Grand Dream Development Alliance.” The reports do not resolve that discrepancy.

Kenyans.co.ke gives the total denominator: 105 parties submitted rules, of which 94 were asked to amend and 11 passed. Capital FM also reports 94 failures and 11 compliant parties but does not state how many parties submitted rules.

The reports differ on when the commission announced the findings. Kenyans.co.ke dates its account to Friday, November 20, and says the statement was issued that day. Capital FM says the statement was issued on Thursday, without giving a date in its text, and published its account the following morning. The two reports do not explain the difference.

Their lists of flagged parties also vary. Capital FM names Linda Mwananchi, while Kenyans.co.ke names DCP, United Green Movement and Tujibebe Wakenya Party, among others. Neither report presents a complete list of all 94, so the differences may reflect selection rather than conflicting accounts of the commission’s full findings.

The IEBC says it communicated specific deficiencies to affected parties and asked them to correct their rules within the applicable timelines. It also says nomination procedures must be transparent, fair and inclusive, with meaningful participation for women, youth, people with disabilities and other special-interest groups.

What remains open is the resubmission deadline, which neither report gives, and whether the flagged parties will make the required changes in time for nomination activities. The commission’s first review is an early check on party readiness, not a final decision on who can contest. Only two outlets in this coverage reported the review, so the accounts offer a limited view of the full list and follow-up process.
