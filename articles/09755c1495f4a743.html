Nyeri takes mental health beyond hospitals, bringing research and community action together

WhatsAppFacebookX

Five-year partnership between Aga Khan University and Nyeri County will equip community members to recognise mental health needs, strengthen suicide prevention, address dementia and generate local evidence to shape health services.

Mental Health : What if a community health promoter could identify someone in distress and help them access care before their situation becomes a crisis? And what if local research could help a county determine which mental health interventions work best for its people?

These are among the questions the County Government of Nyeri and Aga Khan University’s Brain and Mind Institute seek to address through a five-year strategic partnership that aims to bring mental health and brain health closer to the communities where people live, work and grow older.

The partnership, established through a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) signed on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, will combine the Brain and Mind Institute’s research and academic expertise with the county’s healthcare delivery structures to strengthen prevention, early identification, community-based interventions and access to appropriate care.

At its heart is a recognition that mental health cannot be addressed by hospitals and specialists alone. Families, schools, workplaces, faith communities and local health workers all have a role to play in creating communities where people can recognise mental health challenges, seek help without fear of stigma and receive appropriate support.

Speaking at the signing ceremony, Prof Zul Merali, Founding Director of the Brain and Mind Institute at Aga Khan University, said that research must go beyond generating knowledge and must translate into practical interventions that make a meaningful difference in people’s lives. “What makes this partnership particularly important is the County Government of Nyeri’s forward-thinking commitment to making mental health a priority. By working together, we can take research into communities, understand their needs and develop solutions that respond to the realities people face.”

A key feature of the partnership will be the establishment of the Nyeri Living Lab , which will bring researchers, county health teams and communities together to identify local challenges, develop and test interventions, and establish what works in the county’s specific context. Speaking on the initiative, the Governor Dr. Mutahi Kahiga, EGH, highlighted the importance of investing in mental health, noting that the county continues to grapple with mental health challenges affecting some of its residents and is keen to find sustainable, locally relevant solutions. He emphasised that research will be central to this effort, providing the evidence needed to guide county decisions and ensure that interventions respond to the actual needs of communities.

Rather than relying solely on approaches developed elsewhere, the Living Lab will create opportunities to generate evidence within Nyeri and translate that knowledge into practical improvements in healthcare delivery.

For residents, one of the most immediate areas of focus will be strengthening the ability of communities to recognise and respond to mental health needs.

Through Mental Health First Response training, county staff, teachers, faith leaders and other community members will be equipped with knowledge and skills to recognise when someone may need support, respond appropriately and help connect them to care. Community Health Promoters will also receive additional training and support to identify potential mental health concerns earlier and facilitate referrals to appropriate services.

The approach recognises that people experiencing mental health challenges may first turn to someone they know and trust, rather than a health facility. Strengthening the capacity of these community networks could help reduce delays in accessing care and make mental health support more accessible.

The partnership will also address the needs of different population groups across the lifespan. Its priorities will include suicide prevention, mental health, healthy ageing and dementia, alongside the social and environmental factors that influence brain health and wellbeing.

“Addressing mental health issues requires an integrated approach that goes beyond treating illness to prioritising prevention, early identification and timely intervention. Evidence shows the importance of bringing mental health support closer to communities, where challenges can be recognised early and people connected to appropriate care. Through this partnership, we have an opportunity to use research to strengthen community-based interventions and support a health system that is better equipped to prevent mental ill-health and respond to people’s needs at every stage of life,” said Prof Lukoye Atwoli, Deputy Director, Brain and Mind Institute and Dean Medical College, EA, Aga Khan University,

The partnership will place research at the centre of efforts to improve mental health service delivery in Nyeri. The two institutions will work together to understand local needs, evaluate interventions and strengthen the use of evidence in health planning and policy development.
