Kenyan music veteran Bahati has proudly introduced his eldest son, Morgan Bahati, to the music world, with the teenager dropping his debut single, Zoza, on November 20, 2026.

The song, which carries the subtitle Nikifika 18, premiered at noon on the Bahati Kenya YouTube channel, marking a significant milestone for the family.

The timing was no coincidence: Bahati has also confirmed he is stepping away from active music to concentrate on his bid to become the Member of Parliament for Mathare.

The wait appears to have been worth it. Bahati was clearly delighted with what he saw, noting that Morgan came across as composed, fluent and confident, even managing to outdo his father&#039;s English.

The gospel-turned-pop star, who has been a prominent name in Kenyan entertainment for over a decade, is now setting his sights on the Mathare constituency, taking a sabbatical from his music career to pursue his political ambitions.

Handing Morgan the spotlight at this particular moment feels deliberate: a passing of the torch as the elder Bahati pivots to a new chapter.

Morgan, still in high school, now steps into the public eye on his own terms, with a debut that his father has been carefully nurturing behind the scenes.

Whether Morgan can carve out his own identity beyond his famous surname remains to be seen, but the foundation has clearly been laid with great care.

Elsewhere, Bahati denied duping Kenyans with a wedding during an appearance on NTV&#039;s Fixing The Nation, explaining that his hyped "Bahati&#039;s Big Day" campaign was a deliberate month-long marketing strategy to build demand and launch his 2027 Mathare MP bid.

He disclosed that the event raised KSh 10 million , with contributions varying widely, which was directed toward his Bahati Cares initiative supporting families nationwide.

Bahati maintained that the public only assumed it was a wedding because he left the nature of the day open, insisting the attention generated proved his reach as an entertainer and connection to Mathare.
