Monthly grant: German embassy asks Kenyans to apply for fully funded ITK course

The German Embassy in Nairobi has called on Kenyan sports coaches and physical education teachers to apply for the 2027 International Coaching Course (ITK), a fully funded postgraduate programme hosted by the Faculty of Sport Science at Leipzig University in Germany.

The programme runs in a hybrid format from June to December 2027, with online modules scheduled for June, July, and December, and an in-person residential phase at Leipzig University between August and November 2027.

It is financed by the Federal Foreign Office and the Ministry of Science, Culture and Tourism of the Free State of Saxony, as part of Germany&#039;s cultural promotion abroad.

Participants choose one of four specialisations delivered in different languages: Sports for the Disabled in English, Football in French, Kids&#039; Sports U12 in Spanish, and Basketball in Arabic.

Supporting modules cover general movement and exercise science, sports medicine fundamentals, sports didactics, project management, strength training, and inclusion in sports.

The funding package covers rent, health and accident insurance, tuition fees, and a public transport ticket in Leipzig.

These costs are deducted before the monthly grant is disbursed, with participants receiving the remaining balance as a living stipend.

The one expense participants must cover themselves is travel to and from Leipzig.

The ITK targets sports professionals from developing countries on the OECD&#039;s DAC list of ODA recipients, a category that includes Kenya.

Applicants must be aged 40 or below and hold an academic degree in sports.

Prior experience as active athletes, physical education teachers, or coaches is required, alongside a demonstrated capacity to handle physical and mental demands.

The application process involves two steps: registering through the programme&#039;s online portal and completing the required forms, then submitting all documents to the German Embassy in Nairobi.

The deadline for documents to reach Leipzig University is December 15, 2026, meaning Kenyan applicants should submit their materials to the embassy well ahead of that date to allow time for processing and transmission.

The German Embassy in Nairobi directed interested candidates to the link in its social media bio and the QR code on its post for further details and to begin the application process.

In other news, the European Union is offering Kenyan graduates fully funded master&#039;s scholarships under the Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters programme, providing a monthly stipend of €1,400 (approximately KSh 196,000) for up to 24 months.

The scholarship covers tuition and participation fees, health insurance, and contributions towards travel and relocation costs, with students studying at university consortia across multiple European countries.

Applications for the 2027–2029 intake are open through individual programme portals, with deadlines ranging from December 2026 to February 2027, including Neurasmus on December 1, EMMIR on December 20, GLOBE on January 4 and InterMaths on February 17.

Applicants must hold a bachelor&#039;s degree or recognised equivalent and submit documents such as academic transcripts, certificates, a CV, motivation letter and academic references, although individual programmes may impose additional requirements.
