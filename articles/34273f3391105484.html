83pc of maternal deaths reviewed linked to delays in hospital care

WhatsAppFacebookX

NAIROBI,Kenya Nov 24 &#8211; Delays in receiving medical attention at health facilities were identified in 83 per cent of maternal deaths reviewed in a Ministry of Health surveillance dataset, exposing persistent gaps in staffing, essential medicines and emergency response in Kenya’s healthcare system.

The findings, presented by University of Nairobi researcher Dr Njoki Kimani during a Kenya Editors Guild forum in Nairobi on November 19, point to weaknesses in the quality and timeliness of care even as more women deliver in health facilities.

The analysis also found that 71 per cent of the maternal deaths involved delays in deciding to seek medical attention, highlighting challenges that begin before women reach hospitals.

“We know that 71 per cent of deaths were due to the first delay and 83 per cent were due to delay within the facility,” Kimani said.

The findings examined three categories of delays: deciding to seek medical care, reaching a health facility and receiving appropriate treatment after arrival. Some deaths involved more than one category of delay.
