Australia Introduces New Mandatory Visa Law Banning Visitors From Visa Hopping From November 28

Australia will enforce a sweeping immigration restriction from November 28, 2026, barring most visitor visa holders from applying to switch to other visa categories while remaining in the country.

The changes were formalised through the Migration Amendment (Preventing Further Stay for Visitor Visas) Regulations 2026, signed by Governor-General Sam Mostyn on November 12, 2026, under the Migration Act 1958.

The regulations amend the existing Migration Regulations 1994.

The amendment makes Condition 8503, known as the "No Further Stay" condition, mandatory across three visitor visa categories:

Previously, the condition applied automatically to some Subclass 600 streams but could be applied at the immigration minister&#039;s discretion in others.

The new regulations remove that flexibility and standardise the restriction across all three visa types.

Under Condition 8503, a visitor generally cannot lodge a valid onshore application for any other substantive visa, with the exception of a protection visa, unless the minister grants a waiver.

The explanatory statement accompanying the regulations confirms this, stating that the condition "provides that the holder will not, after entering Australia, be entitled to be granted a substantive visa, other than a protection visa, while the holder remains in Australia."

This effectively closes the door on visitors attempting to transition onshore to employer-sponsored, student, partner or skilled migration pathways after arrival.

The Australian Government says the amendments are designed to ensure visitor visas serve their intended purpose: facilitating short-term stays.

The changes aim to "facilitate short-term visits and limit onshore visa hopping and pathways that facilitate extended onshore residence."

The government also expects the regulations to contribute to managing overall migration levels and reducing net overseas migration, while discouraging non-genuine applicants from using visitor visas as a gateway to successive temporary visa applications.

The new conditions apply only to applications lodged on or after November 28, 2026.

People who already hold valid visitor visas will not be retrospectively affected, nor will applicants who submit their applications before the commencement date.

The government confirmed it will retain the power to waive Condition 8503 in qualifying circumstances under the existing provisions of subregulation 2.05 of the Migration Regulations.

Waivers are not automatic and must be applied for individually.

Foreign nationals planning to visit Australia are advised to determine their intended immigration pathway before travelling.

Those considering employment, education or long-term residence in Australia should pursue the appropriate visa category from outside the country rather than assuming they can make the switch after arrival.

In other news, Australia will introduce stricter assessments of Bridging Visa B (BVB) applications linked to employer-sponsored visa pathways from November 30 , 2026, requiring foreign workers to provide stronger evidence that overseas travel is necessary.

The changes affect applicants awaiting decisions on Skills in Demand (Subclass 482), Employer Nomination Scheme (Subclass 186) and Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Subclass 494) visas, as well as their included family members.

Applicants will need to provide specific travel dates, itineraries and supporting documents such as medical records, business invitations or evidence of family events, with general holiday or family visit explanations potentially insufficient on their own.

The BVB application fee stands at AUD 575 (approximately KSh 48,000), while applications lodged before November 30 will continue under the existing assessment approach.
