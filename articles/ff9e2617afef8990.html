Ichaweri vs Caledonia: Comparing Uhuru Kenyatta's Village and Nairobi Homes

Few properties in Kenya carry as much weight as the two homes that have come to define the Kenyatta family legacy: a rural compound in Ichaweri and a city mansion in Nairobi known as Caledonia.

Ichaweri sits along Kenyatta Road in Gatundu South, Kiambu County , and has been central to the Kenyatta family&#039;s public identity for generations.

Jomo Kenyatta used it to host Cabinet meetings and receive state visitors, and it was within this compound that more than 600 schoolchildren gathered in 1963 to select Kenya&#039;s national anthem.

The property stands out as the largest residential compound in the village, marked by a V-shaped entrance flanked by the coat of arms and two stone lions, with uniformed guards maintaining a constant presence at the gate.

A new mansion inside the compound was started in 2018 and was reportedly privately funded, while Jomo&#039;s original house within the same grounds also underwent renovation.

Uhuru has continued to use the compound for significant occasions, including hosting high-profile meetings.

Just across the road, however, the contrast is stark. A local shopping centre facing the family home features only a handful of shops, a few bars, butcheries, kinyozis and M-Pesa outlets.

No restaurant operates there, and there is no church nearby apart from a private chapel inside the compound.

The road to the home was only tarmacked during Uhuru&#039;s presidency, having suffered years of potholes and broken streetlights. In March 2023, 30 security officers were deployed to the property following an invasion of the nearby Northlands farm.

In Nairobi, the Caledonia property on Dennis Pritt Road tells a different story.

The house shares a fence with State House and is divided into two wings, one featuring a swimming pool and the other a helipad.

It was originally bequeathed to Uhuru by Jomo, who had himself received the property from the Aga Khan family, reportedly gifted a week before Kenya&#039;s independence in 1963.

After Uhuru assumed the presidency, the original bungalow was demolished and replaced with a modern mansion.

The former first family temporarily relocated to State House during construction, which was heavily guarded by the GSU.

The cost of the rebuild has been widely cited in media reports as Sh700 million, though this remains an estimate rather than a confirmed figure.

Reported security features at the property include bulletproof windows, a concrete perimeter wall, an electric fence and an alarm system.

Since Uhuru left office, Caledonia has evolved into something beyond a private home.

After negotiations with the Ruto administration, the property was earmarked to serve as the Office of the Fourth President and later became the headquarters of the Uhuru Kenyatta Institute.

In July 2023, Uhuru stated he was not living there due to ongoing remodelling works.

One home holds the family&#039;s roots and village memory; the other holds proximity to the corridors of national power.
