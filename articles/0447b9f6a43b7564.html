Saudi Arabia Travel Ban on Kenyans Holds Even as UAE Reopens Doors to Ugandans

Saudi Arabia is holding firm on its travel and visa suspension against Kenya even as the United Arab Emirates has begun reopening its doors to travellers from Uganda , exposing a sharp divide in how Gulf nations are responding to the Ebola outbreak that swept through East Africa.

The Saudi restrictions, which took effect on October 30, 2026, bar Saudi nationals from travelling to Kenya and block entry to anyone who has been in Kenya or any other Ebola-affected country within the 21 days before arriving in the kingdom.

The measures apply regardless of whether a traveller transits through a third country.

Saudi authorities tied the restrictions to their own national health assessments, triggered after Kenya confirmed an imported Ebola case on October 6, 2026, involving a traveller who had entered from the Democratic Republic of the Congo via Uganda.

Kenya&#039;s last confirmed case was recorded on October 19, and the final patient was discharged on November 11, 2026. A 42-day countdown to an official Ebola-free declaration began on November 12 and is due to conclude on December 23, 2026, if no new cases arise.

Kenya&#039;s Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale has described the Saudi measures as "excessive" and "not backed by the current epidemiological situation".

The Foreign Ministry has characterised them as a "blanket, punitive measure" unsupported by evidence. Diplomatic talks with Riyadh are ongoing, but no shift in Saudi policy has been announced.

The UAE had imposed a comparable suspension in June 2026 covering Uganda, the DRC and South Sudan. On November 11, 2026, its National Emergency, Crisis and Disasters Management Authority, acting in coordination with health and identity authorities, lifted the restriction for Uganda alone .

UAE lifts visa ban on East African country, imposes strict 21-day entry condition

Ugandan nationals can now apply for UAE visas and gain entry provided at least 21 days have passed since leaving Uganda.

Students, embassy staff and diplomatic passport holders are exempt from the waiting period, while restrictions on the DRC and South Sudan remain active.

Uganda&#039;s Foreign Affairs Minister, Ambassador Adonia Ayebare, publicly thanked the UAE government for reconsidering the restrictions.

The continued Saudi ban carries real consequences.

More than 300 Kenyans who travelled to Saudi Arabia in search of work were turned back at entry points in early November.

The kingdom has long been a major destination for Kenyan domestic workers, healthcare professionals, drivers and construction labourers, and remittances from Saudi Arabia were already under strain in 2026.

The situation differs markedly with the UAE, which has not imposed a blanket ban on Kenyan nationals.

The Emirates recently extended its visa-on-arrival programme to include eligible Kenyans, keeping that corridor open.

Kenya&#039;s diplomatic path forward rests significantly on the countdown to the December 23 Ebola-free declaration, which would give Nairobi a firmer basis to press Riyadh for a formal review.

Saudi Arabia has not signalled, however, that a Kenyan declaration would automatically result in the lifting of its restrictions.
