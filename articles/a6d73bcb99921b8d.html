Investment and financial sector leaders have called on Kenyans to rethink how they build, protect, and pass on wealth, sharing eight practical tips at the Abojani 6th Economic Empowerment Conference held at the Radisson Blu Hotel in Upper Hill, Nairobi, on November 21, 2026.

The conference, themed "Invest Intelligently", brought together representatives from banking, insurance, asset management, capital markets, and entrepreneurship to guide individuals, families, and businesses on making sound financial decisions.

Don&#039;t rely on one salary, business, or property; spread your wealth across different investments.

Interest-bearing accounts alone won&#039;t build long-term wealth; consider equities and ownership in strong companies.

Develop a deep, curious understanding of financial markets before putting money in.

Don&#039;t follow the crowd; stick to your strategy even when others are panicking or chasing trends.

Build a financial safety net through insurance, emergency funds, and diversified assets.

Old age is inevitable; begin investing for retirement now rather than postponing it.

Think about how your assets will pass to your beneficiaries and structure them to avoid unintended taxes.

Investing is a journey, not a destination; consistent participation in markets like the NSE builds wealth over time.

In other news, the UK government has launched plans for Forge Africa, a pan-African investment platform headquartered in Kenya and backed by £40 million (approximately KSh 6.81 billion) to help 100 businesses attract long-term investors .

UK Minister for Africa Kirsty McNeill announced the initiative during a visit to Octavia Carbon&#039;s Project Hummingbird in Gilgil on November 19, 2026, with Kenya set to host its initial rollout before expansion into other African markets.

Separately, the UK unveiled a new phase of the Kenya Catalytic Jobs Fund targeting technology, artificial intelligence, biosciences and women-led enterprises, with an ambition to mobilise £50 million (approximately KSh 8.51 billion) for more than 500 start-ups by March 2029.
