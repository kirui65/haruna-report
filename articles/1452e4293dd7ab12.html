Ruto and Oburu Hold Talks to Strengthen Broad Based Government Pact

WhatsAppFacebookX

President William Ruto has held high level consultations with Orange Democratic Movement Party Leader and Siaya Senator Oburu Oginga to cement the Broad Based Government partnership ahead of the 2027 General Election.

In a statement posted on his official social media platforms on Tuesday afternoon, Ruto announced that the discussions focused on deepening political cooperation between ODM, the ruling United Democratic Alliance, and the wider Kenya Kwanza coalition.

"Met Orange Democratic Movement Party Leader and Siaya Senator Oburu Oginga to discuss various matters, including strengthening the Broad Based Government partnership between ODM and the United Democratic Alliance and Kenya Kwanza," Ruto stated.

The Head of State emphasised that both political formations remain committed to placing national stability and public welfare above partisan interests.

"We underscored the importance of sustained political cooperation, national cohesion, equity and a shared commitment to putting the interests of the country and the welfare of Kenyans above all else. Kenya's progress must always remain our common priority," he added.

Tuesday's engagement signals the steady return of the 83 year old ODM leader to active national politics following a two month medical absence that sparked widespread speculation and false death rumours online.

Oburu, who travelled to the United Kingdom on September 10 for medical treatment before being admitted to The Nairobi Hospital in October, only resumed his parliamentary duties in the Senate last Thursday where he received a standing ovation from fellow lawmakers.

During his emotional return to the Senate chamber, the veteran politician dismissed the online propaganda surrounding his health and declared that he was determined to complete his political mission.

His direct talks with Ruto also come at a critical moment for the ruling alliance as UDA and ODM strategists move to harmonise grassroots structures, address contentious zoning demands, and ward off mounting opposition pressure from Rigathi Gachagua's Democracy for the Citizens Party and Edwin Sifuna's Linda Mwananchi Movement.
