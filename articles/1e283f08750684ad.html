Gachagua opens DCP’s Nairobi office and vows free nominations ahead of 2027 polls

WhatsAppFacebookX

News summary

The Mt Kenya Times The Democracy for the Citizens Party leader used yesterday&#8217;s Nairobi launch to signal grassroots intent, promising smart-card membership, open nominations and no favoured candidates. By James Mwangi Rigathi Gachagua, leader of the Democracy for the Citizens Party, yesterday officially opened the party&#8217;s Nairobi County office, using the occasion to announce plans for a smart-card membership system and to pledge that the party&#8217;s 2027 nominations would be open, competitive and free of preferential treatment for any aspirant. The launch, held at a party meeting in Nairobi, marked the latest step in DCP&#8217;s accelerating effort to build a credible organisational presence in the capital ahead of what is shaping up to be one of Kenya&#8217;s most consequential general elections. &#8220;The DCP party will hold free and fair nominations, with smart cards for party members to be introduced soon,&#8221; Gachagua told those gathered. &#8220;There are no special individuals or favoured aspirants. I encourage all aspirants across the country to put their best foot forward and strengthen their grassroots networks ahead of the nominations in March.&#8221; The opening of a dedicated Nairobi County office carries significance beyond the symbolic. Nairobi is Kenya&#8217;s most populous county and its most politically volatile, a battleground where no single party has historically commanded undisputed loyalty and where the urban vote has repeatedly proven decisive in shaping national outcomes. For DCP, which has built its strongest foundations in Mount Kenya and Maasailand, establishing a visible institutional footprint in the capital represents a deliberate signal of national ambition. Gachagua was direct about what the new office represents. &#8220;Today, I was honoured to officially open our new DCP party office for Nairobi County,&#8221; he said. &#8220;This office is a statement of our commitment to serving Kenyans and strengthening our grassroots presence across the county and the country. We must continue listening to the people and acting on their wishes, as enshrined in Chapter One of the Constitution of Kenya: all sovereign power belongs to the people.&#8221; His remarks on party growth carried the confidence of a leader who believes the political tide is running in his direction. &#8220;Our party is the party of the moment and the future. We are growing every single day as we work to earn the mandate of the great people of Kenya,&#8221; he said, closing with a phrase that has become something of a DCP rallying call: &#8220;Green is wealth. Green is the future.&#8221; The timing of the Nairobi launch is not incidental. With the IEBC&#8217;s published electoral calendar placing party primary nominations between 17 March and 10 April 2027, and membership list submissions required by 16 March, the window for organisational preparation is narrowing faster than many in the political class appear to have fully absorbed. Gachagua&#8217;s announcement of a smart-card membership system — a move that would give DCP a verifiable, digitalised record of its membership base — suggests the party is thinking carefully about the administrative requirements of a credible nomination process, not merely the political theatre of rallies and launches. DCP has grown rapidly since its establishment. The party currently has three elected Members of County Assembly and one Member of Parliament among its ranks, and has attracted figures of political weight from both the Mount Kenya and Maasailand regions. It is widely regarded as one of the most consequential new political forces heading into 2027, with Gachagua himself described by analysts as a potential kingmaker whose endorsement could prove structurally decisive for any presidential candidate seeking a majority. The question of whether Gachagua will seek the presidency himself or transfer his support to another candidate — including DCP Secretary-General John Methu, whose name he has previously floated — remains publicly unresolved. Yesterday&#8217;s event offered no fresh clarity on that front. What it did offer was a portrait of a party leader investing seriously in the machinery of political organisation: offices, membership systems, open nominations and a constitutional framing of popular sovereignty that speaks to voters wary of elite deal-making. Whether DCP can translate that organisational investment into electoral dominance across a city as complex and competitive as Nairobi will be one of the defining tests of the party&#8217;s national credibility in the months ahead. &nbsp; Some of the moments as captured in pictures
