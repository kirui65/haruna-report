"No other way": CS Murkomen issues new order ahead of police recruitment

Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen has urged Kenyans not to dispose of land or other assets to fund bribes for police jobs, warning that the upcoming recruitment exercise will be conducted transparently and that anyone involved in irregularities will face stern consequences.

Murkomen made the remarks on Saturday, November 21, while speaking at the Amaya Youth Empowerment Initiative in Tiaty, Baringo County. His message came two days after the National Police Service (NPS) formally opened applications for the constable intake scheduled for Thursday, December 10.

"We have announced police recruitment. It will be fair and transparent. We don&#039;t want corruption in it," he said.

Murkomen&#039;s Warning to Families and Brokers

The CS directed his caution squarely at young people from poor and rural backgrounds, who are frequently targeted by brokers promising guaranteed placement in exchange for money.

"No one should sell their land or other property to raise money for bribe. We will invite observers to recruitment centres. When the recruitment ends all who did not make it should be informed right there," he said.

Murkomen further instructed the NPS to publicly announce the names of successful candidates at each centre so that independent observers and members of the public can verify the outcome. He added that gender representation must be factored into the process.

"There is no shortcut. It will be done transparently to ensure those who qualify are recruited and those who don&#039;t are informed immediately. And gender must also be considered so that our girls also get in," he said.

The NPS has separately warned the public against paying anyone who promises a guaranteed spot, noting that the exercise is free and that corruption and the submission of false information during recruitment are criminal offences. The service urged anyone aware of suspected malpractice to report it at the nearest police station.

December Recruitment: What Applicants Should Know

The NPS recruitment notice, issued on November 19, states that the exercise will run from 8am to 5pm on December 10 at 423 designated centres nationwide, and candidates must arrive by 8am.

Eligible applicants must be Kenyan citizens aged between 18 and 28, hold a valid national identity card, and have attained at least a D+ in KCSE, including a D+ in English or Kiswahili. Physical requirements are a minimum height of 5 ft 8 in for men and 5 ft 3 in for women, and candidates must be medically fit with no criminal record. Female candidates must not be pregnant at the time of recruitment or during training.

Application forms are available through the NPS, Kenya Police , Administration Police and DCI websites, as well as at Huduma Centres, and candidates are required to present both originals and copies of their documents at the recruitment centres.

This is not the first time Murkomen has issued such directives. Ahead of the November 2025 intake of 10,000 constables, he similarly called for the prompt public announcement of results and threatened legal action against anyone caught paying or receiving bribes. The government has since reported recruiting a combined 13,860 officers, comprising 10,000 police constables and 3,860 prison officers.
