Kenya opposition snubs IEBC and gives commission a December deadline

WhatsAppFacebookX

The People&#8217;s Coalition on Electoral Reforms warns that if the electoral body fails to engage meaningfully before January, it will take its case directly to Kenyans — by all means necessary.

The People&#8217;s Coalition on Electoral Reforms rejected the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission&#8217;s invitation to talks on Wednesday, with former Chief Justice David Maraga and former National Assembly Speaker Justin Muturi leading a united opposition front that includes former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua, People&#8217;s Liberation Party leader Martha Karua, former Interior Cabinet Secretary Fred Matiang&#8217;i, Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro, and several other senior figures.

The coalition was unequivocal about its reasons. &#8220;Independence is not a licence for IEBC to operate in isolation or disregard stakeholders, particularly political parties,&#8221; Maraga said, accusing the commission of convening meetings designed to validate its existing position rather than address substantive electoral concerns. The leaders condemned what they described as President William Ruto&#8217;s interference in the commission&#8217;s affairs, insisting he has no standing to speak on its behalf.

At the core of the dispute is Section 39 of the Elections Act — amended so many times, the coalition argues, that Kenya currently has no coherent legal framework for transmitting and announcing presidential results. The opposition is demanding electronic transmission and mandatory public livestreaming of results as non-negotiable preconditions for a credible 2027 poll.

The ultimatum is now formally on the table: engage meaningfully before December, or face the coalition taking its demands directly to Kenyans from January through every democratic avenue available.

The IEBC has been warned. The clock is running.
