From Cabinet to governor: Ruku’s City Hall gamble

WhatsAppFacebookX

Public Service Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku during an interview at his office at Harambee House, Nairobi on November 11, 2026.

The battle for the United Democratic Alliance (UDA) ticket in Nairobi is emerging as an early test of President William Ruto’s ability to manage competing political ambitions within his own party, with two high-profile aspirants seeking to succeed Governor Johnson Sakaja and both positioning themselves around the Head of State.

Public Service Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku and Kasarani MP Ronald Karauri have declared interest in the August 2027 governorship contest, setting up a potentially difficult nomination battle for a party seeking to reclaim City Hall following Mr Sakaja’s defection to the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM).

On Saturday, Mr Ruku officially launched his bid at the UDA Party headquarters, where he was received by a section of party officials and other grassroots leaders. He was also accompanied by UDA ward representatives in Nairobi.

Public Service Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku gestures during an interview at his office at Harambee House, Nairobi on November 11, 2026.

He has presented himself as an experienced administrator capable of transforming the capital. Mr Karauri is seeking to build a citywide campaign from his parliamentary base in Kasarani, backed by a reception at UDA headquarters that brought together some of the party’s senior figures.

The emerging rivalry is complicated by the competing claims of presidential support. Mr Karauri was welcomed into UDA by National Assembly Majority Leader Kimani Ichung’wah and UDA Secretary-General Hassan Omar, with Mr Ichung’wah revealing that President Ruto had personally called him to facilitate the legislator’s reception into the party. Both leaders were, however, absent when Mr Ruku launched his bid at the party headquarters.

Mr Ruku said his decision to contest on the UDA ticket was informed by his association with the President’s leadership, although he has been reluctant to discuss whether he has held direct conversations with the Head of State about the matter.

“I identify with UDA, though I was elected to Parliament on the Democratic Party. The Head of State has transformed this country and I fully identify with UDA ideals. The transformation that we want to achieve in Nairobi and impact can only be achieved through UDA,” he said.

Asked whether he had consulted the President about his transition from the Cabinet to City Hall, Mr Ruku told the Daily Nation, “Let’s leave the President out of the discussions.”

The competing ambitions expose a dilemma for the ruling party on how to accommodate two politicians who both want to be its face in Nairobi without creating divisions that could undermine its efforts to regain the county’s top seat.

Mr Sakaja’s departure has created an opening for UDA, but the party’s attempt to capitalise on it is already facing internal competition over who should inherit its political machinery.

Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja addresses ODM supporters during a mega rally in memory of Raila Odinga at Jacaranda Grounds on October 31, 2026.

Mr Karauri’s entry into UDA on November 9 was accompanied by a public show of support from senior party officials, raising questions about whether the event was simply a welcome ceremony or an early indication of the party leadership’s preferred candidate.

Mr Ichung’wah welcomed the legislator and described him as the party’s candidate to challenge ODM in Nairobi, remarks that appeared to place Mr Karauri ahead of other aspirants who had already expressed interest in the ticket.

Mr Ruku has already hit the ground running and is already building a grassroots machinery that will help him capture the seat. He has held public participation meetings in South B, Kasarani and Woodley before the launch on Saturday.

“I have lived in this city since I was 18 years old. My children, all of them, have been born in this city, five of them. My children go to school in this city,” he said.

He added that having lived in different parts of Nairobi, he understands the challenges confronting residents in both established estates and informal settlements.

“Whether you are living in Kawangware, or in Doonholm, or in whatever corner of the city, these are problems which can be taken care of, if we get it right as far as the leadership of the city is concerned,” he said.

He is also questioning Mr Karauri’s development record in Kasarani and his contribution to Parliament. The Cabinet Secretary argued that the MP should first demonstrate what he has achieved in his constituency before seeking a bigger political office.

He claimed that residents of Kasarani were dissatisfied with Mr Karauri’s performance and questioned whether the legislator could secure another parliamentary term.

“Go to Kasarani and ask people who live in Kasarani whether they can vote him back as a member of parliament and they will tell you a big no,” he said.

Mr Ruku further accused the legislator of seeking the gubernatorial seat as an escape route from his constituency’s political challenges.

“Running for a senior position cannot be an escape route to run away from your misdeed and to run away from your omission or errors of omission or commission,” he said.

Mr Ruku is positioning himself as an experienced administrator who can transfer his national government experience to the management of Nairobi, citing his time as Mbeere North MP and his current Cabinet position.

“I can manage a ministry which covers the entire Republic of Kenya,” he said, arguing that the experience has prepared him to manage the complex affairs of the capital.

Mr Karauri, on the other hand, brings parliamentary experience from Kasarani and a business background, while his reception at UDA headquarters has given his entry public visibility among the party’s senior leadership.

The biggest challenge for UDA could be managing the ambitions of its aspirants without triggering defections or independent candidacies.

Mr Ruku has already indicated that he is prepared to accept the outcome of a competitive nomination process, even if Mr Karauri emerges as the party’s candidate.

Kasarani Member of Parliament Ronald Karauri.

“If we go for nominations and I am not successful, I accept and I will support whoever will win the nominations for UDA,” he said.

Mr Karauri, however, has also signalled that he is determined to appear on the ballot, saying he could pursue an independent candidature if he believes the party’s nomination process has been manipulated.

“I’m hoping that we will have a transparent nomination process. When we get to around March next year when the nominations are done, in case again I’m played like I was done in Jubilee Party, then I will run as an independent candidate,” Mr Karauri said, adding that he must be on the ballot by all means possible.

Unlike CS Ruku, who declared that he would support the winner of the nominations, Mr Karauri maintained that his minimum requirement will be to be on the ballot, where voters would decide his fate.
