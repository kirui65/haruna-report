Munyiri ditches diplomacy to try his hand in elective politics

WhatsAppFacebookX

Former Kenyan High Commissioner to India Ambassador Peter Munyiri has thrown his hat into the ring in the quest to succeed Dr Mutahi Kahiga as the next Governor of Nyeri.

If he pushes past the DCP nominations, Munyiri will have to battle it out with former New Democrats founder Dr Thuo Mathenge, Senator Wahome Wamatinga, Kenya Patriots Party leader Dr Agnes Kagure and current Deputy Governor Warui Kinanire for the highly coveted seat.

Ambassador Munyiri, a trained economist, has spent close to four decades serving at various capacities in several banks and says his first priority would be to transform the agricultural potential of the county.

Munyiri, who says he officially left his New Delhi office on November 14 to try his luck in the political arena, has listed value addition on agricultural produce, youth unemployment, health care and fiscal discipline as his rallying call in his quest to be elected.

“My vision is to transform the fortunes of Nyeri County through practical solutions that can make the lives of our people better. To achieve this monumental task, we must tap into our agricultural and human potential which we possess as a county. Nyeri is endowed with tea, coffee, milk and hard-working people and, if harnessed well, can change the face of the county for the better,” he told Inooro TV on Tuesday.

Among the steps he hopes to undertake in the event he succeeds in his gubernatorial aspiration is to boost milk production by coming up with incentives that will bring down the cost of animal feed.

To attain this, Munyiri said he plans to work alongside other stakeholders, including relevant state bodies, to push for policies that will ensure animal feeds are sold at subsidised costs.

He cited this as one way of wooing many farmers to take up dairy farming as an enterprise.

In addition, the former envoy has promised to invest in strengthening the cooperative movement to enable farmers to get an avenue for marketing their agricultural products besides accessing credit facilities to expand production.

“We need to transform Nyeri into an agricultural production hub where what we produce is of high quality. We cannot continue harvesting and marketing our coffee in its crude form while we can do value addition right here. To attain this, we also need to invest in more cottage industries and do away with middlemen who buy agricultural products from farmers at throwaway prices only to go ahead and make a kill out of this once they get to the market,” he said.

On youth unemployment, the former envoy hopes to ride on the emerging AI wave by creating IT call centres across the county where young people can work from.

He noted that with the ever-shrinking space in the formal job market, counties need to devise alternative strategies of tackling the current unemployment crisis facing the country through achievable targets.

Munyiri has also promised to come up with a business support programme in which all students graduating from the local technical and vocational training institutions get a fully equipped working tool kit and grants as an incentive to venture into self-employment.

“To address the unemployment level in Nyeri, I intend to open up 30,000 job opportunities for our youth in selected IT call centres within the first three years upon assuming office as governor. The IT sector is an area that has not fully been exploited to date despite the fact that it presents the most ideal opportunity for our youth in the face of the ever-shrinking formal employment in the country,” he pointed out.

To win the war against illicit brew and substance abuse in the county, Munyiri has called for closer working relationships between the national and county government in eradicating the vice.

He said the war against counterfeit brews in the larger Mount Kenya region can never be handled singlehandedly and will require both county officials and state agencies such as NGAO and security agents for the problem to be eradicated in totality.
