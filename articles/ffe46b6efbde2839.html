Seven Kenyan Counties Present Sustainable Cooling Projects for Investment

WhatsAppFacebookX

Projects span cold chains, healthcare refrigeration, dairy cooling and efficient buildings as SEforALL launches a finance-readiness guide.

Seven counties in Kenya presented sustainable cooling projects to government officials, development partners, technology providers and industry representatives at the Sustainable Cooling Marketplace in Kisumu. The event, organized in collaboration with the County Government of Kisumu and supported by Sustainable Energy for All (SEforALL), also marked the launch of SEforALL’s Investment Prospectus Guide for Heat and Cooling Action Plans.

Representatives from Homa Bay, Kilifi, Kisumu, Makueni, Nakuru, Taita Taveta and Tana River identified seven potential projects covering agricultural and fisheries cold chains, healthcare refrigeration, dairy cooling and energy-efficient buildings. Proposed installations include solar-powered ice-making plants, fish cold rooms, solar cold storage for fishing communities and a vegetable packhouse with 15 tonnes (approximately 16.5 US tons) of cold storage capacity for African leafy vegetables.

Other proposals include six solar-powered milk-chilling plants, new and replacement vaccine refrigerators and cold chain equipment for healthcare facilities, and the replacement of air conditioners in 30 public classrooms with high-efficiency units. Counties also proposed passive cooling measures, green building design and urban cooling interventions.

The Investment Prospectus Guide provides a five-step process for developing finance-ready projects, covering project definition, stakeholder engagement, costing, financing scenarios, investment documentation and connections with financing partners. Potential funding sources include public budgets, grants, concessional finance and blended finance. Participants identified limited technical capacity, insufficient data and project preparation requirements as key barriers to securing investment.

Kenya’s National Cooling Action Plan, launched in 2023, prioritizes energy-efficient cooling appliances, low-global-warming-potential refrigerants and expanded access to sustainable cooling, including agricultural cold chains. William Melau, Assistant Coordinator of the National Ozone Unit at the Ministry of Environment, Climate Change and Forestry, also highlighted solar-powered cooling, passive cooling and Kenya’s commitments under the Kigali Amendment. SEforALL plans to continue supporting counties in identifying priority projects and engaging potential financiers.
